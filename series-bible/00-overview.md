# THREADBORN — Series Bible / Overview

**Working title:** THREADBORN (सूत्रजात — *Sutrajat*)
**Format:** Webtoon, vertical scroll, full colour
**Languages:** English (primary) + Hindi (Devanagari) per page
**Target length:** 500+ chapters → then a trading card game
**Status:** Chapter 1, Page 1 — in production

---

## The pitch in one line

> Everyone is born owing the world a thread of power. **Ira Sutar was born with hers already taken** —
> and she is the only person alive who can *mend* the weave instead of pulling from it.

## The pitch in a paragraph

The world of **Rangbhoomi** ("the Stage") is not a planet. It is a **Loom** — a weave of reality
called the **Sutra**. Every living being is a thread in it, and every thread is born **owing** the
Loom. At fifteen, a child undergoes the **Unspooling**: their **Sutra-mark** opens and they draw
their first thread of power. The thread is a loan. It must be repaid, and repayment is written on
the skin as **debt-marks**. Spend too much and you **Fray** — you come apart at the edges and become
a monster. The worst cases become **Asura** (demons).

Nine **Sectors** (*Khandas*) divide the world, and each Sector obeys a **different law of pulling** —
fire is burned, water is drowned, shadow is stolen. Nine **Kinds** of being are woven into the Loom:
humans, humankin, demons, elves, goblins, beastkin, wraiths, serpent-born and celestials.

Against all of it stands one fifteen-year-old **Manavkin** thread-mender from the ash-slums of
**Agnikhand**, who draws *nothing* at her Unspooling — and then discovers a **seam** stitched across
her empty palm.

## Why this world supports 500+ chapters

| Lever | How it scales |
|---|---|
| **9 Kinds × 9 Sectors** | 81 base combinations, each with its own culture, art direction and fighting style. A new character is never a repeat. |
| **Debt economy** | Every power use has a visible, escalating cost. Power creep is self-limiting, so fights stay tense for 500 chapters. |
| **Fraying** | Any character can become a monster. No permanent safety. Endless antagonist supply from the existing cast. |
| **The seam** | Ira's origin is a locked box. It can be opened slowly across hundreds of chapters. |
| **The 10th Kind** | **Yantra** (machine-born) is hidden in canon and reserved for a mid-series reveal. |
| **A sealed Sector** | **Shunyakhand** (Void Sector) is off-limits from chapter 1. It is the final act. |

## Why it becomes a good card game later

The card game does not need inventing — it is already the world's physics.

- **Card = a Thread.** Kinds are the card's *race*, Sectors are its *faction/element*.
- **Cost = Debt.** Every card costs debt-marks. Overspending a turn triggers **Fray** — a real board
  mechanic, not flavour text.
- **Mend cards** (Ira's family) are the control archetype: they *cancel* debt rather than pay it.
- **Frayed cards** go to a shared **Fray pile** and can return as enemy Asura.

## Two rejected world concepts (kept for reference)

Both were viable; the Loom was chosen because it makes the card game *free*.

**B. "THE LEDGER" (Karmakosh)** — Every soul is born owing a numeric Debt, written as glowing
counting-marks on the skin. Nine Counting Houses administer the Sectors. Defaulters are repossessed
and become Asura. *Strongest mechanics, weakest visuals* — a debt ledger is hard to draw beautifully.

**C. "SEVEN SEAS OF ASH" (Bhasmasagar)** — The continent is a dead god's corpse. Nine Sectors were
carved from its organs, and the races evolved from whatever fed on it. *Strongest visuals, weakest
mechanics* — no natural resource economy for a card game.

**THREADBORN fuses the best of both:** the Loom gives the visuals and the mythology; **Debt** is kept
as the Loom's core mechanic.
