THE INSPECTOR — निरीक्षक

Status: Recurring institutional figure — the Office with a face First appearance: Chapter 008, Page 001 (first bell, the stall: two clerks, a slate, a sealed case, and him). Named by function in Ch. 009's cast lists; never named by himself. Kind: Manav (मानव) — single-strand Sector: Agnikhand (Ash Sector), up-terrace — the Reckoning Office's compliance floor Age: middle-aged; greying at the temples, freshly shaved, unremarkably fit Occupation: compliance inspector — serves the ordinance, reads it, waits Mark location: none that is ever shown. Where it is, nobody has been told. Ref sheet: inspector-ref.png — 8 panels Ref sheet panels: front · side · back · face · hands (clasped behind his back) · detail (the coiled compliance chain and the blank seal) · kit · action (the doorway at first bell)

Appearance (art continuity)

  • Build: medium, ordinary, comfortable. Not thin like Rekhak, not broad like Bhan. A body that has never had to be the message — so the coat has to be.
  • Face: plain, forgettable, freshly shaved; neat grey hair silvering at the temples. Hooded eyes, wide apart, slow to move. His whole expression is a faint, polite half-smile that does not change with the sentence being delivered. He never looks triumphant, never angry, never bored. He looks patient, and patience is the threat.
  • Hands: always, by default, clasped loosely behind his back. It is not a stance he adopts — he is drawn that way in every panel unless a document is being handed over. When the hands come out, the page has turned.
  • Costume: formal slate-grey Office greatcoat, high plain collar, double row of plain grey buttons, grey waistcoat over a white-collar shirt, grey trousers, plain dark shoes. No ornament, no mend, no crimson. His clothes are the only clothes in the basin that have never been repaired by hand — the same note Rekhak carries, worn by a man who does not need to be frightening.
  • The chain: a coiled metal compliance chain clipped at the left hip — brass-grey links, and exactly one link darkened. It is a tool, not a decoration: he does not wear it, he carries it.
  • The seal: a plain grey wax signet on the right thumb that stamps a blank seal. No device, no letters, no house mark. An Office signature that identifies nothing is the scariest object in room nine, and it is drawn in slot 6 of the ref sheet.
  • Kit: sealed grey case, blank slate tablet, the chain laid straight across a desk, a small brass bell — the bell is how he enters a scene. Kit panel on the ref sheet is the canon layout.
  • Silhouette test: upright grey column, hands invisible behind the back, chain loop at the left hip. He must be recognisable with no face at all.
  • Ash: he does not have any on him. That is the whole point.

Rule: the Inspector is drawn mild. No close-up sneer, no raised voice, no hand on anybody. When the Office finally does something cruel, it is his clerk who does it, and he is on panel watching, hands behind his back.

What he is

The Office's delivery mechanism: he arrives at first bell with a slate, a sealed case and two clerks, and he serves — an ordinance, a summons, an inventory of a door. He never asks for anything he is not entitled to, and he never takes a single thing more than the paper says, which is why the basin cannot hate him cleanly. He is the reason Chapter 009's chain-stop lands on Rekhak's oath and not on Ira's stall: the Office does not retaliate, it codifies, and the Inspector is the hand that hands over the codification.

He is also the only up-terrace man drawn standing in the basin's ash-and-water, at first bell, in working hours, without a subordinate to hold the case.

Voice

Office-flat, courteous, complete sentences, no idiom. He asks a question and then does not speak until it is answered. He thanks people for things they had no choice about. His lines are the shortest in any room because they are the only ones that cannot be argued with:

"A slate. Nothing more." "You are not accused. You are scheduled." "I am required to be here until it is served. I will be quick about it." "That is not mine to decide, which is why I will not decide it."

Continuity notes (do not break)

  1. He is the man from Ch. 005 Page 006 and Ch. 006 Page 008 — the same compliance face, never a new character. Draw the chain, the clasped hands and the blank seal every time.
  2. Never named, never shouted, never hurried. If a panel shows him angry, the panel is wrong.
  3. Never draw his own mark. It is a later reveal; keep it under the coat.
  4. The blank seal is canon. If a seal is being shown at close range elsewhere, it is grey wax with nothing stamped in it.
  5. Later chapters may attach a number of clerks to him (first bell, Ch. 009–010) — the count matters for the Office's arithmetic; keep it exact to the script.
