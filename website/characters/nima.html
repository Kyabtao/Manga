NIMA — नीमा

First appearance: named on Chapter 009, Page 004 (the reader-second's row on the certification sheet); first on panel in Chapter 010, Page 003 (room nine, as the Office's reader). Kind: Kshudra · Age: nineteen · Enrolled: at nine years old, through the hatch, ninth terrace — one forearm, two taps, in the dark, and sent home. Function: Reader-second of the Reckoning Office. The girl the school made and never finished, now seated at the instrument that indexes her own people. Ref sheet: nima-ref.png — 5 panels (front · side · back · face · detail) Ref sheet panels: front · side · back · face · detail (wrist turned inward — the school's braid under an Office cuff)

Visual sheet

  • Silhouette: small and non-descript on purpose — the Office's grey is a size that fits nobody, and she wears it the way a girl wears a coat issued to a taller person. A short braid, pinned flat.
  • Hands: a mender's hands. Callus at the thumb, two old burns, one knuckle thickened by years of teaching herself to hold a needle quietly. No ink stains: she is trained never to write.
  • The tell: she turns her left wrist inward, against her leg or under the desk, whenever a palm is being read or a sleeve is rolled. The braid is the school's, and it is the one thing about her the Office would have to enter if it ever saw it.
  • Face: careful, attentive, deliberately unreadable — the face of a person who has been trained that the finding, not the reason is what gets written down.

Voice

Office-flat, correct, and only occasionally her own. She reads procedures as if they were sentences somebody else wrote, which they are. When she does speak for herself — "If they produce her, do I have to read her?", "Don't write me" — the register changes temperature, because those are the only lines in the building that are hers.

What she is

The Office's translation made flesh: a Kshudra girl taught to count, given a desk and a chain, and never told what the knots were for. She has read nothing but copies since the spring. She assumed the cards were paper, and said so, flatly, in a corridor, to the one person in the basin who could tell her what she had been doing with her afternoons.

She was enrolled by Sutar, M.'s own hand, and does not remember it as anything. She also has the school's second strand — laid at nine years old — which means she is the only person alive who has carried a braid through an Office career without being entered.

Position

Not an ally of the school; not yet an ally of the Office. Her position is structural and it is the sharpest thing in room nine: Office pay depends on her not being declared, so the Office's own reader cannot be entered without losing her wage. She offers the school the only thing she owns — to say no out loud, in the room, on the day she is told to read a hand — and she says plainly that this is the most she has to give anybody.

She is also one of the few people in Agnikhand who has been told, to her face, what her own enrolment says: Nandi handed her her page in a corridor and read it out — ties two threads at once, counts to forty without a slate, will teach. The amended rule three reaches her one chapter before it reaches anybody else.

Ongoing threads

  • The unpaid wage. Being declared costs her the desk, the lantern and the two-bell schedule; being kept in the Office's book is the price of her usefulness. Chapter 011 tests which she prefers.
  • The inward wrist. Her tell has not been noticed on panel yet. When it is, she becomes a line in somebody else's register.
  • The refusal she has already promised. One day when I am told to put a hand on that stand I will say no in the room. The Office has written instructions for exactly that contingency — the schedule requires the keeper to read aloud in the same words at the same time — which means her refusal will be recorded before it is heard.
  • What she and Jadi are building. Nandi's condition gave the school a reader; the corridor gave the reader a sheet with three lines on it; the alley gave her three women learning to count. She is the second school's second teacher, whether or not anybody has told her.
