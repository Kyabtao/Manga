NANDI — नंदी

First appearance: Chapter 006, Page 003 (the Mendery archive) Kind: Kshudra · Age: very old — older than the binding, older than the school's charter Function: Keeper of the Mendery archive. The only other person alive who knows the name on the Roll of Hands.

Visual sheet

  • Silhouette: small, dense, permanently stooped — a woman shaped by forty years of reaching the bottom shelves. Shorter than Kessa. Never drawn standing straight; when she looks up, the spine stays bent and only the neck moves.
  • Eyes: Kshudra cat-eye, catching lamplight as a hard horizontal sliver. This is her identifying feature on panel and her only "magic" — she has no Sight, no thread of her own she uses, no mark.
  • Hands: gnarled, thick-knuckled, permanently stained dark at the ridges from file dust and old thread-wax. Her hands are the archive's hands.
  • Hair: grey-brown, tied hard back with archive twine — not string, twine, the same stock the quires are bound with.
  • Dress: layers of Kshudra working clothes, unbleached, with an apron whose pockets have worn through at the corners from carrying quires. No ornament. No wax. No ink on her, ever.
  • Tell: she never picks anything up in a hurry. Nandi's hands move like a person who has spent a lifetime moving things that tear.

Voice

Dry, factual, unsentimental. She tells the truth the way Kessa appraises — flat, in order, without softening. She does not comfort. She supplies the record and lets the listener do their own arithmetic.

Nandi's lines are short. She almost never uses two sentences where one will carry the weight, and when she does add a second, it is the one that hurts:

"You were not invited. You were scheduled." "Her freedom was the rent on that shelf." "Ask your mother. She is the only one on that roll who is allowed to say it."

What she is

The Mendery's archivist. She has kept the cellar since before Sutar, M. was bound — long enough that the Council's own index of the Mendery is younger than she is. She is the only person in Agnikhand who can read the back wall's knot-script labels fully, which means she is the only person who has ever known the complete contents of the archive.

She was complicit in the mother's twenty-year operation and has never described it as anything other than what it was: a woman with a spool and a door.

Position

Not an ally of the Council. Not an ally of Ira. Nandi's loyalty is to the shelf. She has spent forty years deciding what a record is for, and her position is consistent: a record is not a weapon and not a treasure — it is a thing that must survive being read. She will withhold information (page 008) and hand over a sentence she was asked to hold (page 009) in the same half-hour, because those are two different duties and she does not confuse them.

She let the mother into the archive on the second night of Chapter 007, and unlocked it behind her, and then sat in a lit room with a needle in her hand for three days waiting for Ira to come down the stairs by herself.

Ongoing thread: the unwritten name

Nandi has known the principal's name for forty years. She has never written it, never spoken it, and has told Ira only that it is on the Roll. She will not say it in a cellar with the door open.

Payoff is long-arc. When she finally says it, it should cost her something visible — this is the character whose entire life has been the act of not saying it.
