THREADBORN — Chapter 03 — Page 005

Chapter title: The Hand That Opens / वह हाथ जो खोलता है Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, dawn Page type: The cutter comes — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

Beats carried from Page 004: the supply line, the second piece of thread, Kessa's tally-knot. Page 003: Bhan's arm left readable, the charcoal mark.

PANEL 1 — Wide, dawn at the dock (~14%)

Camera: Dawn, dock three: Bhan asleep against a grain-lift, left sleeve rolled up, forearm exposed. The charcoal mark Ira left is visible at the mend's edge. The dock is empty — the night shift ended, the day shift has not begun.

Image: Bhan sleeping, patient even in rest. His arm is a canvas waiting. The dock lamp flickers.

CAPTION (none spoken): Night fifty-three. The arm waits, as it has waited every night.

PANEL 2 — Medium, the figure arrives (~13%)

Camera: A figure at the dock's edge, hooded, small — not a large person. The shadow falls correctly (not a Preta). Hands emerge from the hood: gloved, precise, ash-grey thread wound round one finger. The figure kneels beside Bhan.

Image: The cutter: hooded, careful, moving the way Kessa moves — unhurried, final. The ash-grey thread is visible on the gloved finger. The cutter's hands are steady and surgical.

SFX: (soft, thread through skin) tsss

PANEL 3 — Macro, the cut (~14%)

Camera: Extreme close on Bhan's forearm as the cutter works: the nightly reopening, the turn-back at each end, the stitch-grammar that matches Ira's palm. But tonight the cutter pauses — the charcoal mark is in the way. The cutter's gloved fingertip touches the mark.

Image: The cutter reads the mark. The mark says: someone was here before you. The cutter's hand does not withdraw. It stays.

CAPTION (none spoken): The mark said: read. The cutter read it.

PANEL 4 — Close, the response (~13%)

Camera: The cutter's hand, still on the charcoal mark. A new gesture: the cutter takes a second piece of ash-grey thread — the supply line's second piece — and lays it across the mark, sealing it into the seam. Not hiding it. Acknowledging it.

Image: The second thread is identical to the cut-end in Kessa's box. The cutter is not erasing Ira's mark — they are filing it, sewing it into the evidence, keeping it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): They didn't remove my mark. They kept it. They sewed it in.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the departure (~13%)

Camera: The cutter stands, rolls Bhan's sleeve down — careful, gentle, the way you close a book you intend to read again. The hood turns toward the stall-row for one frame — not toward the Knot & Nail, but toward the basin's list-post. Then the figure walks into the pre-dawn grey.

Image: The cutter's departure is unhurried. The hood's angle toward the list-post is the hook's seed: the cutter knows where things are filed in this basin.

CAPTION (none spoken): Night fifty-three. The arm is closed. The mark is kept. The cutter walks toward the filing.

PANEL 6 — Medium, morning at the stall (~13%)

Camera: Morning, Knot & Nail: Ira arrives to find Kessa already behind the counter, loupe down, lockbox open. The cut-end is out. Kessa is comparing something — a fresh length of thread, left on the counter overnight by an unknown hand.

Image: A third piece of ash-grey thread on the counter. Not the cut-end (that's in the box). Not Ira's (she carries needles, not thread-spool). Left by someone who knows the stall's hours.

KESSA (dialogue, dry): Someone left thread on my counter overnight.

KESSA (dialogue): Same thread. Same gauge. A gift, or a receipt.

PANEL 7 — Macro, the three pieces — HOOK (~19%)

Camera: The counter, flat: the cut-end from the box, the thread from the counter, and — laid beside them — Ira's own mending thread from her kit. Three pieces of ash-grey thread, same colour, same gauge, same age-range: one forty years old (cut-end), one fresh (counter), one fifteen years old (Ira's, the age of her stitch).

Image: Three pieces of thread, side by side. A family photograph of the supply line.

KESSA (dialogue, quiet): Box. Counter. Yours. Same thread.

KESSA (dialogue, quieter): Whoever is running this supply line wants you to know they exist.

CAPTION (none spoken): The cutter left a receipt. The thread is the message: I have the same thread you do. I have always had it. And now you know.

Writing & art notes

  • The cutter is shown but not named. Hooded, small-handed, unhurried. The shadow falls correctly (not Preta). Do not reveal face, voice, or Kind.
  • The charcoal mark is read and kept — the cutter acknowledges Ira's presence without removing her evidence. This is a conversation conducted in stitch-grammar.
  • The third thread on the counter is the chapter's escalation: the supply line is active and confident enough to deliver to the stall itself.
  • Chain budget: unspent. Four notes silent. Sewer unnamed.
