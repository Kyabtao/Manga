Chapter title: The Hand That Opens / वह हाथ जो खोलता है Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the Knot & Nail (counter, daylight) Page type: Chapter opening / comparison scene — 7 panels, ends on the letter Canvas: vertical webtoon strip, full colour

Beats carried from Chapter 002: the folded grey+crimson letter (Page 010), the unpick-grammar and turn-backs (Page 009), the reopened stall and one-word Thread. (Page 010 Panel 6), Patra's harvest receipt — the grain is already collected (Page 010), the commission refused as pending (Page 010), Rekhak posted to the basin as Reckoner-of-record (Page 010 Panel 4), Lekh's cuff-chit entry two (Page 010 Panel 5). From Chapter 001: the lockbox's shadowless cut-end (Page 010), the whorl in the sixty-first knot and in the cut-end's end-knot (Page 009–010), the four notes stored in Kessa's thumb-rub (Page 007 / Ch. 002 Page 001).

Chapter 003 chain-stop budget: ONE. Not to be spent before a page the narrative earns it. Four notes: must match wrongly first before anything audible may match them correctly. Sewer: still unnamed. The box is open this page — the cut-end is on the counter — but the name stays locked.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the reopened counter (~14%)

Camera: Morning, Knot & Nail: shutters up, counter wiped, ash settling on the loupe-cord's shadow. The stall is open and ordinary and the basin is adjusting to it — a fruit-seller two stalls down has moved his display one inch closer to the Knot & Nail, the smallest unit of trust the row knows. Kessa is behind the counter; Ira stands across it with the mender's kit on the wood, already open.

Image: The first post-opening morning, normalised: the Knot & Nail as it used to be, except the counter holds more than its usual clutter today. In the far right of the counter, wrapped in a second layer of oilcloth, sits something rectangular and old — the lockbox, carried from wherever Kessa keeps it, and set down in daylight for the first time in the series. Kessa's loupe is down: she is already appraising.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, small): She opened the stall before the first bell. I brought the rubbing before the second. Neither of us said what today was for.

PANEL 2 — Medium, the box on the counter (~13%)

Camera: Over Kessa's small broad hands as they unwrap the oilcloth one fold at a time. Beneath it: the lockbox. Small, black-basalt, Kshudra-made — older than anything else on the counter. One keyhole, no key visible; the lock turned already (we hear it, same grammar as Ch. 002 Page 009, but this time the box is open). Inside, on a bed of old grey felt, lies a shadowless cut-end: a piece of thread, roughly a hand's width, severed cleanly at both ends — and utterly, impossibly shadowless, as if it belongs to a light source that does not exist in this room.

Image: The cut-end is the star of the panel. It should look wrong — not dangerous, not magical, just physically impossible. Thread casts shadow. This thread does not. Kessa's loupe-eye is fixed on it; her bare eye is on Ira. First on-panel consultation in the series.

KESSA (dialogue, dry, loupe down): Forty years in that box. It has never been on this counter.

KESSA (dialogue): Today it answers a question, or I put it back for another forty.

PANEL 3 — Two-shot, the comparison (~14%)

Camera: Counter-level, flat and clinical: Ira's charcoal rubbing of Bhan's turn-backs laid beside the cut-end, both on the counter's grey surface. Kessa's loupe-hand holds the rubbing flat at one edge; Ira's bare fingers hold the cut-end's oilcloth bed at the other. Two objects, one question.

Image: The rubbing is rough charcoal on cheap paper — dock-fibre stock, gritty. The cut-end is shadowless, old, precise. Side by side they look like a student's homework next to a master's thesis. The turn-backs in Bhan's rubbing are at each extremity of the nightly cut-stroke: the cutter's knot-off, the stitch-grammar unpick (Ch. 002 Page 009). The cut-end's own ends have turn-backs too — identical in school, the same hand-family. But the cut-end's turn-backs are ancient, tight as a forty-year-old scar; the rubbing's are fresh, still learning the stroke.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, small): The same turn-back. The same angle of exit. The same hand-school.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smaller): One is a week old. The other was old before I was born.

PANEL 4 — Close, the mismatch (~13%)

Camera: On Ira's face, eyes on the two objects, reading them the way she reads a tear: with her whole body still and her hands wanting to touch. The loupe-lamp's circle of light crosses her palm-stitch as she holds the oilcloth — the stitch catches the light and does the same thing it has always done: nothing. It holds. She is comparing three hands now: the cutter's, the box's, and the one sewn across her own palm.

Image: Her mismatched eyes are level. She has understood something before she has words for it. Behind her, the counter's back wall; in the corner of the frame, Kessa's loupe still down, still watching.

IRA (dialogue, quiet): The cutter copied my stitch.

KESSA (dialogue, from behind the loupe, dry): No.

PANEL 5 — Macro, the reversal (~14%)

Camera: Extreme close on the two turn-backs side by side — the cut-end's ancient knot-off and the rubbing's fresh one — with Kessa's scarred fingertip touching the gap between them. The loupe fills the background. Her nail is blunt; her hand is steady; her finger is the only thing in the world that can hold this still.

Image: The reversal, visual: the fresh turn-back is the copy. The old one is the original. The cutting that opened Bhan every night was not someone learning from Ira's stitch — it was someone who already knew, practising on a dock worker the same hand-school that was sewn into Ira's palm before she could speak. The cutter taught Ira's stitch; Ira's stitch did not teach the cutter. The age-gap is forty years.

KESSA (dialogue, one sentence, the chapter's thesis): Child, your stitch didn't teach the cutter.

KESSA (dialogue): The cutter taught your stitch.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the weight (~13%)

Camera: Back to the two-shot: Ira and Kessa across the counter, the box between them. Ira's hands have moved — one is on the counter's edge, gripping; the other is over her own palm, covering the stitch, the way you cover a wound someone tells you was made for you, not against you. Kessa does not reach across. She wraps the cut-end back into its oilcloth — one fold, two folds, the box's felt bed — and closes the lid. The lock does not turn; the box is open but the cloth is closed.

Image: Kessa's wrapping is methodical, unhurried: the old appraiser putting the evidence away before it can hurt the witness further. Ira's face is not devastated — it is re-aiming. The same expression as when she sits down at a tear, except now the tear is her own.

IRA (dialogue, flat, to the lid): Then the sewer and the cutter share a hand.

KESSA (dialogue, oilcloth folded): Share a school. The hand is yours, child. It always was.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): Two strangers share a school. I am the diploma.

PANEL 7 — Macro, the letter — HOOK (~19%)

Camera: From behind Ira's shoulder, looking down at the counter as Kessa's hand — not Ira's — draws something from under the counter's lip. A folded slip of paper: grey wax on one half, crimson wax on the other, both unbroken, the fold crease running between them — the letter from Chapter 002 Page 010, the one Kessa read last night while the lock turned. She sets it on the counter beside the closed box and opens it with one thumb, flat, the way a woman opens a letter she has already read once in private and is now reading in front of a witness.

Image: The letter's interior, on-panel at last. It is not a demand. It is not a threat. It is formatted like a Council posting order — the same grey stamp, the same procedure columns — with one addition in crimson at the foot: a second signature, a second seal, touching the Council's grey. The letter transfers custody of the blink census — the pot-row's list, the one with the crimson cross-out, the one the Grey Clerk bought on Page 003 of Chapter 2 — from the Office to the Knot & Nail. The Council and the principal have agreed to file the basin's most dangerous piece of paper in Kessa's lockbox. Both sides have agreed. Kessa was not asked.

KESSA (dialogue, flat, to the letter): Two owners on one box. My box.

KESSA (dialogue, quieter): Nobody asked.

CAPTION (none spoken, bottom): The cutter taught her stitch. The letter teaches her name. And in the Knot & Nail's safest box, the basin's only lock that never opens for names now opens for a posting order with two owners — and neither of them is the woman whose lock it is.

CAPTION (final box, smaller): The letter is not a demand. It is an appointment.

Writing & art notes

  • First on-panel lockbox consultation: the box is open in daylight, the cut-end is on the counter, Kessa's loupe is down. This is the first time the reader sees the shadowless thread clearly. The sewer's name stays locked; the box is open but the person behind it is not.
  • The cut-end looks physically wrong, not magical: shadowless thread in a room with one lamp. Let the art sell the impossibility before the story names it. No glow, no aura — just the absence of a shadow where one should be.
  • The reversal is the chapter's thesis in miniature: Kessa's single sentence ("The cutter taught your stitch.") re-aims the entire series' orientation around Ira's palm. What the reader assumed was Ira's origin is actually Ira's inheritance. Do not over-explain it; the sentence and the two turn-backs side by side are enough.
  • The letter is read twice: Kessa read it privately last night (Ch. 002 Page 010's lock-turning was the consultation). Now it is read in front of Ira as a witness. The art should show the re-fold crease — one fold, Kessa's hand — distinct from the original fold.
  • Kessa's interiority is hands-only: wrapping the cut-end, opening the letter, the oilcloth folds. No monologue, no thought balloons, no eyeline close-ups for emotion. Her feelings are furniture arrangement.
  • The four notes stay silent. Ira does not hum, does not remember aloud. The notes must match wrongly first; nothing audible may match them before then.
  • Chain budget: unspent. No chain-stop on this page. Rekhak is off-panel (posting geography only; he may appear at the stall later this chapter as procedure). The oath-link travels with him unseen.
  • The Loom never speaks. No hum change, no Spindle mention. The cut-end's shadowlessness is a property of the thread, not an intervention by the Loom.
  • Ash falls as always in Panel 1's wide; the counter's grey surface reads clean in Panels 3 and 7.
  • Patra: off-panel. The harvest receipt was the last act; the commission is pending, not dead. Do not introduce a new broker appearance this early in the chapter.
  • Palette: daylight at the Knot & Nail — warm lamp, ash-grey wood, counter-worn brown. The cut-end's shadowlessness reads best against a surface with a clear shadow (the oilcloth, the lamp-light on the counter). The letter's grey and crimson waxes are the only colour-break in the panel.
