Chapter 007 — The Crimson Line / क़िरमिज़ी पंक्ति — CLOSE-OUT

Status: IN PROGRESS — 10 pages (001–010) scripted (EN + Hindi), 9/10 images generated (page 010 pending), cast files + glossary + locations grown in-step. Arc: II — The Mendery · Sector: Agnikhand · Open PR for this work: Kyabtao/Manga#3 Chain-stop budget: ONE, not spent this chapter — second chapter running. Held for the Arc II turn.

Synopsis

The principal's letter opens. Ira takes the grey seal; Kessa takes the crimson. The grey snaps clean — never touched. The crimson lifts, soft and cloudy, because it has already been broken once and pressed back down by a thumb.

Inside the fold, when the sheet is pressed perfectly flat, there is a line of handwriting drawn in sealing wax — eleven words hidden in the crease where only a mender would ever look: "The founder will not appear. Do not sign. — a hand of the school."

Ira works out the method: somebody broke the principal's crimson seal, dug a bead of wax out of it, wrote the line with a stylus, and re-pressed the remainder flat. She lays the crease across the stitch, and the blank strand lifts out of her palm and settles into the writing. The braid opens — and for the first time, the crimson under the grey is visible. Four notes, off-pitch and correct. Then two dry stitches: present tense. Her mother is working, right now, somewhere in Agnikhand.

Kessa reads the needlework from the sound: doubled folds, heavy needle, slow turn. That is binding, and the only place in the basin that binds by hand is the Mendery. Ira goes back in. The archive is disbound — every quire opened, every spine unthreaded. Nandi has spent three days extracting forty years of the mother's own thread, because every file in the cellar was held shut by Sutar, M.

On the back wall, one file's width of clean dust. The Roll of Hands is gone: the school's pupil register, forty years deep, every hand taught, name and kind and the year the strand went in. One name on that roll is not in the Council's registers. Nandi will not say it: the school taught one hand that went up the terrace and never came back down.

Why twenty years bound? Because the chair was the deal — her freedom was the rent on a shelf, and the shelf held a name the terrace has been hunting for forty years. She heard the audit coming before the Inspector did, and took the roll out ahead of it.

Nandi delivers two messages left by the mother: "Tell her I did not write her name in the roll. Tell her I sewed her instead." And: "Tell her not to sign anything that asks her to exist."

Ira answers. Not by signing, not by burning — by mending the letter. She works a second bead of wax out of the letter's own crimson seal, draws her own three lines into the same crease in the same script ("The founder's hand has not signed. The stall is open. — a hand of the school"), closes the fold so that both messages vanish into the paper, and sews the fold shut with the blank strand, finished with a Kshudra child's first knot. Three seals on one letter: grey, crimson, braid. Kessa takes a copy of both crease-writs into her tally-thread — the only ledger in Agnikhand that cannot be audited.

Ira posts the letter down the Mendery's supply chute, and then, on the step, re-sews her own stitch — closing the channel to her mother so it cannot be followed. Nobody said who has to hold the needle.

END OF CHAPTER SEVEN.

Canon rules added in Chapter 7 (binding forward)

  1. The third register is a private hand's book. The Council keeps three registers: the debt-roll, the kind-roll, and a crimson-bound third register with no head, no spine seal and no clerk of record. Entries in the third register license nothing — they inventory. At term's end, an unanswered provision reverts to the register's owning hand.
  2. The principal's licence is an inventory. Four clauses: one stall, one basin, one name; threads submitted at each second bell; the mark entered on the Council kind-roll; and the school to produce its founder before the seal within one Unspooling. Clause four is a writ aimed at the mother, with her name on it.
  3. Crease-writ is school method. A line drawn in sealing wax — worked soft in the fingers, not melted — along the inside of a fold, invisible unless the sheet is pressed perfectly flat. Both the mother's warning and Ira's reply are crease-writs on the same page.
  4. The letter's crimson seal was opened before Ira got it. A bead of wax was dug out with a thumbnail and re-pressed flat, leaving a two-pour edge. The grey seal was untouched.
  5. The braid's second colour is crimson under the grey. Visible only next to something the mother touched. Ira has looked at her own hand every day for fifteen years and never seen it in daylight.
  6. The thread now carries live sound, not just memory. Four notes, correct order, correct off-pitch — and then two dry stitches of actual needlework, present tense. The mother is working during Chapter 007.
  7. Every spine in the Mendery was held shut by the mother's thread. Forty years, one spool. The archive was not merely her cover — the archive was hers. Nandi spent three days extracting it, because a thread-owner can be read.
  8. The Roll of Hands is gone. The school's pupil register: kind, the year the teacher's strand was sewn in, and the pupil's first knot, kept continuously from founding through the twenty years of binding. No names were ever written in it — a name is a handle and a handle can be read off a page. Taken by the mother two nights before the audit could reach it. (Ch. 008 reveals the roll had no names; the store of names was kept by Jadi in her head. Ch. 007 p008 was corrected to match.)
  9. One name on the roll is not in the Council's registers. The school taught one hand that went up the terrace and never came back down. Nandi has never written it out and will not speak it.
  10. The twenty years was rent. The mother stayed bound because a bound woman in one room is not worth auditing; her freedom bought the safety of the shelf. She left when the audit made the shelf indefensible.
  11. Ira was never entered in the roll. "I did not write her name in the roll. I sewed her instead." The school's only unwritten pupil — because a book can be audited and a stitch cannot.
  12. The letter went back down the supply chute. Sealed with the braid and a first knot, into the Mendery's mortar — the only road in Agnikhand that has ever belonged to the mother. The principal's letter has been answered by someone he did not write to.
  13. Ira closed her own stitch. Deliberately, at the supply step, with her mother's needle and her mother's strand. The channel is shut so it cannot be traced. Nobody said who has to hold the needle.
  14. Kessa's tally-thread now holds both crease-writs. The only copy of the mother's handwriting outside the stolen roll, kept in the only ledger in Agnikhand that keeps no ink.
