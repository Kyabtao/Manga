THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 006

Chapter title: The Fourth Step / चौथा पायदान Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the alley's end, the stall at night Page type: The list; the measurement — 7 panels, ends on a hand that matches

Beats: the survey closes and the twenty-two stop being a sum and become a list of addresses; Nima is used — she is told to hold, and Ira watches the machinery she built run a girl she has known for years; Ira asks Kessa the better question, and Kessa names the cost of the reading out loud; the reading fails, because there is no hand in the mother's thread; and Ira works out the instrument that would work — measuring instead of reading — and how badly it is out of time.

This page: the numbers go in tonight.

Chapter 011 budget: ONE chain-stop, not spent on Page 006 — brought within reach and shown not to fit. Loom never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009 — fourteen days now.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the survey closes (~12%)

Camera: Medium: the end of the hatch alley, late afternoon. The water-butt. The grey clerk shuts the survey book on the last door counted. The counting women behind him are packing up their tallies. Nothing dramatic: a book closing.

Image: A book closing. That is all it takes to turn a sum into a list.

GREY CLERK: Survey closed at the water-butt. Numbers go in tonight.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): He has been walking one alley since Monday, one door at a time, and now he stops at a water-butt and shuts a book.

CAPTION: Doors with hands in them. Hands with no entry. By morning that is not a sum any more. It is a list of addresses, and a claimant is paid by the address.

PANEL 2 — Medium, Nima is used (~13%)

Camera: Medium: Nima at a counting table, and she is not counting. Her hands are on other people's hands, turning them, holding them steady while someone else reads. Ira watches from the counter and Nima does not look at her once.

Image: The machinery running exactly as written, on a girl the keeper has known for years.

SECOND CLERK: Reader-second. Hold.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): They told her she would hold when she was told to hold, and this afternoon they told her.

CAPTION: She is good at it. She has been good at it for years, which was always the trouble — a girl who is good with her hands in this basin gets asked for them.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the better question (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the stall, the shutters partway down, the lockbox on the counter between Ira and Kessa. Ira asks the question she should have asked on Page 003, and it is not what and it is not who.

Image: A pawnbroker being asked to open the one thing she has never opened for anybody.

IRA: I need to take them out of the box.

IRA: There are twenty-two people on that thread with no name written anywhere. The Office has a list of doors by tonight. The only way I find them first is the one instrument the Office cannot audit.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Forty years she has kept them without asking what they were, because a pawnbroker who asks loses the thing she was told to keep. I am asking.

PANEL 4 — Two-shot, Kessa names the cost (~14%)

Camera: Two-shot, tighter: Kessa with the fold of cloth in her scarred hands, and she is not refusing — she is pricing it, which is what she does. Then Ira's answer, and then Kessa's hand opening.

Image: A woman who has spent forty years not asking, saying out loud exactly what asking costs.

KESSA: Every reading under the schedule is read aloud, keeper. You have stood in front of her and heard her do it yourself.

KESSA: You take my box to that woman and she names twenty-two hands into an Office book, in front of a clerk. Then they are exactly as hidden as the forty-one are.

IRA: Not under the schedule. At night. With the book shut.

KESSA: (and after a moment she opens her hand) Forty years I kept these without asking what they were. Do not make me sorry I let you look.

PANEL 5 — Close, the reading fails (~13%)

Camera: Close: night, the stall, lamp low, no clerk, the big book shut and face down on the table. Nandi has the fold of cloth in her hands and the pinned sleeves she always works in. She reads. And stops.

Image: The instrument that has never once been wrong, held up against work it cannot see.

NANDI: Kind: basin. Year: nineteen. Hand: —

NANDI: There is a year in it. There is no hand in it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): She has never been wrong about a hand. Not once, in all the time she has been doing this in front of the whole basin.

CAPTION: She is holding my mother's work and there is nothing in it for her to find. That is why nobody has ever put my mother's people on a list — not cunning, not hiding. Just thread that will not hold a hand.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the instrument and the clock (~15%)

Camera: Medium: back at the counter, the box open, sixty-three lengths laid out. Ira's hands doing what her hands do: measuring, sorting, matching. She has worked out the instrument. She has also worked out the clock.

Image: A mender finds the answer with her hands, and then finds out how late it is.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A hand's width of thread for everyone she enrolled. That is not a name. It is a measurement.

CAPTION: I can find them. Not by reading — by measuring. Every hand in this basin against sixty-three lengths of grey thread, and the ones that match are hers.

CAPTION: Twenty-two people. One night. I would have to put my hands on every hand in the basin before the numbers go in.

CAPTION: I have one thing in this stall that stops a chain. I have had it since the year the loom stopped speaking to anybody, and I have never spent it, and tonight would be the night.

CAPTION: It stops a chain. It does not stop a list.

PANEL 7 — Macro, the match — FINAL HOOK (~19%)

Camera: Macro, straight down at the counter. The twenty-two lengths set apart in their own row. And on the wood beside them, two hands laid flat, palm down: Ira's gloved hand, and beside it a second, older hand with the sleeve pinned up. Both the same width as one length of grey thread. Nandi put her hand down without being asked.

Image: A measurement that matches, and the hand that put itself there.

NANDI: That one is mine.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Twenty-two people my mother taught in the open and never wrote down. One of them has been standing at my counter every morning this week reading my register aloud into an Office book.

CAPTION (smallest): And she has never once been entered.

Writing & art notes

  • Panel 1 must be small. A book closing at a water-butt. The whole page's pressure comes from that, and if it is played as a threat it will read as melodrama. The clerk is not triumphant. He has finished a job.
  • Nima's beat is watched, not intervened in. Ira does nothing on Panel 2 and must be seen doing nothing. Nima does not look at her, because being looked at would make it worse. The caption "she has been good at it for years, which was always the trouble" is the series' whole argument about competence in this basin — do not soften it.
  • Kessa does not refuse. She prices it. That is her character exactly: a pawnbroker does not say no, she says that will cost you this. Ira's answer — not under the schedule, at night, book shut — is the first time anybody has found a way past the read-aloud condition, and it should feel like finding a loose thread, not like winning.
  • The reading failing is the payoff of shadowless thread. Nandi can identify any knot's kind, year and hand and has never been wrong. She gets kind and year and then nothing. The mother's work has no hand in it to read. This is why the mother has never been on a list: not concealment, just material that will not take an impression.
  • Panel 6 is two discoveries in one: the instrument (measure, don't read — it is exactly the gesture this character has always had) and the clock (twenty-two people, one night). Then the chain-stop is brought within reach and shown not to fit — it stops a chain, it does not stop a list. That is the reason the budget survives this page, and it must be stated as a flat fact, not as anguish.
  • The hook is a hand being put down, not a face reacting. Macro on the counter: the twenty-two lengths, and two hands laid flat beside one of them, and the older hand with the pinned sleeve got there without being asked. Nandi gets four words. No gasp, no close-up on Ira's face — the caption carries the whole weight.
  • Canon care: Nandi is revealed as one of the twenty-two. This retroactively explains the two times she declined to name a hand (Pages 003 and 005) without contradicting them, and it does not settle whether she knew the Page 003 entry was a lie — it makes both readings worse. She has been reading the Office's register aloud while unenrolled herself, which means the Office has been hearing her every morning.
  • Continuity: the four notes have been silent fourteen days and Ira does not expect them tonight — say so, so the reader stops waiting for rescue from that quarter. Rekhak's recorded readings and the readers-for-money stay unspent. The mother is off panel; her presence is thread that will not hold a hand.
  • Numbers: forty-one names, sixty-three lengths, twenty-two people. Nandi's length reads year nineteen — she is older than the Freedom Year and was enrolled decades before the basin's roll was written.
