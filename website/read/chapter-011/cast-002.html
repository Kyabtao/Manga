Chapter 011 — Page 002 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one, including the unnamed ones.

IRA SUTAR — इरा सुतार (PROTAGONIST)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "I tied it."

Page 002 note: claims the second knot as her own work under clause two because clause two is the only instrument she has and it requires an owner she cannot name. This is the first lie entered in her own register. Her face does nothing while she says it. She then asks Kessa the question the old appraiser has avoided for forty years.

THE GREY CLERK — धूसर लिपिक (the survey)

No full sheet; Ch. 009–011. The Office's voice with a pen in it.

Grey Clerk / Manav / Agnikhand / grey, procedural / none — spends other people's /
Survey: ask what is on the step, then write down whatever answer you are given /
"Reader-second declines. Entry noted."

Page 002 note: never reacts. He writes the false entry without looking up, and he writes Nima's refusal down too — which is worse than punishing her, because it makes the refusal part of the record.

NIMA — नीमा (reader-second)

Full sheet (Ch. 010): ../../chapter-010/characters/nima.md · Art: ../../chapter-010/characters/nima-ref.png

Nima / Kshudra / Agnikhand / grey, undeclared / none declared — that is the bargain /
Count: tally hands without reading them; cannot read a braid /
"I count. I don't hold."

Page 002 note: the thread pays off. Asked to hold a subject's hand for the count, she sets the card-tray down — the first time all morning — and refuses out loud, in the alley, with the row watching. Her bargain was about a book; the Office has just asked for her hands.

KESSA — केसा

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"Forty years I've had that question sitting under my counter and I never once asked it out loud."

Page 002 note: asked what the cut-end was cut from, and does not answer. Her beat is a delay: "now you have said it in my stall, so it is in the room, and I will have to answer it." Loupe down, hand flat on the counter, eyes never leaving the work.

THE COUNTING WOMEN — गिनती वाली स्त्रियाँ (three, unnamed)

No full sheet; Ch. 011 Pages 001–002. Pupils of Jadi's counting lesson. On this page they stop counting and watch, hands still half-raised. They do not intervene — they witness.

Counting Woman / Manavkin / Agnikhand / grey, self-tied / light, self-held /
Count: declare your own knots before a clerk can declare them for you /
"Six, seven. Seven since the lesson."

THE TWO CLAIMANTS — दो दावेदार (unnamed)

Claim: enter a hand that is not yours for a reward that is not yours /

THE MOTHER — माँ (never on panel, never speaks)

No full sheet, by canon. Her presence on this page is thread, a teaching hand inferred, forty years of arithmetic, and ten days of silence.

— / Manavkin / Agnikhand / shadowless, cut / unknown; never entered /
Lift: take a knot out of a record without cutting it; enrol without entering a name /
"Do not enrol me. — S.M."

Page note: the survey reaches the keeper's own step and asks what is on it; Ira uses clause two without an owner and puts the first lie in her own register; the Office asks Nima to hold a hand and she sets the tray down and refuses out loud in the alley, which the clerk writes down rather than punishes; Ira asks Kessa what the cut-end was cut from and gets a delay instead of an answer. Hook: in the open lockbox, the cut-end and the second knot lie side by side under the loupe — same gauge, same twist, same refusal to cast a shadow, and the same whorl at each end. Two ends of one thread, forty years apart. The mother has spent forty years handing out the rest.
