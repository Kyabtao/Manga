THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 001

Chapter title: The Fourth Step / चौथा पायदान Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the hatch alley and the Mendery counter Page type: Chapter opening — 7 panels, ends on the thread

Beats: the knot the mother lifted out of the register is back on the Mendery's step, and beside it lies a second knot in an untrained hand; the queue at the counter is no longer only claimants; the Office surveys the hatch alley entry by entry, from the fourth step to the water-butt, with the keeper of the register obliged to attend her own survey; Nandi's condition makes the school's reading public business; and the second knot turns out to be tied in shadowless thread.

This page: hands. A queue that is not only claimants, an alley with a lamp on a pole, and a knot on a step that somebody tied on purpose so that Ira would know she is not the only teacher in Agnikhand.

Chapter 011 budget: ONE chain-stop, not spent on Page 001 (held — the arc will need it). Loom never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009. Wax: grey for the Council and the Office, crimson for the principal's private hand.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the alley before first bell (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the hatch alley at first light, seen from the Mendery's step. The Office's grey lamp on its pole at the alley mouth, still burning in daylight, standing where the school's brass lantern hung for forty years. Two neighbouring stalls' shutters down for the second day. On the step, in the lamplight, two knots tied into the same scrap of register paper — one worn, one new. Ash on everything. Nobody has come down yet.

Image: The school's door posted like an address, and the founder's answer left on the step where anybody could step on it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The knot she lifted out of the register, back on my step, with the paper still under it. Nine days late and eighty-one steps up a stair, and she read it, and she sent it home.

CAPTION: And beside it — in the same paper, tied with a thread that has never been in my stall, in a hand that has never crossed two strands in the dark — a second knot.

PANEL 2 — Medium, the queue at first bell (~13%)

Camera: Medium: the Mendery counter at first bell, and the queue is not what it was. Three women from Jadi's counting lesson stand with their hands held out palm-up to one another, counting their own knots aloud — one, two, three — the way you check a purse in the street. Behind them, two men with a claimant's posture, rehearsing with their own palms turned up. The register lies open on the counter; the bounty sheet is underneath it, face-down.

Image: Two trades in one line, and only one of them pays.

WOMAN (counting): …six, seven. Seven since the lesson. (beat) Yours?

SECOND WOMAN: Five. I had six on Tuesday and I undid one.

SFX: FIRST BELL.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Fourteen claims at first bell last week. This morning it is nine claims and eleven hands that want to be counted, and I have one counter, one pen, and a licence with a timer on it.

PANEL 3 — Medium close, the two knots (~14%)

Camera: On the step: Ira kneels in the lamplight with the two knots in her gloved hand, held up the way a mender holds a seam. The worn one is hers — the register's knot, lifted out four days ago. The new one is thick, uneven, tied with too much thread, and the crossing is wrong: not a right hand's, not a left hand's, not anybody's who was ever shown twice.

Image: A mender reading a knot the way another woman would read a letter — for the hand that tied it, not the words.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Worn knot: mine. She took it out of the register four days ago and put it back on my step last night, which means she was here, after dusk, within arm's reach of my shutter.

CAPTION: The second one is not a mender's knot and it is not a thief's knot. It is what you get when somebody is shown once, in daylight, by somebody who had somewhere else to be.

CAPTION (smallest): The four notes have been silent since the eighth chapter's ninth page. She has not hummed in this alley in nine days, and she taught somebody in it anyway.

PANEL 4 — Wide, the survey arrives (~13%)

Camera: Wide: the alley mouth at first bell. The grey clerk with the bigger book under one arm; a second clerk with a folding slate and a folding stool; Nima behind them with the card-tray, Office grey, sleeves pushed up, the tray not yet put down. The Inspector does not come — he has no need to. The survey begins at the fourth step from the hatch and will end at the water-butt.

Image: The Office walking a forty-year-old address one step at a time, with the keeper of the register obliged to attend her own survey.

GREY CLERK: Keeper of the register will attend. From the fourth step to the water-butt, entry by entry, hand by hand. Record: the place of keeping is named as the hatch alley.

IRA: I'm the keeper.

GREY CLERK: Then bring the register and stand where I can see your hands. (beat) Clause four, keeper. The licence lapses if the place of keeping fails to produce.

IRA: Produce what?

GREY CLERK: Whatever is in the register, keeper. You're holding it.

PANEL 5 — Two-shot, the reading (~14%)

Camera: Two-shot at the counter's end: Ira with the register open, Nandi beside her with the bigger book, sleeves pinned, hands flat on the wood, not touching anybody. The schedule's condition, written by Nandi herself: the reader of the bigger book and the keeper read every entry aloud, in the same words, at the same time. Down the alley, the second clerk writes.

Image: One woman's condition, written to protect the basin, doing exactly what the Office needed — making forty years of the school's reading public business, out loud, in the street.

NANDI: Entry four: hand enrolled at the hatch, year one, no name entered. Reading: held.

IRA: (out loud, in the same words, at the same time) Entry four: hand enrolled at the hatch, year one, no name entered. Reading: held.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): She wrote that condition so that no entry in this basin would ever go unread. It is the only reason the Office can hear the school's handwriting from the street.

PANEL 6 — Close, the arithmetic of forty years (~13%)

Camera: Close on the clerk's folding slate: Nandi reads, Ira repeats, the clerk writes. Year one. A hand enrolled at the hatch before the water-butt had a stand, before the alley had a lamp, before there was an Office form for any of it. And at the foot of the Book, in the mother's own hand, the last knot: Do not enrol me. — S.M.

Image: Forty years of arithmetic, legible from outside for the first time, in two women's voices and a clerk's handwriting.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Year one. She put people through that hatch for forty years and she never once put herself through it — and the Office has just heard the only living copy of that say so, out loud, on the record, in my voice.

CAPTION: They are not counting pupils. They are counting the years, and the years make a shape: one woman, four nights down a list of forty-one, and a school with no founder in it.

PANEL 7 — Macro, the thread — FINAL HOOK (~19%)

Camera: Macro: the second knot lying on Ira's gloved palm, lifted into the grey lamplight. The thread it is tied with is ash-grey and it casts no shadow. Everything else in the panel casts one — the glove does, the register paper does, the step does, the lamp-pole does. Only the thread does not. Behind it, out of focus, the survey's second clerk turns a page.

Image: The school's thread, in a stranger's knot, on the keeper's step, in the Office's light.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The cut-end in Kessa's lockbox casts no shadow. My palm was sewn with it. Forty years of it, under her counter, and she never asked what it was cut from.

CAPTION: And this is that thread. Tied by a hand I have never taught, into a knot I have never tied, and left where the Office's own lamp would shine on it.

CAPTION (smallest): My mother is not hiding and she is not being careful. She is teaching, openly, with my own thread — and she has put it in my hand so that I would know it before the Office does.

Writing & art notes

  • This page must open on hands and end on thread. No establishing skyline, no Loom, no stair. The chapter's first image is a lamp on a pole and two knots on a step; its last is a thread that fails to cast a shadow. Everything between is people holding their hands out.
  • The lamp is the Office's, in the school's place. Same height, same hook, grey instead of brass, and it is still burning at first light because the Office pays for oil and the school never did. Draw it plainly: no guards, no rope, no crowd.
  • The queue is the thesis of the chapter. Two trades in one line: hands that want to be counted (Jadi's lesson, loose in three households now) and hands that want to be claimed (the bounty, still open). Draw them facing different directions — the counting women face each other, the claimants face the counter.
  • Clause four's timer is now spoken aloud by the Office, not by Ira. It has been a worry she carried since Chapter 008; this is the first time a clerk says it to her face, and it should land as procedure rather than threat. "Produce what?" / "Whatever is in the register, keeper. You're holding it." is the page's coldest exchange and it is delivered flat.
  • Nandi's condition pays off as a double edge. She wrote it so nothing in the basin goes unread; it is now the instrument by which the Office hears forty years of the school. She knows this. Her face in Panel 5 must not show that she is being used — it shows a woman doing her job exactly as she wrote it.
  • Nima appears and is not yet tested. She carries the card-tray and has not put it down. The thread to carry: the day she is told to put a hand on the stand, she will refuse out loud, in the room, and the Office will test it. This page only shows the tray.
  • The mother never appears. Her presence is a knot, a thread and a hand at the foot of a page. The four notes stay silent — their silence is now nine days old and Ira counts it.
  • The shadowless thread is the hook and must be drawn literally. Every object in the panel casts a shadow; one thread does not. Do not render it as a glow, an aura or a sparkle — it is a physical filament that simply fails to darken what is under it.
