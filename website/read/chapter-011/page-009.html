THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 009

Chapter title: The Fourth Step / चौथा पायदान Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the alley at dusk Page type: The fourth step; thread answers thread — 7 panels, ends at an open drawer

Beats: the fourth step — the keeper crosses her own threshold with the lamp and the chapter title lands on-panel; the reading in the open — Nandi reads the twenty-two into the alley by lamplight, and each line is answered by a raised fist holding its half; the requisition arrives mid-reading — the count runs short; the roll will be called at morning — read over the reading in the same voice, at the same volume; and the page ends on the realization the twin halves were always going to produce: the Office does not need their names. It has the other halves.

This page: both things stand — the reading and the order.

Chapter 011 budget: ONE chain-stop, not spent on Page 009 — the page ends at the open drawer; the spending is Page 010's to decide. Loom never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009 — seventeen days now.

PANEL 1 — Medium-wide, the step (~13%)

Camera: Medium-wide, from inside: Ira lifts the lamp off the counter and steps down — one step, over the threshold, into the alley. The thirteen do not move. Behind her in the doorway, Kessa, lamp already out, holding the dark of the stall behind her like a coat.

Image: Nine years of counter-geography, ended by its keeper's own foot.

KESSA: Go. The box stays with me.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A counter's design: everything comes to it. Nine years I kept that design, and it was never once wrong. It was never wrong because I never once stepped over it.

CAPTION: One step is all the design takes to break. I am the one who built it. Of course it is my foot.

PANEL 2 — Wide, the alley-room (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the lamp set on an upturned crate, mid-alley. The thirteen in a loose half-circle around it, fists at their sides, saying nothing. Ira stands in the open with the light behind her and nothing between her and them — no counter, no book, no box.

Image: A room with no book in it, made on purpose, in the street.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Thirteen people taught to be silent in rooms with books. The alley is not a room. It never was. That is what she taught in it.

CAPTION: I set the lamp on an upturned crate. I do not know if it is the one she stood on. I will never know. That is what it is to be taught by somebody who never wrote anything down.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the reading in the open (~15%)

Camera: Medium: Nandi comes out of the stall with the big book unasked — she was never told to bring it — and sits at the crate's edge with the book on her lap, pinned sleeves, lamplight. She reads the night's entries into the alley. As each line lands, a fist goes up out of the half-circle: the half in it answering the line in the book.

Image: Publication. Thread answers thread, in the open, at dusk, for free.

IRA: Nandi. Read.

NANDI: (already sitting) The schedule puts me at the big book at dawn. It does not say the rest of the day is mine to keep shut.

NANDI: Kind: basin. Year: seventeen. Hand: —

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A fist goes up in the half-dark. The half in it matches the line in the book. That is the whole of the transaction: thread answers thread. No name is said. No name is needed. Nobody is paid.

PANEL 4 — Wide, the basin gathers (~14%)

Camera: Wide, farther back: the alley filling. Doorways. The row from the declaration days. One of the nine — the riveter — with his fist already up. The counting women, off duty, not counting. Nobody pushes. The reading goes line by line into the dark and the dark keeps all of it.

Image: The queue from Page 004, come back as an audience.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A secret read aloud stops being a handle. That is not philosophy. It is what the Office taught this basin itself: readings are evidence — they said it the first morning, in the square, and they have been right in front of everybody since.

CAPTION: By the time she reads the ninth line, the twenty-two are not a list any more either. They are a roomful. You cannot buy what everybody already heard.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the requisition (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the grey clerk at the alley's mouth with a paper in both hands, and he reads it aloud over the reading — same voice, same volume, no hurry. The Office does not compete. It counterpoints.

Image: Two public readings at once, and both of them legal.

GREY CLERK: By order of the Office. The basin's count runs short. Entered hands stand required for the count at morning. The roll will be called.

NIMA: (from the stall's doorway, in the coat, to Ira, quietly) Required. He said required the way they said hold.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The first hand the roll ever lent is standing in my doorway wearing the lender's coat, and the roll is about to get bigger by twenty-two, and the count runs short at morning.

CAPTION: The Office never bluffs. It was never going to argue with the entries. It was going to keep them.

PANEL 6 — Three-shot, the collision (~14%)

Camera: Three-shot: the clerk lowers the paper. Nandi does not stop reading — the next line goes over his. The clerk: the objection. And from the crowd, the purchased reader, fee unspent since noon, who settles it in four words because he is the only neutral instrument present.

Image: The row defending a reading with the Office's own rule, refereed by the man the Office's rule was supposed to corrupt.

GREY CLERK: Keeper. This reading is not on the schedule. An off-schedule reading is not evidence.

THE RIVETER: (a fist still up) Your Office taught us different, clerk. First morning of the survey. In the square.

PURCHASED READER: A reading is a reading. I charge for mine. She does not. That does not stop it being one.

NANDI: (reading, unhurried, over the top of all of it) Kind: basin. Year: nineteen. Hand: —

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The four notes have been silent seventeen days, and nothing has come from that quarter, and it does not matter tonight. Everything that came tonight came up out of the ground I am standing on.

PANEL 7 — Wide, the drawer — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Wide: both things stand at once — the clerk at the alley's edge with the order, the reading circle lit and going, the thirteen with their fists up. And through the stall's doorway behind them: Ira, back at her counter, putting the lamp down where it always stands, and her hand on the drawer under it.

Image: The keeper coming back to the one object in the stall that has never been asked to work.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Both things stand. The reading stands because the Office made readings evidence. The order stands because the Office wrote the roll. I built nothing tonight that the Office cannot pick up in the morning and use.

CAPTION: Thread answers thread. That was the whole of tonight. And by morning they will have thought of it too: the roll will be called, and it does not need their names. It has the other halves.

CAPTION: One thing in this stall stops a chain. It has stopped every chain it was ever offered, and I have never spent it, and I have never once told anybody what it is.

CAPTION (smallest): I put the lamp where it always stands, and I open the drawer.

Writing & art notes

  • The step is the page's centre and it must be heavy, not triumphant. Nine years of counter- design broken by one step down. Ira does not address the thirteen in Panel 1 and Panel 2 — she carries a lamp and sets it on a crate, and the captions carry the weight. The chapter title is never printed; the step IS the title. If the art makes her look defiant, redraw it until she looks like somebody doing the only thing left, which is worse and correct.
  • The crate is the mother's only prop. Never on panel, never speaking — but the upturned crate in the middle of the alley is where she stood, maybe, and Ira's caption says she will never know. Do not resolve it. The uncertainty is the mother's whole method in one sentence: taught by somebody who never wrote anything down.
  • Nandi comes out unasked. Nobody recruits her; she was never told to bring the big book. Her one line — the schedule puts me at the big book at dawn; it does not say the rest of the day is mine to keep shut — is her choosing, for the third time in the chapter, to read. After Page 007 the question was what a person is who reads this in and keeps reading; this page is her answer, and it is not forgiveness. It is craft. She reads because the reading is hers to give.
  • Publication is the basin's protection and it must work. A secret read aloud stops being a handle — the twenty-two stop being a list by becoming a roomful. This is the school's own method applied to the record: taught in the open, read in the open. The Office's dilemma from the brief is audible in Panel 6 and unresolvable: an off-schedule reading either counts (the basin heard it) or doesn't (the Office disowns its own instrument), and the clerk loses either way — which is why the Office does not argue. It counterpoints, with a requisition.
  • The requisition is read OVER the reading, and that is the horror. Same voice, same volume, no hurry. The Office never competes. And it was never going to refuse the entries — it was going to keep them: the roll the clerk copies now holds sixty-three hands, forty-one named and twenty-two not, and the count runs short at morning.
  • The twin cuts both ways — the page's last realization. The halves were protection (a word made portable) and they are also a roll-call by thread: the Office does not need names, it has the other halves. This is the chain: entered, then required, then called by thread. It was built across the whole chapter out of the keeper's own protection, and the page must let the floor drop in a caption, not in a scream.
  • The purchased reader's four words are the page's most dangerous canon. A reading is a reading — a paid instrument certifying the free one. He is not redeemed and not hostile; he is a professional protecting the market. Do not give him a second line and do not bring him inside.
  • The chain-stop is not spent on this page. The page ends at the open drawer — the object is not shown, only the drawer, the lamp, and the hand. The spending decision, and whatever it costs, is Page 010's. The mirror is exact and deliberate: Page 008 ended "I pick up the lamp"; Page 009 ends "I put the lamp where it always stands, and I open the drawer."
  • Continuity: Kessa holds the stall and the box — the custody never leaves her. Nima's line ties required to hold — the Office's verbs repeating across pages. The four notes get one caption and are laid to rest for the chapter's close. Rekhak stays off-panel. Numbers: sixty-three hands on the roll, forty-one named and twenty-two not; thirteen fists; four notes silent seventeen days; chain-stop ONE, unspent, drawer open.
