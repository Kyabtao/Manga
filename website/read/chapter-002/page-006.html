Chapter 002 — Page 006

Chapter title: The Minute That Owes / वह मिनट जो ऋणी है Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — up-terrace (re-review chamber, first bell) → basin (one bell-cutaway) → corridor Page type: The held stop — Chapter 2's one chain-stop, SPENT — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

Beats carried from Page 005: the held-stop demand, the pre-filed docket ("two owners of the bell"), the auditors' quarter-turn ceremony, the door never closed. Beats carried from Chapter 001: the commissioned hour (Pages 008–009), the fold-not-a-door vision, the chain grammar (momentary stop = tell), Ira as the room's one reader of chains (Page 009), the Spindle's once-in-four-centuries flicker (Page 008).

BUDGET SPENT HERE. This page contains Chapter 2's single chain-stop. It is spent on three sentences that are smaller than the truth and entirely true. No further stop may occur in Chapter 2; the chain runs in every later panel it appears in.

Registers: the sworn sentence omits the silent second by being precise about the palm; the truth register is therefore touched only as an absence. Nobody says "the second" aloud.

PANEL 1 — Wide (~14%)

Camera: The chamber at first bell: stone light a shade earlier and colder than Page 005's; the door still open its half-hand (it is never closed in Chapter 2). Rekhak standing at the curved table; the three auditors seated; the docket-slate open before the middle seat. Image: The ceremony's negative space: no oath-object, no raised hand, no book — only Rekhak's fist at his side with the chain running, and the middle auditor's grey palm flat on the slate, waiting. Through the open door, faint on the corridor stone, the terrace bell's first vibration is visible as a grain of light shaking off a hinge — the bell heard in architecture, not in sound.

CAPTION (none spoken): First bell. No oath-object, no raised hand, no holy book. In the Office, an oath is only a chain asked to stand still while its owner speaks.

PANEL 2 — Macro (~13%)

Camera: His fist at his side, extreme close; the brass chain mid-run — and then, at the first sworn word (which lands between panels), stopped. Image: The first still chain in Chapter 2. Draw the stop as physics: links settling a half-degree, the run's blur gone, brass going suddenly, horribly detailed. One link brighter than the rest where his thumb last touched it. No sound-effect lettering; the silence is the SFX.

REKHAK (dialogue, off-panel, first sworn word arriving as the chain dies): The palm held a fold, not a door.

PANEL 3 — Medium close (~13%)

Camera: Rekhak waist-up, eyes closed — the same closed eyes as Chapter 001 Page 008's kneel; the still chain in his fist at frame bottom; the auditors' three grey silhouettes soft behind. Image: The sentence continues, three balloons, uninflected, each landing on the same unmoving brass. His face gives nothing; the collar hides the neck-line; the brow suture is the only mark on him that does not owe money.

REKHAK (dialogue, sworn, second balloon): I saw the fold.

REKHAK (dialogue, sworn, third balloon): In the palm, I saw nothing else.

PANEL 4 — Medium (~14%)

Camera: The table from the middle auditor's side: three seated greys, and the standing grey coat beyond them; the held stop now older than the sentence — he has finished speaking and has not released the chain. Image: The quarter-flinch, drawn as millimetres: Auditor Two's folded hands tighten by one knuckle. Auditor Three's open palm closes by one finger. The middle auditor's slate-edge tilts a degree toward him, as if to read the still brass from here. At the tilted slate's corner, unfocused by the tilt itself: a dry crimson initial that was there before the bell — the chamber's own record pre-touched, like the docket downstairs; nobody at the table looks at it, which is how they survive it. Caption states what they know: a held stop that never deposits means every word was true — all of it — and a true sentence that small is the most dangerous document in the room, because it proves how much true sentence was left over.

CAPTION (none spoken): No deposit. Which means every sworn word was true. Which means the hour holds more truth than the oath spent, and everyone at this table now knows exactly how much room is left between the fold and the hour.

PANEL 5 — Wide (~13%) — the bell-cutaway

Camera: Basin, same bell-moment, day six: the stall-row from Ira's crate; the shut Knot & Nail dead closed behind her (no slit, sixth day); the market owned-quiet; ash falling; and high above the terraces, the Spindle's pillar of light, steady. Image: Ira looks up at the Spindle with the bell, the way the whole basin does and never admits doing. The light does nothing. No flicker, no dim, no bend — nothing, steadily, which after the night it flickered (Chapter 001 Page 008) is not neutral. Her mismatched eyes hold it one beat, then drop to her gloves. She feels nothing. The Loom never speaks.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, small): The bell rang up-terrace. I felt nothing — I never feel theirs. But the Spindle's light did nothing too. Steady. After that night, nothing is not neutral.

PANEL 6 — Medium (~13%)

Camera: Back in the chamber: release. His fist opens one finger at a time; the chain starts — and the middle auditor turns the docket-slate a quarter-turn back, the ceremony reversed: accepted. Image: The acceptance is one word from Auditor One, level as ever. But Auditor Three's eyes stay on the running chain one beat after the slate has turned — he is the only one of the three who watched the release instead of the record. Plant, do not explain.

AUDITOR ONE (dialogue, level, one word): Recorded.

CAPTION (none spoken): Chapter 2's one stop, spent on three true sentences. The smallest bill in the building.

PANEL 7 — Macro (~17%) — HOOK

Camera: Corridor after: his hand at his side mid-stride, chain running through the fingers — and one link does not run: a single darkened, still link amid the moving brass, seated a half-degree deep, like a bone set wrong. His thumb hovers over it, not touching. Beyond, the corridor rail's docket-slate, out of focus, crimson initial unfocused to a warm smear. Image: The stored oath. Caption delivers the consequence seeded on Page 005: a held stop writes itself into the brass; anyone who reads chains can read the oath off him now, forever, word for word — and this city has exactly one chain-reader. He knows her name. He has known since the basin morning she read his stop and said nothing about it. His oath is hers now, whenever she chooses to look at his hand.

CAPTION (none spoken): An oath on a held stop is stored where only a reader of chains can read it. He knows one reader. She owes him a falsified audit and a stopped chain's silence, and now his sworn words are sewn into his brass like a fold in a palm — waiting for a mender's eyes.

CAPTION (none spoken, smaller): The smallest bill in the building, and the only one addressed to a girl who was not in the room.

Writing & art notes

  • BUDGET: this is the stop. No chain stops anywhere else in Chapter 2 after Panel 2's stop and Panel 6's release; later panels show the chain running (with its one dark link).
  • The sentence is entirely true. "In the palm, I saw nothing else" — the silent second was citywide, never in the palm. No deposit, therefore; the auditors' flinch is at the size of the truth left over, not at a lie. Do not letter or caption the silent second anywhere on this page.
  • Draw the stop as physics, not magic: settled links, vanished motion-blur, sudden detail. The release in Panel 6 is one finger at a time — the chain starts like a heart, not a switch.
  • The dark link (Panel 7) is a relic from this page forward: it stays in every later Rekhak panel of the series until a much later arc spends it. Continuity: one darkened still link, thumb-side, always.
  • Auditor Three's beat (watching the release, not the record) is a plant for his later-arc differentiation; do not give him a tick, a ring or a second glance this chapter.
  • Basin cutaway is exactly one panel (Panel 5). The Spindle's steadiness is the event; no flicker, no hum, no voice — the Loom never speaks, and the four notes stay silent until Chapter 3.
  • Shutters day six: dead closed, no slit (the pattern break established Page 004 continues). Kessa's absence is the stall's absence; do not show interior.
  • Colour rule: crimson appears twice, both dry, both unfocused or edge-placed: the pre-bell initial on the chamber slate (Panel 4, art canon) and the docket smear (Panel 7). The oath page otherwise belongs to brass and stone; the principal's hand stays at the edges because for once he did not get what he scheduled — only pre-touched what recorded it.
  • No lettering in art: slate lines and the docket initial remain abstract strokes/glyphs.
  • Lekh's cuff-chit sleeps; the Grey Clerk stays gone; Bhan, Guthli, Pira sleep off-panel (the owned quiet continues, unshown).
