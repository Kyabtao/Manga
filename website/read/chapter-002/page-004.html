Chapter 002 — Page 004

Chapter title: The Minute That Owes / वह मिनट जो ऋणी है Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — up-terrace (the filing end, first interior) → Ashfall Basin (silent pot-row) Page type: Procedure's choice / record self-corrects — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

Beats carried from Page 003: the sold blink census, the crimson cross-out with no owner, the new-struck coin, the pot-row's loss of ownership. Beats carried from Pages 001–002: Lekh's rail table, the seated-or-leaving rule, the minute-chit as the smallest record the Office keeps.

Grey Clerk reconciliation: his Page 003 cast rule was no second scene this chapter; the precise rule is no second market scene — Council fear enters the basin once. Page 004 gives him a hand-off at the rail (standing, delivering, leaving in the same panel) and then nothing, ever, this chapter.

Registers: the census is now Council property; its suppression is the rumour register's new state. The truth register (one silent second) is not mentioned on this page by anyone.

PANEL 1 — Wide (~14%)

Camera: Up-terrace, the filing end of the Reckoning Office — first interior this series: a wall of shallow drawers and folio slots behind the visitors'-rail table, intake slot at the rail's centre, zero ash, stone light. Morning, day four. Image: LEKH seated at his rail table (he is always seated or leaving). Across the stone, the GREY CLERK sets down the rolled census and one grey intake chit — and is already turning to leave in the same breath; his market errand ended on the stair. Two words, procedure-speak, no ceremony. Behind Lekh, the filing wall's drawers stand open in ranks like a held breath.

GREY CLERK (dialogue, level, already turning): Count. Basin.

CAPTION (none spoken): Names are a count. The count is a filing category. The category wins. Procedure always wins; that is what makes it procedure.

PANEL 2 — Close (~13%)

Camera: Down the unrolled census on the black stone, Lekh's pale-cyan sleeve at frame edge. Image: The list read as record: ink strokes, charcoal strokes, the fingernail scratch — and Lekh's finger pausing at the foot on the three Kshudra knots. He can read them (knot-rub is Kshudra writing; the basin's clerks learn it or starve). Procedure cannot: knots are not signatures. His other hand already makes the margin stroke that turns three knots into the numeral three. Then his finger travels one line lower, and stops.

CAPTION (none spoken): Three knots, three blinks, one numeral. Translation is allowed. Authorship is not.

PANEL 3 — Macro (~13%)

Camera: The cross-out, extreme close; and cut into the same panel as a narrow inset strip: an initialled folio page — a column of cross-outs, each with a small clerk's initial beside it, grey on grey, row after row. Image: The census's foot: the fine formal ink line crossed in crimson, dry — and beside it, nothing. No initial. The inset folio states the rule without a word: every cross-out in the Office is initialled by the hand that made it. The crimson one was made by no hand the Office owns.

CAPTION (none spoken): Rule 9 of the filing end: a cross-out is a claim. A claim has a hand. A hand has an initial. This cross-out has none. Therefore, procedurally, this cross-out was never made. Therefore, procedurally, the name under it is still on the list.

PANEL 4 — Medium (~14%)

Camera: Lekh's table from his side: two objects at his elbow — a grey file-chit and a black flag-chit (the flag opens an audit of whatever it touches, including the audit). His hand between them. Behind the rail, out of focus: the rows re-stamping, the Office's fear-grammar murmuring on. Image: The choice played as stillness: his ink-stained cuff hovers; the flag-chit is the honest object and the dangerous one — flagging the cross-out audits the census, and the census is Council coin's purchase, and Council coin does not like being audited. He chooses file: the census slides into the intake slot. But his other hand, under the table's lip, writes a minute-chit of his own — cross-out, crimson, dry, no initial, time, drawer — and puts it in his cuff, not the drawer. An off-book record. The first seed of the basin's trusted up-terrace face.

LEKH (dialogue, only words he speaks, to the stone, barely): Filed as found.

CAPTION (none spoken): Clerks never stand for anyone. They also never lie in ink. He has found the width of the gap between those two rules, and he lives in it now.

PANEL 5 — Wide (~13%)

Camera: Basin, same morning: the pot-row from the stall-row's side. Day four of the shutters. Image: The silent row: no queue, no marks being added, the list-post standing with its weeping nail-holes and nothing nailed to it. Guthli's pot works for nobody; her ladle hand is idle — first idle hand she has had in the series. Pira is present and not speaking, laundry under her arm, eyes down, moving past the post without touching it. A rumour that belongs to the Council is a rumour nobody dares repeat. At frame edge, on her crate: Ira, watching the post, not the stall. Behind her the Knot & Nail shutter stays dead closed — day four is the first morning the slit does not open at all. Kessa's comment on the owned quiet is a shutter that refuses even its own slit.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, small): The row went quiet the day the list got sold. Not sadder quiet. Owned quiet.

PANEL 6 — Medium close (~13%)

Camera: Stair mouth / dock road, midday heat: BHAN hauling, and the frame counts his load. Image: Two sacks where the page-003 haul was one; sleeve down in the heat; his gait even, unhurt, patient as ever — the itch-less mark letting him work double shifts without the fray's tax. Nobody watches him; the basin has its own owned quiet. At frame edge, blurred, Ira's crate-side profile as his haul passes her sightline.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, small): Bhan took a double shift. The mark lets him now. That is the kindest thing that tear has ever done — and I don't believe it for one second.

PANEL 7 — Macro (~17%) — HOOK

Camera: Next morning, filing end: the census's home drawer standing open; LEKH seated again at the rail (seated-or-leaving), folio on his knee, morning light, zero ash. Image: The cross-out in the filed census now has an initial: one crimson character, dry, flaking at the edges — in no clerk's hand in the Office, because no clerk's hand was here at night. The drawer's lock shows no scratch. The folio's rule-column, inset again at frame edge, now holds a second exception under the first. Lekh's hand goes to his cuff — where his minute-chit says no initial, yesterday, this drawer — and procedure will say it was always there. His off-book chit is now the only true record in the building, and the only provable lie.

CAPTION (none spoken): By morning the record had initialled itself. Which means the Office now holds a lie Lekh can prove — and proving it is an audit of the audit, and he is a clerk, and clerks do not stand for anyone.

CAPTION (none spoken, smaller): He stands up.

Writing & art notes

  • Lekh's rule breaks once, at the last word. "He stands up." is the only standing Lekh does in Chapter 2; the Ch. 001 reservation ("clerks never stand for anyone — except once, in a later arc") is NOT spent here: he stands for his own record, not for a person. Keep the later-arc standing reserved and different (he will stand for someone, there).
  • Chain-stop budget: unspent. Rekhak does not appear; no chain on panel. The re-review stays off-panel until Page 005.
  • The four notes stay silent. No humming anywhere, including the basin panels.
  • Colour rule: crimson appears twice (Panels 3, 7), dry both times, never touching grey wax in the same panel. The flag-chit is black, not red — do not let art warm it.
  • No lettering in art: the initial in Panel 7 is a single abstract crimson glyph, not a readable letter; the folio columns are strokes; the census remains strokes and knots.
  • Kessa = shutters, day four: for the first time the slit does not open at all (Panel 5). The pattern break is her comment on the owned quiet — three days of slits made today's dead shutter a sentence. No face, no hand, no interior.
  • The Grey Clerk exits the chapter here: standing, delivering, leaving within Panel 1. No further appearance, no name, ever, unless a much later arc re-hires him.
  • Suppression is the rumour register's new state: nobody on this page repeats the blink story; the silence is owned, not afraid-of-Guthli. Pira's downcast eyes are loyalty to the sold list, not shame.
  • The Loom never speaks. No hum change on panel.
