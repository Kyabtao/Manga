Canon rules added in Chapter 2 (binding forward)

  1. Wax grammar: the Council's wax is grey; crimson belongs to no Sector on record and is the principal's paperwork. The two waxes share a surface only on the two-wax chit (Page 008, one arm) and the folded letter (Page 010, one girl) — reserved icons, do not reuse casually.
  2. The principal never appears; acts only through paperwork; and pre-files (docket dated before the lie, Page 005; chamber slate initialled before the bell, Page 006). Fear in this series is procedural: re-stamps, census purchases, Rule 9 initials, night self-initials, inspection chits.
  3. Chain grammar, harmonised: a momentary stop is a costless tell (Ch. 001 grammar); a held-stop oath (chain still, first sworn word to last) opens the owner's ledger — a false word under it is a deposit. After an oath, one oath-link stays dark and still in the chain, series-long, readable word-for-word by any chain-reader (Ira).
  4. Rumour vs truth registers stayed separate all chapter: the blink census recorded witnesses, never the event; the sold census's suppression (owned quiet) is the rumour register's state from Page 004.
  5. The four notes (Mother's Four Notes, Page 001) stayed silent all chapter; stored only in Kessa's memory via thumb-rub on the tally-thread; must match wrongly first in Chapter 3; nothing audible may match them before then.
  6. Kessa's interiority = shutters only: slit days 1–3; dead shutter day 4 (pattern break as sentence); voice through the boards day 9 (a voice is not an opening); open day 11 (verdict on Ira's choice). The lockbox lock is heard, never shown (Page 009); the sewer box stays locked.
  7. The cutter's method: nightly cuts end in turn-backs — unpick-grammar, stitch-grammar copied from Ira's own palm-suture (Page 009). The method's family is named; the cutter's name is unrevealed until Chapter 3; the sewer's name stays locked longer.
  8. Whorl-grain gets no owner on panel; as of Page 010 the principal has harvested every grain sewn in Chapter 2 ("thanks you for the seams so far") — Chapter 3 treats grain as collected data.
  9. Fray-itch is universal; an itch-less fray-adjacent mark is sabotage's dividend (Bhan, Page 002).
  10. Lekh: seated-or-leaving; stood once, for his own record (Page 004) — the later-arc standing-for-someone reservation remains unspent and must differ. Cuff-chit entries: 1 = the uninitialled cross-out; 2 = the date Ira's name rode crimson paperwork.
  11. Rekhak: Reckoner-of-record for the basin market (Page 010) — demotion in form, watch in fact; Chapter 3's geography. Oath-link travels in every panel of his.
  12. The Loom never speaks; the Spindle's steadiness after its once-in-centuries flicker is an event (Page 006). Bells are civic time the basin feels in its lamps; the dock farm keeps Office hours (bell-tallied cuts, Page 008).

Chapter 003 setup

Title: The Hand That Opens / वह हाथ जो खोलता है · Arc I — The Unspooling. Opening brief (Page 001): morning of the letter: Kessa and Ira compare the lockbox's shadowless cut-end (out of the box, on the counter, first on-panel consultation) against Ira's rubbing of Bhan's turn-backs: the turn-backs match in school, not in age — and the cut-end's are forty years older than Ira's stitch. The re-aim reverses: Kessa, dry: "Child, your stitch didn't teach the cutter. The cutter taught your stitch." — the sewer and the cutter share a hand-school, and Ira's palm is its diploma. Hook: the folded letter, opened at last on panel: its contents are not a demand but an appointment — a grey-clerk posting order transferring the blink census's custody to… the Knot & Nail. The Council and the principal have agreed to store their correspondence in Kessa's stall: the basin's safest lockbox is now both sides' filing cabinet, and Kessa did not say yes.
