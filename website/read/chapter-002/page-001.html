THREADBORN — Chapter 02 — Page 001

Chapter title: The Minute That Owes Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin morning → Up-Terrace, the Reckoning Office Page type: Chapter opening / rumour — 7 panels, ends on a seal Canvas: vertical webtoon strip, full colour

Beats carried from Chapter 001: the silent second (008), the sixty-first knot (009), the mother-debt (007), Rekhak's falsified audit "Nothing more" (009), the crimson wax (003–009). Chapter 2 opens in a city that remembers a second it will never admit to — and in a debt paid early, between two women who do not hug.

PANEL 1 — Medium wide, the steam-pot rumour (~14%)

Camera: Street level in the basin, morning. Steam rising off a food pot between two stalls. Image: GUTHLI (broad, ladle still in hand) and PIRA (thin, arms full of somebody else's laundry) with their heads together over the steam. Around them the market works one octave too quietly. Ash falls as always. Nobody says the word yesterday.

CAPTION: The basin woke with a memory it had agreed to call a story.

GUTHLI (dialogue, low): The Spindle blinked.

PIRA (dialogue, lower): Spindles don't blink.

GUTHLI (dialogue): Then I blinked. Four hundred of us blinked together, and my marks went quiet in me, and I felt the Loom stop.

PIRA (dialogue, already walking away): Spindles. Don't. Blink.

PANEL 2 — Wide, fear looks like paperwork (~13%)

Camera: Down the Reckoning Office's rows of black stone desks, morning light hard and clean. Image: Every clerk re-stamping last night's records. Twice. The second stamp lands on the same page as the first, half a finger off — a page with two seals is a page somebody might later say was never sealed at all. No ash anywhere. No talking anywhere.

CAPTION: Up-terrace, fear does not run or shout.

CAPTION: It files.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the Knot & Nail, morning (~13%)

Camera: From the stall door, the same framing as Page 007 Panel 2 — but daylight this time, shutters up, and Ira entering without being sent for. Image: Kessa behind the counter with the tally-thread. Ira sets nothing on the counter: no coins, no needles, no folded form. She sets her own two hands down, flat, and sits.

IRA (dialogue): One true thing. Early.

KESSA (dialogue, hands stopping): The debt isn't due, be.

IRA (dialogue): I know what it's worth before it's due.

PANEL 4 — Tight two-shot, the four notes (~15%)

Camera: Foreheads close across the counter, lamp unlit in the daylight — the rhyme of Page 007 Panel 4 with the fire out. Image: Ira's eyes on her own hands while she remembers aloud. Kessa's loupe stays up: this is not appraisal, it is receiving. Her thumb rubs the tally-thread once, the way Kshudra write without writing.

IRA (dialogue): She hummed while she sewed. Off-key. Always the same four notes.

IRA (dialogue): I thought it was a work-song. Something to keep the needle's time.

IRA (dialogue, smaller): She never sang words. Only the four.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the stall closes (~13%)

Camera: On Kessa's hands as they do three things in order: fold the tally-thread away, shut the counter ledger, and reach for the shutter pole. Image: Ira watching, one eyebrow up — she has never seen the stall dark before noon in her life.

KESSA (dialogue, storing the four notes where ink can't reach them): Four notes. Off-key. Never words.

KESSA (dialogue): Stall's closed today.

IRA (dialogue): It's barely morning.

KESSA (dialogue, shutters starting down): Then it'll be a long day of closed.

CAPTION: In four Unspoolings, the Knot & Nail had never closed for weather, debt, or death.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the slip arrives (~13%)

Camera: Rekhak's own desk up-terrace: a bare stone table, one lamp unneeded in the daylight, his ledger-slate closed for once. LEKH — the young clerk from the visitors' rail — sets a single slip on the stone and is already backing away, because clerks know which papers have weather around them. Image: Rekhak's chain runs. His collar stays high. The slip on the stone carries a seal the colour of old blood.

LEKH (dialogue, from the door, already gone): For the Reckoner's hand only.

SFX: tk (the slip, set down)

PANEL 7 — HOOK — grey wax, crimson wax (~19%)

Camera: Macro on the slip under Rekhak's thumb, held at the edge, unbroken. Beyond it, out of focus, the window onto the clean terraces. Image: The crimson seal in daylight: no crest, no lettering, only a colour that does not belong to any Sector on record. Rekhak's thumb does not move. The chain in his other hand has not stopped — and somehow that is worse.

REKHAK (dialogue, to the empty room, procedural to the last): An audit… of the audit.

REKHAK (dialogue, softer): He read my nothing more.

CAPTION: The Council's wax is grey.

CAPTION (final box, bottom): This slip was not sealed by the Council.

Page notes for continuity

  • The rumour is the silent second's public face. Nobody names the hour, the hum or the flicker in the same sentence as Ira. The basin's story stays folkloric ("four hundred of us blinked") while the reader knows the count was one second. Keep the two registers separate forever; the day they merge is a reveal.
  • Two stamps on one page = the Council's fear grammar. A re-stamped record is deniable. Use the doubled seal as the up-terrace visual for "this never happened" from now on.
  • The mother-debt instalment is paid early and received without ink. Kessa's thumb-rub is Kshudra writing; nothing is written down anywhere. The four notes are now stored in exactly one place in the world: a goblin's memory. That is both the safest and the most stealable vault in Agnikhand.
  • The four notes are a planted key, not a payoff. Off-key, four notes, never words. Do not let them match anything audible before Chapter 3 at the earliest; when they finally match, it must match wrongly first.
  • Kessa closing the stall is her alarm bell. She has heard a seam in the world (unpulled thread, a sealing, a swatch in her lockbox) and now a melody that she recognises without being able to say from where. She closes the stall to think. Do not show her thinking; show the shutters.
  • Lekh is named here (reserved on Page 005): the visitors'-rail clerk. Young, procedural, and the only up-terrace face the basin will ever trust, later. Keep them seated-or-leaving in every scene; clerks never stand for anyone, except once, in a later arc.
  • Crimson wax grammar, now hard canon: the Council's wax is grey; crimson belongs to no Sector on record. Any future crimson seal is the principal's paperwork, and the principal never appears — only slips, call-ups, re-reviews. The buyer reads lies for what they are: locks.
  • Chain budget for Chapter 2: one stop. Do not spend it before Page 006, and spend it on something smaller than the truth.
  • Sewer locked, principal unseen, knot-shape ownerless, Loom silent.
