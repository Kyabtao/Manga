THREADBORN — Chapter 01 — Page 006

Chapter title: The Girl With No Thread Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office, the broker's small room Page type: Negotiation — 7 panels, ends on a real smile Canvas: vertical webtoon strip, full colour

Beats carried from Page 005: the three blank lines, the hour's price, Patra's held-sharp face, Rekhak's silence and running chain. This page is Ira negotiating the way she mends: inspect the tear before you sew. It ends with the first word Rekhak has spent in two pages.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the form turned over (~13%)

Camera: Level across the table. Ira has pulled the registration form toward her — and turned it over, blank side up, like a workpiece on a mending bench. Image: Her cut-finger glove flat on the blank back of the paper. Patra's almost-smile waits. In the doorway Rekhak has not moved; the chain runs.

IRA (dialogue, flat): Nothing gets sewn till I've seen the tear.

PATRA (dialogue, mild): Then look, child. Looking is free.

IRA (dialogue): Nothing's free. It's just billed later.

PANEL 2 — Medium close, three terms on three fingers (~14%)

Camera: On Ira's gloved hand counting the terms down, one finger at a time. Behind it, out of focus, Patra's folded translucent hands. Image: Finger one: the witness. Finger two: the ground. Finger three: the question. The blank form under her palm like a board.

IRA (dialogue): One. The hour gets a witness who can count. Kessa of the Knot & Nail holds the other end of the thread. Her knot times it, not your clock.

IRA (dialogue): Two. The hour is spent in the basin, at her stall, before dawn. My ground, my air, my ash.

IRA (dialogue): Three. One question answered first. And answered true.

PANEL 3 — Two-shot, the acceptance that costs nothing (~13%)

Camera: Side-on across the table again. Patra accepts all three terms in a single mild sentence — no pushback, no pause. Ira's eyes narrow a fraction: too easy. Image: On the wall, Patra's wrong shadow sits perfectly still while Patra leans forward.

PATRA (dialogue): All three, child. Witness, ground and question.

IRA (thought, italic): Three terms. No pushback.

IRA (thought, italic): The price was never the hour.

PATRA (dialogue): And one term of mine: when the hour ends, you do not ask him what he saw.

PATRA (dialogue): He means to lie to you about it. I would rather the lie wait until the hour is paid.

PANEL 4 — Tight on Patra, the half-line crack (~15%)

Camera: Close on Patra's face as the question lands. For half a line, the mildness cracks: the shadow jerks toward the lamp twice, the translucent edges flare bright, and the held-sharp face appears a page early — then settles back to plain. Image: Ira's question hangs in the air between them, unlettered but visible in both their stillness.

IRA (dialogue): What thread was the stitch sewn with?

PATRA (dialogue, quiet, and absolutely true): Thread that has never been pulled.

IRA (dialogue): There's no such thread. Everything pulls.

PATRA (dialogue): Everything owed pulls, child. That is the whole of the law.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the lend (~13%)

Camera: Wider now; all three in frame for the first time this page — Ira seated, Patra leaning, Rekhak in the doorway gone very slightly rigid. Image: Ira is doing arithmetic out loud; the room is doing it with her. The chain in the doorway has not changed rhythm, which is itself a statement.

IRA (dialogue, slow): Then whoever sewed me wasn't borrowing.

PATRA (dialogue): No, child.

PATRA (dialogue, softer): Whoever sewed you was lending.

PANEL 6 — Medium, one step and one word (~12%)

Camera: On Rekhak in the doorway. He moves — the first step he has taken in two pages — one step into the room. The brass chain is still running, rhythmic, controlled: whatever this is, it is not panic. Image: Patra and Ira both turn. Ash from his coat settles on the broker's clean floor; nobody in the up-terrace tracks ash like that unless they came down fast.

REKHAK (dialogue, one word, flat): Don't.

PANEL 7 — HOOK — the real smile (~20%)

Camera: Close on Patra. The almost-smile becomes, for one instant, a real smile — warm, private, and entirely alarming. The shadow on the wall smiles a half-beat late. Image: In the soft focus behind Patra: Rekhak one step inside the room, chain running; Ira watching Patra, not him — she has learned this page where the truth lives.

PATRA (dialogue, delighted, quiet): Oh good. He's invested.

CAPTION: In two pages of silence, the Reckoner had spent one word.

CAPTION (final box, bottom): Three adults in the room. Three debts in the air. And one girl who owed nothing, holding the blank side of her own name.

Writing & art notes

  • Ira negotiates like a mender. "Nothing gets sewn till I've seen the tear" is her professional idiom turned into foreign policy. Every term she sets is a tool she already owns: Kessa's knot-count, her own ground, her own question. She never asks for anything she cannot hold.
  • Patra's added term is a warning dressed as a condition. "He means to lie to you about it" pays off on Page 009 (the chain stops as Rekhak records his lie). Patra never lies — so when Patra forecasts a lie, treat it as weather.
  • "Thread that has never been pulled" is a material answer, not a name. The box stays locked. But the implication is canon-sized: the first law says thread can only be borrowed; unpulled thread was never borrowed — so it was never owed, or never made by the Loom at all. Do not explain it this chapter. Let it sit like a needle in a cushion.
  • "Whoever sewed you was lending" is the chapter's biggest drip and still not a name. Kessa will react to the material, not the metaphor, on Page 007 ("then it wasn't a mending, it was a sealing").
  • Rekhak's "Don't." is ambiguous by design. Chain running = boxed fear, deliberate act. The reader must not be able to tell whether he is warning Ira, warning Patra, or begging. Keep his face neutral; the chain carries the meaning.
  • The held-sharp face appears a half-beat early in Panel 4 (Patra's crack). That is deliberate: the one-held-face-per-page rule is bent only when Patra touches the unpulled thread. From now on, if the sharp face appears twice on a page, the unpulled thread is in the room.
  • Ira addresses the hour as hers to schedule ("Dawn. The Knot & Nail.") on Page 007, not here; this page ends before she accepts aloud. The acceptance is her descent in Panel 1 of Page 007.
