THREADBORN — Chapter 01 — Page 004

Chapter title: The Girl With No Thread Arc: I — The Unspooling Sector: Agnikhand (Ash Sector) — The Knot & Nail → the Council Stair Page type: Confrontation / contract — 7 panels, ends on a closed door Canvas: vertical webtoon strip, full colour

Beats carried from Page 003: the name spoken (Ira Sutar), the two-ledger contradiction, the crimson-sealed page, the locked box. This page gives Ira her first words to an official — flat, contractual, unimpressed — and ends by turning her appraisal back onto the appraiser.

PANEL 1 — Medium three-shot (~13%)

Camera: Level with the counter again, same framing as Page 003 Panel 1 — but now Ira has pulled her wrist free and is standing straight. She does not step back. Image: Ira, Kessa and Rekhak in one frame for the first time as equals-of-negotiation. Ira's face is not frightened; it is pricing. Rekhak's ledger-slate is half-open. Kessa is very still, one eye still magnified behind the loupe she never flipped back up.

IRA (dialogue, flat): Is this an arrest?

REKHAK (dialogue): An arrest requires a name on the register.

IRA (dialogue): I don't have one.

REKHAK (dialogue, almost warm): Correct. Which is why this is an invitation.

PANEL 2 — Over-the-slate (~15%)

Camera: From behind Ira's shoulder, looking down at the opened ledger-slate Rekhak has turned toward her. Two ruled columns, inked in a clerk's perfect hand. Image: Column one and column two, as rules of record. Tucked behind them, the folded page with the crimson wax seal shows one corner of itself — unlettered, unexplained. Rekhak's long finger rests between the columns like a knife laid on a table.

REKHAK (dialogue): Column one: you climb the Council Stair at the third bell tomorrow. You meet the person who wants you. You come down.

REKHAK (dialogue): Column two: the Council corrects the ledger. The Court's page is struck. You become exactly what the register already says you are — nothing. No record. No rights. No writ needed for the Mendery to bind you.

REKHAK (dialogue, closing the slate): I recommend column one. It has fewer teeth.

PANEL 3 — Tight two-shot, Kessa and Ira (~12%)

Camera: Side-on, foreheads almost touching. Rekhak is out of frame entirely — for three lines, the Council does not exist. Image: Kessa's enormous hand closes on Ira's shoulder. Not gentle, not rough — final. Ira's jaw is set; she is already gone, and Kessa can see it.

KESSA (dialogue, quiet, final): You don't climb, be. Things that go up-terrace come back as ledgers.

IRA (dialogue): Holes get filled either way, Kessa.

IRA (dialogue, smaller): I'd rather walk in on my own feet.

PANEL 4 — Wide, the market exhales (~13%)

Camera: From inside the stall, watching the Council Stair — the exact reverse of Page 003 Panel 4. Image: Rekhak climbing away, coat swallowing the stair-light. Behind him the guttering lanterns re-ignite one by one, warm colour flooding back down the alley toward the stall. Shoulders drop. A steam pot resumes boiling. Nobody looks at Ira.

REKHAK (off-panel, from the stair, not turning): Third bell. Alone, or not at all.

REKHAK (off-panel): And Kessa — Knot and Nail. Light debt, honest hole. Keep it that way.

CAPTION: The lanterns came back one by one, the way they had gone out.

SFX: pf. pf. pf. (lanterns, re-lighting up the alley)

PANEL 5 — Macro, borrowed lines (~15%)

Camera: Straight down at the counter: Kessa's huge scarred hand painting three thin dark lines on the inside of Ira's left forearm with a stencil-brush and a cracked pot of grey-black ink. Ira's cut-finger glove is pushed down to her wrist. Three coins sit beside the pot, already accepted. Image: The painted lines are convincingly debt-like — thin, dark, slightly under the skin's shine. Ira watches her own fake debt dry. Kessa's other hand holds the brush steady as a surgeon's.

KESSA (dialogue): You don't go.

KESSA (dialogue, same breath, not looking up from the arm): And since you will go — wear some debt. Ash-ink. It fools every eye in the terraces. Guards, clerks, creditors.

IRA (dialogue): Fools him?

KESSA (dialogue): Nothing fools Sight, be. But he is one man, and the terraces are full of eyes.

KESSA (dialogue, pocketing the coins): Three coins. The worst deal I have made this year.

PANEL 6 — The Council Stair (~13%)

Camera: Low from below the stairs, looking up. Grey daylight and falling ash above; lantern warmth below. Ira stands three steps under Rekhak, who has stopped and turned — nobody follows a Reckoner up his own stair. Image: Ira, small, sleeves down, chin up. Rekhak halfway into the cold light, the brass counting-chain running through his fingers as always. Ash settles on his shoulders; he does not brush it off.

IRA (dialogue): One question. Free of charge.

REKHAK (dialogue): My rates are set by the Council.

IRA (dialogue): You read every debt in a room at a glance. And yours is climbing your throat.

PANEL 7 — HOOK — extreme two-shot, the other closed door (~19%)

Camera: Faces close enough to share breath, profile to profile, ash drifting between them. Image: The hairline crack of dark marks under Rekhak's left Sight-mark has tightened. And his hand — for the first time since Page 003 — has stopped counting: the brass chain hangs motionless through closed fingers. Ira's mismatched eyes do not blink. Somewhere under the panel, a hum.

IRA (dialogue, small, exact): What do you owe, Reckoner?

REKHAK (dialogue, softer than he has been all chapter — and the hum underneath it): It is the one ledger I am not permitted to open.

REKHAK (dialogue): My own.

CAPTION: Ira had spent the day learning that her hand was a closed door.

CAPTION (final box, bottom): On the stair, she found another one.

SFX: (under everything, in his throat) hmmmm

Writing & art notes

  • Ira's first words to authority are contractual. No gasp, no plea, no awe: is this an arrest, invitations come with a price, one question, free of charge. She negotiates because negotiating is the only language the city ever taught her. Keep every line of hers under ten words where possible.
  • The ash-ink fools eyes, not Sight. Kessa says so on-panel; do not let a later page forget it. When Rekhak sees the painted lines (he will), the tension is that he knows that she knows he can see through them. The fake debt is armour against the crowd, never against him.
  • Kessa's contradiction is the character. She forbids the climb and equips it in the same breath, and never acknowledges the contradiction. The three coins are her love language; "the worst deal I have made this year" is a hug. Do not let her explain herself.
  • The counting-chain is Rekhak's emotional meter. It runs when he is in control (Panels 6, Page 003 Panel 5). It stops only when he is afraid or lying. Panel 7 is the first stop in the series. From now on the artist must track chain-motion in every panel he appears in.
  • His unreadable own-debt mirrors Ira's unmendable palm. Two closed doors, same chapter. Do not explain the mechanism. Do not let Sight read himself later "because it would be useful."
  • Lantern rule is now two-way: officials arriving = lanterns gutter toward the stair (Page 003); officials leaving = lanterns re-ignite behind them (Panel 4). The market's weather is the Council's timetable.
  • The crimson page shows one corner in Panel 2 and nothing more. No lettering on the seal, no name, no faction. Not this chapter.
  • The third bell is tomorrow. Page 005 opens on the climb; do not insert a night scene between them.
  • The sewer stays a locked box. Nobody on this page knows it, says it, or guesses it.
