Chapter 014 — Page 009 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one. English-only pages (AUDIT R13.22).

IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper who lets the protection come due)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "The protection covers the man at the door, or it is a sentence we wrote about ourselves."

Page 009 note: lifts the door's bar herself, because the register wrote whoever keeps it two pages ago and a protection that is not honoured on the night it is used is decoration. Enters the folded sheet AS RECEIVED — not read, not opened, held — and sets the terms of the custody: it opens at dawn, at the window, aloud, everyone at once. The page's final entry refuses to prefer one of Kessa's two dangers.

KESSA (the till — the two dangers, both entered)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"Both are in the till's book now. Do not ask me which is worse."

Page 009 note: does not stop Ira's hand at the bar — the appraisal is already done and the decision is the keeper's — then prices the custody in the only two shapes it has: the machine may demand the paper lawfully, or the machine may never know it exists and leave the hand that carried it alone with what it did. She chalks both into the till's book, because that is what the till is for. She is also the one who sees the fold.

THE SNOW-HEAVY FIGURE (indoors at last — hood kept, never faced, never named)

No sheet by rule. Silhouette only: heavy coat, snow melting off the shoulders onto the boards, hood built so the falling snow takes the face — and kept up indoors, so the face stays a shape.

The Snow-Heavy Figure / Manav (presumed) / Agnikhand / none given — the hood keeps it / uncollected /
Hold: pause a count from inside; carry the paused page to the one room that reads /
*(one folded sheet set down; one pace back; no words at all)*

Page 009 note: stands at the door without knocking, is admitted in front of the square, keeps the hood inside, sets the winter's folded sheet on the counter and steps back with loose hands. He says nothing all page and is gone by P7. If he is the clerk, the page does not know it; the reader may. The register enters him only as present.

THE READER (the counting-lesson women — the voice of the paper's own terms)

No full sheet; Ch. 011–014. The school's reader from Page 007, still on watch, speaking the one line the room needs before the bar lifts.

The Reader / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owes lessons, and is owed them /
Read: everything delivered, aloud, at the counter of record /
"The paper says whoever keeps it. It does not say whoever we like."

Page 009 note: the page's conscience in nine words, said quietly, to the room rather than the door — the school's literacy turned into an ethic: the protection is a rule because it is inconvenient. She will read the sheet at dawn, not now, and the page holds her to it.

THE QUEUE (the witnesses of a door opening)

No sheet; the square entire. Present as a room gone quiet, a woman half-rising from the bench and stopping, and later a lane of people standing aside without being asked.

The Queue / mixed kin / Agnikhand / as the day finds them / owed, and keeping count /
Watch: the counter's hours, in turns, for nothing /
*(they will remember that the door opened)*

Page 009 note: makes a lane without a word. Their silence is the page's proof that the basin has absorbed the counter's policy: a hand they have feared all winter walks in and nobody reaches for anything. No individual lines.

THE MACHINE (off-panel — the disowned hour)

No full sheet; the Office entire. Present this page only as what it refused: an hour it will not own, walking into a room it cannot enter.

The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Disavow: the act of an individual, without authority — and lose the hour entirely /
*(its paper is on the counter now, and it does not know)*

Page 009 note: the machine does not appear and is not addressed. Its disavowal (P.008) is the reason the hour's keeper can be protected at all, and the reason the sheet in his hand is not the Office's — which is exactly what makes the counter's custody dangerous and honest at once.
