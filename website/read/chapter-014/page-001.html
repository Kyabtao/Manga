THREADBORN — Chapter 14 — Page 001

Chapter title: Year Nineteen Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the stall and the school, first snow to the year's turning Page type: Chapter opening — 7 panels; the first late delivery, the volunteer's paper, and the year that arrives un-invoiced

Beats: first snow, and the delivery is late — the machine's weekly paper misses its day for the first time since the terms were accepted, and the basin discovers its new dependency by standing in it; the register enters the miss — of record, without excuse: the machine is late; the register is not; the volunteer — Rekhak, off shift, lays his folded paper on the counter at last — carried since before the counter existed — and asks for it ENTERED UNREAD, under protection: the register protects the fold, not the contents; the queue watches a machine-hand made safe voluntarily — the institution grown past its first, forced use; the year's business — Nandi at the school's oldest rack, the year-knot under the lens: the old school's year turns tonight, and she can read it — at her price, an ask properly made; the thin delivery — the machine's paper arrives near midnight, under seal, and it is THIN: the permit-ledger is missing from the bundles, and nobody says so out loud; the turn — midnight at the oldest rack: the year-knot waits under the lamp, the ask is on the table, and the year nobody has invoiced stands in the doorway of the new one.

This page: the second Office's first winter — everything it built, weighed in cold.

Chapter 014 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet; her last answer still working. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed.

PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, first snow (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the square under first snow — rooftops white, breath standing over the queue. The line at the stall is wrapped in scarves and shawls, papers kept dry inside coats, and the window is open anyway, lamplight on fresh snow. Kessa at the till with the oil-cord's THREE knots hanging by the frame. No delivery bundles on the counter. The counter is bare where the machine's paper should be.

Image: The second Office's first winter. Everything it built, weighed in cold.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Ten weeks since the terms were accepted. Nine deliveries, each one on the day, each one thin or thick as the machine's week had been. Today the square is white, the queue is at my rail, and the count-house's satchel is nowhere on the road.

PANEL 2 — Medium, the miss entered (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the counter — Ira writing while the queue watches, breath fogging past the rail. The register open; the entry short. Kessa pours a cup of something hot and sets it inside the window-frame for the first in line. No apology anywhere in the stall.

Image: The new dependency, made visible: the basin can now wait on the machine's paper — and does.

IRA: (of record, entering) Entered: the weekly delivery is late. First miss since the terms. The register holds the day open until dusk — then the miss itself becomes the record, and the record will not care why.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): I built a counter that owes the basin an honest account of the machine. Nobody told me the heaviest thing on it would be a road that snowed shut.

PANEL 3 — Medium-close, the volunteer (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: the counter's end — Rekhak, off shift, snow on his shoulders, and his folded paper OUT of his coat for the second time ever, laid flat on the wood under his bare hand. He is not steady. He is decided.

Image: Carried since before the counter existed. Presented on the first day it might matter.

REKHAK: (quiet, and settled) You have seen the fold. Time you saw it entered. My paper, keeper: I have carried it since before your window was a window. I am not here to show it. I am here to ask what I watched you give the clerk.

IRA: (level) Say it of record.

REKHAK: Entered unread. Under protection. My name with it — the way you held his.

PANEL 4 — Close, entered unread (~13%)

Camera: Close: the folded paper — creased soft, edges gone cloth-like with seasons of a pocket — going into the register's back pocket UNOPENED, beside the courtesy slip's ghost-page, Kessa's hands doing the fold-in and the wax along the seal. The paper's contents never shown. The wax carries the keeper's knot and — beside it, small and new — Rekhak's own thumb-mark, pressed carefully, the way a man presses a thing he has decided to be able to deny nothing about.

Image: The register protects the fold, not the contents. That is why it can be trusted with both.

KESSA: (sealing, not unkind) The box would have kept it, you know. Mine keeps things longer.

REKHAK: Your box keeps things private, mother. The register keeps them TRUE. Different needs.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the queue watches (~13%)

Camera: Medium: the queue's front — basin folk looking at Rekhak with his empty hands and his new-pressed mark: the man who was the machine's coat for years, protected at the counter because he asked. Nobody cheers. Somebody makes room on the bench. In winter, that is the basin's whole speech.

Image: The institution grown past its first, forced use: the second protection, given freely, to a hand that used to be the machine's.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The first protection I granted because a gate-keeper bargained for it. This one walked in through snow and asked for nothing but the entering. The basin made room on the bench. In this square that is a citation.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the year's business (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the school's archive wall — the oldest rack, and Nandi at it, scarf down, the brass lens held to the lowest rod: the oldest one, silver thread, dust in the grain, the dense year-knot at its head. Ira holds the lamp. The cold in the room has a texture.

Image: Tonight the old school's year turns. The knot that says which year has waited longer than any roll the Office keeps.

NANDI: (to the knot, not to Ira) I can read this. You know my price, seamstress. A reading is an ask, and an ask is a line in somebody's count. For this one — mine or yours, seamstress?

IRA: (of record, before she can think better of it) Mine. Entered tonight. Read it.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the turn — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: midnight — the year-knot under the lens and the lamp together, Nandi's hands steady on the rod, Ira's pen at the ready over the open register, and OUTSIDE the small deep window: the count-house hand trudging up through the snow at last with the delivery satchel — near midnight, under seal, LATE — and visibly THIN: one bundle where there are usually three. The permit-ledger is missing, and nobody has said so out loud.

Image: The year nobody invoiced stands in the doorway of the new one — and the machine's own paper has come in thin.

NANDI: (reading, slow) Old school. It says: this rod was opened in the year of the second chain — nineteen years before their Freedom. The knot is not a date, seamstress. It is a DIRECTION.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Nineteen years before their Freedom. The Office's roll is nineteen years old. The school's archive is thirty-nine. Everything they have ever counted, they counted inside a room that was already full.

CAPTION (smallest): Invoice it.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the first late delivery (the dependency weighed in cold); Rekhak's paper (entered unread, under protection — voluntarily); the year-knot armed (the ask, the reading, the direction). The winter must be FELT in every panel — breath, snow on shoulders, papers kept inside coats, the bench — this chapter's antagonist is the cold and the calendar, not the Office.
  • Rekhak's paper is never shown. Entered UNREAD is the point: the register protects the fold, not the contents. His arc's warmth is the ASK — the man who watched the clerk get protected deciding he wants the same, on the first day it might matter. His thumb-mark goes in the wax beside the keeper's knot: the basin's grammar, chosen this time, not learned under duress. Do not explain what the paper is. Not this page, maybe not this chapter.
  • Nandi's price is canon, not villainy: readings are asks, asks are lines in counts — she has priced this way since Ch. 012. Ira paying with her OWN line (mine) is the chapter's first spend of record: the keeper enters herself into somebody's count to buy the year. One line, no elaboration.
  • The year-knot's reading must land as DIRECTION, not date: this rod was opened in the year of the second chain — nineteen years before their Freedom. The Office's count is nineteen years old; the school's archive is thirty-nine. Everything the machine ever counted, it counted inside a room that was already full. Do not unpack the consequences — the smallest caption (Invoice it.) is the ladder run and the chapter's marching order; the cost of knowing arrives over the chapter.
  • The thin delivery is the winter's cold current: one bundle where there are usually three; the permit-ledger missing; nobody says so out loud. The machine is under strain too — fuel is the basin's winter and the Office's both. Leave it visible, unexplained, entered as received.
  • The imperative ladder opens its chapter run: Invoice it.
