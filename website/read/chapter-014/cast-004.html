IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper who counts the silence instead of cracking it)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Enter the shape and not the contents."

Page 004 note: enters the hold's size in the machine's own units — one paused count, three weeks dated on the notice, one missing ledger — and does not chase the contents. Completes the arithmetic one step on-panel (a public counter turns a paused delivery into a paused reading), then writes the page's smallest and largest line: no one sent, cord left tied, Let him. The keeper's restraint is the page's action.

KESSA (the till — the ruling and the price)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"Cheaper than a closed door. Take his gift and let him watch us be careful with it."

Page 004 note: rules the winter's policy from the sill, loud enough for the queue: the window keeps its hours, the terms stand, the basin stays readable to a hand it cannot name. Then prices the risk honestly — every reading teaches the machine the basin's grammar — and pays it without ceremony. Trust as tactics, entered like a debt.

THE SNOW-HEAVY FIGURE (never faced, never named — the protector)

No sheet by rule: the page may not identify him. If he is the clerk, the page does not know it. Silhouette only: heavy coat, snow-loaded shoulders, hood and collar built so the falling snow takes the face; the head is a shape, never a face.

The Snow-Heavy Figure / Manav (presumed) / Agnikhand / none given — snow takes the face / uncollected /
Hold: pause a count from inside, and maintain it where the basin can see /
*(he reads the board back by touch, and re-ties what he finds)*

Page 004 note: at dusk, at the hatch board, reading the season notice back knot by knot — then changing two knots on one public cord in the machine's grammar. No dialogue, no eye contact, no perspective that could name him. His maintenance is a message the register refuses to answer directly: I will keep my hold in your sight; leave it standing. The page's answer is entered in Panel 7, and he never learns it.

THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (witnesses)

No full sheet; Ch. 011–014. The basin's institutional eyes at the far window of the stall, going still as the figure works the board — the queue noticing on the counter's behalf.

The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Read: the machine's paper, aloud — and now, who touches it /
"...he is reading it back. Nobody reads it back."

Page 004 note: no lines of record; two of them stop at the stall's far window in Panel 5 and watch. Their stillness is the page's proof that the basin is keeping the public side of its bargain without being told to: it sees him, and it does not move.

THE MACHINE (off-panel — the paper under the hold)

No full sheet; the Office entire. Present this page as a quantity only: a paused count, three weeks of fuel, one ledger that never travelled.

The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Shelter: a hold that pauses its own paper before the basin can read it /
"Machines do not guess. Somewhere a hand counted the weeks."

Page 004 note: the page never shows an Office interior and never explains the shortage. The machine's presence is arithmetic — its notice dated three weeks out, its ledger absent, its paper paused. Whatever is happening inside those walls this winter stays inside them; the register holds the size of it and moves on to policy.
