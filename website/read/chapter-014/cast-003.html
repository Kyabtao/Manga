IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the register with a knot in its own thread)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Paper paused cannot be read."

Page 003 note: enters the machine's reorganization without flinching — for the season, of what, it does not say — pays her second line for the tag's reading, states the winter's shape of record (the thin week was a shield, tied from inside, using the counter's public-reading rule as cover), and closes with the problem she cannot yet enter an answer under: how to make a machine that is protecting you tell you the truth.

NANDI (the reader — the grammar and the hand)

Full sheet (Ch. 006): ../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md · Art: ../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png

Nandi / Manav / Agnikhand / sight-white, lens-borne / reads, and is paid /
Read: thread-years at the price of an ask, properly made /
"The grammar is theirs. The HAND is not."

Page 003 note: takes her second line (same words, same grammar — the repetition IS the discipline) and reads the tag in two layers: a HOLD mark — the machine's instrument for pausing a count by its own office — tied by a hand that does not match the machine's current hand. She has been reading Office thread-work longer than the Office has been reading her. One turn of the tag, one shake of the head, one slow sentence.

THE CLERK (unseen — the hypothesis)

The Clerk / Manav / Agnikhand / unnamed by his own office / erased for being useful /

*(the man we have seen cross a threshold with that grammar in his fingers)*

Page 003 note: the clerk, or his like — the tag's hand learned the machine's grammar inside its walls and is not tying for the machine. Nandi says exactly that far; Ira's caption does the rest. If it is him, the man the register protected is protecting the register back — and has told no one.

THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (readers again)

No full sheet; Ch. 011–014. The machine's explanation, read aloud at the counter of record in their own voice — the institution running without its founder's mouth.

The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Read: the machine's paper, aloud, at the counter of record /
"...for the season. So it is filed."

Page 003 note: the reading of record is now simply what the counter sounds like: the basin's voice, the machine's paper, the keeper entering. The women read; Ira listens to her own institution work and enters the obvious — the machine told them which shelf it hid the shortage on.

THE MACHINE (off-panel — the reorganization)

No full sheet; the Office entire. Speaks this page through one sealed notice.

The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Reorganize: fold the light into the fuel, for the season /
"For the season. The season of what — it does not say."

Page 003 note: its paper admits by reorganizing what its silence would not: it is short of something, and it told the basin which shelf. Whether the shortage is coal, hands, or the room to be read — the notice does not say. The register enters the shortness, not the guess.
