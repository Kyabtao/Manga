THREADBORN — Chapter 14 — Page 010

Chapter title: Year Nineteen — THE FINALE Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the stall at dawn, the window, the stair, and the year's last light Page type: The reading — 7 panels; the sheet opened in daylight, the shortage revealed as a redistribution, the paper returned already read, and the chapter closed

Beats: the shutter goes up — dawn, the square gathered, the cord lifted off the folded sheet and the paper opened at the window where everything delivered is read; what is on it — the night shift's own count in the Office's own measures: fuel drawn at the yards after dark and carried DOWN into the basin, a measure at a time, through a hatch that was never on any roll, all winter; the sum read flat — the square does its own arithmetic: the Office's reported shortage and the basin's uncounted deliveries are the same fuel, and the thin week was not scarcity but a redistribution, made by hand, at night, by somebody the machine has already disowned; the decision in public — Kessa prices publication against silence, and the register chooses to publish: read, entered, and returned up-terrace ALREADY READ, with the counter's reading in the margin, so the machine cannot punish the hand without admitting the hour and cannot demand the paper without admitting it is its own; the runner carries it back — one page, one stair, one afternoon; the close — the protector gone before the reading finished, the school's book taking the winter's last line, the till showing its oil and its purse in one column, the door open and the window with it.

This page: an audit read in the open air, and the counter's coldest kindness — to protect a hand by making what it did impossible to un-know.

Chapter 014 budget: ONE chain-stop — SPENT on Page 009, on the threshold. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced: in the reading crowd and the empty door he is a coat and a line of prints, nothing more. Rekhak's paper stays folded, untouched — the fold is not mentioned this page. Nandi takes the school's last line.

PANEL 1 — Wide, dawn, the shutter goes up (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the stall at first light, the shutter rolled up, the window open, the whole square standing in the snow in a half-circle — not a queue: a room with no walls. The reader at the window with the folded sheet in both hands; Kessa at the till; Ira beside the reader with the register open on the sill; the cord that ringed the paper lifted off and laid aside, still untied. The street is blue; the lamp is out.

Image: The counter keeps its promise in daylight: nothing it holds is read in the dark.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): We opened the shutter at first light and the square was already there. Nobody had been told an hour. The basin keeps the counter's hours better than the counter does.

IRA: (of record, to the square, plain) It came last night from a hand we cannot name, and it has been under a cord, not a lock. It will be read once, aloud, slowly. If you need a number repeated, say so and we will repeat it.

PANEL 2 — Close, the sheet opened (~15%)

Camera: Close: the sheet on the sill in grey daylight, opened flat — its outer face blank with the small machine knot at the head, its inner face a grid of tally groups and knot-rows in the Office's own counting: columns of measures, an hour-mark at each row, nothing written, everything counted. The creased fold still furrows the paper across its middle. The reader's two hands hold the edges down against the dawn wind.

Image: A night's work in the machine's own numbers, carried under a coat for a winter.

THE READER: (reading it out, flat, one clause at a time) Night of the first miss. Fuel-yards, sixth hour after dark. Drawn: four measures. Struck from the roll: none. Carried down: four.

(she moves her finger to the next row)

Night of the thin delivery. Drawn: four. Struck from the roll: none. Carried down: four.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the arithmetic in the square (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the crowd's faces in the blue light — a woman with a child on her hip doing the sum with moving lips; an old man counting on his fingers against his own knee; two of the counting-lesson women looking at each other; one of the queue's young hands staring at the sheet as though it had insulted him. In the foreground, the reader's finger still moving down the rows. Nobody interrupts. Somebody at the back says the number out loud, once.

Image: The basin's own arithmetic, done in the street, out loud.

A VOICE AT THE BACK: (not shouted; carried) Four a night. That is the whole thin week. That is the whole thin week, given away.

THE READER: (continuing, steady) Last row. Night of the Office's notice. Drawn: four. Struck from the roll: none. Carried down: four. Signed: no name. Marked: the hour is not the Office's.

IRA: (of record, very quiet) Entered: the shortage and the deliveries are the same fuel. The rolls were honest. The hole was real. It was a hole somebody made on purpose, at night, for us.

PANEL 4 — Wide, the sum in the street (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the square in daylight, the crowd changed shape — not a queue now: people standing in small knots, hands on shoulders, a woman holding up a fire-measure as if it were evidence, a young hand with his cap crushed in his fist, the snow trodden into slush. On the stall's step, one of the elders has sat down heavily. The window is open above them; the reader waits with the sheet, and nobody is looking at her.

Image: A quarter learning, in one reading, what was done for it and by nobody it can thank.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Nobody cheered. It is not that kind of number. Half the quarter had been warm all winter on fuel they had been told did not exist, and the telling had been the point: a shortage can be complained about, but a gift cannot be demanded, and a gift from the Office would have had to be owned.

KESSA: (to Ira, under the crowd's noise, dry) They will be angry in an hour. Let them be angry now, while they are still counting.

PANEL 5 — Close, the trap laid out (~14%)

Camera: Close: the register on the sill, the sheet laid beside it, Ira's pen moving in the register's margin and then on the sheet's own blank outer face — the counter's reading, entered in knot-work: four small knots and the keeper's seal, the same grammar the demand used. Kessa's grey glove holds the sheet flat at the corner. The sheet's counted grid shows at the frame's edge.

Image: The counter's whole answer: not to keep the paper, but to give it back read.

KESSA: (pricing it, flat) Two ways, keeper. Publish it and the hand is safe: the machine disowned the hour in public, so it cannot punish what it says does not exist. Keep it quiet and we hold a debt over the Office — which is the same as holding a rope we would have to pull.

IRA: (of record, writing the margin) Publication. Entered: read aloud at dawn, in the basin's hearing, by the basin's reader. Entered on the sheet itself: four knots and the keeper's seal — the counter's reading of the machine's own count. And then it goes back up the stair, already read.

KESSA: (and she lets herself smile, once, at the arithmetic) They cannot demand a paper they have disowned. They cannot punish a hand they have denied. Give it back read, and the machine has to hold its own dodge in its hands.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the page goes back up (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the counter at midday — the sheet folded again with its new margin of knots and the keeper's seal, tied with the counter's own cord, handed across to the runner, who takes it with both hands and does not put it in his coat. Behind him, through the open door, the stair in daylight; the square's people standing back to let him go. Kessa at the till; Ira's hand still on the register; the reader watching from the window.

Image: The counter's demand, returned in the machine's own grammar with the machine's own numbers inside it.

THE RUNNER: (taking it, careful) What do I say, keeper? If they ask me what it is.

IRA: (of record) Say what it is. Say: read at dawn, at the window, by the basin, aloud. Say the counter does not send it back unread, and does not send it back sealed. And if they ask who gave it to us, say the truth: a hand the Office says does not exist.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the year's last light — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

Camera: Medium-close: late afternoon, gold winter light through the open window across the counter — the register open at the year's last page, the school's thick old book beside it with one fresh line in Nandi's dry hand, the till open showing its chalked oil-column and the school's purse in the same column, the cord that ringed the paper now coiled and put away. Beyond the window, the snow in the street is trodden to slush and there is a single line of prints going away from the door, already softening. The sill is empty except for two small knots, side by side, exactly where they were left.

Image: The year closed by a counter that read everything it was given, out loud, and gave it all back.

IRA: (of record, the year closed) Entered: one night shift, uncounted, disowned, and read. Entered: the fuel, the sum, and the hand. Entered: the paper returned up the stair already read. Entered: the school's winter closed, its lessons paid, its book older than the Office by twenty years and now one line longer.

NANDI: (from the table, closing her book) The year is whole, keeper. It ran forward and it did not break. That is all a year is asked to do.

CAPTION (smallest): Give it back read.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the reading (numbers, not speeches); the decision (publication as protection, and the trap it sets); the close (the smallest image the chapter can end on). The page is the chapter's audit held in the open air. Do not let anyone explain the economics in a speech — the reader reads clauses, the square does arithmetic, Ira enters the result in one line.
  • The reveal must be arithmetic, not revelation. Nothing on the sheet is dramatic: a column of drawn, a column of struck from the roll, a column of carried down, four measures a night, signed by nobody, marked the hour is not the Office's. The drama is that the numbers match the shortage the Office reported and reorganized around. The square's reaction is counting, not shouting; the loudest sound on the page is one voice at the back saying the sum.
  • The margin is the chapter's best move and must be visible in the art. Four knots and the keeper's seal on the sheet's own blank face: the counter has read the machine's count and written its reading where the machine will have to see it. Not hidden, not sealed — already read.
  • The trap is legal, not magical, and Kessa says it plainly: the machine cannot demand a paper it disowned, cannot punish a hand it denied, and cannot answer the counter without holding its own dodge in its hands. Do not have anyone predict victory; the page simply sends the paper back.
  • The protector is gone before the reading finishes and the page does not stage it. No farewell, no look, no hood at the back of the crowd: only the single line of prints going away from the door, softening in the slush. The two knots on the sill are the last thing the chapter shows of him.
  • The close is Nandi's line and the register's entry. The year is whole, keeper. It ran forward and it did not break. Keep it small, in her flat voice, from the table, closing her book. The ladder's last run — Give it back read — sits alone in the smallest caption with no speech over it.
