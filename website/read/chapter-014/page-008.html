THREADBORN — Chapter 14 — Page 008

Chapter title: Year Nineteen Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the archive table, the counter, the Council Stair, and the stall at night Page type: The invoice for an uncounted hour — 7 panels; the counter bills the machine for a name, the machine disowns its own hour, and the register protects a hand it has never met

Beats: the demand drafted — the school's table: Nandi ties the counter's demand in the machine's own grammar while Ira writes its copy, because a demand written in the language of the thing addressed is the only kind that cannot be pretended not to be read; the demand's terms — not what happens in the uncounted hour; WHO KEEPS IT; read before it leaves — the counter's law applied to the counter's own paper: the demand is read aloud at the window before the runner takes it, so the basin knows what is being asked in its name; the long stair — the runner walks it up alone at midday, and his distance is the page's measure of how formal this is; the answer is a dodge — back comes a slip with a machine seal and one knot-sign: the act of an individual, without authority; the hour is not the Office's, the shift is on no roll, nobody up-terrace will answer for it; what a disavowal is — read aloud at the counter, then entered coldly: the machine has just disowned its own posted hour in front of the whole basin, which means the hour belongs to a person, and a person can be protected; protection, extended blind — the register enters protection of record for whoever keeps the night hour: not read aloud, entered in the clerk's own form, for a hand that has not asked.

This page: the machine's politeness used as a subpoena, and then its oldest dodge used as a gift.

Chapter 014 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent — and this is the page that notes it must be spent by Page 010. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced, not on the page — though the formula he was erased by returns in the Office's answer, and nobody in the basin points at it. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear.

PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the demand drafted at the school's table (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the archive table with the lamp on it, the year's rods racked behind; Nandi at the table's head with a short cord between her thick fingers, tying slowly, the wrong-hand grammar reversed into a demand; the counting-lesson women on the benches with their own cords, practising the same tie badly and watching hers; Ira at the table's far end, writing the register's copy. Kessa at the door-frame with the wax-pot, waiting to seal.

Image: A demand written in the language of the thing addressed, so it cannot pretend not to read it.

NANDI: (tying, not looking up) Their grammar has one word that means this must be answered. I am not using the rest of it. Four knots, then the question. Anything more and they will say we were rude enough to be refused.

IRA: (of record, writing the copy) Entered: the demand. Not what happens in the uncounted hour. Who keeps it.

PANEL 2 — Close, the demand sealed (~14%)

Camera: Close: the finished paper on the table — the demand's four knots tied through the form's own holes, the register's copy beneath it in Ira's hand, and Kessa's wax-pot steaming at the frame's edge; the keeper's seal pressed into the wax, the small knot-mark at its centre. Nothing written on the form's face but the knot-work.

Image: The counter's seal: not a machine's authority, a counter's question.

KESSA: (pressing the seal, dry) A counter can ask a question it has no power behind. It is the only weapon we have that does not need a sharp edge. Make sure the runner knows it is not a threat.

IRA: (of record) Entered: one demand, four knots, keeper's seal. It asks for a name, and it asks in public.

PANEL 3 — Medium, read before it leaves (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the stall's window at midday, the demand held up in both hands by one of the counting-lesson women, reading it aloud — a short reading, four lines of plain speech — to the queue crowded under the awning. The runner stands at the counter's end with his cap in his hand, waiting; Ira beside him, watching the basin hear what it is sending. The window is open in the snow.

Image: The counter's law applied to the counter's own paper: nothing goes up-terrace unread.

THE READER: (aloud, clear, to the queue) To the Office. The counter asks: who keeps the uncounted night hour at the fuel-yards? The counter does not ask what is done there. The counter asks who is there. Answer, or say plainly that you will not.

IRA: (of record) Entered: read aloud before it left. Whatever we send up that stair, the basin hears first. If it is answered, the answer is the basin's too.

PANEL 4 — Wide, the long stair (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the Council Stair from below in midday snow-light — the runner a small grey figure halfway up, the stair's black basalt flights cutting back and forth to the Office's blank face, the whole terrace above him, ash falling with the snow. On the stair's lower steps, the basin's queue has stopped to watch him go. Nobody follows.

Image: The distance a paper walks when a counter has no authority to send with it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): One man, one page, one stair. We have no seal that means anything up there and no threat that costs them anything. What we have is a question asked in their own grammar, in front of everyone, and a square that watched it leave.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the answer comes back (~14%)

Camera: Medium: late afternoon, the counter — the runner back with his coat still on, setting the Office's slip down with both hands and stepping back a pace, as he was taught; his face doing something careful that he does not let reach his mouth. The slip: machine seal, one knot-sign, the form's face otherwise blank. Ira's hand already reaching; Kessa behind her, still.

Image: The Office's oldest, cheapest grammar, delivered by a man who has to watch it be read.

THE RUNNER: (flat, word for word, the way he carries things) They read it twice. Then they gave me this and told me to walk.

(a pause he was not told to take)

It is not an answer. I am sorry.

IRA: (of record, taking it) Everything delivered here is read aloud. Sit down. This one is short.

PANEL 6 — Close, the disavowal read aloud (~14%)

Camera: Close: the slip flat on the counter in lamplight — the machine seal, the single knot-sign at its centre, the form's face blank around it; Ira's finger under the knot-sign as she reads; Kessa's shadow across the paper; behind, out of focus, the queue's faces pressed close to hear.

Image: The machine disowning its own posted hour, in the one grammar it uses for things it will not protect.

IRA: (reading it out, level, so the back of the queue gets it) The act of an individual, without authority. Not on any roll. So answered.

(she sets her finger down on the blank space beside the seal)

IRA: (of record, and then the whole of it) Entered: the Office has disowned its own posted hour, in public, on paper, in front of the basin. Which means the hour belongs to a person. A disavowal is the only paper the machine gives away that can be used against it — because it tells you exactly what it will not protect.

KESSA: (quiet) And a thing it will not protect is a thing we can.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, protection, extended blind — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

Camera: Medium-close: night, the stall — the register open at the new page, the clerk's own protection form laid beside it, blank except for the knot-work Ira is tying through it: the same four knots, the same seal, no name in the space left for one. The sill beyond the window is empty and snow-dusted. Kessa's hand rests on the till; the lamp is trimmed low; three knots on the oil-cord.

Image: The register has just protected a person it has never met, in a form it invented for an enemy.

IRA: (of record, entering it — not reading it aloud) Entered, not read aloud: protection of record for whoever keeps the night hour. No name in the space for one. It stands until a name can go in it, and it is not the basin's business whose it is.

KESSA: (from the till, pricing it) Protection of a stranger costs nothing to be accused of favouring and everything to be accused of being a fool. I have entered both. Do not spend the paper before spring.

CAPTION (smallest): Whoever keeps it.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the demand (a question in the machine's own grammar, read publicly before it leaves); the disavowal (the machine's oldest dodge, read aloud and re-read for what it is worth); the blind protection (the register's own form, extended to a hand it has never met). The page never learns a name and never learns what happens at the fuel-yards. It wins a category.
  • **The demand must look like a question, not a summons.** Four knots and a plain sentence, a counter's seal in wax, a reader holding it up at the window for the queue to hear. Nandi's craft note is the page's method: their grammar has one word for this must be answered, and she uses only that, because a longer demand could be refused for its manners.
  • The long stair is the page's only movement and it must stay small. A grey figure halfway up black basalt in midday snow, ash falling with the snow, the queue stopped at the stair's foot to watch, nobody following. No guards, no gates, no confrontation. The distance is the point.
  • The dodge is not a defeat and must not be played as one. The Office answers in the formula it used to erase the clerk (Ch. 013): the act of an individual, without authority. Ira reads it level, then turns it: the machine has disowned the hour in front of everyone, so the hour belongs to a person. Do not let anybody in the panel smile. Kessa's line is the whole turn: a thing it will not protect is a thing we can.
  • Panel 7 is the page's quiet close and the chapter's most radical entry. Protection of record was invented for the clerk — an enemy, entered and not read aloud. Now it is extended to a stranger with no name, in the same form, unread. The empty space where a name goes must be visible in the art. Kessa prices the risk in one line and forbids spending the paper before spring; the register enters it anyway.
  • The chain-stop stays unspent — and the page says so in the record. Three pages remain (008–010); the chapter's ONE must be spent before the close-out summary, or the summary cannot carry the gated line.
  • The imperative ladder takes its next run: Whoever keeps it.
