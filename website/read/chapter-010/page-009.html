THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 009

Chapter title: Room Nine / कमरा नौ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the hatch alley behind the Mendery, dawn Page type: Lesson page — 7 panels

Beats: a claimant with a thumbprint will produce the founder at fourth bell; a second crimson sheet makes the producing party the party of record; the school has decided to teach in the open and let every person decide for themselves.

This page: the second school's first public lesson, held in daylight, in the alley, on purpose.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the alley at dawn (~13%)

Camera: Wide: the alley behind the Mendery at first light. Wet stone, ash falling, the back door with its hatch — shin-high, forearm-sized, worn shiny at the edges by forty years of use. No lantern on the wall, no shelter, no secrecy: the shutters of three neighbouring stalls are up, and two of their tenants are leaning in their doorways watching, which is the point. Five people stand in the alley. Ira has her needle-roll open on the rim of the water-butt.

Image: Forty years of the school, conducted through a hole, at night, in the dark — and today, a morning class in the alley with the neighbours watching.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Forty years. My mother did this through that hole, at night, one forearm at a time, in silence, to people who were never told why.

CAPTION: I did it standing up in the open, at dawn, with an audience, and I did it out loud, and I told every single one of them exactly what I was going to do before I touched their hand.

PANEL 2 — Medium, who came (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the five. The sister from the counter, in her fifties, arm already out of her sleeve. The woman with the seven healed cuts, standing very still. A young rope-splicer. An old man who has come with a crutch and no patience. And a middle-aged dockhand who has come to watch, not to be taught — arms folded, back against the wall, and he does not move for the whole page.

Image: A class of four and one observer, at dawn, in an alley, above the pot-row.

IRA: Rules. Rule one: thread is braided, never single. Rule two: a braid carries two strands. Rule three, as I teach it: the teacher's strand goes in first, and then the teacher tells you what she did.

IRA: So here's the telling. If I put a second strand into your palm, a compliance chain can't date your hand. It reads two years at once and gets neither. That means no Office instrument can count you, ever again.

IRA: And it means you cannot hold a licence, a stall, a tenancy or a claim in this sector, and you cannot be owed a debt, and no licensed mender may work on you. That is the price, and I have just said it out loud, and that is the whole school.

PANEL 3 — Close, the one who leaves (~14%)

Camera: Close: the young rope-splicer, listening, and then — without ceremony, without apology, while Ira is still speaking — turning and walking out of the alley past the neighbours. Nobody calls after her. Ira does not stop mid-sentence. The panel should make the reader feel how much this costs the page: the school has just lost a pupil by being honest.

Image: Consent, working.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): She left. Third sentence. She didn't argue with me and she didn't thank me and she didn't say goodbye, and my mother's school never once had that happen, because nobody in my mother's school ever got told enough to leave.

CAPTION: I have thought about that girl more than anybody else who was in the alley that morning.

DOCKHAND (off-panel, from the wall, flat): One gone. Three to go. That's four in ten minutes, teacher. Your mother did forty a winter.

IRA: Then she had forty years. I've got a morning.

PANEL 4 — Large, the first strand (~16%)

Camera: Large panel, macro: the sister's palm in the lamplight of a hand-lamp, and Ira's needle — not yet entering. Ira is showing her the seam first: pointing at the root, at the old grey strand, at where the new one will lie. Both women's hands in frame, one old, one working, side by side, and the image should be quiet and domestic and slightly cold, because it is dawn in an alley.

Image: The lesson delivered on the hand itself, before the hand is touched.

IRA: Here's your first strand. Second year of the school, so it's forty-two years old and it's flat as a board. I'm laying the new one alongside it, same seam, so they cross twice — here and here.

IRA: You'll feel two pricks and then nothing. It will itch for three days. And when you look at it in a month, you'll see a braid, and that braid is the only thing about you the Office can't read, and I am now telling you that with your hand in front of me and my needle on the seam.

SISTER: Get on with it, girl. I've been counted forty-two years. I'd like a year off.

PANEL 5 — Close, what it costs to teach (~14%)

Camera: Close: Ira's face, mid-work, and the panel is composed so the reader sees both her concentration and the young grey clerk who has arrived at the alley's mouth and is standing there, watching, holding a satchel. He has been there for two pupils. He is not stopping anything. He is watching the way a clerk watches, and Ira has known he is there since Panel 4.

Image: The Office, in the audience of the first public lesson, taking notes.

CLERK: (quietly, from the alley mouth, when the second hand is finished) Keeper. I'm not here to stop you. Nobody up-terrace told me to stop you. I'm told to observe and report the procedure, not the class.

IRA: Observe what, exactly?

CLERK: Whether the person is told first. That's the only line in my instructions — if the practitioner tells the subject beforehand, the practice is not concealment. Keeper, whoever wrote my instructions wrote them about you and wrote them a week before you did it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A week before. Which meant that up-terrace, in a building full of benches, somebody had already worked out what I would decide, and had already decided to let me do it, and had written a line about it, because the Office would rather have a school it can watch than a school that goes back into the dark.

PANEL 6 — Close, the third hand (~14%)

Camera: Close: the third pupil — the young rope-splicer's friend, a woman of twenty-two — and Ira's hands are slowing, because she has been working since before first light and the panel shows the particular exhaustion of a mender doing fine work in cold weather. Jadi is holding the hand-lamp now, wordlessly, and has been for two pupils.

Image: The second school, four hands old, already running on somebody else's endurance.

JADI: Third one's done. Sit down before you fall over.

IRA: One more before the bell.

JADI: No. (flat, final, holding the lamp) One more and you'll put a strand through a tendon, and then who's the teacher? Me? I'm forty-four and I can't read.

JADI: (quieter) Girl. Your mother did one a week for forty years. You've done three in a morning and you've got a building to be in at fourth bell. Sit. Down. I'll teach them the counting.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): So I sat down on the rim of a water-butt in a wet alley, and the school's first pupil, who cannot read, took the hand-lamp and spent the last hour before the bell teaching three Kshudra women how to count their own knots, so that no clerk in Agnikhand would ever be able to tell them what their own hands said.

PANEL 7 — Close, the fourth bell — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Close: the young clerk coming forward with the satchel open, and taking out — with the miserable care of a man who has begun to dislike his own errands — the fourth bell's instruction: a single sheet, and on it the day's procedure, item by item, and one item circled in ink. Behind him, the alley empties; Ira's hand is still wet from the water-butt.

Image: The routine sentence that makes the morning's work irrelevant and the afternoon's unbearable.

CLERK: Fourth bell, room nine, production of the founder. Claimant attends, keeper attends, instrument stands, reader attends —

IRA: Reader. Nima.

CLERK: (reading, and the words are not his) The reader of the bigger book. The entry is to be read by the witness-certified reader, and by the keeper aloud, in the same words and at the same time, as per the schedule of the third day of the current bell-cycle.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): Nandi's condition. The one she wrote into the schedule so that no record would ever go unread.

CAPTION (smallest): Which means that at fourth bell, when somebody puts my mother's hand on that stand — or a hand they say is hers — the reader of the bigger book will be Nandi, who sewed beside her for forty years.

CAPTION (smallest): And the keeper, reading it out loud in the same words at the same time, will be me.

Writing & art notes

  • **This page is the second school's opening and it must look like a small practical morning, not a rally.** Five people, one lamp, three hands, a wet alley, neighbours watching, and one pupil who leaves. No crowd, no applause, no music. The school's return to the world happens at dawn with three pupils and a woman counting on her fingers.
  • The rope-splicer leaving is required by the chapter's ethics. The amended rule three only means anything if somebody can hear it and refuse. She does not speak and her decision is respected in one sentence. Nothing else is said about her on this page.
  • Jadi teaching the counting in the last panel is the page's heart and its best invention. She cannot read and she teaches the school's own numeracy to three women, so that no clerk can read their hands to them. This is the school's answer to the Office's translation room, and it is delivered by the one pupil who was never finished.
  • The clerk's instruction is a legal trap dressed as mercy. If the practitioner tells the subject beforehand, the practice is not concealment. Written a week in advance: the Office has decided it would rather have a school it can watch than a school that goes back into the dark. Ira must not respond to this on the page; the reader should work out that being permitted is the punishment.
  • **The fourth-bell instruction must arrive as a procedure item.** No drama, no pause in the clerk's voice. The hook is that the schedule was written by Nandi's own condition, which means the two women who can read knots and the woman who sewed beside the founder will all be in one small room being asked about the same hand.
  • Continuity to carry into Page 010: three hands braided in an alley before first light, with consent on the record and a clerk as witness; the school's method now public knowledge; the rope splicer's refusal entered nowhere, because nothing was written; Nima absent, and Nandi required.
