THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 001

Chapter title: Room Nine / कमरा नौ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Council Stair, the Reckoning Office vestibule Page type: Chapter opener — 7 panels

Beats: Chapter 009 closed with the Book of the Hand lifted out of its pages, thirty-one knots returned to their owners, one unnamed knot left on the register's last line, and a bounty entered in crimson on the reserved first line: PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED.

Chapter 010 premise: the Office does not send anyone. It builds a counter, and the basin walks to it. Today the school walks up the stair on its own feet, with a hand and a knot and no name.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the stair at first bell (~13%)

Camera: Wide, cold, first light. The Council Stair from the bottom: forty-one steps, ash falling slow, the Office at the top with its plain unmarked doors. Ira in her working coat with the wrapped attestation under her arm, and Jadi beside her with her left sleeve already rolled and her knot sewn into the cuff. Both walking up. In the alley below, drawn small: the pot-row, and people at their stall doors, watching two women climb.

Image: The school, entering the building it has spent forty years not existing in.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Rule one of that building, which nobody tells you: you do not arrive at the Office. You are delivered to it. You come up a stair that has been climbed by everybody who ever owed this basin anything, and by the fortieth step you have already started paying for being here.

JADI: Forty-one steps.

IRA: You counted.

JADI: I count everything, girl. It's why I'm alive.

PANEL 2 — Close, the doors (~14%)

Camera: Close on the vestibule doors from outside: plain, unmarked, no seal, no number, and worn in two places — at the handle and below it, at the height of a hand held out palm-up. Somebody's palm has rubbed the wood pale over decades.

Image: The one door in Agnikhand that has been held open by palms for a hundred years.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): No sign. No seal. No kind of anything. I stood in front of it with a register book in my bag and a woman with a knot in her cuff and I could not find a single word on that building to tell me what it was for.

CAPTION: And then Jadi put her hand flat on the pale place without being told, like a woman touching her own doorpost, and it opened, and I understood that everybody who has ever come here has done that.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the vestibule (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the vestibule inside — low benches in rows, a high desk at the far end, a slate on the wall with numbers chalked on it, and one clock. It is full. Not a crowd: a queue that has been trained. Basin people, most of them Kshudra, sitting where they were told, holding chits, and three clerks moving along the rows like people counting sheep. Nobody is talking.

Image: The Office's actual weapon: a room with numbers in it.

CLERK: Keeper. Production of a hand, room nine. Take a chit. You'll be called by chit number, not by name — we don't use names in the vestibule.

IRA: Why not?

CLERK: (already walking away) Nobody ever told me. It's just how it's done.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Chit, not name. I have spent nine days being terrified of that register upstairs, and the Office has been running the same trick at the front door for a hundred years, on everybody, for free.

PANEL 4 — Close, the slip (~14%)

Camera: Close on the chit: a number, a room, a time, and one line of small print at the foot that Ira reads twice. Jadi, beside her, is counting the room — benches, rows, people, chits — with her lips moving slightly, the arithmetic of a woman who has survived by counting.

Image: The small print that turns a visit into a debt.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Foot of the chit, in the smallest letters I have ever squinted at: attendance is entered; entries may be audited; auditing is charged to the party whose entry is audited.

CAPTION: There it is. I hand them a hand, and the form I was given to hand it over with puts a fee on me for the privilege of being looked at. Not a fine. A fee. You cannot protest a fee. Fees are lawful all the way down.

JADI: Sixty-two in here. Nine clerks' worth of waiting.

JADI: (and then, quietly, with real wonder) They've got it arranged so nobody has to be cruel. That's cleverer than the Council ever managed.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the pot-row below (~14%)

Camera: Cut, one panel only: down in the Ash-sLums, morning, the pot-row — and at the corner by the water-carts, a crowd of twenty-odd people standing around a man Ira knows (a chandler from the fourth terrace) who is holding up a palm, somebody else's, and talking fast. Two women are already walking away from him in opposite directions, and one of them is holding her own hand against her chest.

Image: The bounty, working without a single Office employee present.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): And I did not know, standing in the vestibule with my chit in my hand, that it had already started below. Not a raid. Not a list. Eleven words in crimson and one promise, and by first bell the pot-row had produced its own inspector — because a reward needs no clerks, no benches and no stair.

CAPTION: It just needs everybody to be poor at the same time.

PANEL 6 — Close, Jadi's answer (~14%)

Camera: Close: Jadi, standing in the middle of a room built to number her, looking at the slate. She is not afraid and she is not defiant. She is a woman who has been invisible in this sector for forty-four years, reading a wall of numbers with a butcher's calm satisfaction, the way you might read a weather report that has finally mentioned your street.

Image: The first pupil, at the Office's own front desk, refusing to be invisible about it.

JADI: Fourth bench, third seat. I counted the chits when they handed them out. We're nine away.

IRA: Jadi. When we get in there, they'll read your palm with a chain and write down that it can't be read. That's all today is. That's the whole thing.

JADI: I know what today is. Today somebody writes me in a book I can't read, and I stand there while they do it, and I answer the questions they ask.

JADI: That's more than any Kshudra in this basin has ever got from that building, girl. Smile or don't, but count it.

PANEL 7 — Close, the call — CLOSING HOOK (~16%)

Camera: Close on the high desk at the end of the vestibule: the clerk on duty looking up at the slate, chalk in hand, and calling the next chit — and above his head, on the board beside the slate, a second board that was not in any description of this room: small, crimson-bordered, and on it four lines in a hand the whole basin is about to learn to read:

CLERK (dialogue, flat, to the room): Eighty-eight. Room nine.

CLERK: Production of a hand, keeper and subject. Let the record show both attended voluntarily.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, reading the board): PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED.

CAPTION: And underneath, in the same crimson, in smaller letters, the line that nobody in that room needed explained because two hundred people have already figured it out on their own:

CAPTION (lettered, art, smallest): Payment on verification. No name required. Any hand.

CAPTION (last line): The school never wrote a name in forty years, and the Office has just told the whole basin that it doesn't need one either.

Writing & art notes

  • The Reckoning Office is a building, not a character. No menace in the architecture: benches, a clock, a slate, a clerk who walks away mid-sentence. The threat is throughput. Draw it clean, municipal and slightly under-lit, and never let a guard appear.
  • The vestibule's chit system is new canon and it binds the chapter: chits, not names; attendance entered; audits charged to the audited party. This is how the Office has been collecting for a century without an ordinance, and it should make the Chapter 009 ordinance read as codification of a habit rather than a new invention.
  • Draw the palm-worn pale patch below the door handle in Panel 2 and reuse it. It is the only sentimental object in the building, and it is the readers' own history: hands held out palm-up.
  • Jadi counting the room is the page's best character beat and must stay light. She is not intimidated and not brave about it; she is good at arithmetic in hostile rooms, which is what forty-four years of invisibility teaches. Her line "They've got it arranged so nobody has to be cruel" is the chapter's thesis in her register and no caption should repeat it.
  • One panel of the pot-row only (Panel 5). The bounty's first effects must be glimpsed here and paid off on Page 007; do not name the chandler, do not show the palm's owner, do not explain the crimson strand. Twenty people and somebody else's hand.
  • The crimson board is the chapter's ticking image. It stands in the vestibule for the whole chapter; every time the scene returns to the stair, it should be in the frame, and on Page 010 it will have a chalk mark against it.
  • No one from the school has been in this building before. Ira's captions should carry that once and only once (Panel 1), and everything after should be pure procedure.
