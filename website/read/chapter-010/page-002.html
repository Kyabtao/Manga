THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 002

Chapter title: Room Nine / कमरा नौ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office vestibule Page type: Waiting-room page — 7 panels

Beats: the chit says attendance is entered and the audited party pays for the audit; the bounty board stands in the vestibule; the pot-row has already started producing its own inspectors.

This page: what eleven words in crimson do to a room of benches by mid-morning.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the benches (~13%)

Camera: Wide: the vestibule benches, four rows in, and now we can see who is in them. A woman with a boy on her lap. Two men holding a farmer's chit between them. An old woman asleep upright. A young man with his sleeve already rolled, rehearsing something with his lips. Nine chits away is a claimant: a chandler from the fourth terrace, sitting forward, holding a folded cloth in both hands, and beside him, on the bench, an old woman with her left hand wrapped in the same cloth's twin — dirty rag, dry blood at the edge.

Image: A waiting room with one bounty and two hundred poor people in it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The Office never once said the word bounty. There is no such word on the board. There is a promise and there is a room, and a room does the rest on its own.

CAPTION: Bench three, the chandler, the rag, the old woman. I saw the rag and I knew what it was before I saw anything else, the way you know a smell. Somebody had gone looking in a palm for a crimson strand.

PANEL 2 — Close, the claimant called (~14%)

Camera: Close at the high desk: the chandler standing with the cloth open on the counter, and in it the old woman's hand — palm up, and across the palm a cut, straight, deep, badly closed, made by somebody who was looking for something under the skin and did not find it. No strand. No mark. The clerk looks at it the way a clerk looks at weather.

Image: The bounty's first attempt, submitted as paperwork.

CHANDLER: Under the skin. There's a strand under the skin, everyone knows there is, the school puts them under —

CLERK: Claim four. No strand verified. (writing) Claim four, declined, no strand verified.

CHANDLER: Read it again! There's a colour under there, you can see a colour —

CLERK: There is a cut, sir. The Office pays for the founder's strand. It does not pay for damage.

PANEL 3 — Close, the fee (~14%)

Camera: Close: the clerk's hand turning the chit over and tapping the small print at the foot with one finger — the same print Ira read at the door: auditing is charged to the party whose entry is audited. The old woman on the bench has woken up. The chandler's face is doing the arithmetic and losing.

Image: A fee, applied to a man who has just cut his own mother's hand for nothing.

CLERK: The claim is an entry. Entries are audited. The audit is charged to the party entered — that's the chit. Two coppers.

CHANDLER: I haven't got two coppers.

CLERK: Then it's entered against you. (writing) Claimant unable to discharge. Entered. Next.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Two coppers. He hadn't got two coppers, so the Office wrote his name down for the first time in his life, and the sum of the entry was unable to pay for having failed. That is what the Office is. That is the whole building, and it took eleven words in crimson to make the pot-row feed it.

PANEL 4 — Medium, Ira's hands (~14%)

Camera: Medium: Ira has stood up. In her right hand, the needle-roll is out; in her left, a slip of stall paper — the ordinary kind, with the Mendery's address. She is not going to the old woman, because a clerk has already stepped into the aisle between them, and the clerk is not being cruel, he is doing his job, which is worse.

Image: A mender, in a building where mending is out of scope, doing the only thing that is left.

CLERK: Keeper. Sit down. You don't work in the vestibule.

IRA: I'm not working. (handing the slip to the chandler's mother, past him) — Ma, this is my stall. Ashfall, slot four, first light. Bring the hand. I'll close it and I'll charge you nothing and nobody has to write anything.

CLERK: (flat, honest, not unkind) She's entered now. If she comes to your stall, you're working an entered hand, and that's clause three.

IRA: She's not declared unreadable, she's damaged —

CLERK: Then it's clause one: she can't be entered in a wage-book, a tenancy or a claim, and you can't take her as a customer either, because customers have to be somebody on a page. Sit down, keeper. You know the schedule better than I do.

PANEL 5 — Close, Jadi's arithmetic (~14%)

Camera: Close: Jadi, seated, watching the whole thing with her knot at her cuff, and her voice level and slow, and one hand on Ira's sleeve — not restraining, just counting.

Image: The school's first pupil teaching her teacher the only lesson that room can teach.

JADI: Sit down, girl. You can't mend in here.

IRA: I can't do anything in here.

JADI: Right. In here you're not a mender, you're a number that hasn't been called. Out there you're a stall with needles. That's the difference between this building and the pot-row, and it's the only difference, and you've been up here eleven minutes and you've already forgotten it.

JADI: (releasing her sleeve) Count the chits. We're five away. Then we go and be a number, and then we go home and be a school.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the rule (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the high desk again, and the clerk standing up to address the room — the standard announcement, delivered twice a day, to a room that already knows. Behind him, the board.

Image: The Office publishing the mechanism of its own bounty without once using the word.

CLERK: Notice to claimants. Claims are entered on the register of unreadable hands, which is kept at the place of keeping — the Mendery, Ashfall, slot four — with the keeper of that register present. If your claim is verified up here today, the entry is made down there within the day, by the keeper.

CLERK: That is the procedure. It is written on the board at the foot of the stair.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): And the arithmetic of it arrived all at once, in a bench row, holding a cloth with somebody's hand in it.

CAPTION: Every claim in Agnikhand — every cut palm, every hopeful neighbour, every man who has heard there is money in a colour — will be entered at my counter, in my handwriting, in the register I am the keeper of, in my own cellar.

CAPTION: They have made me the bounty's clerk.

PANEL 7 — Close, the call — CLOSING HOOK (~16%)

Camera: Close on the slate and the clerk's chalk: he crosses out eighty-seven, writes eighty-eight, and reads it out. Then a second thing: at the same moment, from the door at the far end of the vestibule, a second clerk comes out and puts a fresh sheet on the board beside the crimson one — and on that sheet, in Office grey, blocked and legible, is a schedule of the day's hearings in room nine, and one line is already circled.

Image: Two women walking into a room that is going to have three purposes before the day is out.

CLERK: Eighty-eight. Room nine. Production of a hand.

IRA: (standing, to Jadi, quietly) Sleeve, then. Left arm, palm up, and you keep your right hand free.

JADI: Why?

IRA: Because I've been in this building eleven minutes and I've learned one thing for certain.

IRA (last line): They never schedule one thing in a room.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, reading the board as they pass it): Room nine, this afternoon. Witness to be asked: Nandi, of the Mendery, in custody.

CAPTION (smallest): Same room. Same clerk. Same tray of knots, I should think, since a witness is asked about things.

Writing & art notes

  • The chandler is not a villain. He is a poor man who heard there was money in a colour, and the page must make the reader feel how cheaply the Office bought him: eleven words, no officer, no threat. Play his dialogue as grievance, not greed; he brought his mother because he could not imagine a reason not to.
  • The two-copper fee is the page's horror and it must not be underlined twice. Ira gets one caption for it and then the scene moves. The point is that the Office's cruelty is accounting, and that it converts failure into a permanent entry against the poorest man in the room.
  • Clause one/three mechanics must be precise, because they are what stops Ira. A damaged hand is not unreadable, but an entered person cannot be taken as a customer, and an unreadable hand cannot be entered in a wage-book, tenancy or claim. The clerk knows the schedule better than the mender does; that asymmetry is the scene.
  • The reader should notice that Ira's slip of stall paper is refused for a reason, not for spite. Nobody in the vestibule is cruel. The building is a form. Draw the clerk tired, not smug.
  • The announcement in Panel 6 is the chapter's structural turn: all claims are entered at the place of keeping, by the keeper. Chapter 010's remaining pages and Chapter 011's opening problem both live in the consequences of that one sentence.
  • Nandi's name on the board must be almost missable. No music, no reaction panel: Jadi and Ira are walking to their own hearing when the reader sees it. Ira's caption is quiet and stops at the inference.
