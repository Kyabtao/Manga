Synopsis

The Office's answer to Chapter 009 was a department: a copyist, a bigger book, and four words in crimson on the register's reserved first line — PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED. Chapter 010 is the day that instrument is used.

Ira walks up the Council Stair with Jadi and with the register's line two, to produce a hand. Nobody arrests anybody. The building does what buildings do: it seats them, numbers them, and asks for the paperwork — and by the time they reach room nine, the bounty has already begun to work on the pot-row below, because a reward needs no enforcement officials, only neighbours.

Room nine holds the chapter's three clocks at once: the production of Jadi's hand (the Office's portable chain, read by its new reader-second — a Kshudra girl named Nima who can count but cannot read a braid), the hearing of Nandi (produced as a witness and asked to identify knots from a tray), and the register's reserved line one, which is not about the school at all.

The chapter's cost is Nandi's honesty. She has never lied about a knot in forty years and she does not start in room nine: she gives kinds, years and hands — everything the school ever recorded and never a name, because the school never wrote one. It is enough. Nandi's answers complete the translation the Office began in Chapter 009, and they hand the Office its instrument. The Office offers the woman who kept the shelf forty years a desk of her own — and offers, in front of her, to hire her rather than charge her. Jadi's production is filed as verified: unreadable, which costs her the right to hold, own, license, claim or be owed anything in the basin: legal non-existence, granted at a counter, with a receipt she refuses to mark.

The chapter ends on the first effect of the bounty down in the pot-row: an old mender brought to the Mendery with her palm cut open by neighbours who had heard there was money in a crimson strand — and on Kessa's tally-thread, which is still the only record of any of it that cannot be copied.
