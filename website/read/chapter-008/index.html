Synopsis

The Office comes down to the Ash-sLums. Not a raid — a visit with a warrant: two grey clerks, a slate, a lamp, and the Inspector with his hands behind his back. They find the Mendery open and the archive taken apart on the floor, which makes the room evidence rather than the records.

Ira intervenes and saves the archive by declaring it third-register property — the principal's, not the Council's — using the entry the licence put her in. It works, and it costs her exactly what it looks like it costs her: the Inspector now has a standing reason to return every second bell for as long as the entry stands, and he tells her so while a clerk writes it on a slate.

They take Nandi instead — not for the school, for forty years of unauthorised custodial custody in a basin the Office has no index for at all. She walks out on her own feet and leaves one instruction on the way past: the shelf — the empty one. Look at what's still standing next to it.

Alone in the cellar with one bored clerk, Ira finally reads the back wall for presence. Beside the gap stands a second volume: the Book of the Hand — the school's method book, re-bound twice, forty years of finger-diagrams drawn for pupils who could not read. Its back third holds one page per pupil: no names, only a first knot stitched down to the paper. The mother took the names; she left the knots, because a knot cannot be looked up. The last page is less than a year old.

At the stall, Kessa reads the book with her hands and finds that the oldest half of the school is all Kshudra. And she catches the detail everyone else missed: the entry in the third register was made eleven days ago, before the letter was ever delivered. The principal does not react. He pre-files.

Then the school walks in the door. Jadi — Kshudra, seventy-odd, first pupil of the School of the Braided Thread — holds up a palm with a braided mark whose seam has opened, and calls Ira the teacher. She has come to ask for the strand to be pulled out.

Her account rebuilds the school's missing twenty years: after the binding, the school ran as a hatch cut low in the Mendery's back door, one forearm at a time, in the dark, two taps to end. Jadi kept the roster in her head; the Roll itself held kind, year and knot and never a name. And for nine months the Office has been calling pupils up the terrace to be dated — a chain across the palm, a number on a slate, no arrest, no violence. Four days later the hand stops knowing what a needle is for. Forty-one measured; six unable to mend.

The Roll was copied years ago and the copy is what the Office has been using. That is why the mother took it and why she came to the hatch five nights ago for Jadi's roster of the forty-one — said twice, both times out loud. She has spent four nights going down the list.

Ira refuses to cut the strand and finds the real answer in the school's own first page. Rule three enrolls; rules one and two finish. The chain reads a single thread because a single thread is a single date. A braid carries two years and a reader gets neither. The school was never teaching mending — it was making people the Office cannot count. And Jadi, the root, was never finished: the Council took the mother two months after enrolling her.

Ira opens her own closed stitch and does the work where the Office cannot see it — at the hatch in the alley behind the Mendery, at night, where the school has always actually lived. She lays a second strand into Jadi's palm, braiding a hand that has been single for forty-four years. And she breaks rule three while she does it: the teacher's strand goes in first, and then the teacher tells you what she did.

Jadi looks at her hand and says it feels like one thread — and that it is the first time in her life anything has been done to her and her hand agreed to it. She asks when the lesson is next.

And up-terrace, on a wall, a compliance chain that has measured every hand in the Ashfall for nineteen years reads a braid for the first time and returns both years at once. A clerk writes one word on a slate, underlines it twice, and sits down in a chair that is not his.

The word is unreadable. There is no procedure for that — which means by morning there will be one.

Canon rules added in Chapter 8 (binding forward)

  1. The school's three rules are a mechanism, not a creed. Rule three enrolls (one strand in before speech). Rules one and two finish (a second strand woven in as the pupil learns). A single strand is a single date and can be read by a chain; a braid carries two years and returns neither. The school's purpose is to make people the Office cannot count.
  2. Ira amends rule three. The teacher's strand goes in first, and then the teacher tells you what she did. Consent is the difference between her mother's school and hers. This is the canon going forward.
  3. The Roll of Hands held no names. Kind, the year the strand went in, and the pupil's first knot. Jadi kept the store of names in her head for forty years. (Ch. 007 p008 was corrected to match.)
  4. The Book of the Hand — the school's method book, standing on the back wall beside the gap. Front: forty years of knot diagrams drawn for pupils who could not read. Back: one page per pupil, a first knot stitched down, no names. The mother took the names and left the knots, because a knot cannot be looked up.
  5. The hatch. After the binding, the school ran as a forearm-sized hatch cut low in the Mendery's back door. One hand at a time, in the dark, never a word, two taps to end. Forty years. This is what the sewer actually was.
  6. The Office dates hands. Nine months of measuring: a compliance chain across the palm, a year written on a slate, no detention and no violence. Four days later the hand stops knowing what a needle is for. Forty-one Kshudra measured; six unable to mend.
  7. The Roll was copied years ago. The Office has had a drawer copy all along; the nine months of measuring existed to prove which hand matched which year. Taking the Roll was damage limitation, not a rescue.
  8. Jadi is the first pupil and the unfinished root. Enrolled at twelve, two months before the Council bound the mother; she has carried a single strand for forty-four years because the school never got to teach her the second one.
  9. The principal pre-files. The third-register entry was made eleven days before the licence was delivered. He does not react; he files in advance and then waits to be right.
  10. Nandi is in Office custody. Taken for the archive, not the school. One day in as of the chapter's close. She left one instruction on her way out and it produced the Book of the Hand.
  11. Ira's stitch is open again, with a live channel running. The Office's chain felt it open within four minutes.
  12. Jadi wears the school's first braid and has asked for the next lesson. The second school has one pupil.
