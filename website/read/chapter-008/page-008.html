THREADBORN — Chapter 08 — Page 008

Chapter title: The First Pupil / पहला शिष्य Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Knot & Nail, Ashfall Basin Page type: Reveal page — 7 panels

Beats: Page 007 — the Office has a drawer copy of the Roll. Nine months of measuring existed to prove which hand was which. The mother took the Roll and the roster of forty-one and has spent four nights going down the list.

This page: what the three rules actually do. And the reason the school was ever worth shutting down.

PANEL 1 — Medium, Ira refuses (~13%)

Camera: Counter. Ira puts her hand flat over Jadi's palm — not on it, over it, the way you cover a wound you are not ready to work on. She has not answered the request and she has stopped stalling.

Image: A refusal, made physically, before it is made in words.

IRA: No.

JADI: Teacher —

IRA: I heard the whole case. I'm not arguing with it. If I pull that thread out of your hand you'll lose the last four years of use you've got in it and you'll still be on somebody's list, because the list was made off the copy, nine months before you ever picked up a knife.

IRA: You're not cutting yourself out of a book. You're cutting yourself out of a school.

PANEL 2 — Close, Jadi pushes back (~14%)

Camera: Jadi's jaw. She is not persuaded and she is not going to pretend to be. Her case is material and she has had nine months to build it.

Image: The root of the school disagreeing with the school's new teacher, on the merits.

JADI: A school that can't protect a hand from a chain is a hobby, teacher.

JADI: I've buried four people off that roster in nine months and none of them died — they just stopped being able to work and then they stopped being able to stay. Two of them left the basin. One of them sold her tools. And the receipt said registered.

JADI: Tell me what the strand in my hand has ever done for me except tell the Office where to find me.

PANEL 3 — Large, Ira's realisation (~16%)

Camera: Ira goes very still. Then: she takes the Book of the Hand and turns it to the front — page one, rule one — and reads it properly for the first time, out loud, in order.

Image: The lesson book, open at the beginning, with the three rules drawn as fingers. The panel should let the reader see the three rules next to each other, which no page has done before.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Rule one. Thread is braided, never single.

CAPTION: Rule two. The braid carries two strands.

CAPTION: Rule three. The teacher's strand is sewn into the student before speech.

CAPTION: I have treated those as three separate ideas my whole life. Two of them are decoration and the middle one is the enrolment.

PANEL 4 — Close, the mechanics (~15%)

Camera: Ira's fingers on the page, tracing the drawn braid. Underneath the drawing, in the school's own diagrams, a chain is drawn across a single thread — reading it — and the same chain across a braid, with nothing to read.

Image: The book's own illustration of the reason it exists. The diagram has been in the book for forty years.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The chain doesn't read a hand. It reads a thread, and it reads it because a single thread is a single date. One seam, one year, one number on a slate.

CAPTION: A braid has two strands in it and they went in at different times, and a reader trying to date a braid reads both years at once and gets neither.

CAPTION: She wasn't enrolling children. She was finishing them. Rule three puts the first strand in before they can talk, so that they grow up with a thread in their hand and never think about it. And then rules one and two — the craft, the lessons, the four moves, forty years of it — teach them to weave a second strand in beside it.

CAPTION: The school doesn't teach mending.

CAPTION: The school makes people the Office cannot count.

PANEL 5 — Close, Jadi's horror (~14%)

Camera: Jadi, close. Her face is doing something Ira has not seen on her all page. Not grief. Guilt.

Image: The first pupil discovering that she was never finished.

JADI: Then it worked for her and it worked for you and it never once worked for me.

JADI: I was the first one through the hatch. Twelve years old, thread in and sent home. And she got taken two months later — before she'd taught me the second strand. Before she'd taught anybody.

JADI: She spent forty years putting a first thread into people and never got to finish one of them.

JADI: I've had a single strand in my hand for forty-four years and I have been walking around this basin carrying the exact thing the Office has been looking for, and I told everybody I was the root.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the offer (~14%)

Camera: Ira has stood up. She has the needle-roll in her hand — not open. She is looking down at Jadi's palm and then at her own, closed.

Image: The teacher, holding the tools, and the door in her own hand that she shut two nights ago.

IRA: I can finish it.

IRA: Not mend the seam — put the second strand in. Braid it. Then you're not a single date any more and the chain reads nothing off you, and it doesn't matter what list they've got, because a list of years with no hands against it is just paper.

JADI: You'll need thread.

IRA: I've got thread.

JADI: You've got a closed hand. I saw it at the door. You'd have to open it, and you told me yourself the Office comes to that cellar every second bell.

PANEL 7 — Close, the cost is named — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Kessa, who has been silent for three pages, finally speaks. She has not moved from behind the counter. Her hands are flat on it. She has already priced the whole thing.

Image: The appraiser putting the number on it, because somebody has to.

KESSA: Say it out loud, child, so she can hear it in money.

KESSA: Open that stitch and there's a live channel in the middle of this basin again. Your mother's been using that channel for forty years and the Office has been trying to find it for forty years. You open it in front of a clerk's slate and you've handed it to them in a morning.

KESSA: That's the price. One braid in an old woman's hand, against every hand on the roster and your mother besides.

IRA (last line): ...Then I'll open it where they can't see it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): And I look at the pot-row through the shutters, and I think about a man walking up the Mendery step every second bell with a slate, and I know exactly where to do it.

Writing & art notes

  • This is the single most important reveal of Arc II. Rules one and two are not flavour text — they are the school's product. The mother was not teaching mending; she was building a population the Office could not index. Set the three rules as three separate, equal, isolated lines in Panel 3 so a scrolling reader reads them as a set for the first time.
  • Jadi was never finished. She is the school's first pupil and the one person the school never completed — because the Council took the mother before she could teach the second strand. Jadi's forty-four years of single-strand invisibility has been luck, not design, and she knows it.
  • The chain's mechanics must stay consistent with Ch. 005 Page 006: the compliance chain reads a mark and returns a Kind and a year. It reads single threads. A braid defeats it by returning both years at once. Do not upgrade the chain into something magical; keep it a measuring instrument.
  • Ira's line "I'll open it where they can't see it" is the chapter's hinge. She is not choosing between safety and Jadi — she is choosing to be clever, and Chapter 009 tests whether clever is enough.
  • Kessa's pricing speech is her thematic role. Every chapter she converts a moral problem into a number. Let her do it here, once, and then get out of the way.
