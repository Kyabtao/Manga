THREADBORN — Chapter 08 — Page 006

Chapter title: The First Pupil / पहला शिष्य Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Knot & Nail, Ashfall Basin Page type: Regular — 7 panels

Beats: Page 005 — the first pupil of the school walked into the stall, called Ira the teacher, and asked her to pull the teacher's strand out of her palm.

This page: who Jadi is, and what the school became after the binding.

PANEL 1 — Close, the name (~13%)

Camera: Jadi drinks the tea and sets it down. Ira has not answered the request. Instead she asks the question she has been holding since the doorway.

Image: Two women at a counter, a cup of tea, and a transaction that is not about money.

IRA: What do I call you?

JADI: Jadi.

JADI: She named me. First day, when she put the thread in. She said: you're the root, everything after you grows off you. And then she never used it again in forty years.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Jadi. Root.

CAPTION: I'd have remembered that name. I've read every file in the Mendery's front shelves at least once. There's no Jadi in the Council's basin register, which means the Council has never had a name for this woman, which means she has been invisible in Agnikhand for forty-four years.

PANEL 2 — Large, the drop (~15%)

Camera: Jadi's account, drawn as her memory — the Mendery's back door at night, a hatch cut low in the door, and one hand at a time pushed through it into the dark. Inside, in the dark, a woman's hands taking the hand and working on it. Nobody's face visible on either side.

Image: The school after the binding. Not a stall, not a room — a station.

JADI: After they bound her, she didn't stop. Nobody shut her down, girl — there wasn't anything to shut. A school is not a building. A school is hands.

JADI: They cut a hatch in the back door. Low down, the size of a forearm. And once a week, after the third bell, people walked down to the Ash-sLums, put one hand through the hatch, and she worked on it in the dark on the other side. Never spoke. Never opened the door. You'd feel her hands and then you'd feel the thread go in and then she'd tap the wood twice and you'd take your hand back and walk home.

JADI: Forty years of that. I ran the roster.

PANEL 3 — Close, Ira (~14%)

Camera: Ira, close, working through it. She is not shocked. She is assembling.

Image: The daughter learning what her mother's twenty years actually were.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Twenty years bound and she was teaching the whole time. Through a hatch in a door, in the dark, one forearm at a time, tapping twice when she was finished.

CAPTION: Nandi said she had students long after the Council shut her, and that is the entire point of the sewer. I thought she meant the thread went out.

CAPTION: The thread didn't go out. The students came in.

PANEL 4 — Medium, the roster (~14%)

Camera: Jadi's hands, close, over the Book of the Hand — the knots in the back. She turns the pages without reading them. She is going down a list in her head.

Image: The keeper of the roster and the book she kept the roster off.

JADI: Names were my job. She never wrote one down — not one, not ever, not in forty years. She said a name is a handle. She said you can put a name in a book and then somebody reads the book.

JADI: So the Roll wasn't names you'd recognise. Kind, year, and the note of the thread that went in. And then the knot — the first one they'd tied — stitched down on the page next to it so she could check a hand against it when they came back years later.

JADI: A name never went in that book. Which is why the Roll of Hands has got your mother's name on the cover and nobody else's in it at all.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Then the principal didn't take the Roll for its names.

CAPTION: He took it for its kinds and years — a count. A count of how many of us there are, in what proportions, in what parts of the basin.

PANEL 5 — Close, Jadi's mark again (~14%)

Camera: Back on the palm, under the lamp. Ira's thumb is at the seam's edge, not touching the thread. She is reading it the way she read Bhan's arm, and the panel should show the mender's gaze — measuring the depth of the grown-over section against the gauge of the strand.

Image: The mender at work on a problem. She has not agreed to anything and she is already reading the tear.

IRA: Nine months, you said. You started cutting at it nine months ago. Why then?

JADI: ...

JADI: Ask me the other question first, teacher, and I'll answer both.

PANEL 6 — Close, Jadi answers at last (~14%)

Camera: Jadi, very still. This is the page's payload and she delivers it in the same tone she delivered the roster.

Image: The first pupil telling the new teacher what has been happening to the school for nine months.

IRA: What's the other question?

JADI: Why I'm cutting out the only thread I've got.

JADI: Because the Office has been calling us up the terrace. One at a time. Kshudra, basin, from every part of the Ashfall. Nine months, forty-one of us.

JADI: They don't arrest anybody. They measure — that's their word for it. They put a hand on a slate and run a chain across the palm, and then they write a number, and then they let you walk home.

PANEL 7 — Close, the number — CLOSING HOOK (~16%)

Camera: Jadi's face, and behind her, drawn small in the corner of the panel, the pot-row — with a grey figure walking it with a compliance chain. The same chain from Ch. 005 Page 006.

Image: The school's first pupil, who has been measured, sitting in the school's newest stall.

IRA: Forty-one people. In nine months. What number did they write?

JADI: Mine was forty-four.

JADI (last line): Nobody in my family has ever lived past fifty, teacher. It's arithmetic, not a threat. Same number as the seam.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): The year the strand went in. She's telling me what the Office wrote on the slate was the year she was enrolled.

CAPTION: They aren't measuring hands. They're dating us. And there's only one document in the world that would let them check those dates.

CAPTION: The Roll of Hands.

Writing & art notes

  • The hatch is the central image of Jadi's page. Low in the door, forearm-sized, one hand at a time, two taps to end. It is warm and horrible at once, and it makes the mother's twenty years concrete in a way Nandi's summary never did.
  • The Roll has no names — this corrects and deepens Ch. 007. Ch. 007 Page 008 said the roll listed "name, kind, the year the strand went in." Page 006 reveals the store of names was kept by Jadi in her head, and the book held kind, year and knot. Establish the correction from Ira's side, as a mender re-reading a seam she thought she understood. Update the Ch. 007 summary and the Ch. 008 close-out so the canon is consistent.
  • The Office's method is dating, not arresting. Forty-one people measured in nine months, no detentions, no violence — a number written on a slate. This is the series' political register and it should be much more frightening than a raid.
  • Jadi's "mine was forty-four" is the chapter's coldest line. She is not asking for pity and the page must not offer any.
  • Keep the argument moving. Ira is being handed a case for pulling the strand out that is materially correct. Her answer on Page 009 must be better than the case.
