THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 001

Chapter title: The Reading / पाठ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Ash-sLums, the Mendery, first bell Page type: Chapter opener — 7 panels

Beats: Chapter 008 closed on a slate — a compliance chain read a braid, returned both years at once, and a clerk wrote one word, unreadable, and underlined it twice. There is no procedure for that, which means by morning there will be one.

Chapter 009 premise: the Office does not punish what it cannot read. It rules on it — and a rule can be served on a stall, at a counter, by a clerk with a bag, before the coffee is poured.

PANEL 1 — Wide, the Council Stair at first bell (~13%)

Camera: Wide, cold morning. The Council Stair, and on the notice-board at its foot, at eye height, a new sheet: fresh ink, grey wax, an ordinance number, and the Office's seal. Two basin people have stopped to read it; a third is reading it over their shoulders. Nobody is talking.

Image: The first morning of the new procedure. It is a piece of paper on a board, and it is worse than the raid.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): By morning there was one. I want that on the record, because I am the one who said it out loud on a slate in an alley four minutes before it existed.

CAPTION: The Office did not send anyone. It posted. That is the difference between the Council and the Office and it took me eight days to learn: the Council sends people, and people can be argued with.

PANEL 2 — Medium, the clerk at the stall (~14%)

Camera: The Mendery's counter, early. A grey clerk — young, damp, a leather bag on the counter that has been rained on — has laid out three things: a copy of the sheet, a receipt block, and a brass ring of seals. Kessa is behind the counter with her hands flat. Ira stands. The clerk has clearly rehearsed the sentence.

Image: Paperwork arriving at a stall like weather.

CLERK: Provisional Ordinance forty-one. Unreadable Hands. I have to read it to you. I'm sorry, it says I have to read it, in full, at the place of keeping.

KESSA: Then read it fast, child, the tea's on.

PANEL 3 — Close, the ordinance (~15%)

Camera: Close on the sheet, but the reader is meant to follow Ira's eyes, not the lettering: the panel is composed so the three numbered clauses sit between the two women like a third person at the counter. No legible prose — clause numbers and ruled lines only.

Image: The instrument. Three clauses, and every one of them aimed at a hand.

CLERK: One. A hand whose reading returns no year may not be entered in any ledger, licence, wage-book, charter or roll, and may not be counted toward any trade, tax, tenancy or claim.

CLERK: Two. Every such hand shall be declared, by its owner or by any person who knows it, and entered in the Register of Unreadable Hands, kept at the place of keeping, within three days of declaration.

CLERK: Three. No licensed practitioner shall mend, work, or instruct a declared hand.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Three days. I had three minutes last night and I thought that was the whole price.

PANEL 4 — Close, Ira doing the arithmetic (~14%)

Camera: Ira, close, and the reader should watch her get there in stages: first clause one is relief — an uncountable hand is outside the ledger, and outside the ledger is what the school has been building for forty years. Then clause two. Then clause three, and the relief goes.

Image: A mender understanding that the Office has not stopped counting. It has started counting something that cannot be hidden.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Clause one is what my mother spent forty years making. A hand that isn't in the book. A woman who isn't in any book. No tax, no tenancy, no claim on her, no way to sell her.

CAPTION: And then I read clause two properly.

CAPTION: They can't date us. Fine. So they'll count us — because a braid is a thing you can see, and you cannot hide a thing by making it unreadable, you can only hide it by making it nobody's business to look. And they have just made it the Office's business to look.

PANEL 5 — Medium, Kessa's counter-position (~14%)

Camera: Kessa, taking the receipt block and turning it around with two fingers so it faces the clerk — not refusing, redirecting. She has not raised her voice and she is not going to. Behind her, the shutters are up, which they have not been at this hour in three days.

Image: The appraiser, doing what she does with a moral problem: converting it into a document.

KESSA: Register's kept where?

CLERK: Place of keeping. It's on the schedule — third-register property in the sector, the Office doesn't open premises, it uses the ones that already answer for themselves.

KESSA: Say the address, child.

CLERK: ...The Mendery. The stall. Slot four, but they will not build a slot, they will put a desk in the cellar where the third register is.

KESSA: (to Ira, without turning her head) You did that.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): I did that. Eleven days ago, in front of an Inspector, I saved the archive by saying out loud that this stall answers to the principal and not to the Council.

CAPTION: They wrote it down. Of course they wrote it down. That is what they do with things that are said.

PANEL 6 — Close, the cost, named (~14%)

Camera: Ira's face, close, and behind her — small, in the panel's lower corner — the Mendery's cellar door standing open onto a room with a desk in it that was not there last night. The Office has already delivered the desk.

Image: The new teacher being made the keeper of the thing that indexes her people.

IRA: They're going to keep the list of every uncountable hand in the basin in my cellar.

KESSA: They're going to keep it in the principal's cellar, which you told them was yours to answer for. Same sentence, child. That's how the Office writes. One clause you agreed to, and eleven days later it has a desk in it.

IRA: So I refuse the desk.

KESSA: Then you refuse the licence, and the entry lapses, and clause four hands the whole archive to the Council by the end of the week — knots, book, roster, every page your mother kept. Now refuse it.

PANEL 7 — Close, the fourth clause — CLOSING HOOK (~16%)

Camera: Close on the clerk, who has been waiting to say the last part and has been dreading it. He turns the sheet over. There is a second side — one line, ruled, and the seal pressed at the bottom corner where it cannot be removed without taking the paper.

Image: The Office closing a loop it did not have to invent. The reader should see Ira realise that the ordinance has a timetable, and that the timetable is about Jadi.

CLERK: There's one more. It's on the back. Extent and commencement.

CLERK: This ordinance takes effect from the third bell of the previous night.

IRA: ...The previous night.

CLERK: Yes. Whatever was done to a hand at or before third bell last night is already inside it. Three days from declaration, and the declaration can be made by any person who knows.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, last line): The braid in Jadi's palm went in at about the fourth hour past third bell.

CAPTION (smallest): Which means the first thing the new law ever touched was the first thing I ever did.

Writing & art notes

  • **The ordinance is the chapter's antagonist and it must read as *paper.*** No soldiers, no inspectors, no door. A damp clerk with a bag, a receipt block, and three clauses. The horror is administrative and the reader must feel that the raid in Chapter 008 was the polite option.
  • Clause one is deliberately attractive. It gives the school exactly what forty years of work was for — people outside every book — and then clause two turns it into a category. The sequence (relief → re-read → dread) is the page's whole architecture; play it in Ira's captions and give her no dialogue for two panels.
  • "Place of keeping" is Chapter 008's cost returning. Ira's own legal move (declaring the archive third-register property) is what puts the desk in her cellar. Kessa names it flatly. Do not let Ira win this exchange; she loses it and knows she loses it.
  • The desk is already delivered (Panel 6). The Office does not ask; it schedules. One desk, in a cellar, unstacked, with nothing on it yet — the most frightening object on the page.
  • The commencement clause is the hook and it must be exact. "From the third bell of the previous night" — Ira braided Jadi's palm hours after that bell, and the clerk read the sentence before anyone had declared anything. The law was written to be earlier than the crime, which is the Office's entire method (Ch. 008: the principal pre-files; Ch. 009: the Office back-dates).
  • Kessa's stance is set here: he can have the books, I keep the reading. Plant it in her Panel 5–6 tone, land it properly on Page 003.
