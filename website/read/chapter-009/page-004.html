THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 004

Chapter title: The Reading / पाठ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Mendery, the cellar Page type: Demand page — 7 panels

Beats: the register is kept in the Mendery's cellar; the summons is served; Rekhak must swear the Office's sentence on a held stop at first bell tomorrow, and the sentence is a lie he has already admitted is a lie.

This page: the form of words, the second reader, and what the Office actually came for.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the certification (~13%)

Camera: The cellar. The register open at its first spread, the desk, the lamp. The young grey clerk has laid a certification sheet beside it — a single leaf, ruled, with a place for a keeper's mark and a reader's mark, and at the head of it four printed lines. Ira stands at the desk; Kessa sits on the bottom step of the stairs with a bowl; the Inspector is up on the cellar stair, half in shadow, hands behind his back.

Image: Two signatures standing between a list and a law.

CLERK: Certification. Reader sworn, keeper present, marks in the boxes. Until both boxes are marked the register is a list and nothing can be done with it.

IRA: And if the keeper's box stays empty?

CLERK: (a glance up the stairs) Then certification is taken up-terrace by the Office, and the schedule says — there's a note, hold on — where the keeper declines, the entry to which the keeping attaches lapses, and the property reverts. Reverts to the Council. That's clause four of your letter, I think. I'm reading it off the sheet, keeper, I don't know what any of it means.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Clause four. Sign or the school goes. The letter said it nine days ago and here it is again, wearing a different clerk.

PANEL 2 — Close, four lines (~14%)

Camera: Close on the certification sheet's head, four printed lines. No legible prose except one word at the top of the third line, drawn in art as a legible Office word because it is the point of the page: RESERVED. Around it, the keeper's box and the reader's box, both empty.

Image: The instrument, showing its teeth in a schedule.

CLERK: Line one. Entry one stands reserved to the Office and shall not be completed by declaration.

IRA: Reserved for what?

CLERK: Doesn't say what, keeper. It says reserved. There's a date under it, and the date's eleven days old, same as the drawing.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Forty-one lines. One reserved. Forty left for a basin that has had forty-one people measured, and the reserved line is drawn in the shape of somebody the Office has never once been able to find.

CAPTION: They did not build a list of the unreadable. They built a seat, and put it at the top, and left it empty for my mother.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the second reader (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the Inspector comes down two steps into the lamp-light, unhurried, and puts a small grey card on the desk next to the sheet — a reader's warrant, blank, with a training note in the margin. The clerk takes a step back without being asked to.

Image: The Office explaining, courteously, that it does not need this particular reader.

INSPECTOR: You are entitled to know the extent of the instrument, keeper, so I will tell you plainly. There is a reader-second in training. Up-terrace. Three months, they say.

IRA: Then wait three months.

INSPECTOR: Why would we? He is here, he is competent, and he is willing. If he declines, we train the boy, and the sentence goes in the same on the day. The Office is not short of time. He is short of time. That is the whole difference between us.

PANEL 4 — Close, Kessa's arithmetic (~15%)

Camera: Close: Kessa's bowl set down, and her hand flat beside the sheet — not on it. She is looking at the sentence as the clerk has copied it out for Rekhak to read aloud, and she reads it twice, and the second time her finger stops under the second half.

Image: The appraiser finding the trap in the words before anyone else in the room does.

KESSA: Read me the sentence again.

CLERK: A braided hand returns no year, and no hand in this basin can be made to return two.

KESSA: They know that one's false, child. They watched it fail on a wall four hours before they wrote it.

KESSA: So ask yourself why the Office would draft a sentence it knows is a lie and then require the only reader in Agnikhand to swear it on a held stop.

KESSA: (to the Inspector, without heat) You don't need the sentence. You need the deposit.

PANEL 5 — Close, the leash (~15%)

Camera: Close on the Inspector's face — the same mildness, unchanged, and the reader must understand that this is a man who has been waiting for the question so he can confirm it. In the panel's corner, small: the chain on the wall up-terrace, still, hung on its hook; and beside it, on the same wall, a much older hook with nothing on it.

Image: A regulatory apparatus admitting, without a raised voice, that it buys people.

INSPECTOR: Yes.

INSPECTOR: We put a false word in the mouth of the basin's only reader, under oath, on the record, in the presence of a keeper. From tomorrow, every reading he has ever entered answers to that page. Twenty years of them. Every tenancy he certified, every debt he counted, every wage-book he closed.

INSPECTOR: He is a good reader. He will swear it anyway, because he thinks he is buying a record. And he is — he is buying exactly one record, and the Office will own it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): And there it is, said out loud in my cellar in the same mild voice he used to thank Nandi for the chairs: they are not trying to catch forty-one people.

CAPTION: They are buying the man who could have proved they were wrong.

PANEL 6 — Close, Rekhak's answer (~14%)

Camera: Close: Rekhak, who came down the stairs at some point in Panel 4 and has been standing in the dark of the doorway, chain loose in his hand. His face does nothing. The chain's one dark link is in frame, thumb-side. He has heard all of it.

Image: A man being told, to his face, that he is being purchased, and deciding to be sold.

REKHAK: You've said your part. Keeper, I've got a question and it's for you and not for him.

REKHAK: When it's done — when the deposit lands — who reads it? Because it won't be in your register. It won't be on a slate. It goes where a held-stop word always goes, and the only eyes in this basin that can read that are a mender's.

IRA: ...Mine.

REKHAK: Then say yes or no to the keeping, and I'll say yes or no to the swearing, and we'll both know what we're for by the first bell.

PANEL 7 — Close, Ira's terms — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Close, Ira's hand going flat on the certification sheet, on the keeper's box — deliberately, in front of the Inspector, so that the act of marking it is witnessed. The clerk's pen is already out. Above them, on the stair, drawn small: Jadi, arrived early for a lesson she is not going to get, watching the law being signed into her own counter, with her left hand closed around her own palm.

Image: The new teacher taking the job the Office built for her, and setting her own price on it.

IRA: I'll mark the keeper's box and I'll stand at that desk tomorrow, and I'll administer the oath exactly as the schedule says.

IRA: On two conditions. First: you tell me the name of the reader-second, out loud, now, and you tell her — when she's trained — that the sentence she'll be swearing has already been sworn by a better reader, on this register, in this room.

IRA: And second: the Office enters on my keeping sheet that the keeper objected to the form of words, in writing, before she marked the box.

IRA (last line): Put that on the record. If the Office is going to own the record, let it own a good one.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): He wrote it. Keeper objected to the form of words. Entered at her insistence.

CAPTION (smallest): Thirty years from now, somebody is going to read that sentence and go looking for whatever the keeper was objecting to. That is the entire trade of my family, and I have just used it on paper.

Writing & art notes

  • This page is a negotiation, not a confrontation. Nobody raises a voice, no threat is made, and the Office wins everything it came for. Keep the temperature low and the frames tight; the only violence is a sentence being drafted knowingly falsely and a man agreeing to say it.
  • **"You need the deposit'" is the chapter's hinge and Kessa owns it.** It must land before the Inspector's confirmation, and the Inspector must agree immediately and without shame — his mild register is the horror. No menace, no grin: he is a man describing procurement.
  • Entry one is reserved. Draw it as a seat, not an entry: a ruled line with a date and a smaller drawn version of the same closed-seam palm from Chapter 008 Page 010's slate, and the word RESERVED legible. This is the instrument's actual purpose — the founder is the target and the forty-one declared hands are the net.
  • The reader-second exists and must stay almost invisible. One grey card, one margin note, no name revealed in art — Ira asks for the name and the Inspector gives it (let it be a basin name the reader has never heard; do not seed it as a character yet). It costs Rekhak the moral cover of being irreplaceable, which is the reason he swears now.
  • Ira's insistence on the objection is her mother's trade and her own. It is the first thing she does in this chapter that the Office does not expect, it is legal, it is free, and it is the only seed of Chapter 010's counter-move. Letter the sheet's line in art as abstract strokes plus the one legible word objected, and do not explain it in dialogue twice.
  • Jadi must be watching from the stair in Panel 7 and must not speak. She is the reason the register exists, and she is being consulted about nothing. She never again enters this cellar willingly.
