THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 003

Chapter title: The Reading / पाठ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Mendery, the cellar; the pot-row Page type: Turn page — 7 panels

Beats: the register is kept in the Mendery's cellar with forty-one ruled lines and a first entry drawn eleven days before the ordinance. The Inspector will come every second bell. Ira has three days and a desk she cannot refuse.

This page: the only copy that was never a document. And the sentence somebody has to swear to.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the pupil at the counter (~14%)

Camera: The Mendery, afternoon. Jadi has come for her lesson and is sitting at the counter with her left hand up in the lamp-light, the way she held it on Chapter 008 Page 006. Between her and the cellar door, in the middle distance, the desk is visible with the register open on it. Jadi has not looked at it once — she has looked at it once.

Image: The first pupil, at the counter, next to the instrument that was built for her.

JADI: The lesson.

IRA: Not today.

JADI: Today. I'm forty-four and I've got a hand that works and a law that says you can't touch it, and you're the teacher, so teach me under the law.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): That is the entire school in one sentence, and she said it without knowing she'd said it.

PANEL 2 — Close, Jadi reads the drawing (~15%)

Camera: Close on the register, and Jadi's thumb on the page — she cannot read a line of it and she does not need to. She is reading the drawing: the palm, the seam, the way the seam is drawn shut. Her thumb is at the margin, at the date, which she cannot read at all.

Image: The Office, without meaning to, has made a register that the unreadable can read.

JADI: That's the first line, then. Nobody's written one in yet.

IRA: Can you read it?

JADI: It's a hand and a closed seam, girl, it's drawn. Forty years I was taught with drawings. Every page of the Book is drawings. They've ruled the register in the one language they never learned and we did.

JADI: (quieter) That's not your seam. Line one isn't yours. Yours is open in the middle.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): She's right. Mine is the gate — the one my mother sewed into me. Line one is a hand that has never been opened at all.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the tally-thread (~15%)

Camera: Medium: Kessa at the counter, pulling a thread out of her cuff — the tally-thread, the one she has kept since the crease-writs, worn as a strand, not a document: knots, groups, the crease readings and the reply, all of it in knots. She holds it up between two fingers in front of the register.

Image: The only copy of the school's business that is not made of paper, held up next to the instrument that will catalogue the school's people.

KESSA: Hear me once, both of you. When they come for the books — and they'll come for the books — he can have them.

KESSA: I keep the reading. This is every crease-writ this stall ever received, every clause, every sentence your mother sent down that chute, and it isn't a document, it's knots on a strand, and nothing in that ordinance knows how to count it.

IRA: If they find it —

KESSA: Then they find a bit of string in a woman's cuff, and they write string on a slate, and they go home.

PANEL 4 — Close, the summons arrives (~14%)

Camera: Close: a summons, laid on the counter. Cheap paper, grey band, one line of lettering, and at the bottom a chain-impression — the Office's mark for a reader, not a clerk. The hand that laid it down is still in frame: Rekhak's, cuff, thumb, the counting-chain at his wrist with its one dark link (Ch. 002 Page 006).

Image: The basin's only chain-reader arriving at the only place the register can be sworn.

REKHAK: Keeper. I'm to be sworn tomorrow at first bell, at the place of keeping, by you, on the register.

IRA: Why me?

REKHAK: Because you're the keeper and the instrument says in the presence of the keeper of the place of keeping. They put it in the schedule this morning. They wrote the sentence they needed and then they wrote the building it would happen in.

PANEL 5 — Close, the sentence (~15%)

Camera: Close on Rekhak's face, and in the panel's lower third the chain at his wrist going totally still for one beat — the small costless tell of Chapter 001, the one that means he is about to say something he does not want to say. He is not lying. He is quoting.

Image: The words the Office drafted, in the mouth of the man who has to mean them.

REKHAK: The words I swear are these. A braided hand returns no year, and no hand in this basin can be made to return two.

REKHAK: Those are theirs. I have not written them. I have to say them at the first bell with the chain held still from the first word to the last, and then the register opens with my name under it as the reader who certifies the instrument.

IRA: Then don't.

REKHAK: Keeper, if I don't, the Office binds the register unsworn — and the register says the same thing anyway, with the sentence quoted, and it will be true, because nobody who could have said otherwise went on the record saying it.

PANEL 6 — Close, what the held stop costs (~14%)

Camera: Close, two hands on the counter: Ira's, and Rekhak's with the chain. Under the counter's edge, drawn small: the reader's own ledger — the one from Chapter 001, the one he told her he is not permitted to open — lying closed, with a hair-thin line of light along its fore-edge.

Image: The mechanics of the held stop, restated for the reader in one panel: a momentary stop is a tell; a held stop is a ledger.

REKHAK: You know what a held stop is. You're a mender, you've read thread your whole life.

REKHAK: A stop that lasts a breath tells on me. A stop that lasts a sentence opens my ledger. Every word under it gets entered — mine, in my hand, on the page I answer for and have never been allowed to read.

IRA: So the sentence is a lie, and a lie under a held stop is a deposit.

REKHAK: The sentence is a lie. Every word of it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): And I stand there and I do the arithmetic I have been doing since I was eleven years old, and the arithmetic says: he is going to swear it, and the reason is in this cellar, open on a desk, ruled in forty-one lines.

PANEL 7 — Close, why he will swear it — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Close on Rekhak, and behind him, in the frame, the cellar door and the desk and the register, all in one line, so the reader sees the two things he is standing between. He does not look at either. The chain has started moving again — slowly, as if it has been somewhere.

Image: A man choosing to spend the only thing he has that the Office cannot take, and being entirely clear about the price.

IRA: Why? You've got a ledger with one lie in it already. You put a second in and they own the page.

REKHAK: Because there's a hand on that register they haven't filled in yet, and when they fill it they'll fill it with somebody who can't answer. They'll walk the pot-row with a slate, and they'll ask forty-one people to declare themselves, and forty of them will, because of that line about no liability.

REKHAK (last line): And I'm the only reader in Agnikhand. Which means I'm the only man who can stand in the middle of that and make the reading disputed.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest): Disputed. Not wrong.

CAPTION (smallest): He's not buying them time. He's buying them a record.

Writing & art notes

  • Jadi reading the drawing is the page's best idea and must not be explained. She cannot read a word of the register and she knows exactly what it says, because the school taught hands and the Office drew a hand. Play it light, in her flat register, and let Ira's caption carry the irony.
  • **The tally-thread is now a character.** From this page forward it is the only surviving copy of the crease-writs and the reply, and it lives in Kessa's cuff. Draw it once, clearly, at thumb scale: a strand with grouped knots. Never open it, never caption its contents.
  • Rekhak's sentence must be quoted exactly, twice (Panel 5 and implicitly in the register tomorrow): a braided hand returns no year, and no hand in this basin can be made to return two. Chapter 009 Page 006 swears it; the deposit is the second half of that sentence.
  • The held stop is physics, not magic — one full panel of chain at rest, then the ledger's fore-edge line of light. Keep the instrument a measuring tool with a legal consequence, and keep the consequence bureaucratic: a word entered in a book that the owner must answer for.
  • Rekhak's motive is not nobility and must not read as nobility. He is a man whose one asset is being the only reader, and the Office is about to industrialise reading. His play keeps him necessary. Ira should be unsure whether he is protecting the forty-one or protecting his own indispensability, and the caption should say so without resolving it.
  • Continuity: the counting-chain's one dark link (Ch. 002 Page 006) is in frame in Panels 4–5; the clerk's slate in the Mendery cellar is still being written every second bell (Ch. 008); Nandi is one day into custody with no charge; clause four's timer has not been extended by the keeping.
