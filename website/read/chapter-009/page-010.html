THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 010 — FINALE

Chapter title: The Reading / पाठ Arc: II — The Mendery Sector: Agnikhand — the Council Stair, the Reckoning Office, and the Mendery at dawn Page type: Chapter finale — 7 panels

Beats: the Book of the Hand goes up the stair at first bell with thirty-one pages cut clean and a keeper's attestation that the seats of the knots are with their owners. One knot, unnamed, has been left on the register's last line.

This page: what the Office does with full compliance, and what the school does next.

Chapter 009 budget closed: chain-stop SPENT once (Page 006, Rekhak, held stop; deposit entered in his own ledger). No other stop in the chapter. Loom never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the delivery (~13%)

Camera: The Council Stair, first bell, cold. The young grey clerk carries the wrapped Book up the steps with both hands, the way you carry something that belongs to somebody else. On the notice-board at the foot, the ordinance sheet from Chapter 009 Page 001 is still there, curling, with a second sheet wired under it.

Image: A private archive entering the most public building in the basin, lawfully, on foot.

CLERK: (to himself, quietly, as he climbs) Source record. Seats with owners. No name entered. Keeper's attestation attached. I read it twice. It doesn't say anything wrong. It doesn't say anything.

PANEL 2 — Close, the attestation (~14%)

Camera: Close up-terrace: the Inspector, seated, reading the keeper's attestation with the wrapped Book on the table in front of him, unopened. Behind him, a clerk has already cut the twine. The Inspector does not look pleased and does not look displeased; he reads legal language the way other people read weather.

Image: An administrative man discovering he has been lawfully outmanoeuvred and adjusting.

INSPECTOR: Lawful.

CLERK: The pages are cut, sir.

INSPECTOR: I know they're cut. I read it before it was cut. (he turns the attestation over) She has complied with the requisition, entered an attestation on the certification sheet, and produced the source record in a condition the ordinance does not describe — because nobody who drafted it had met a mender.

INSPECTOR: Note the acceptance. Note the condition. Do not note an opinion.

PANEL 3 — Small cold panel, the translation room (~14%)

Camera: A small, cold, lamplit room up-terrace, drawn tight and grey: a single high desk, a stool, and a clerk — a different clerk, an older one — with the register's copy open in front of him and a second, larger volume beside it, into which he is transcribing the register's lines one at a time: kind, year, and beside each line a small drawn knot, copied from the register's pages. He has copied three.

Image: The Office's answer to a language it does not speak: hire someone to write it down twice.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): They didn't send it back. They didn't refuse it. They started copying it — one line at a time, into a bigger book, with the knots re-drawn by a clerk who has never held a needle.

CAPTION: That's the translation the Inspector promised me on Page Seven. It is not a punishment. It is a department.

PANEL 4 — Close, the reserved line (~15%)

Camera: Close: the register's copy at the high desk up-terrace — the original still stands in the Mendery's cellar, because the schedule requires the register to be kept at the place of keeping — and on the copy's first line, the reserved one, something has been newly entered in a hand that has never appeared in any Office document: a date, today's, written small at the margin, in crimson, and under it four words that are the panel's payload.

Image: The seat at the top of the register, filled at last — not with a name, with a price.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): On the reserved line, in crimson, in the principal's own hand, for the first time in twenty years of paperwork that never once left his building, four words were entered.

CAPTION (lettered, art): PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED.

CAPTION: Not the name. Not the crime. Not the woman. The hand. Any hand with the founder's strand in it, produced by any person, at any counter, by any means.

KESSA (one line, flat, off-panel over the page): Now every poor soul on that pot-row has a reason to look at your palms.

PANEL 5 — Close, two days (~14%)

Camera: Close: the clay token from the Office's custody on the Mendery counter, and beside it a hearing slip — two days, a room number, one line of print, and a box ticked: witness: to be asked. Kessa's hand is flat on the slip, holding it in place, and she is reading it for the third time.

Image: The chapter's other clock, arriving in the ordinary post.

KESSA: Nandi. Two days, room nine, witness to be asked. They're not charging her, child, they're going to put knots in front of her and ask her to say whose they are.

IRA: She won't.

KESSA: She'll look at them. That's all I'm saying. She held that shelf forty years and she never once said out loud that she knew a knot from a name, and if they ask her straight, in a room, with a clerk writing — she is going to answer the question they asked, because that is the only kind of honesty she has ever had.

PANEL 6 — Medium, dawn at the counter (~15%)

Camera: Medium, dawn: the Mendery counter, shutters going up, ash-light. Ira with her sleeves rolled, taking the first shutter off its hook. Jadi at the counter with her left sleeve already rolled to the elbow and the scrap of Book page sewn into her cuff, visible, deliberate — worn the way Kessa wears hers. On the counter between them: the third-day notice, the identification form (copy), and the register's own line two, which is — for the first time in the school's life — a pupil's name in a public document, in the school's own language.

Image: Two women and one piece of paper, on the last morning before the stair.

IRA: Two days to produce a hand. Ours is line two.

JADI: Then we go up and show them line two.

IRA: Jadi, they will put a form in front of you and they will ask you to make a mark under your line, so they can say the hand was produced by its owner. If you mark it, your name goes in that register in an Office hand, and every page in the basin with a Jadi on it becomes theirs.

JADI: I know.

PANEL 7 — Close, the last line — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Close: Jadi's hand on the counter, palm up, the braid plain in the lamplight — grey strand and new strand crossing at the root, the school's first finished palm. And above it, in the frame's upper corner, the Council Stair, drawn small in morning ash. Ira's hand comes in from the panel edge and turns Jadi's palm over, gently, the way a mender examines a seam she has already sewn.

Image: A teacher and her first pupil agreeing to walk into the building that indexes them — for the reason the school exists.

JADI: You said rule three. So here's what I'm telling you, teacher, and you can write it in your own book. Whatever they ask me up there, they can ask me as me — no mark, no name in their hand, no line they can pull.

JADI: I've been uncountable for forty-four years and nobody ever once asked me a question. I'd like to see the room.

CAPTION (Ira's voice, last line): Two days. Nandi in a room with knots and a clerk. The principal's four words on a reserved line. My mother four nights down the same list the Office is reading from the other end.

CAPTION (smallest): And tomorrow the first pupil of this school walks up the Council Stair on her own two feet, because her teacher told her what she did.

END OF CHAPTER NINE.

Writing & art notes

  • **The Office's answer is departmentalism, and it must be drawn as small and cold.** No raid, no arrest, no warrant: a high desk, one clerk, a bigger book, three lines copied. The horror is that the Office has decided to learn the school's language at a desk, permanently, at cost.
  • The four words in crimson are the chapter's final escalation and they must be lettered in art: PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED. Establish, through Ira's caption only, that this is a bounty on the founder's strand — which is the crimson-under-grey in Ira's own palm, and in her mother's, and in nobody else's in Agnikhand. Then get off the page.
  • Do not show the principal, the mother, or the Loom. The principal is four words in crimson. The mother is a list and an unlit lamp. The Loom is silent. Nandi is a token and a slip.
  • Jadi's ending must be joyful and flat at once. "I'd like to see the room." She is not marching and she is not frightened; she is a woman who has never been asked a question. The reader should feel the school's whole ethic in that line: the answer to being indexed is to walk up and answer in person, in the school's own language, without a mark.
  • Chapter 010 opens on that walk. The stair, the waiting room, room nine, the clerk, and the register; Nandi's hearing is the other clock and it is in the same building on the same day. Carry forward: entry one is a bounty; the register's line two is a knot with no name; Rekhak's ledger holds a deposit and his readings are reviewable; Kessa's tally-thread is the only uncopyable record; the licence's clause four still runs on the keeper's failure to produce.
  • Nothing on this page may resolve. No one is rescued; no instrument is defeated; the compliance was lawful and the result is a reward notice, a hearing date, and a walk up a stair.
