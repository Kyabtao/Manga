"My price is not coin. When your school next ties a hand in, it ties mine."

Page 008 note: names her price like a professional and it is the chapter's heart in miniature — not coin, not revenge: the only book that ever counted me fairly lost the page. Her hands knew the truth before her politics did.

THE GREY COAT (the fee, exact as ever)

Page 008 note: watches them walk in the front, because the school is not a secret and sneaking is a confession. Writes its exact report. The Office has known where the door is for forty years.

Page note: two professional readers, and a grammar older than both. Nandi's terms (when the school ties a hand in, it ties mine); the open walk past the paid coat; the kept room (lamp cut, sweep fresh, hook empty); the cradle-hold that ruin never touched. Then the double reveal, dry as a ledger: the oldest rod's year-grammar is the Office's own, in an older hand, from before the Office's year one — this is my grammar's grandmother; the Office learned to count here — and the first hand the school ever taught is signed where a name would go with a whorl: the mark on the note filed in Ira's register. Discovery made, not decoded. Hook: "Ask her, keeper."
