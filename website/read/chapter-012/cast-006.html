Chapter 012 — Page 006 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one. English-only page (AUDIT R13.22).

IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the standing answer)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "The register of the keeper does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err."

Page 006 note: answers the summons of record, standing, carrying her own law. Turns every question into a filing, hears the count stumble through the wall, and dismantles the signature trap with one rule-shaped sentence — not a refusal, a filing, the only kind of no that cannot be made into a confession. Leaves the amendment on the table unsigned, knowing exactly what its patience is for, and walks out with the page's knowledge: her hand is the last working part they do not own.

THE GREY CLERK (the interview that was a receipt)

No full sheet; Ch. 009–012. The Office's voice with a pen in it.

The Grey Clerk / Manav / Agnikhand / grey, procedural / none — spends other people's /
Copy: reproduce any record in the Office's own hand / "The Office is patient. Rolls open. Keepers err."

Page 006 note: loses every round and wins the interview. Produces the third paper — the amendment that could not be posted in public — and lays the signature trap with the survey settlement's own hook; when her counter leaves him unable to declare his own posting wrong, he does the thing he came to do: files her refusal in his book, like a receipt. The amendment stays on the table, armed, waiting for a keeper who errs — or a keeper who will.

THE COUNT (through the wall)

No full sheet; Ch. 012. The Office's mouth-count, heard in relay — a name, a line, a name, a line.

The Count / Office process / Agnikhand / none — read aloud, line by line / spends the readers' voices /
Stall: it stops where a name should be — every one of the twenty-two is a held pause /
No line on this page. The whole Office can hear it being slow.

Page 006 note: the race's first audible beat. Somewhere over the yard the Office reads its roll aloud the old way — the cord is still dead in the chest — and the count by mouth is slow, and slower at the nameless. Nothing is resolved; a clock is simply heard, stumbling.

Page note: nine feet by eleven, one lamp, no audience. She attends of record and does not sit (seating is posture, and posture is testimony); the cushion waits where it has waited since the survey; the count reads aloud through the wall and stalls at the nameless. Then the third paper: the amendment — the roll means the Office's roll — pre-cut, pre-sealed, needing the keeper's counter-signature because a posting that touches the count co-signs with the register. Her counter is a filing: the register does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err — which forces him to declare his own posting wrong or leave it. He leaves it — and files her refusal in his own book: the trap has no door because it is a trap that waits. The chain hangs dead on its stand all morning: grammar is the only thing left in the building that pulls. Hook: "Sign nothing, keeper."
