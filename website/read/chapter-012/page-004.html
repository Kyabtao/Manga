THREADBORN — Chapter 12 — Page 004

Chapter title: The Whole Roll Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the archive behind the first door Page type: The ask — 7 panels, ends with a sentence loaded

Beats: the ask, properly made — entered of record, in the Office's own grammar, marked by the asker; the fee — the ask-log is a record, and records get read: the keeper becomes a line in somebody's count; the reading — rods to the lamp, by length and turn, no names needed; the caveat that keeps the clock — whole as of the last finished teaching, and three hands are mid-thread; the verdict he cannot withhold — the school's roll is whole; the assembly — the Office's definition never said whose roll; and the load — the basin's most dangerous sentence is now legal tender, and she is the only one holding it.

This page: the count the Office postponed until wholeness can be demanded — because wholeness, by the school's own records, already exists.

Chapter 012 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent — Rekhak reads by craft; the chain stays cold. Mother never on panel, never speaks — one touch only: the newest entries on the newest rod are tied in her grammar, and there are no newer ones. Loom never speaks, does not appear.

PANEL 1 — Medium, the ask (~14%)

Camera: Medium: among the racks, lamplight. Ira faces Rekhak Vahni across a rod laid flat between them like a counter. She does not hold out coin. She does not raise her voice. She asks the way the Office asks.

Image: An ask is not a plea. Done properly, it is a filing.

IRA: Then I am asking, properly. Read the roll. Of record.

REKHAK: (already reaching for paper) Good. An ask is an entry, keeper, or it is talk. Entries outlive the people who make them. That is the entire point of a record — and the entire price.

PANEL 2 — Close, the entry (~13%)

Camera: Close: the warrant's blank verso on the rod. Rekhak writes the ask in the Office's knot-grammar, quick and exact — request, reader, subject, standing. Then he turns it toward Ira. She marks it with the only mark she owns: a plain press of her thumb, the mendery way.

Image: The reader-of-record now carries a record that the keeper asked.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): There. Her no is on file in one basin, and my ask is on file in the other, and I am the only person alive whose name is on both papers.

REKHAK: The fee. The ask-log is a record, and records get read. Someday somebody will read who asked the Office's reader to read the school. You have just become a line in somebody's count, keeper. That is the whole fee. I do not take coin for exactness.

CAPTION: Shield and tether, and now this. Everything I love about paper, I learned from what paper does to people.

PANEL 3 — Wide, the reading (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the racks at night, lamp-low. Rekhak walks the rows with the marked slip tucked in his coat and reads — aloud, by length, by turn, by years-at-the-craft. No names. None needed. Ira stands at the end of the row holding the lamp for him, the way you hold a light for a man doing his job.

Image: The basin's nameless, read better than the basin ever read its own.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): He reads them the way I mend: entry by entry, with the respect of procedure. A hand of this length. This turn. This many years at it. Entered this season, taught this season, gone this season.

CAPTION: Twenty-two people in my basin have no names on the Office's roll. They have length, and turn, and years. They have been enrolled all along, by somebody who kept better records than any of us.

PANEL 4 — Close, the newest rod (~13%)

Camera: Close: the newest rack, lowest rod, Rekhak's fingers stopping on the last three knots. This season's thread — grey, old-style, shadowless. Tied in her grammar. And after them, windings beyond the knots: three hands mid-teaching, not yet tied in.

Image: The exact man finds the exact edge of the verdict before the verdict.

REKHAK: (professional, to the rod) Caveat for the record. The roll is whole as of the last finished teaching. There are windings past the last knot — three of them, fresh. Mid-thread. When they finish, somebody ties them in, and wholeness is a moving number until then.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Tied in her grammar. Of course. The school's entries have been her handwriting all along.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the verdict (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the two of them at the row's end, lamp between. Rekhak straightens. Puts his fingers flat on the rack the way he closed the ledger on Page 003. Gives the basin the sentence he was asked for, of record.

Image: The Office's reader-of-record, asked properly, cannot withhold the reading. It is what he is for.

REKHAK: The reading, of record. The school's roll is whole.

(a beat, exact to the end)

As of the last finished teaching.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Every hand the school ever taught stands entered on it. Length, turn, years. He said it the way he says everything — like a man reading weather — and the room did not even creak. Verdicts that heavy never make noise going down.

PANEL 6 — Close-medium, the assembly (~15%)

Camera: Close-medium: Ira's face in lamp-light, thinking — and we stay with her while she assembles it, panel-long, no one speaking. The definition. The words. The hole where whose should have been.

Image: The basin's most dangerous sentence, built at lamp-speed out of the Office's own paper.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The count will be taken when the roll is whole. The roll is whole when every hand taught by the school stands entered upon it.

CAPTION: He never said the Office's roll.

CAPTION: The count is postponed until the roll is whole. The school's roll is whole — as of the last finished teaching. Read plainly, in the Office's own grammar, the postponement is over. The count is owed. Now. By their own definition, read back to them by their own reader-of-record, on my ask, of record.

PANEL 7 — Medium, the load — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the threshold. Ira at the door with empty hands — the roll stays here, nothing leaves the archive, what leaves is a sentence. Rekhak behind her among the racks, marking nothing, keeping his own count of what just happened.

Image: The paper stays. The sentence walks.

REKHAK: You understand what you are carrying out of here.

IRA: A sentence. Heavier than the chest it lives in.

REKHAK: For the record: I was asked, and I read. What the basin does with a plain reading is not my fee.

CAPTION (smallest): Read it back to them, keeper.

Writing & art notes

  • The ask must be procedural, not sentimental. No trembling, no speech about trust. Ira asks the way the Office files: of record, in the Office's grammar, marked. Rekhak's dignity is that he was always going to be honest about the price — and the price is not coin, it is becoming a line in somebody's count. The ask-log mirrors the mother's no: Ira is now the only name on both papers — her shield and her ask are on file in the same world of paper that eats people. One caption carries that; do not hammer it.
  • The reading is the chapter's emotional floor and it must be dry. Rekhak reads by length, turn, years — the nameless read better than the basin ever read its own. Do not have anyone weep. The respect is procedural, and that is exactly why it lands: they have length, and turn, and years.
  • The caveat is load-bearing. Rekhak the exact man finds the edge of his own verdict before the verdict: whole as of the last finished teaching — three hands are mid-thread, windings past the last knot. This keeps the chapter's clock alive (wholeness is a moving number; the Office's plug still has purchase) while letting the detonation arm. It also lands the page's one mother-touch: the newest entries are tied in her grammar — the school's entries have been her handwriting all along — and there are no newer ones. No whorl-mark; she is never on panel; one inference, no more.
  • The verdict is delivered flat. The school's roll is whole — said like weather. The room does not react. Rekhak adds the caveat as a matter of procedure, not mercy. The horror of the chapter remains that the Office's own machinery is being used in the basin's defense, correctly.
  • The assembly is the page's payload and it must be built in public. Three captions, quoting the definition exactly (as posted on Page 001), then the hole: he never said the Office's roll. Then the conclusion: the postponement is over; the count is owed now. Let the reader get there one caption ahead of her; do not let anyone else on-panel say it — this sentence belongs to Ira until she spends it.
  • The hook is restraint. She does not go to the clerk this page. Nothing leaves the archive but the sentence. Rekhak marks his own position (I was asked, and I read) — he is positioning for the fallout without melodrama. Smallest caption mirrors Pages 002–003's imperative run: Read it back to them, keeper. Page 005 is the firing: the definition read back at the list-post, in the Office's own voice, in public.
  • Continuity: chain-stop budget Chapter 012: ONE, unspent (chain cold all page; he reads by craft). Mother never on panel, never speaks — her grammar on the newest entries is the only touch. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The note rides in Ira's inside pocket. The step-knot is still tied on the Mendery step. The ask is of record: request, reader, subject, standing — knot-grammar, marked with Ira's thumb. The day-warrant came back unwritten (Page 003); drain-mend remains the report's technical truth until she fires the sentence.
  • §3.1: no electric light, no modern furniture, no printed signage; the archive lit by one oil lamp; rods, racks, hook: wood, iron, thread. No lettering anywhere — the ask-entry is knot-grammar and a thumb-mark; the definition is quoted only in captions, never on paper in art.
  • Numbers: one ask, entered and marked; one reading, of record; verdict: whole, as of the last finished teaching; three hands mid-thread, not yet tied; twenty-two on the school's roll by length; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
