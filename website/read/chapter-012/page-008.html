THREADBORN — Chapter 12 — Page 008

Chapter title: The Whole Roll Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the stall at dawn, the low gate, the archive Page type: The reading — 7 panels, ends with a mark that answers a question nobody has asked yet

Beats: the terms — Nandi's price is not coin: when the school next ties a hand in, it ties hers; she was counted once, by the only book that ever counted her, and it lost the page; the walk in — through the low gate, openly, past the paid coat, because the school is not a secret and hiding would cost more; the kept room — lamp trimmed, floor swept, hook empty: somebody maintains the school before anyone arrives; the cradle-hold — the Office cut Nandi's standing, not her hands; the grandmother — the oldest rod's year-grammar is the Office's own year-grammar in an older hand, older than the Office's year one: the Office learned to count here; and the whorl — the first hand the school ever taught is wound on the rod, and where a name would go, a maker's-mark that Ira has seen exactly once before.

This page: two professional readers, and a grammar older than both.

Chapter 012 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent — no chain on this page; Nandi reads by craft alone. Mother never on panel, never speaks — her maintenance is the only presence: the lamp, the sweep. Loom never speaks, does not appear.

PANEL 1 — Medium, dawn, the stall (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the stall at first light, lamp still lit. Kessa between them at the counter, broker's posture — and across the wood, Nandi: grey-streaked hair pinned, plain shawl, the brass reading-lens on its cord. She has heard the whole ask through once. She names her price.

Image: The Office ruined her standing. It never got her craft.

NANDI: My price is not coin. I was counted once — by the only book that ever counted me fairly — and it lost the page. So: I read your year, keeper. And when your school next ties a hand in, it ties mine.

IRA: The roll takes taught hands. I do not set its rule.

NANDI: Then it takes pupils. I was tallying the loom-band before you were born, girl. I can be taught to hold a hand steady. What I will not do is ask twice.

PANEL 2 — Wide, the low gate (~13%)

Camera: Wide: mid-morning, the rim road down to the low gate — Ira and Nandi walking it side by side, in the open, unhurried. Far back at the fixed interval, the grey coat, watching, writing.

Image: They do not sneak, because the school is not a secret and sneaking is a confession.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The coat sees us go in, and will report it exactly, because exactness is its fee: the tally-keeper entered the school. Let it. The Office has known where the door is for forty years. Knowing is not the same as daring.

PANEL 3 — Wide, the archive at dawn (~14%)

Camera: Wide: through the first door. The racks in morning-cold air, the lamp on its bracket lit and trimmed, the floor swept clean along the racks — and the iron hook by the door, empty, its circle of swept stone undisturbed. Nandi stops on the threshold and reads the ROOM the way she reads a ledger.

Image: Somebody keeps the school before anyone arrives.

NANDI: (quiet, professional) Lamp cut this morning. Sweep fresh. Somebody tends this room, keeper, the way a tally-house is tended.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): I do not tell her who. I am not sure I know. The room is kept, the hook is empty, and there is no signature anywhere in the building except thread.

PANEL 4 — Medium, the racks (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the oldest rack, and Nandi's hands going to the lowest rod — the cradle-hold, palms under, thumbs light, the grip of a craft the Office cut her standing for. Her hands are steady. Ira watches the steadiness, and understands something about ruin.

Image: The Office cut her count. It did not cut her hands.

NANDI: (lifting the rod) Forty years since I held a tally of this school's. The Office cut my count out of me by inches — a knot here, a braid there, until nothing I counted counted. You know how it is done. You have the book for it.

IRA: I know exactly how it is done.

NANDI: Then hold the lamp, keeper, and we will see what the grammar remembers.

PANEL 5 — Close, the year-knot (~16%)

Camera: Close: the lens down over Nandi's eye, the oldest rod's head in lamplight — the first knot, dense, deliberate, the old grammar. Her face changes by fractions: recognition, then disbelief, then something colder. The professional's horror, arriving in order.

Image: She knows this grammar. She knows it too well. That is the horror.

NANDI: This is a year. I can read it. That is the wrong part, keeper — listen to me. This is the OFFICE'S year-grammar. The registry's own counting of years — tied in an older hand, by a better craftsman, before the Office's year one.

NANDI: (setting the lens down like it burned) This is my grammar's grandmother. The school was not hiding from the Office's books. The Office's books were cut from the school's.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): And there it is, and it will not go back into the knot. The Office learned to count here. The two books are one grammar — one gone to clerkhood, and one kept in thread, in a wet room, by somebody's hands.

PANEL 6 — Close, the second knot down (~14%)

Camera: Close: Nandi's thumbs walking the rod down past the year-knot to the first winding — the first hand the school ever taught, silvered with age, wound tight and kept. And at the head of the winding, where a name would go on Office stock: a maker's-mark. Small, deliberate, old.

A whorl.

Image: The school's first entry, signed the way its keeper signs.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): I know that mark. I have seen it once — folded small, kept forty years, filed by my own hand in a book that is starting to become an Office.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the two readers — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: Nandi holds the rod out flat between them — the grandmother-grammar at one end, the whorl at the other — and looks at Ira the way she read the room at the threshold. Ira's gloved hand rests on the register under her arm. Neither of them says the name. There is no name. There is a mark, and a room that is kept, and a knot on a step that was tied by hands that never once asked to be counted.

Image: The discovery made. Not decoded. Not yet.

NANDI: (level, craftsman to craftsman) You know the hand, keeper. Do not tell me you do not. I watched your face do the arithmetic.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): The school's first entry is signed with her mark. The no on file in my register is signed with her mark. The founder of the grammar the Office counts in does not want counting.

CAPTION (smallest): Ask her, keeper.

Writing & art notes

  • Nandi's terms open the page and they are the chapter's heart in miniature. She does not want coin and she does not want revenge; she wants back onto the only book that ever counted her fairly. When your school next ties a hand in, it ties mine — and when Ira cites the school's rule (taught hands), Nandi's answer is the pivot: then it takes pupils. The Office's most ruined reader applying to become the school's oldest pupil is the arc the rest of the chapter can spend. Do not resolve her admission this page.
  • The walk-in is public on purpose. Sneaking is a confession, and the school does not confess. The coat writes its exact report; the caption carries the beat (knowing is not the same as daring). No confrontation — the page has better things to spend its panels on.
  • The kept room is the mother's only appearance. Lamp cut this morning, sweep fresh, hook empty. Nandi reads the maintenance like a tally-house — she has stood in kept rooms before, and she says so. Ira's inference stays inference (there is no signature anywhere in the building except thread). No whorl-mark anywhere except the rod.
  • Panel 5 is the page's payload and the horror must be professional, not gothic. Nandi recognizes the grammar because it is HERS — the Office's year-grammar, which she spent forty years serving, in an older hand, by a better craftsman, from before the Office's year one. This is my grammar's grandmother. One consequence caption only (Ira's): the Office learned to count here; the two books are one grammar. Do not extrapolate the consequences (what it means for the definition, the count, the Office's legitimacy) — those detonate in later pages. Let the craftsmanship carry it: her hands know the truth before her politics do.
  • The whorl lands in a single word and a single panel. The first hand the school ever taught, signed where a name would go — with the mark on the note in Ira's register. The page does not decode it. Ira's caption admits the recognition and stops. Nandi's read of Ira's face (I watched your face do the arithmetic) is the only acknowledgment — and her demand for honesty is the bridge to the ask.
  • The imperative ladder continues. The chapter's smallest captions have become a signature: Knock, keeper / Ask him, keeper / Read it back to them, keeper / Sign nothing, keeper / A second Office, keeper. Keep it better — and now Ask her, keeper. The ask is no longer a procedure. It is the one thing Ira does not know how to do, aimed at the one person who does not answer.
  • Continuity: chain-stop budget Chapter 012: ONE, unspent (no chain; Nandi reads by craft). Mother never on panel, never speaks — maintenance only. Loom absent. The register travels under Ira's arm with the filed note inside (the whorl she has "seen once" is IN that register — say it in caption, not by producing the note). The amendment original stays armed on Room Nine's table. Step-knot still tied. Ask-log first line stands. The three mid-thread hands remain untied (the race runs off-page this page). The paid coat remains at its fixed interval, exact, unnamed.
  • §3.1: no electric light, no modern furniture, no printed signage; the archive lit by one oil lamp; Nandi's lens is a brass reading-lens on a cord (a tool, not a watch). No lettering anywhere — the year-knot and the whorl are thread-marks only.
  • Numbers: one reading, of no office; one grammar, grandmothered; one year-grammar older than the Office's year one; one whorl, twice seen; one price named (a tying-in, not coin); chain-stop ONE, unspent.
