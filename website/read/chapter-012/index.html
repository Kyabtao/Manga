Chapter 012 — The Whole Roll — CLOSE-OUT

Arc: III — The Second School · Sector: Agnikhand, Ashfall Basin · Pages: 10 Format note: pages 001–003 bilingual (EN + Hindi, floors cleared); pages 004–010 English-only by user instruction (AUDIT R13.22).

Synopsis

The Office stops banging on doors and posts a definition: the count will be taken when the roll is whole; the roll is whole when every hand taught by the school of the braided thread stands entered upon it — wholeness is a plug, and the basin has three days of winter and no way to out-hurry arithmetic. The count returns to Rekhak Vahni, whose first finding breaks the Office's instrument on the record: the Loom's last knot, spent by the basin to stop one requisition, has stopped the counting-cord from inside the Office's chest — the cord is dead — so the count is by mouth now, line by line, and twenty-two of the sixty-three hands on the roll have no names.

The blank note leaves Kessa's box and is read aloud: "Do not enrol me" is the owner's standing no, on file, older than the roll — clause two contains nothing about the word being yes. At dusk the four notes return after eighteen days and add a fifth — and on the step, a knot tied the old way: it is not a song. It is directions. The keeper follows them out of the basin, past the Office's purchased eyes (Rekhak, honest at any fee, a coat at a fixed interval), to the first door: behind the low gate, in the sluice gallery, a wall of seasons of Office seals that have always gone around one small door, never across — the log plate reading void, no hands. The only true line the Office ever filed. The archive: the school kept its own roll all along, in thread, its own way — the twenty-two on it by length; the oldest rod beginning with a year-knot; and by the door, a swept iron hook: the step-knot was tied from here. The Office's reader enters lawfully, reads by craft, and prices the reading at an ask, properly made.

The ask is entered of record (the fee: you become a line in somebody's count), and the verdict lands flat as weather: the school's roll is whole — as of the last finished teaching. Ira assembles the basin's most dangerous sentence alone: he never said whose roll — the postponement is over. She reads the definition back to its poster in the square, verbatim; the clerk's hand rises to take the paper down and drops (posted means posted); and the summons comes out of the coat — Room Nine, where the amendment (the roll means the Office's roll) waits for a counter-signature the register will not give (the register does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err). He files her refusal in his own book — keepers err; rolls open — and the register crosses its line: a second Office.

Nandi comes to the rods and reads the year: the Office's year-grammar, in an older hand, from before the Office's year one — this is my grammar's grandmother; the Office learned to count here — and the first hand the school ever taught is signed with the founder's whorl: the mark on the note in Ira's register. Ira asks in thread (a turn and a loop left open) and the answer comes at dawn as two knots: the direction retied, and an entry-knot left loose, its long end running down-lane — loose is a condition; nobody tightens it but the book that counts her fair.

The chapter closes the way a knot ends: the three mid-thread hands hold steady and are tied in by Nima's hands; Nandi's price is paid to the hour (enter me whole); and the founder's entry is tightened by the last hand it was waiting for — the daughter's. The whorl enters beside the year. In the square, the clerk names the machine to the whole basin and stops the clock with it: the count is suspended, not postponed — the founder stands entered on the school's roll and against entry on the Office's, by her word, on file, older than their paper. The register takes the street's paper. On the step at dusk the four-note sentence is whole again. The chapter's smallest-caption ladder — knock / ask him / read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in — lands flat: "The roll is whole, keeper." And the number that closes the arc's forward clock, unspoken on-panel, is the one that does not fit: year nineteen.
