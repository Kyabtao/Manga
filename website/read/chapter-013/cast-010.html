IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the chapter's last entry)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Nobody owns the table."

Page 010 note: attends her own counter's reading in silence — a basin voice reads the machine's paper — and says one line of record: the terms accepted, nothing withdrawn, nothing regretted. Receives the first weekly delivery with both hands. The chapter's last entry is a list of the whole state of things: terms, delivery, hours struck, the three still unnamed, the mother still off the record — and a counter where paper goes to be read, and nobody owns the table.

KESSA (the till; the benediction of the cord)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"We will keep the count anyway, keeper. Ours is older."

Page 010 note: unties ONE knot of the oil-cord — three remain, and a winter in them: nothing learned in fear gets unlearned because the fear moved on. In the final beat her hand rests on the TILL, not the box — the safety the chapter earned. The till does not throw away a good counting.

THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (seventh appearance — the readers of record)

No full sheet; Ch. 011–013. The elder reads, the younger points the line: the machine's acceptance read aloud at the counter of record BY THE BASIN.

The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Read: the machine's paper, aloud, at the counter of record /
"So it shall be filed."

Page 010 note: the arc lands: queue (P001), fetchers (P004), readers (P010) — the institution completing itself. The reading is halting and clear, not eloquent. Half the square no longer needs it read; they walk strangers through the knot-rows anyway.

THE COUNT-HOUSE HAND (one-off — routine, which is the true victory)

No full sheet; Ch. 013. Not the runner: a lesser clerk — nerveless, efficient, mildly bored — passing the first weekly delivery through the window-frame. The machine intends this as ROUTINE.

The Count-House Hand / Manav / Agnikhand / dust and lunch-warm wax / counts for wages /
Deliver: the machine's weeks, to the keeper's grammar /
"Sign here. Both copies. Every week."

Page 010 note: the revolution arrives looking like clerking, because it IS clerking. A bored stranger on a schedule is the strongest grammar there is — the terms are not a truce anymore; they are a route.
