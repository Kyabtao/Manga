Chapter 013 — Page 002 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one. English-only pages (AUDIT R13.22).

IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the register's hand)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Enforceable — and unbindable."

Page 002 note: rules the lease of record — a finding, not a favour: the paper binds names, the tenancy holds hands, and the register so finds. Then answers the stamp question without becoming the Office: the register certifies, it does not stamp. Sets her own limit into the register — one certificate per disputed debt, no interest, void at the count's end, every one signed with her name.

KESSA (the till; the appraisal and the bridle)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"The paper is real, keeper. What it claims does not exist."

Page 002 note: crowns the ruling with the till's verdict — genuine stock, genuine seal, would not lend a cup of water against it — and names the complainant as a name that never signs its own paper. Then, when the counter-book mints its first credit, she bridges it the same hour: a keep is not a mint. Certify the debt, never create it. Her box stays shut; her hand goes on the lid.

REKHAK VAHNI (the costs, delivered)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/rekhak-vahni.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/rekhak-vahni-ref.png

Rekhak Vahni / Manav / Agnikhand / cold-white Sight thread / deep debt /
Audit: reveal target's Kind, thread colour and debt level; each use adds 2 debt to self /
"You ruled before noon; they answered before dusk."

Page 002 note: back at the counter at end of shift with the Office's answer on the wood: an inspection demand filed on the school's alley room, and the amendment file sent for — dusted off, not fired. He delivers the bill the way he delivers weather: quietly, and on time.

THE CLAIMANT (the certificate's first holder)

No full sheet; Ch. 013. The worn yard hand from Page 001, back for his answer.

The Claimant / Manav / Agnikhand / worn brown, unpaid / owed a winter /
Ask: what a paper is worth when its office stops /
"Then his no is on file too?"

Page 002 note: the stamp question gets its ripened answer through him — the register cannot stamp, so it certifies, and he carries the first keeper's certificate to a yard gate. The foreman honours it before last light; the basin learns the counter-book's paper is worth something by watching him walk home with weight in his sack.

THE FOREMAN (off-panel — the honour)

No full sheet; Ch. 013. Never on-panel this page.

The Foreman / Manav / Agnikhand / ledger-brown, cautious / pays against paper /
Honour: accept the certificate or defy a record by name /
"No stamp, no pay — unless the no is on file, too."

Page 002 note: makes the page's quiet third decision: he honours the certificate before the last light goes, because defying a record — in writing, with his name where the keeper's is — is more expensive than paying. His honour is the moment the counter-book mints credit.

THE LANDLORD (a name on the docket — never on-panel)

No full sheet; Ch. 013. Does not appear. May never appear.

The Landlord / Manav? / Agnikhand / ink borrowed, thread unknown / owed nothing he signed for /
Lend: a name to a paper; never his own hand /
"A name that has never signed its own paper."

Page 002 note: the lease's complainant, filed proper under a name Kessa knows belongs to somebody else's man. Front or fool — the register does not need to know which, and the page does not say. The chapter's villain remains procedure; the landlord is procedure's glove.

THE THREE ENTERED HANDS (unnamed — now armour)

No full sheet; Ch. 012–013. Entered by knot, never by name. Referenced this page; not shown.

The Three Entered Hands / Manavkin, Kshudra, Manav / Agnikhand / knot-entered, no names /
Hold: possession by knot, entry by knot /
"No names are written on it that the Office itself never entered."

Page 002 note: Page 001 made their namelessness the lease's leverage; Page 002 converts it to armour. The register's finding — the tenancy holds hands, not names — means the Office's own grammar protects the room the school holds. Still unnamed. Still three.
