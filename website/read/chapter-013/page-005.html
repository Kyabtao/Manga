Chapter title: The Second Office Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the stall and the fuel-yard gate, one day from owed answer to paid debt Page type: The answer at the gate — 7 panels; the register pays its debt in writing, the foreman honours at a price, and the clerk's trap lands clean

Beats: the answer written in duplicate — the register's owed answer leaves the counter before dusk: one copy to the claimant, one to the yard gate, both under the keeper's knot and seal; the grammar of the answer — a definition rules forward; it cannot reach backward over a record already entered — the certificate stands until the count ends; the gate receives the record — the foreman, on-panel for the first time, reads the register's paper long, in his own yard's light; the payment in view — he honours the certificate before his men, and names the gate's rule after it; the foreman's price — at the counter in person at dusk: his name goes no further than the counter — can the book keep a name quiet? Entered, not read aloud: protection of record, granted, the cost standing visible; the trap — the Office answers by fresh notice: certificates valid only when presented for endorsement at the Office's counter — the counter that is shut; the basin wobbles once more; the trap read aloud — Ira enters it of record by the counted lamp. The register will answer in the morning.

This page: a gate, a payment, and a trap drafted in clean grammar — and the second Office learning what it costs to be worth joining.

Chapter 013 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent — this is the page that wanted it: had the foreman defaulted, the chain would have stopped here. He paid. The budget holds.

PANEL 1 — Medium, morning, the answer written in duplicate (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the stall, morning. Two papers flat on the counter, one already sealed; Ira finishing the second in the register's cleanest grammar, the open register beside for entry. Kessa trims the lamp's wick with small scissors, exact. The queue at the rail, quiet — the whole street knows what today is.

Image: The register owed an answer by dusk. The register pays its debts before noon.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): A register that lets its answers go overdue is just a woman with opinions. Two copies. One for the hand that asked. One for the gate that wobbled.

PANEL 2 — Close, the grammar of the answer (~13%)

Camera: Close: the sealed copy flat under the lamp — rows of knot-marks and tally groups, one wax seal, and at the foot a small careful knot: the keeper's own sign, pressed into the wax.

Image: The legal engine, stated in the Office's own grammar because it is the only grammar that bites.

IRA: (of record, to the queue, before the runner takes it) Entered before, answered against after. A definition rules forward — it cannot reach backward over a record already entered. The certificate stands until the count ends. They may rule what their stamp means. They may not rule what was already witnessed.

PANEL 3 — Wide, midday, the gate receives the record (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the fuel-yard gate, midday glare off split wood. The grey runner holds up the register's sealed paper — and the foreman takes it with both hands: a broad, careful man, heavy jaw, leather apron over a wool coat, gate-keys at his belt, a gate-keeper's face that gives away nothing early. The claimant stands to one side with his certificate in his fist. Men pause mid-load to watch.

Image: The Office sent its definition to this gate yesterday. Today the gate receives a better paper.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): He reads the way gate-keepers read — slow, twice, like a man checking a knot he will have to trust his whole weight to.

PANEL 4 — Medium, the payment in view (~15%)

Camera: Medium: inside the yard's mouth: the foreman hands the claimant a full measure-sack — the weight visible in the claimant's arms — in front of the watching men, the certificate held up between them a moment like a receipt.

FOREMAN: (carrying, for the yard to hear) Paid against the keeper's paper. It stands till the count ends — that is this gate's rule now. Any man who argues it can argue with me first.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): He did not wait to be sure. He waited to be seen — and then he made the gate's rule on the spot, the way I made mine: out loud, where the people who need it can hold him to it.

PANEL 5 — Medium-close, dusk, the foreman's price (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: the stall at dusk, the counted lamp low. The foreman at the counter IN PERSON, cap in both hands, leaning in, voice down. Kessa still at the till, missing nothing.

FOREMAN: (quiet) I will honour every paper that comes off this counter. But my name is on a record now, against the Office's own word. A gate is not a wall, keeper. When their clerk comes for names, mine is the easy one. Can your book keep a name quiet?

IRA: (level, of record) Entered, not read aloud. Protection of record: the register holds your name the way it holds the basin's debts — usable when you say, not before. That is the best my book does. It is not nothing.

CAPTION (smallest): Protect the name.

PANEL 6 — Wide, night, the trap (~14%)

Camera: Wide, night: the square black except for a ring of hand-lamps at the hatch board. A fresh long notice, one seal, going up under Office escort — and the basin reading it by lamp-flame: certificates valid only when presented for endorsement at the Office's public counter. The claimant at the ring's edge, his paper in his fist, staring.

Image: The trap, stated plainly: honour the paper — after a journey that cannot be made. The only endorsement counter in the basin is the one they shut.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): There is the clerk's best work. He learned from my certificate. He did not forbid it — he gave it a road, and the road ends at a door he boarded up. Honour on paper. Refusal in fact. And the basin left holding a promise with no road to keep it.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the trap read aloud — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

Camera: Medium-close: the stall by the counted flame — the oil-cord's four knots hanging by the till. Kessa lays the fresh notice on the counter; Ira reads it once, then writes. The queue watches the pen the way it watched the ruling.

IRA: (of record, entering) Entered: the endorsement demand. A valid paper, an impossible road. Entered: the answer is owed the basin by morning.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): They built a door out of a rule, and they think that settles it. But a counter is not a place, keeper. I keep having to tell people this.

CAPTION (smallest): Then we open a window.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the register pays its debt first; the foreman pays at a price; the trap lands and is read aloud. The page's spine is credibility: the register said "by dusk tomorrow, by name, in writing" — so the answer goes out before noon, in duplicate, sealed. Never let Ira gloat about being early; the caption frames it as bookkeeping, because it is.
  • The foreman is on-panel for the first time — keep him CAREFUL. Broad, heavy-jawed, leather apron, gate-keys; he reads twice; he makes the gate's rule out loud because that is how gates hold; he comes to the counter in person at dusk because names matter more than pride. He is not a convert and not a rebel — he is a gate-keeper choosing which paper his gate will trust, and then buying protection for the choice. Do not make him warm; make him solid.
  • Protection of record, granted, cost visible: "entered, not read aloud" is the second institution-building beat after the mint-limit. The register gains a new function (sealed names) and a new dependency (people worth protecting). Do not resolve the tension — the register now holds names the Office would pay to read. Leave that on the books.
  • The trap must be stated plainly and left armed. The clerk's move: certificates valid only on endorsement at the Office's own counter — the counter that is shut. Do not have anyone solve it on-panel; Ira enters it and owes the basin an answer by morning. The smallest caption (Then we open a window.) is the turn — the endorsement window in the stall's shutter is Page 006's opening image. DO NOT fire it this page: no shutter opened, no window built, only the sentence.
  • The chain-stop check is explicit this page: had the foreman defaulted, the register's credibility would have broken and the chain-stop budget would have spent itself here. He paid. Note it in the captions' confidence, not on-panel: the budget holds at ONE, unspent.
