Chapter title: The Spring Term Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the school's table, the hatch board, the window, and the step at dusk Page type: The licensed teacher — 7 panels; a purchase declined, a licence that was addressed to women one by one, and a school that answers a split with a ledger line

Beats: the ruling — the school takes the licence, answers it in the margin, and gives it back: we will teach the Office's grammar unsealed and unlicensed, and we will keep our count; a permission that forbids counting is not a permission, it is a purchase, and the school does not sell the one thing it has never sold; returned read — the licence goes back the way everything goes back: read, answered in four knots and the school's plain cord, nailed to the board in public with the answer on it; the knife in the wording — the Office licensed TEACHERS, not schools, one woman at a time, so each of them may choose: one of the school's own counting-lesson women has already accepted, and stands at the benches with a sealed copy in her hand; her reason — lawful work and lawful bread after a winter of teaching for nothing, and she is right; the school's rule — the register enters her in the school's book, the same book the licence forbids, as a member in good standing, and reads the entry aloud; the first sealed lesson — she teaches the Office's grammar to eleven children on the school's own step, off the school's own benches, with the school's book open in the window behind her; the split — nothing is expelled and nothing is resolved: the school now contains its own licensed teacher, and the count it keeps proves it.

This page: a purchase declined, and a school that answers disloyalty with a ledger line.

Chapter 015 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear; the sill's knots are untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper stays folded, unopened, unmentioned.

PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the ruling at the school's table (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the archive table with the licence laid on it and the school's women on the benches along the wall, cords in their laps; Nandi at the table's head with her hands flat on either side of the leaf; the counting-lesson reader standing with the schoool's plain cord ready; Ira at the room's edge with the register. The lamp is not lit; the window does the work.

Image: A school ruling on a purchase, with no keeper in the chair.

NANDI: (flat, to the benches) A permission that forbids counting is not a permission. It is a purchase, and it is priced at the one thing this school has never sold. We will teach their grammar. We will not sell our book for the right to teach it.

THE READER: (carefully) Then we answer it?

NANDI: We answer it and we give it back. Four knots, the way we answered the charter — and then it goes on the board where the whole quarter can read what we said.

PANEL 2 — Close, the answer in the margin (~14%)

Camera: Close: the licence's margin under the reader's fingers — four plain knots tied through the paper's corner with the school's own cord, the Office's seal untouched at the foot, the border-fine clause visible as a hairline under it. Beside the leaf, the school's thick old book, open, its dry knot-marks on the page.

Image: The same four knots the school used on the charter, tied again to decline a purchase.

IRA: (of record, writing it as it is tied) Entered: the licence answered and returned. Entered: the answer, in four knots and the school's plain cord — the grammar, yes; the count, no.

THE READER: (tying the last knot, dry) The Office will read this in a clerk's hand and call it insolence. It is not insolence. It is the shortest way to say a thing we have already said once.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the answer nailed up (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the hatch board in wet noon light — the licence pinned up beside the school's spring term, its answer-cord hanging loose, the seal and the four knots facing the street; a small crowd reading it, two of them mouthing the knots; the reader stands beside the board with her arms folded, not reading it out, because the street can read it now.

Image: A refusal published in the same grammar as the offer — and in the same place.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): They nailed it up at noon with the answer on it and let the street read it without help. That was the first time the quarter read an Office paper by itself, in Office grammar, and got the joke.

A MAN IN THE CROWD: (slowly, tracing the four knots with a finger) Grammar, yes. Count, no. That is what it says. That is what it says, plain.

PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the knife in the wording (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: the benches inside the side room — the women seated, cords in laps, and one of them standing: a counting-lesson woman in the same undyed clothes, holding a small sealed leaf of her own in both hands, its corner cordless and its Office seal unbroken. Around her the others have turned; nobody speaks; one woman's hand has stopped halfway through a knot.

Image: The licence was addressed to women one by one, so the school's split arrives from inside its own benches.

THE LICENSED WOMAN: (steady, not defiant) It came to me by name. Not to the school — to me, and to eight others, each on her own leaf. I read it twice. I am to be paid for teaching, lawfully, in an Office hand, with a seal on my lessons.

(she looks at the benches)

I taught all winter for nothing. If that is a betrayal, then it is one I can feed my sister with.

PANEL 5 — Close, the entry (~14%)

Camera: Close: the school's thick old book open on the table, a fresh line being laid in by Nandi's own hand in the old grammar — the licensed woman's entry, with the Office's leaf laid beside the page as the thing being entered, and the empty name-space of the protection visible further up the same page; the four-knot answer-cord still tied through the licence.

Image: The book the licence forbids, used to enter the woman the licence licensed.

NANDI: (writing, flat) You are counted here. That has never depended on what you carry in your hand — it depends on where you sit, and you have not moved.

(she sets the pen down)

Keeper. Read it aloud, so nobody in this room can say later that it was done quietly.

IRA: (of record, reading it out to the room) Entered: one of ours is licensed by the Office, and remains a member of this school in good standing. Entered: our book does not care what is in her hand. Counted, not expelled.

PANEL 6 — Medium, the school's rule (~14%)

Camera: Medium: the side room, the benches — the licensed woman has sat down again in her own place, the sealed leaf on her knee; the women around her have gone back to their cords, one of them leaning to correct the licensed woman's grip on a knot out of pure habit; Nandi at the table with her book open; Kessa in the doorway with the till's slate under her arm, having heard it all.

Image: The split absorbed by the ordinary: afternoon, benches, cords, a corrected grip.

KESSA: (from the doorway, dry) I have chalked nine licensed leaves and one sealed lesson into the school column and it still says carrying. Nobody is paying her either, you understand. The Office pays her — the till does not.

NANDI: (not looking up) Then the till has nothing to forgive her for. Sit down, Kessa. The second hour is starting.

PANEL 7 — Medium-wide, dusk, the first sealed lesson — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

Camera: Medium-wide: the step at dusk, the school's benches carried out crooked on the drying flags, eleven children seated with slates, and the licensed woman standing before them with her sealed leaf nailed to the wall beside her and a lesson-cord in her hand; behind her, through the open window, the school's own book is visible on the table, open, its page full of old knot-marks. The other women teach their own groups two paces away, unsealed and unlicensed. Nobody stands apart.

Image: Two lessons on one step: one sealed and paid, one unsealed and counted, and the same children in both.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): She taught the Office's grammar at dusk with the seal on the wall behind her, and two paces away the others taught the old grammar with nothing on the wall at all, and the children went from bench to bench because children do not know they are supposed to choose.

IRA: (of record, the day closed) Entered: one licence declined by the school, one accepted by a woman, both entered in the same book. Entered: the school now contains its own licensed teacher. The count proves it, which is precisely what the licence was written to prevent.

CAPTION (smallest): Count her too.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the refusal (a purchase declined, publicly, in the Office's own grammar); the split (the licence was addressed to teachers, not schools — and one of the school's own women is right to take it); the ledger line (counted, not expelled). The page has no villain in it. The Office wins a legal point and loses the thing the point was for; the woman wins bread and keeps her place; the school keeps its book by spending nothing but ink.
  • The refusal must not be dramatic. No tearing, no burning, no speech on a bench. Four knots and a plain cord, the answer tied through the corner, and the leaf nailed back up beside the term so the street can read it without help. The pleasure of Panel 3 is that the quarter reads Office grammar by itself and gets the joke.
  • The licensed woman is the page's hardest note and must be played straight. Give her the reason and only the reason: the leaf came to her by name, the work would be lawful and paid, and she taught all winter for nothing, and she has a sister to feed. No defiance in the art, no tears, no triumphant framing from the benches; one woman's hand stops halfway through a knot, and that is the room's whole reaction.
  • The rule is a ledger line, not a speech. Nandi writes her in, then hands the reading to Ira because the register's entry has to be heard by everyone — counted, not expelled. The art's proof of the rule is Panel 6's ordinary: benches, cords, a corrected grip, Kessa in the doorway with her slate.
  • The final panel is two lessons on one step and must not stage a contest. The sealed lesson on the wall, the unsealed ones two paces away, the same children drifting between them because children do not know they are supposed to choose. Do not draw a line down the step. Do not draw the women facing off. The school's book open in the window behind them is the only comment.
  • The imperative ladder takes its next run: Count her too.
