Chapter 015 — Page 002 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one. English-only pages (AUDIT R13.22).

NANDI (the reader — the school answers for itself)

Full sheet (Ch. 006): ../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md · Art: ../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png

Nandi / Manav / Agnikhand / sight-white, lens-borne / reads, and is paid /
Read: thread-years at the price of an ask, properly made /
"The Office may own the grammar. The count stays here."

Page 002 note: answers the charter because the school is what is being bought — not the keeper, not the till. Her division is dry and practical: five clauses accepted (lessons, seals, money, licensed hours, Office grammar taught to the quarter's children at the Office's cost) and one refused (the school's book as a roll). Two hands, two books: one on the charter, one on her own. She states the collision in two sentences and does not try to solve it.

IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper at the edge of the room)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Nothing is entered yet. Hear the last clause twice."

Page 002 note: has the charter taken down and read where papers are read, holds the mother at the window until the sixth clause has been heard twice, writes the school's answer of record without ruling on it — and closes the page by naming the wheel: the Office's best paper and the register's best protection want the same empty space.

KESSA (the till — the mechanism, named quietly)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"They give you a door and a bill for the hinges, and they make the door so good that saying no is the unnatural thing."

Page 002 note: names the courtesy's mechanism under her breath and then, at the door, the symmetry of the outcome: the machine will call it a victory because it got a school; the basin will call it one because it kept a book; both are right, which is how a real ruling looks. No chalk this page — the columns are already closed.

THE READER (the counting-lesson women — the charter read in full)

No full sheet; Ch. 011–015. The school's reader takes the charter off the board flat, seal up, and reads all six clauses at the window, the empty one last and twice.

The Reader / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owes lessons, and is owed them /
Read: everything delivered, aloud, at the counter of record /
"Six clauses. Five of them gifts. One of them a list of names with nothing on it yet."

Page 002 note: reads the Office's best paper as flatly as she read the Office's worst, which is the counter's whole discipline in one performance. It is she who notices the document's shape before its words — five gifts and a list — and she says so before the crowd can be won by the gifts.

THE WOMAN IN THE SHAWL (the quarter's honest yes)

No full sheet; one-off on this page. A mother in a patched shawl with a nine-year-old daughter who already reads a tally, stepping forward before the reading is finished.

The Woman in the Shawl / Manav / Agnikhand / patched, practical / owes nothing, and wants one thing /
Ask: sealed lessons — the one gift that cannot be taken back for being ours /
"Enter her — she is nine and she can read a tally already."

Page 002 note: the page's fairness test. She is right, her reasoning is sound, and the machine's offer is materially good for her child. The register does not overrule her and the art must not patronise her: the charter's generosity is real, and the page says so out loud before it names the price.

THE MACHINE (off-panel — the charter)

No full sheet; the Office entire. Present this page as one document: six clauses, five gifts, one seal, one budget-cord, and a schedule ruled for names.

The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Charter: buy the school properly — grammar, seals, hours, teachers, money — and take its roll /
"Five of them gifts. One of them a list of names."

Page 002 note: the new grammar from Chapter 014's last page, in full flower: a machine that cannot disown an hour offers to own the institution instead, at real cost to itself, and asks only for the roll. The page never shows an Office interior and never explains the offer's motive; the terms do that work.
