THREADBORN — Chapter 15 — Page 003

Chapter title: The Spring Term Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the hatch board, the stall's window, the step, and the school's table Page type: The withdrawn gift — 7 panels; five clauses narrowed to one licence, the money taken out, a school that teaches in the street anyway, and a condition nobody read to the end

Beats: the reply on the board — a smaller notice in the Office's flat regular hand: the charter withdrawn in five clauses and narrowed to one, a licence; the licence read aloud — the school may teach the Office's grammar, with Office seals and licensed teachers; the Office will NOT pay for it, because an unfiled roll is an uncounted school, and the Office does not fund what it cannot count; the cost lands — the mother from yesterday hears unfunded and understands that the paid lessons her daughter was to have are now a permission and nothing else; the till pays what it can — Kessa reopens the school column as a carrying column and prices the gap out loud; the school teaches anyway — Nandi takes the licence and teaches the term on the step, in the street, for nothing, with the counting-lesson women teaching unpaid, which the record says plainly because an unpaid teacher is the exact thing the till existed to prevent; the condition — the licence's own last line, found by the reader: a licensed teacher may teach the Office's grammar and may not keep a count in any other grammar; the choice — the licence is a permission to teach and a prohibition on counting; the school must hold one and abandon the other, and the page does not say which.

This page: the machine's economy as a knife — a gift withdrawn is cheaper than a request refused. And a school that answers a prohibition by teaching in the street.

Chapter 015 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear; the sill's knots are untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper stays folded, unopened, unmentioned.

PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the reply on the board (~14%)

Camera: Wide: the hatch board in thin morning light, the charter's pegs empty and a much smaller notice pinned where it hung — one leaf, one seal, no cord, in the Office's flat regular hand. The woman in the patched shawl from yesterday stands at the front of a small crowd with her daughter's hand in hers, already there, already waiting. Two of the counting-lesson women are untying the notice; the rest of the quarter is still arriving.

Image: A reply so short the board looks emptier than it did before the Office said anything.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Four days of generosity came off the board in the night and left one leaf behind, the size of a lease. No cord. That was the first thing anybody noticed: the budget-cord was gone.

THE MOTHER: (to the reader, before she has even read it) Is it the school? Say if it is the school.

PANEL 2 — Close, the licence (~15%)

Camera: Close: the small leaf held flat in the reader's hands in daylight — one column of the Office's regular knots, the seal at the foot, the paper otherwise blank, and where the charter's budget-cord hung there is nothing at all: a clean edge, no eyelet, no cord. The absence is the document's loudest feature.

Image: Five gifts narrowed to one permission, with the money gone out of it.

THE READER: (reading the shape, flat) One clause. One seal. No cord. Yesterday this paper had six clauses and a purse on it; today it has a permission and nothing else.

IRA: (of record) Read the clause. Then read what is not on it, out loud, because that is the part they will not say.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the licence read aloud (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the stall's window, the reader on the sill's step with the leaf up, the crowd close in the wet morning; the woman in the shawl and her daughter at the front, the girl's face turned up to the paper; Kessa at the till with the school's column open under her hand; Ira at the window's edge, entering.

Image: Permission without payment, read at the same window that read the six clauses.

THE READER: (flat, unhurried) The Office licenses the school to teach its grammar, with Office seals and licensed teachers. The Office will not fund the school, because an unfiled roll is an uncounted school, and the Office does not fund what it cannot count.

(she lowers the leaf; and then, of record)

The licence carries no money. There is no line in it for a teacher, a bench, a slate, or a child's noon.

THE MOTHER: (quietly, and it carries) So the lessons are still there. They are just not paid for.

PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the till pays what it can (~14%)

Camera: Medium-close: the till's lid up, Kessa's grey-gloved hand ruling a fresh line down the school's column with chalk and turning it into a carrying column; the slate propped where the queue can see it; the woman in the shawl's hand still in her daughter's, close to the counter. Kessa does not look up while she prices it.

Image: The column that exists because the Office withdrew one.

KESSA: (chalking, flat) The school column reopens. Carrying, not funded: benches, slates, oil, and the noon bread. Nine lines in, four paid, five carried — now eleven carried and no new money coming.

(she sets the chalk down and looks up)

I will pay what this till has. I will not pretend it is enough for a whole term of children. If the quarter wants the term to run, the quarter carries the rest.

IRA: (of record) Entered: the licence taken, the money withdrawn, the column reopened as carrying. Entered: the school will run on what the street puts in it, and the street has been asked.

PANEL 5 — Medium, the school teaches anyway (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the stall's step and the street before it at the second hour after noon — benches carried out and set crooked on the wet flags, a plank across two barrels for a table, nine women and eleven children on them with cords and chalk-slates, and Nandi standing before them with the lens put away and her hands empty, teaching. The counting-lesson women teach in pairs, one holding a cord up, one correcting a grip. The awning drips. Nobody is being paid.

Image: A licensed school with no money, holding its term in the street in front of the counter that asked for it.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Second hour after noon, on the step, in the mud, with the licence nailed to the wall beside them. Nine women taught eleven children and were paid nothing. I have entered unpaid labour in this book before, and every time before it was a thing done to somebody. This is the first time it is a thing we chose.

NANDI: (to the benches, flat, a teacher's flatness) The Office has given us its grammar and kept its money. Look at the knot and not at the purse. Hold the cord where I hold it. That is the whole lesson and it is the same lesson it was yesterday.

PANEL 6 — Close, the last line (~14%)

Camera: Close: the licence leaf held again, this time with the reader's finger at its very foot — a short row of small knots below the seal, so fine and so regular that it reads as part of the seal's border; the reader's thumbnail under it; Kessa's shadow across the paper. In the background, out of focus, the street school with its cords and slates.

Image: The condition, small enough to be mistaken for a border, that takes the book.

THE READER: (slow, and it costs her) There is one more line. Under the seal. A licensed teacher may teach the Office's grammar, and may not keep a count in any other grammar.

(silence; the street school goes on behind her)

KESSA: (after a moment, very quietly) Ah. There it is. It was never about the money.

IRA: (of record) Entered: the licence forbids the school's own book. To hold the licence is to give up the count; to keep the count is to lose the licence. The Office has bought, for nothing, the one thing it has wanted since the terms were accepted.

PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the choice — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

Camera: Medium-close: the school's table in the lamplit side room, late — the licence laid flat on one side, the school's thick old book open on the other, its blank name-space and wax seal visible; Nandi seated between them with her thick hands flat on the wood, not on either paper, looking at neither. The benches are empty. The window shows thaw water dripping. In the doorway, Ira, not coming in.

Image: A permission to teach and a prohibition on counting, and a school that has to choose without either side being wrong.

NANDI: (to the room, which is only Ira, and flat as always) If we hold the licence, we teach the Office's grammar and we stop being the school that keeps a count. If we keep the count, we teach in the street with no seal on anything, and every licensed teacher in this quarter is teaching unlawfully by the second week.

(she looks at the book, and not at the licence)

The old school has been unlawful before. It has never once been uncounted.

IRA: (of record, from the doorway) Entered: no decision yet. Entered: the choice is the school's, and the school has not made it.

CAPTION (smallest): Teach it anyway.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the withdrawal (five gifts → one permission, the cord gone); the teaching (a school running in the street on nothing, by choice); the condition (the licence takes the count, not the money). The page's method is absence: the missing budget-cord in P2, the absent payment in P3, the absent wages in P5, and the tiny clause mistaken for a border in P6. Nothing on the page is dramatic except what is not there.
  • The mother is the page's heart and must be handled without condescension. She heard free lessons yesterday and today hears permission; her line — so the lessons are still there; they are just not paid for — is the whole economy of the page in one sentence. Do not give her a reaction shot of despair; give her a practical face, because she is about to enrol her daughter in a school on a step.
  • Kessa's price is a column, not a lament. She reopens the school's column as carrying, reads the numbers out, and says plainly that the till cannot fund a term — and that the quarter has been asked to carry the rest. The record's line is exact: the school runs on what the street puts in it.
  • Panel 5 is the page's image and must look like an institution, not a picnic. Benches carried out crooked on wet flags, a plank across two barrels, cords and chalk-slates, the licence nailed to the wall beside them, Nandi teaching with empty hands and no lens. Eleven children, nine women, no money. The drip off the awning is the only music.
  • **The condition must be found, not announced.** Small regular knots under the seal, so fine that they read as the seal's border; the reader's thumbnail under them; her voice slowing. Kessa's it was never about the money is the page's cold turn, and it should be quiet enough to be missed.
  • The final panel refuses to choose. Nandi's hands flat on the wood between the two papers, her eyes on neither, her two-sentence statement of the dilemma, and her last line — the old school has been unlawful before; it has never once been uncounted — which is a leaning, not a ruling. Ira stands in the doorway and enters that the school has not decided. The caption does the rest.
  • The imperative ladder takes its next run: Teach it anyway.
