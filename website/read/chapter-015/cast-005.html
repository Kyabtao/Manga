Chapter 015 — Page 005 Cast

Everyone on the page. Card-game line format per series-bible/04-card-game-notes.md (Name / Kind / Sector / Thread colour / Debt tendency / Signature ability / One-line hook) — every entry on this page carries one. English-only pages (AUDIT R13.22).

THE LICENSED WOMAN (one of ours, paid — and now the Office's clerk)

Full sheet: no separate sheet; Ch. 011–015. Art reference: ../../chapter-015/images/page-004.png (fourth tier).

The Licensed Woman / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / paid, and holding paper /
Teach: the Office's grammar, sealed — and hold the roll she must give /
"I am their clerk with your children's names in my hand, and I am standing on this step in the rain teaching them for a coin. Those are both true."

Page 005 note: the page's spine. She takes the first coin the Office has paid into the basin, is given the roll-slate with eleven bare lines on it, and tells the parents exactly what the seal buys and what it costs. She guides a child's fingers to write her own first name-line, accepts two refusals without argument, and teaches all eleven anyway — nine sealed, two not. She is not the Office's instrument and not the school's victim; she is a teacher holding two kinds of paper.

KESSA (the till — the coin that will not go in the drawer)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png

Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"It doesn't buy a lesson. It buys a habit — that the Office may set money on our step and be thanked for it."

Page 005 note: rules the coin not ours on her own slate, leaves the drawer open and empty and the coin on the counter, and never touches it. Her accounting is the page's immune system: no debt, no claim, no entry against anyone — and therefore a gift she is more afraid of than an invoice.

IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the counter's copy)

Full sheet (Ch. 001): ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md · Art: ../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png

Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "This book counts the class and not the roll."

Page 005 note: asks the question the reader is asking (so it is a gift?), then does the thing the page is for: copies the roll into the register line by line — nine matched to the Office's paper, two marked with a bare gap and a private notch — so that the quarter holds its own copy of everything the machine now knows. Closes the page by looking at the empty name-space she has kept empty for two seasons.

THE WOMAN IN THE SHAWL (the first name, written by the child)

No full sheet; third appearance (Ch. 015 P.002, P.003, P.005). A mother in a patched shawl with a nine-year-old daughter who reads a tally.

The Woman in the Shawl / Manav / Agnikhand / patched, practical / owes nothing, and wants one kept promise /
Ask: a lesson that cannot be taken back in the night /
"Food in a bad winter. A roof that stayed. A brother who came back. This is the first thing I can promise her that a clerk cannot take off her."

Page 005 note: the page's heart, in three sentences with no rhetoric in them. She holds the slate steady while her daughter writes her own name into an Office roll, because a sealed lesson is the first promise she has ever been able to keep. The brother stays unnamed and unremarked; he is a grief, not a hook.

THE GRANDMOTHER (the refusal that is also right)

No full sheet; new on Page 005. An old woman of the quarter, courteous, unimpressed by seals, with a granddaughter in the unsealed class.

The Grandmother / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, weathered / owes nothing, and will not sign /
Refuse: decline a name from an Office hand, and keep the child in the class /
"Put her name in their hand and she is theirs for good — and I have seen what *for good* means in that grammar."

Page 005 note: speaks the chapter's own objection in a courteous voice, on the same step, in the same hour as the first acceptance. She does not leave the school and does not lose her granddaughter's place: nine sealed, two not, the class intact — the school's rule (counted, not expelled) working from the parents' side this time.

THE ERRAND-BOY (the Office's hands, hooded)

No full sheet; one page only. A boy in plain Office grey, hood down over his face, delivering a purse and a receipt for a wage in bad weather.

The Errand-Boy / — / Agnikhand / Office grey, hooded / delivers, and is paid by another hand /
Deliver: set down what the machine will not put in a letter, and take the slip back /
"For the lesson. There is a slip with it. The slip has to come back."

Page 005 note: the Office's only body on the page, and deliberately not the grey clerk — no name, no face, no menace, one line, a boy in the rain. The page's point is that the machine no longer needs a face to be present; it can be represented by a child with a purse.

THE MACHINE (off-panel — the receipt and the seal)

No full sheet; the Office entire. Present as one stamped purse, one slip reading paid, no claim, and a condition attached to a seal.

The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Pay: coin with no claim attached, in the open, at the end of a lesson /
"A sealed lesson cannot be withdrawn. Only an unfiled one can."

Page 005 note: never appears and never speaks, and does its cleverest thing in the chapter so far: it pays without asking for anything, and the school's own promise (a sealed lesson cannot be withdrawn) does the recruiting. Eleven children's names are now in the Office's grammar, and one of them was written by the child herself.
