THREADBORN — Chapter 15 — Page 006

Chapter title: The Spring Term Arc: III — The Second School Sector: Agnikhand — the market, the counter of record, the step, and the dawn board Page type: The wage bowl — 7 panels; a coin with nowhere to go is spent on the two children the Office will not name, and the quarter pays for what the Office cannot count

Beats: the coin spent — the school's ruling, carried out by Kessa in the open market: two slates, chalk, and noon bread for all eleven, bought with the Office's own coin at a stall that will tell everyone; the receipt kept and sent back with the coin's absence noted on it, spent on children not on your roll; the wage bowl — a chipped bowl on the school's table, started by the two refusing families and filled by the unlicensed benches, for the two lessons the Office does not pay for; the grandmother puts her coin in herself and says she is not paying for the seal, only for the child; the clean arithmetic — the licensed woman is paid twice and, for the first time in the chapter, exactly: the Office for the nine it counts, the quarter for the two it cannot, both entered in one column that finally balances; the Office's answer — at dawn a small notice is nailed beside the licence: the Office will attend the licensed class to verify the roll against the children present, and any child present who is not on the roll will be recorded as taught unlawfully; the last look — the two unsealed children on the step with the slates the Office's coin bought, in a class that is about to be counted.

This page: plain arithmetic as moral argument, and a machine that answers a clever boycott by scheduling an audit.

Chapter 015 budget: ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear; the sill's knots are untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper stays folded, unopened, unmentioned.

PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the market (~15%)

Camera: Wide: the wet market at the first hour — awnings, dripping, handcarts — with Kessa at a chandler's stall holding up the single office coin between two fingers for the stallholder to see, already pointing at two slates and a twist of chalk; a bread-seller's board two stalls along; two porters and a fishwife turning to watch a grey-gloved woman spend money nobody in the street has seen before.

Image: The Office's coin spent as publicly as it was paid, at a stall that will tell everyone by noon.

CAPTION (Ira's voice): Kessa spent it at the chandler's, in the open, at the first hour when the market is fullest — two slates and a twist of chalk for the two children the Office would not write down, and bread for eleven at the next stall. She made the chandler say the price out loud twice.

THE CHANDLER: (squinting at the coin) This is Office money. Where did a counter like yours get Office money?

KESSA: (flat) It was put on our step. Now it is buying chalk. Write the receipt, and write on the receipt what it bought.

PANEL 2 — Close, the receipt and the empty spot (~14%)

Camera: Close: the school's counter — two new slates stacked with a twist of chalk and a wrapped loaf, the market receipt laid on top with a fresh line ruled under it in Kessa's hand; and to the right, the scoured bare patch of wood where the coin stood for four days, now empty.

Image: The absence of the coin written down as carefully as its arrival.

KESSA: (ruling the line, to Ira) Entered: the Office's coin spent. Two slates, one twist of chalk, bread for eleven. Not one penny of it kept. And on the receipt, going back to them with the slip:

(she reads what she has written, flat)

Spent on children not on your roll. Let a clerk read that and find the clause it breaks.

PANEL 3 — Medium, the step, the two slates (~15%)

Camera: Medium: the step — the licensed woman kneeling to put a new slate into the hands of the boy whose father refused the roll, and a second slate into the hands of the grandmother's granddaughter; the two children look at the slates and then at each other; behind them nine sealed children with older, chipped slates, watching; the grandmother stands with her arms folded.

Image: The Office's money in the hands of the two children it refuses to name.

THE GRANDMOTHER: (watching the slate go into her granddaughter's hands, dry) New slates. For the two of them. Paid for with what?

THE LICENSED WOMAN: (not looking up, fitting the boy's fingers round the frame) With the coin the Office set on this step and would not say what it was for. It has been said now.

THE GRANDMOTHER: (after a moment) Then it is the first honest thing that money has done in this quarter.

PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the bowl (~15%)

Camera: Medium-close: the school's scarred table — a chipped clay bowl set in the middle with a handful of small coins in it; the grandmother's hand putting one more in, the father behind her with his own coin ready; the bowl has a hairline crack mended with wire, and a small unlit lamp stands beyond it.

Image: A wage collected in a cracked bowl, from people who have almost nothing, for lessons nobody is paid to give.

THE GRANDMOTHER: (dropping her coin, flat) I am not paying for the seal. I am paying for the child. Understand the difference and we will get on.

THE FATHER: (putting his in, gruff) Two lessons a day for two children the Office will not count. Somebody must pay the woman, and it is not going to be them. It will be us, in petties, from a bowl.

IRA: (quietly, of record) Entered: a wage bowl. Started by two families who refused the roll, filled by the benches that teach without one.

PANEL 5 — Close, the column that balances (~14%)

Camera: Close: Kessa's slate on the counter, ruled into two wage lines — one against the Office's stamped purse, one against the bowl — with the totals ticked at the foot of each and a single chalk line drawn under both; the bowl itself sits at the slate's edge, and the empty stamped purse beside it, both open, both shown.

Image: Two wages, one column, and the first total in the chapter that is honest.

KESSA: (to Ira, hard and pleased) Look at it. Nine counted, two not; nine paid by them, two paid by us; and the column balances. I have kept this counter nineteen years and I have never once seen a column balance with the Office on it.

IRA: Does that make it fair?

KESSA: (dry, chalking the line under both totals) It makes it clear. Fair is a word for people who have not had to do the sums.

PANEL 6 — Medium-close, two wages in two hands (~14%)

Camera: Medium-close: the licensed woman at the counter, holding the Office's stamped purse in one hand and the chipped bowl in the other, both raised slightly, her face unreadable; Kessa behind the till with her arms folded, watching her; the school's window behind shows the step and the children at their slates.

Image: The school's teacher with the machine's wage in one hand and the quarter's in the other, weighing nothing.

THE LICENSED WOMAN: (looking at the two, quietly) Nine lessons paid by a hand I will never see, and two paid by a cracked bowl from people who have nothing. I know which of these two I will be thinking about at the end of the term.

KESSA: (flat) Take them both. A teacher who refuses a wage because she does not like the payer is a teacher who makes her pupils pay for her pride. Put the bowl money in your sleeve and the Office money in your shoe, and teach eleven.

PANEL 7 — Wide, dawn, the notice, and the two with slates — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

Camera: Wide: the board at dawn in thin rain — a new small notice nailed beside the licence and the term-cord, its seal still bright, a thin queue already reading it; in the foreground the step, where the two unsealed children sit with the new slates on their knees beside the nine, all eleven waiting for the second hour; the licensed woman stands before them with her lesson-cord, and two paces away Nandi has stopped with her lens half-raised, reading the new notice over the children's heads.

Image: The Office answers the boycott with an audit, and the two children hold the slates its own coin bought.

IRA: (of record, the day opened) Entered: a notice nailed at dawn. The Office will attend the licensed class to verify the roll against the children present. Any child present who is not on the roll will be recorded as taught —

(the pen stops)

— unlawfully.

NANDI: (from the step's edge, very quietly, to nobody) So they will come and count the class. And the two they cannot count will be the two in the room.

CAPTION (smallest): Pay for the two.

Writing & art notes

  • Three jobs, in order: the coin spent where a clause cannot reach it; the bowl that pays for exactly the two the Office refuses; the audit notice that turns arithmetic into a trap. The page's engine is that every move on it is correct — Kessa's purchase, the grandmother's coin, the licensed woman's double wage — and the machine answers correctness with procedure. Nobody on this page is wrong.
  • Kessa's market scene is comedy with teeth, and it must stay small. She is not making a speech in the street; she is buying two slates and a twist of chalk and making a chandler say the price twice, and the receipt is the weapon. The line spent on children not on your roll should be written once, in Panel 2, and never repeated.
  • The two slates are the page's warmest image and must not be sentimentalised. No tears, no music in the art: two children looking at new slates and then at each other, and nine children with older chipped ones watching. That last detail is the moral centre and must be drawn.
  • The bowl must look like a bowl, not a collection plate. Cracked, wire-mended, chipped at the rim, sitting on a school table between an unlit lamp and a slate; a handful of small coins, not a heap. The grandmother's line — I am not paying for the seal, I am paying for the child — is the chapter's thesis in one sentence and must be given the panel's full width and her full face.
  • Panel 5's arithmetic is the payoff of three pages of accounting and must be legible as a shape even if no figure is readable: two lines, two totals, one chalk rule under both. Do not add a third line.
  • Panel 7 must not stage a confrontation. The notice does the work: a small leaf, a bright seal, a thin queue of poor people reading it, and eleven children sitting on a step waiting for a lesson, two of them holding slates bought with the Office's own money. Nandi reads it over their heads and says one quiet line to nobody. Do not draw an official, a cart, or a soldier.
  • The imperative ladder takes its next run: Pay for the two.
