The Nine Kinds (नव जाति — Nav Jaati)

Every being woven into the Loom belongs to one of Nine Kinds. A Kind determines how your thread is made, not how strong you are. Mixed-blood children exist and are their own Kind: Manavkin.

Rule for the writers' room: a Kind must change how a character thinks, not just what they look like. If a Kind only changes the ears, cut it.

1. MANAV — मानव — Human

Thread: single-strand, short, burns bright and fast. Look: ordinary. Deliberately drawn plain so every other Kind reads as distinct. Culture: the most numerous Kind and the only one spread across all nine Sectors. Build cities fast, forget faster. Run most of Rangbhoomi's institutions purely through numbers. Strength: adaptability — a Manav thread accepts almost any Sector law. Weakness: shortest thread. Humans Fray earliest. Story role: the baseline. The reader's anchor. The Kind that proves the system is unfair.

2. MANAVKIN — मानवकिण — Humankin (PROTAGONIST'S KIND)

Thread: braided — two or more Kinds twisted into one strand. Unstable, variable, illegal to register in four Sectors. Look: deliberately inconsistent on purpose. Mismatched eyes, a patch of scale, one pointed ear, faint antler-nubs, grey-blue skin in patches. Every Manavkin looks like a different Kind's rumour. Culture: no homeland, no Sector of their own. Live in the seams — ash-slums, caravan roads, the underside of cities. Called "half-threads" as an insult and "bridge-threads" as a prayer. Strength: a braided thread can hold two Sector laws at once — the only Kind that can. Weakness: the braid frays from the inside. Manavkin have the shortest lifespan of any Kind. Story role: the wildcard, and the reason the card game has a "multicolour" archetype.

3. ASURA — असुर — Demon

Thread: over-drawn. An Asura is not a separate species — an Asura is what any Kind becomes when it Frays past the point of return. Born-Asura exist: children of the Frayed, born already owing. Look: asymmetric, wrong-jointed, beautiful in the way a wound is. Horns are scar tissue, not bone. Culture: no nations. Debt-bands — packs bound to a single creditor. Strength: enormous raw thread. They spend it all, always. Weakness: they cannot stop spending. Every Asura is dying. Story role: the tragedy engine. An Asura is a character who made one bad loan.

4. VANYA — वन्य — Elf / Forest-born

Thread: long, slow, rooted. A Vanya thread is partly grown into the land. Look: tall, pale-green undertone, hair that behaves like fibre rather than strand. Bark-callus on the forearms. Culture: Dharakhand and the deep forests. Speak slowly on purpose — their language is contractual and a promise spoken aloud is binding. Strength: near-immortal. A Vanya can hold a single thread for centuries. Weakness: cannot leave their root-land for long without sickening. Exiles are the saddest people in the world. Story role: the long view. The Kind that remembers what everyone else forgot.

5. KSHUDRA — क्षुद्र — Goblin

Thread: knotted. Short but tied into dense loops — small power, extremely hard to break. Look: small, large-eared, thick-skinned, enormous hands. Eyes that catch light like a cat's. Culture: Chhayakhand and every city's underbelly. Kshudra run Rangbhoomi's black markets, its courier networks and — quietly — its banks. Strength: a knotted thread cannot be cut. Kshudra are nearly impossible to unweave or steal from. Weakness: cannot hold a large amount of thread. Physically the weakest Kind. Story role: information, leverage, and the best supporting cast in the series.

6. PASHU — पशु — Beastkin

Thread: furred — braided with an animal's thread at birth. Look: animal features by clan: wolf, deer, boar, crane, jackal. Wear clan-markings, never hide them. Culture: Raktakhand. Clan law over Sector law. A Pashu answers to their herd before their government. Strength: share senses and pain with their bonded animal. Fight as two. Weakness: if the animal dies, a piece of the Pashu's thread goes with it. Permanently. Story role: loyalty, and the first Kind to teach the reader that power has a heartbeat.

7. PRETA — प्रेत — Wraith / The Unwoven

Thread: cut. A Preta's thread was severed — by debt, by war, or on purpose. Look: translucent at the edges. Shadows fall the wrong way around them. Faces that are hard to remember. Culture: none permitted. Pretas are legally unregistered in seven of nine Sectors. Strength: they owe nothing, so nothing can be taken from them. Immune to debt-based attacks. Weakness: they cannot pull new thread at all. Whatever they had when cut is all they will ever have. Story role: the horror, the mystery, and the mirror to Ira — she owes nothing either, and nobody knows yet whether that is a gift or the same wound.

8. NAGA — नाग — Serpent-born

Thread: coiled. Stored in loops below the skin, released in one strike. Look: scaled from the collarbone down, split pupils, no external ears. Shed once a year, and the shed skin keeps its power for a week. Culture: Jalkhand — the drowned cities and floating terraces. Keep the world's oldest written records. Strength: burst power. A Naga can release a year of stored thread in one moment. Weakness: nothing between bursts. Predictably powerless right after a strike. Story role: historians, archivists, and the Kind that knows what the Loom actually is.

9. DEVA — देव — Celestial

Thread: spun from above. A Deva does not owe the Loom — the Loom pays them. Look: flawless, luminous, and faintly wrong. Drawn with too-perfect symmetry so the reader feels it. Culture: Tarakhand, the star terraces. Number in the low hundreds. Have not been seen on the ground in four hundred years. Strength: effectively unlimited thread within their own Sector. Weakness: cannot survive outside Tarakhand for more than a few days. Story role: the false ceiling. The reader will believe Deva are gods for about 200 chapters.

10. YANTRA — यन्त्र — Machine-born ⚠️ HIDDEN — DO NOT USE BEFORE CHAPTER ~180

Thread: woven, not grown. Something is manufacturing thread. Look: ceramic-white plating over a hollow frame. Thread runs through them like circuitry. Note: Reserved for the mid-series reveal that the Loom is a machine. Locked out of Chapter 1–179 by editorial rule so the reveal lands. Foreshadowing permitted only as background graffiti and as Yantrakhand being sealed.
