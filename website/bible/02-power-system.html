Power System — The Sutra, the Debt, the Fray

One system. Everything in the story obeys it. No exceptions without a writer's-room note.

1. The Sutra (सूत्र) — the Loom

Rangbhoomi is not a planet. It is a weave. Reality is thread, and thread is under tension.

The Loom is visible in the sky from anywhere in the world: a vast, slowly turning lattice of light, like a warp-frame the size of a continent. It is not a god. It does not speak. This is a hard canon rule — anything that appears to be the Loom talking is a lie, and the series will prove it.

2. The Sutra-mark (सूत्रचिह्न)

Every being is born with a mark on the body — palm, throat, spine, or over the heart, depending on Kind. The mark is closed at birth and opens at fifteen, at the Unspooling (अनुकुलन).

Where the mark sits tells you what the thread will do:

Mark locationThread type
PalmProjection — you put power into the world
ThroatVoice — you put power into words and sound
SpineBody — you put power into yourself
HeartBond — you put power into another living thing
Over the eyeSight — you put power into knowing

3. The Unspooling (अनुकुलन)

At fifteen, in a public ceremony, the mark opens and the child draws their first thread.

The thread has two properties, both visible to everyone in the room:

  • Colour — which Sector law it obeys (see 03-the-nine-sectors.md)
  • Strain — how much it can carry, shown as how taut the thread appears

A taut, bright thread means a strong life. A slack thread means a weak one. The Unspooling is a public ranking of a child's entire future, which is why it is the cruellest day in Rangbhoomi and why Chapter 1 opens on it.

4. Debt (ऋण)

The first law of the Sutra: you cannot create thread. You can only borrow it.

Every pull of power is a loan from the Loom, and every loan is written on the skin as a debt-mark — a thin dark line that grows with what you owe.

Debt levelMarksEffect
ClearnoneNormal life. No powers used.
Light1–3 lines on one limbFull recovery with sleep and food.
Heavylines reach the torsoPowers weaken. Hands shake. Sleep becomes debt-payments.
Deeplines reach the throatVoice starts to fray. Others can hear the Loom through you.
Terminallines reach the eyesFraying begins. Irreversible.

Debt is repaid by time, rest, and sacrifice — never by paying someone else. This is why the rich of Rangbhoomi hire debt-carriers, and why that practice is the series' central injustice.

5. Fraying (रेशा-भंग)

When debt passes terminal, the thread starts coming apart.

  1. Fuzz — hair-thin filaments drift off the skin. Painful, survivable.
  2. Unspool — a limb loses coherence and has to be bound. Permanent loss.
  3. Come Apart — the Kind is gone. What remains is an Asura, still owing, still spending.

Fraying is not a villain transformation. It is an illness. Most Asura in this series were somebody's neighbour. This is the moral spine of the whole 500 chapters.

6. Mending (संधान) — the forbidden skill ⭐ PROTAGONIST'S POWER

Everyone else pulls thread and owes for it. A very few people can mend — reweave thread that has already torn.

  • Mending creates no debt. This is why it is illegal in six Sectors and why the two Sectors that permit it license menders brutally.
  • Mending is slow, physical, and exhausting. It is work, not a superpower. Ira carries needles, thread, and a thimble. She sits down to do it.
  • A mender can mend marks, not people — at first. What Ira can mend by Chapter 1's end is deliberately left ambiguous, and it is the series' engine.

Hard rule: Ira cannot mend herself. Whatever is wrong with her palm, she cannot fix. Ever.

7. What is NOT allowed

  • ❌ Power with no cost. If a character uses power, a debt-mark appears on-panel.
  • ❌ New power systems introduced to solve a plot. Everything routes through thread and debt.
  • ❌ The Loom speaking. See §1.
  • ❌ Healing that reverses Fraying. Fraying is permanent, always.
  • ❌ A character being strong because they are important. Strength is always paid for somewhere.
