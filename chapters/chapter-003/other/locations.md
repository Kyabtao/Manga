# Chapter 003 — Locations

Everything shown or named in Chapter 3's first page, with continuity notes against Chapters 1–2.

## THE KNOT & NAIL — गाँठ और कील — Kessa's pawn stall, basin market (POST-REOPENING)

Chapter 1: shutters up, lamp lit. Chapter 2: shutters down day 1–10, voice through boards day 9,
open day 11 — alarm was Ira's choice. **Chapter 3 Page 001: fully open, loupe down, counter
wiped.** The stall is *normalised* — the basin has adjusted to its reopening. A fruit-seller two
stalls down has moved his display one inch closer (the smallest unit of trust the row knows).

**New canon (Ch. 003 Page 001):** the lockbox is on the counter for the first time in daylight. The
counter is now two things at once: the mender's work surface (where Ira's rubbing sits) and the
appraiser's consultation table (where the cut-end lies). The stall's dual function — repair and
reckoning — is physical in this one image.

**Continuity:** the counter-edge frame signature from Ch. 001 Page 007 Panel 2 is the stall's
establishing shot; reuse with the lockbox as the new counter object. The crate Ira sat on during
Chapter 2 (doorstep scene) is not visible — she stands at the counter this chapter.

## THE LOCKBOX — ताला-बक्सा — under-counter, now on-counter

**First daylight appearance.** Kshudra-made, black basalt, forty years in Kessa's possession.
One keyhole, no key visible. Interior: old grey felt bed. Contents: the shadowless cut-end
(hand's-width thread-segment, severed at both ends with ancient turn-backs). The box has been
opened twice at night (Ch. 001 Page 010, Ch. 002 Page 009 — lock-turning heard, never shown);
this is the first time it is open on-panel in daylight with a witness present.

**Continuity rule:** the cut-end's shadowlessness is a property of the thread, not the box. If the
thread is ever removed from the box, it remains shadowless. The box itself is ordinary Kshudra
craft — the impossibility is in what it holds.

## THE COUNTER — काउंटर — Kessa's work surface

The fixed furniture of the Knot & Nail. Grey-worn wood, loupe-lamp clamped at one end, ledger
usually at the other. Today: the ledger is pushed aside to make room for the lockbox (left of
centre) and Ira's charcoal rubbing (right of centre). The letter appears at the counter's lip,
drawn from beneath it.

**Light rule:** one lamp, warm, counter-lit. The cut-end's shadowlessness reads best against a
surface with a clear shadow (the oilcloth, the lamp-light on the wood). Let the single lamp do
the work.
