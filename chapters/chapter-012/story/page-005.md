# THREADBORN — Chapter 12 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Whole Roll*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the square, the list-post, midday
**Page type:** The firing — 7 panels, ends on a summons and a warning

> Beats: **the basin's one newspaper, read aloud** — Ira takes the list-post in front of the square;
> **the definition, word for word** — read back in the Office's own weather-voice; **the reading, of
> record** — the school's roll is whole, as of the last finished teaching, by the Office's own
> reader-of-record, on a proper ask; **the clerk's grammar** — *the roll means the Office's roll,
> obviously*; **the out that is worse than the trap** — his hand hovers over the posted paper and
> drops: taking it down unmakes every paper the Office ever posted; **the clock turns** — three
> mid-thread hands become the most watched people in the basin, because the count must outrun the
> school; and **the paper moves** — he came with both outcomes pre-drafted, and the summons comes out.
>
> **This page:** the loudest thing in it is a woman reading a posted paper slowly.
>
> **Chapter 012 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent — no chain on this page, only grammar. Mother never
> on panel, never speaks, and makes no touch this page: her machinery is being operated in public.
> Loom never speaks, does not appear. Rekhak present and silent — he was asked, and he read.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the square (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: midday, the square, the list-post. A crowd the size of a hearing: the morning
circle at its front with **Nima** and the **three new faces**; **Kessa** at the edge with her arms
folded; survey veterans, stall-holders, the counting-lesson women. **Ira** steps up to the post.
The posted paper — knot-marks on Office stock — waits at eye height.

**Image:** The basin has one newspaper. Today it gets read aloud.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Word travels the way it always travels here: sideways, by stall. By noon, half the basin had heard
> that the keeper of the register was going to read the Office its own paper, and the other half was
> already standing here.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I have performed one mend in my life that needed a crowd. This is the other kind of stitching.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium-close, the reading (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: Ira at the post, one gloved finger on the posted paper, reading aloud,
level — the speed of somebody who wants every word to land. The crowd is silent the way water is
still before it goes over a lip.

**Image:** Read it back in their own voice. Their own grammar. Their own speed.

> **IRA:**
> *(reading aloud, level)*
> "By order of the Office. The count will be taken when the roll is whole. The roll is whole when
> every hand taught by the school of the braided thread stands entered upon it. Until the roll is
> whole, the count is postponed."
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Word for word. Weather-voice. I read it the way he posted it, because the posting is the law and
> the law is his own paper.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the reading of record (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira turns from the post to face the square — and the **grey clerk**, arrived
somewhere in the last minute, pen already in hand. At the crowd's edge, **Rekhak Vahni**, arms
still, exactly as still as his chain.

**Image:** She does not argue with the definition. She adds to the record.

> **IRA:**
> Now the reading, of record. This week, on a proper ask, marked, the Office's own reader-of-record
> entered the school's own archive and read the school's own roll — aloud, by length and turn.
>
> **IRA:**
> The school's roll is whole. As of the last finished teaching. Of record.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(fast, to the crowd's edge)*
> Reckoner. Report.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And Rekhak Vahni, the Office's reader-of-record, said nothing. He stood at the edge of a square
> full of people and let his silence be read — and the square read it exactly right, because there
> was only one thing it could mean.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the clerk's grammar (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the clerk and Ira at the post, both of them now, pen-hand pointing at the
paper, her finger not moving off it.

**Image:** The trap, met with the tool it was built from.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The roll. The *Office's* roll. The Office counts the Office's rolls. Obviously.
>
> **IRA:**
> Obviously. Then the poster will have written that.
>
> *(not moving her finger)*
>
> Read it again. Slowly, the way you post things. Which roll did it name?
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> Every reasonable reader—
>
> **IRA:**
> You posted it to a basin. Not to reasonable readers. To *these* readers, who heard it posted once,
> and will not need to hear it twice.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the out (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk's hand rising toward the posted paper — two fingers, the beginning of a
take-down — and stopping in the air. The crowd watching the hand.

**Image:** The only way out is to claim the Office never meant what it posted. In front of the basin
that heard it posted.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it is. The whole architecture of paper rests on one habit: *posted means posted.* Take the
> paper down, and every paper the Office ever posted becomes take-down-able — every bounty, every
> licence, every seal. His hand knows what his grammar will not say.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> His hand drops. The paper stays. The square saw the hand do it, which is worth more than any
> reading I could give.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the clock turns (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the whole square's attention swinging, in one motion, to the circle's edge — to
the **three new faces** beside **Nima**, who stand there not knowing they have just become the most
watched people in the basin.

**Image:** The Office's plug, reborn as the basin's window.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The count is owed now. It will be taken by mouth — line by line, name by name — because their cord
> is still dead in their chest. That is slow. Line by line is *slow.*
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And the roll is whole only up to the last finished teaching. Three hands are mid-thread. The day
> they finish, they get tied in, and the roll opens again — and the postponement snaps back like a
> cord under load.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The Office does not have to win the grammar. It only has to run out the clock. So now you know,
> all of you, what those three faces are worth — to them, and to us.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the summons — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the clerk, composed again, producing from his coat a paper already folded,
already sealed — pre-cut, pre-written, waiting. He came with both outcomes drafted. He holds it out
to Ira. Her hand does not take it yet. Behind the crowd's edge, Kessa's chin lifts.

**Image:** He came with both papers. The argument was never going to change what was in his coat.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office summons the keeper of the register. Room Nine. Tomorrow, first bell. The Office will
> discuss its roll with the Office's keeper — *in the Office.*
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Of course. No square. No crowd. No basin listening. In Room Nine the only grammar is his, and the
> cushion is already on the chair.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Bring your own law, keeper.

## Writing & art notes

- **This page is public, procedural, and loud after three quiet pages — but the loudest instrument
  is still dryness.** Ira does not speechify. She reads a posted paper at posting speed and adds one
  entry to a record. The crowd does not cheer; it *memorizes*. A woman in the crowd is mouthing the
  definition back to herself — that is the sound of the trap closing, and it is silent.
- **The definition is quoted verbatim from Page 001 — exactly, no paraphrase.** The verbatim-ness IS
  the argument: the poster wrote a scope-less noun, and she reads the scope-less noun back. Do not
  let Ira embellish the reading; the caption *word for word, weather-voice* carries the discipline.
- **Rekhak's silence is the page's best line and he must not be given one.** The clerk demands
  report; Rekhak stands still; the square reads the silence. His only protection is that he was
  asked and he read — *what the basin does with a plain reading is not my fee* (Page 004) stays his
  position without being restated. Do not let him nod, smile, or signal. Stillness is the whole
  performance.
- **The clerk's hand in Panel 5 is the money shot.** Two fingers rising toward the posted paper,
  stopping in the air, dropping. The basin watching a hand learn what it can and cannot un-post. No
  lettering on the paper in art — knot-marks and seals only; the words live in the lettering and
  captions, and the reading is done aloud on-panel.
- **Panel 6 must do the arithmetic in captions, cleanly.** The count is owed now; the count is by
  mouth; mouth-counts are slow; wholeness survives only up to the last finished teaching; three
  hands mid-thread; when they finish, the roll opens and the postponement snaps back. The Office
  does not need to win the grammar — it needs to run out the clock. This is the chapter's remaining
  engine: the race between a mouth-count and a school. Do not solve it; just make the whole square
  (and reader) see the race.
- **Panel 7: he came with both papers.** The amendment is never drafted in public (posting an
  amendment concedes the reading was arguable); the summons was always in the coat, pre-cut,
  pre-sealed — the Office's real answer to a public trap is a private room. Room Nine's cushion has
  been waiting since Chapter 011; say so once, in caption, and move. The smallest caption begins
  Page 006's job: *bring your own law, keeper.*
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 012: ONE, unspent (no chain on-panel; the Office's chest
  stays shut). Mother never on panel, never speaks — no touch this page; her machinery (the reading,
  the ask, the grammar) is being operated in public by her daughter. Loom never speaks, does not
  appear. Rekhak: present, silent. Kessa: present at the edge, no line — her chin lifts on the
  summons; that is all. Nima and the three new faces: present, no lines (their silence in public is
  discipline — the school does not argue grammar on the Office's square). The note rides in Ira's
  inside pocket. The step-knot is still tied. The ask-log's first line stands. The year-knot is
  still unread. The grey lamp still burns in daylight over the alley.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern furniture, no printed signage; the posted paper, the
  summons, and the clerk's pre-cut drafts are Office stock with knot-marks and seals only — no
  lettering in art. The square's furniture: wood, stone, the post.
- **Numbers:** one post, read back once; one reading of record; one caveat (as of the last finished
  teaching); three hands mid-thread; one hand that rose and dropped; two pre-cut papers in one coat,
  one delivered; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
