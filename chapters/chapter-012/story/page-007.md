## PANEL 1 — Medium, out of the Office (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Office yard, midday, Ira walking out with the register under her arm. Behind
her, the Office's door; ahead, the lane. She is not hurrying. That is the point of her pace.

**Image:** She signed nothing. He wrote everything. That was the interview.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Walking out, I am un-mistaking what happened in that room. I thought I was escaping a trap. The
> trap was a filing cabinet, and it closed behind me with a sound like a pen.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> But a no, of record, is a door left shut on purpose — and a shut door can be opened from either
> side, later, by whoever holds the record of it. He filed my refusal. Someday I will read it back
> to him.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the gate (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Office gate. **Rekhak Vahni** is coming out as she goes — off shift, warrant
folded away, coat grey as ever. Two professionals passing. Neither stops. Both speak, in passing,
at the same walking pace.

**Image:** The only honest man in the building, and the only speed he keeps.

> **IRA:**
> *(in passing)*
> Your count is slow at the nameless.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> *(in passing)*
> It will be exact at the end. I keep one speed, keeper. Ask anybody I have ever audited.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And there is the race, said plain by the man running his half of it: they do not need to be fast.
> They need to be *exact*, and they need to finish before the school does.

---

## PANEL 3 — Wide, the count through the streets (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the lanes below the Office, early afternoon. Above a yard wall, an upper window
stands open to move the air — and out of it, in relay, the sound: a name, a line, a name, a line.
Two clerks' voices trading sentences of the roll. Then the held pause where a name should be. Then
it resumes.

**Image:** The count, out loud, all over the basin's afternoon. It stumbles at the twenty-two, and
the basin can hear it stumble.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They have moved the count to the upper rooms for the air. The basin can hear every word.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> A name. A line. A pause. The pause is a person. Read that way, slowly, by mouth, with the whole
> street listening — the twenty-two are louder than they have ever been. The Office's own honesty,
> reading their emptiness aloud in public.

---

## PANEL 4 — Wide, the school answers (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the alley from a distance, mid-afternoon. The grey lamp burning in daylight over
the lane mouth; the circle around the crate bigger than it has ever been — and at its center,
**Nima's hands** mid-holding, a stranger's hand held steady. The **three mid-thread faces** are not
at the edge today. They are inside the circle, holding hands of their own, unsteady and serious.
The basin watches from every doorway.

**Image:** The school's half of the race. It answers by continuing.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The Office counts. The school teaches. Both are slow. Both are public. Only one of them is a
> lesson.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Look at them — three hands that were pupils a week ago, holding other hands steady, badly, the
> way you hold anything the first hundred times. When they finish learning to hold, the school ties
> them in, and the roll opens, and the postponement snaps back. So the basin watches them the way
> you watch sand in a glass. And the sand runs.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the square at dusk (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the square, dusk, the list-post with its small knot of readers — stall-holders on
the way home, the counting-lesson women, a child. Nobody speaks above a murmur. The posted paper,
knot-marks in the last light. Ira passes through without stopping, the register under her arm.

**Image:** A posted thing, read daily, cannot quietly die.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They come at dusk to read it again — the definition, the whole thing, murmuring it back and forth
> like a psalm with a number in it. The basin is keeping the paper alive on the Office's behalf.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Posted means posted. The square has learned the grammar better than the man who wrote it.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, home (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall, dusk, the oil lamp lit, shutters half down. **Kessa** at the counter,
the box open beside her, the empty fold of cloth laid out flat. She looks up as Ira sets the
register on the counter, and reads the whole day off her face the way she reads thread.

**Image:** The old appraiser's read. She does not ask what the day cost. She asks what it bought.

> **KESSA:**
> The square says the Office bit its keeper, and its teeth broke.
>
> **IRA:**
> The square says half of it. I signed nothing. He wrote everything.
>
> **KESSA:**
> *(turning back to her thread)*
> Then eat first, and show me the paper he wanted signed. Boxes first, wars after. That is the
> whole of appraising.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close-medium, the files — FINAL HOOK (~19%)

**Camera:** Close-medium: the counter, lamp-low. Three papers out of the inside pocket, laid side by
side on the wood: **the note** — knot-script, one turn, the whorl where a name would be; **the
summons**, Office stock, one seal; **the copy of the amendment**, Office stock, one seal, uncountersigned.
And the register opened flat beside them, back board lifted, the archive pocket empty and waiting.

**Image:** Two files she was given. One file she was born with. One book that is starting to look
like a small, stubborn, second Office.

> **IRA:**
> Her no. Their summons. Their paper I refused — a copy, of record, *for the keeper's files*, which
> means he gave me the weapon and kept the receipt.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Kessa keeps a box with a fold in it, and what it holds is worth a stall. The Office keeps a chest
> with a dead knot in it, and what it holds is worth a basin. I keep a register. Lately it holds a
> no, an ask, a reading, and a refusal — everything the basin has that the Office wants and cannot
> take.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> A second Office, keeper. Keep it better.

## Writing & art notes

- **The page is a decompression, and it must decompress without going slack.** Two clocks run all
  page (the count stumbling over the yard wall; the school holding hands under a watching basin), and
  the walk between them is where the reader breathes. Pace the panels midday → afternoon → dusk.
- **Panel 1's captions complete Room Nine without re-litigating it.** *I signed nothing; he wrote
  everything* is the page's thesis and it must be said once, early, then converted into the page's
  discovery: a filed refusal is a shut door, and shut doors open from either side later. That idea is
  why Panel 7 exists — she is collecting doors.
- **Rekhak's gate line is the race in one breath.** *They do not need to be fast; they need to be
  exact, and they need to finish before the school does.* Keep it in passing — two professionals at
  the same walking pace, no stopping, no lingering. His honesty remains total and unnerving.
- **Panel 3 makes the twenty-two audible.** The count has moved upstairs for the air; the basin hears
  every stumble. Play it as sound-through-a-window, never as a scene of counting — no clerk faces, no
  counting room. The stumble IS the politics: the Office's honesty reading emptiness aloud in public.
- **Panel 4 is the school's half of the race and it belongs to the three.** The mid-thread hands are
  INSIDE the circle now, holding other hands badly — the first generation the school has taught since
  the definition, and the sand the whole basin watches. Do not resolve whether they finish; the page
  ends long before the sand does. Nima appears as hands and discipline only — her face may be seen
  from behind/above; no line.
- **Panel 5 keeps the posted paper alive.** The dusk readers are the basin's new institution: a
  posted thing read daily cannot quietly die. One caption of grammar, no speeches.
- **Panel 6: Kessa gets the page's only warmth.** *Boxes first, wars after* — the appraiser's whole
  philosophy in five words. She reads the day off Ira's face without asking. One or two lines, no
  more; the lamp does the rest.
- **Panel 7 is the payoff and the hook.** Three papers, one pocket: her mother's no (the whorl, the
  turn), the summons, and the amendment copy — which the clerk gave her *for the keeper's files*:
  he handed her the weapon and kept the receipt. She files all of it in the register's archive
  pocket, and the register crosses a line the page names plainly: **a second Office — keep it
  better.** The smallest caption is the chapter's turn: the basin's counter-Office is now a thing
  with a spine and a pocket, and it is hers.
- **Continuity:** the amendment ORIGINAL stays on Room Nine's table (armed, waiting); she carries a
  clerk-provided copy — that is the "paper he wanted signed" Kessa asks to see. The summons from the
  square has been in the inside pocket since Page 005. The note goes back into the register's pocket
  with the others — filed, not surrendered: the fold of cloth in the box stays empty; the box keeps
  its fold, the register keeps the paper. Chain-stop ONE, unspent (no chain on-page). Mother never
  on panel, never speaks — her handwriting on the note is visible at Panel 7's distance as knot-marks
  and a whorl, nothing more. Loom absent. Step-knot still tied. Ask-log first line stands. Year-knot
  still unread (next page's business). The grey lamp burns in daylight (Panel 4).
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern furniture, no printed signage; all papers Office stock or
  knot-script — no lettering in art; the register's archive pocket is board and cloth.
- **Numbers:** three papers in one pocket; one register; one stumble per nameless, over and over;
  three mid-thread hands now holding others; two clocks; one dusk; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
