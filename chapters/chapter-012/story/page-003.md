# THREADBORN — Chapter 12 — Page 003

**Chapter title:** *The Whole Roll* / *पूरी पंजी*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the sluice gallery, the archive behind the first door
**Page type:** The knock — 7 panels, ends on an ask that is owed

> Beats: **the knock** — a door filed as nothing answers the way nothing does: by not stopping you;
> **the archive** — the school kept its own roll all along, in thread, its own way; the lamp is lit and
> the room is empty; **the year she cannot read** — the oldest rod begins with a year-knot, and exactly
> one person in the basin has ever pulled a year out of a knot and kept it; **the swept hook** — the
> step-knot was tied from here, recently; the channel widens: writing, then directions, now presence;
> **the warrant at the mouth** — the Office's reader announces himself lawful and comes in because
> nobody is hiding; **the reflex** — show Rekhak Vahni a roll and he reads it; and **the choice, just
> made** — the warrant goes away unwritten, and a price is named that is not coin.
>
> **This page:** the knock answers, but nobody answers it. The Office's eyes enter the school invited
> by honesty.
>
> **Chapter 012 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent — Rekhak's chain stays cold all page; he reads by
> craft, not by Audit. Mother never on panel, never speaks — her channel widens: a swept hook, a lit
> lamp. Loom never speaks and does not appear.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium-close, the knock (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the door. **Ira's** gloved knuckles, one knock. The gallery takes the sound
and keeps it. Nothing answers — and the door, knocked, drifts. It was never latched. It swings inward
on a hinge that has recently been oiled. Lamplight from inside.

**Image:** A door filed as nothing answers the way nothing does: by not stopping you.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> One knock. There is no lock to pick and no bar to lift. There is nothing to force, and so nothing
> to break.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The hinge is oiled. Nobody maintains a nothing.

---

## PANEL 2 — Wide, the archive (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: past the threshold: a low rock-cut chamber running along the channel's wall — and it
is not a classroom. Racks. **Rods.** Hundreds of slim thread-wound rods, season on season, dust on the
deep racks and none on the near ones. One oil lamp, lit, trimmed, full.

**Image:** The school kept its own roll all along. In thread, its own way — filed as nothing, like
everything else in here.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Not a classroom. A roll. Every hand the school ever taught is wound around one of these rods.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Nobody has ever needed the Office to remember who it taught.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The lamp is lit and the room is empty. Somebody maintains a room that does not exist.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the year she cannot read (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's gloved hands lift the oldest rack's lowest rod — grey thread gone silver,
dust in the grain. Its first knot is dense, deliberate, a different grammar: a **year-knot**, the old
school. She tilts it to the lamp. She cannot read it.

**Image:** You can see a knot is a year the way you can see a door is a door — without knowing what
room it opens on.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The first knot on the oldest rod is a year. I cannot read it. Year-knots are their own craft, and
> the craft died everywhere except wherever this thread was cut.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Exactly one person I know has ever pulled a year out of a knot and kept the reading. She did it on
> the Office's own roll. She is not here.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The school is older than every list the basin keeps. Its oldest rack says so in a grammar I cannot
> read. I will need somebody who can.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close-medium, the swept hook (~13%)

**Camera:** Close-medium: inside the door, on the gallery-side stones: an iron hook at shoulder
height, alone. The dust stops around it in a clean circle; the stone beneath is swept pale. On the
hook's curve, a few caught threads — grey, old-style, shadowless. The size and style of the knot on
my step.

**Image:** The step-knot was tied from here. Recently. By hands that sweep after themselves.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> A hook. Swept this week. Knot-scars on the curve, years of them — and nothing on it now.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Writing, for nine years. Then directions. Now presence. She has never moved an inch toward me in my
> whole life —
>
> **CAPTION:**
> — and this week I cannot take a step without landing in her grammar.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the warrant at the mouth (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: from inside, looking out. The doorway now holds daylight and a grey coat.
**Rekhak Vahni**, at the threshold, does not enter. He announces.

**Image:** The Office's eyes, at the school's threshold — invited in by the one defense this room
owns, which is being exactly what it is.

> **REKHAK:**
> Reader-of-record. Day-warrant. I report what I see — exactly. That is the whole of my business here.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(not turning from the racks)*
> The door has no lock, Reckoner. Nobody in this room is hiding. Report exactly.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> I intend to. That is what should worry you.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, the reflex (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: Rekhak inside, walking the racks like a man doing his job. His chain stays cold at
his hand — no Audit, habit only. He reads rods by eye: lengths, spacing, grammar. At the newest rack
he stops. His fingers do the thing they do. He closes the hand.

**Image:** Show Rekhak Vahni a roll and he reads it. Counting is not his weapon. It is his reflex.

> **REKHAK:**
> *(quiet, professional)*
> This is a roll.
>
> **IRA:**
> It is a school's memory. It was never entered because it was never asked for.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> Even your nameless are on it. By length. Your twenty-two have been enrolled all along, keeper — by
> somebody who kept better records than the basin.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The archive needs a reader. The candidates are standing in this room: the man the Office pays to
> read — and the people who never had names to lose, who have never been asked what else they know
> about thread.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the choice — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the two of them in lamplight. Rekhak takes out the day-warrant — blank
paper, one knot-seal — and holds it a long moment. Puts it away. **Unwritten.**

**Image:** Exactness, offered its first taste of mercy by an exact man — priced, not donated.

> **REKHAK:**
> Today's report is a drain-mend. It was always going to be a drain-mend. Nothing here was read.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> But rolls want reading, keeper. It is what I am for. It is what the chain was for, before the
> chain. When somebody wants this one read, that is a different page, and a different fee — and
> somebody will have to ask.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The Office's reader-of-record is standing in a room that does not exist with his fee on the table.
> He cannot be bought. He has just told me, in the Office's own grammar, what buying him looks like:
> an ask, properly made.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Ask him, keeper.

## Writing & art notes

- **The knock must not be dramatised.** No suspense beat, no creak, no held breath panel. The door is
  answered by not stopping her — the horror of this page is maintenance, not menace: oiled hinge,
  trimmed lamp, swept stone. Somebody keeps a nothing the way other people keep a shop.
- **The archive is the page's reveal and it must stay dry.** Racks of rods, season on season, dust
  marking time. The school kept its own roll all along — that is the reveal, and Ira processes it as
  record-keeping, not wonder. Do not let anyone call it beautiful. It is *kept*.
- **The year-knot is parked, deliberately.** Ira can see that it is a year and cannot read it. Nandi
  pulled *year nineteen* off the Office's own roll (Ch. 011, Page 006) — the page knows this and says
  so once, in caption, without naming her. Do not let Ira read the year this page. The oldest rack's
  grammar is a debt the story will collect.
- **The swept hook is the mother's whole appearance.** She is never on panel and never speaks; her
  channel on this page is maintenance: a clean circle in the dust, thread-scars on a hook's curve,
  an oil lamp kept full. Ira's inference is allowed to be certain in feeling and must stay inference
  in fact — *somebody* sweeps; the grammar is her grammar. No whorl-mark anywhere. No second knot
  delivered; the hook is empty and must remain empty.
- **Rekhak enters lawfully and stays lawful.** He announces his warrant; the door has no lock; the
  keeper does not forbid him (and cannot afford to be seen guarding a nothing). His honesty is the
  danger and he says so — *that is what should worry you*. His chain stays cold: no Audit this page,
  chain-stop budget intact. He reads by craft, by eye — the reflex, not the instrument.
- **The detonation is armed but not thrown.** Rekhak's professional observation — *your twenty-two
  have been enrolled all along* — is fact, not verdict. What he does NOT say this page: the school's
  roll is whole, and the Office's definition never said *whose* roll. Let the reader assemble it a
  panel early; let Ira assemble it next page; let the clerk hear it the page after. The arm-and-delay
  is the chapter's rhythm.
- **The choice ends the page just made, and the price is the hook.** The warrant goes away unwritten
  — a drain-mend, exactly as covered — but exactness does not forgive for free: reading the roll
  requires an ask, properly made, in the Office's grammar. Smallest caption mirrors Page 002: *Ask
  him, keeper.* What the ask costs, and who is allowed to make it, is Page 004.
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 012: ONE, unspent (chain cold all page). Mother never on
  panel, never speaks — maintenance only. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The note rides in Ira's
  inside pocket. The cover story is dead and both of them know it; *drain-mend* survives as the
  report's technical truth. The step-knot is still tied on the Mendery step. The three new hands are
  at the morning circle, unnamed, uncounted; every day the keeper is gone, the roll gets less whole.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern furniture, no printed signage; the archive lit by one oil
  lamp; rods, racks and hook are wood, iron and thread. No lettering anywhere — rods carry knots
  only; the warrant stays blank paper with a knot-seal.
- **Numbers:** one door, open; racks: newest this season, oldest unreadable; the twenty-two on the
  school's roll by length; one warrant, unwritten; one ask, owed; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
