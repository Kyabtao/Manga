# THREADBORN — Chapter 12 — Page 006

**Chapter title:** *The Whole Roll*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Room Nine, the Office, first bell
**Page type:** The interview — 7 panels, ends with a paper left armed on a table

> Beats: **she attends, of record, and does not sit** — the keeper answers a summons with her own law
> under her arm; **seating is posture, and posture is testimony**; **the count through the wall** —
> the mouth-count has begun, sixty-three hands read aloud room by room, and it stops where a name
> should be; **the third paper** — the amendment, pre-cut, pre-sealed, that stayed in the coat
> yesterday because posting it in public would concede the reading was arguable; **the trap** — a
> posting touching the count co-signs with the register, so the Office cannot post its way out
> without her hand; **the counter** — *the register does not countersign corrections to papers that
> do not err*; and **the interview's true shape** — he files her refusal, the amendment stays on the
> table unsigned, and the Office's patience takes the chair she would not.
>
> **This page:** nine feet by eleven, one lamp, no audience. The grammar has to do all the work.
>
> **Chapter 012 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent — the portable chain sits on its brass stand all
> interview and cannot pull: the Loom's knot rides in the Office's chest. Grammar is the only thing
> left in the building that pulls. That is why he needs her signature. Mother never on panel, never
> speaks, no touch — the note rides in the inside pocket and stays there. Loom never speaks, does
> not appear.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the room (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: Room Nine, first bell. Nine feet by eleven. One table, two chairs, a high window's
grey light, the **portable chain on its low brass stand** — and on the subject's chair, the cushion,
waiting where it has waited since the survey. **Ira** stands just inside the door, the **register**
under her arm. **The grey clerk** sits, pen squared to the table's edge, composed.

**Image:** She answered the summons the only way a keeper does: of record, standing, carrying her own
law.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office notes the keeper attends. Sit, please.
>
> **IRA:**
> The keeper answers papers standing. Seating is posture, and posture is testimony. Note it.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The cushion stays where it is. It has been waiting on that chair since before I knew this room
> existed. It can keep waiting.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the fencing (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: across the table — the clerk questioning, unhurried, and Ira answering each
question by turning it into a filing. Her thumb rests on the register's strap the whole time.

**Image:** Every question he asks becomes an entry she makes. Interrogation, reversed into record.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office has questions about the square.
>
> **IRA:**
> The keeper has a reading of the posted definition, given of record, before witnesses. Enter it.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office has not ruled on that reading.
>
> **IRA:**
> The Office posted it. I read it. Which of us did more?

---

## PANEL 3 — Close-medium, the count through the wall (~14%)

**Camera:** Close-medium: both of them a little still — and through the high window, from somewhere
across the yard, a low rhythmic sound: voices in relay, reading. A name. A line. A name. A line.
Then, held one beat too long: nothing. The reading stops where a name should be, and starts again.

**Image:** The count, by mouth, through a wall. It stalls every time it reaches one of the twenty-two.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They have begun. Somewhere over the yard the Office is reading its roll aloud — line by line, name
> by name, the old way, because their cord is still dead in their chest.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Every pause is a person with no name. The count by mouth is slow, and it is slower at the nameless,
> and the whole Office can hear it being slow.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the third paper (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk produces from his coat the paper that stayed home yesterday — folded,
sealed, pre-cut — and lays it on the table between them with two fingers. The amendment. He does not
unseal it. He squares it to the table's edge the way he squares his pen.

**Image:** He came with both papers, and this one was always meant for a room with no windows that
open.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> An amendment to the posted definition. *The roll means the Office's roll.* By the survey
> settlement, a posting that touches the count co-signs with the register of the keeper. The Office
> requires the keeper's counter-signature.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it is. He cannot post his way out. The paper that could end the basin's reading needs the
> one hand in the basin it was written against.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Sign, and yesterday's reading dies lawfully. Refuse, and the refusal dies of record in a room with
> no witnesses. He built a trap with no door on purpose. He is careful that way.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close-medium, the counter (~15%)

**Camera:** Close-medium: Ira's gloved hand comes to rest flat on the register's cover — not on the
paper, not on the pen. She does not raise her voice, because she does not need it. The high window's
light moves a fraction.

**Image:** Not a refusal. A filing. The only kind of no that cannot be made into a confession.

> **IRA:**
> The register of the keeper does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(a beat)*
> Are you telling the Office its posted paper errs?
>
> **IRA:**
> I am telling you nothing of the kind. You wrote the paper. You know whether it errs. I keep a
> register. It has one rule above the door: it does not sign what it does not correct.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And now he is where I was at the post — except nobody read him a crowd. To take the signature he
> must declare the posting wrong, on his own record, in his own room: he must un-post with the same
> hand he taught the basin could not un-post anything.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the filing (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the clerk takes back his pen, opens his own slim book, and writes — unhurried,
exact, the pen moving like it has all year. The amendment stays on the table. Unsigned. Facing her.

**Image:** He wanted the refusal. Now he has it, of record, in a room with no witnesses — and he
writes it down like a man filing a receipt.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office notes the keeper declines to counter-sign. The Office is patient. Rolls open. Keepers
> err. The Office will hold the amendment until a keeper signs it — or until a keeper is found who
> will.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And there is the interview's true shape, laid out as neat as his pen-hand: the trap never closes.
> It waits. He did not need my yes yesterday or today — he needed my *no*, on his record, so that
> the day the basin asks what its keeper would not sign, the answer is filed in his book and not
> mine.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the door — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: Ira at the door, register under her arm, three-quarters to the room. Behind
her: the table, the amendment squared to its edge, the pen at rest, the chain hanging dead on its
brass stand, the cushioned chair empty, the high window's grey light across all of it.

**Image:** She leaves the paper exactly where he wants it. Both of them know. Neither of them says.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The Office thanks the keeper for attending.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The chain has not moved all morning. The Loom's knot rides in their chest and their hardware is
> dead, and so the only thing left in this whole building that pulls is grammar. That is why he needs
> my hand. It is the last working part they do not own.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Sign nothing, keeper.

## Writing & art notes

- **The room does the menacing; nobody raises a voice.** Room Nine is nine feet by eleven, one lamp,
  a high window, two chairs, a brass stand — fixed by Chapter 011 and drawn exactly so. The horror of
  the page is patience: the cushion that has waited since the survey, the pen squared to the table's
  edge, the clerk's total unhurried certainty. No threats, no gestures. He wins rounds with furniture.
- **She does not sit — and this must be stated once, in dialogue, as procedure, then never
  dramatized again.** *Seating is posture, and posture is testimony* is the whole of her reason; the
  cushion does the rest of the work silently for six panels.
- **The count through the wall is the page's one race-beat and it must be auditory, not visual.**
  Never show the count room; never name who reads. Voices in relay, a name, a line, then the held
  pause where a name should be — the twenty-two made audible as slowness. It starts and resumes;
  nothing is resolved. The reader should feel two clocks and hear one of them stumble.
- **The third paper is the interview's engine.** The amendment could not be posted in public
  (posting one concedes the reading was arguable — yesterday proved the basin watches), so it needs
  a private room and a counter-signature, and the survey settlement gives him the hook: postings
  touching the count co-sign with the register. Ground this in ONE line and move — do not re-litigate
  Ch. 011's settlement, just cite it the way these two cite law at each other.
- **Her counter is a filing, not a defiance.** *The register does not countersign corrections to
  papers that do not err* is technically true, procedurally airtight, and completely unanswerable
  without self-harm: to press is to declare the posting wrong on his own record — the doctrine the
  basin watched him fail to act on yesterday, now turned in his own room. Play it as bookkeeping.
  The only weapon on the page is a sentence with a rule in it.
- **Panel 6 is the reveal of what the interview was FOR.** He files her refusal like a receipt: the
  trap has no door because it is a trap that waits — *keepers err, rolls open, the Office will hold
  the amendment until a keeper signs it, or until a keeper is found who will.* The menace is the
  Office's patience plus arithmetic. Say the true shape once, in her caption, and let it stand.
- **The chain must be visible in Panels 1 and 7 and never mentioned until the last caption.** It
  hangs dead on its brass stand all interview — the Loom's knot in the chest means the Office's own
  hardware cannot pull — and the smallest caption lands why the whole page happened: grammar is the
  last working part they do not own; her hand is the hand grammar needs. Chain-stop budget: ONE,
  unspent; the dangling IS the beat.
- **Continuity:** Room Nine exactly per Chapter 011 (nine by eleven; one table, two chairs, high
  window; portable chain on its low brass stand; cushion on the subject's chair). Mother never on
  panel, never speaks, no touch — the note rides in the inside pocket and stays there. The register
  is present and under her arm the whole page — the keeper answers papers standing, carrying her own
  law. The step-knot still tied; the ask-log's first line stands; the year-knot still unread; the
  three hands mid-thread (heard only as the count stumbling). Loom never speaks, does not appear.
- **§3.1:** no electric light (one oil lamp on the table, high window for the rest), no modern
  furniture, no printed signage; the amendment is Office stock, sealed, knot-marks only — no
  lettering in art. The clerk's slim book is blank-knot stock.
- **Numbers:** one room, nine by eleven; one cushion, waiting since the survey; one chain, dead, on
  its stand; one amendment, unsigned, left armed on the table; one refusal, filed — in his book, not
  hers; twenty-two pauses in a count heard through a wall; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
