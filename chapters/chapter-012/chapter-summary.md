## Synopsis

The Office stops banging on doors and **posts a definition**: *the count will be taken when the roll
is whole; the roll is whole when every hand taught by the school of the braided thread stands entered
upon it* — wholeness is a plug, and the basin has three days of winter and no way to out-hurry
arithmetic. The count returns to **Rekhak Vahni**, whose first finding breaks the Office's instrument
on the record: the Loom's last knot, spent by the basin to stop one requisition, has stopped the
counting-cord from inside the Office's chest — *the cord is dead* — so the count is by mouth now,
line by line, and **twenty-two of the sixty-three hands on the roll have no names.**

The blank note leaves Kessa's box and is read aloud: **"Do not enrol me"** is the owner's standing
no, on file, older than the roll — clause two contains nothing about the word being *yes*. At dusk
the four notes return after eighteen days **and add a fifth** — and on the step, a knot tied the old
way: *it is not a song. It is directions.* The keeper follows them out of the basin, past the Office's
purchased eyes (Rekhak, honest at any fee, a coat at a fixed interval), to the **first door**: behind
the low gate, in the sluice gallery, a wall of seasons of Office seals that have always gone *around*
one small door, never across — the log plate reading *void, no hands*. The only true line the Office
ever filed. **The archive**: the school kept its own roll all along, in thread, its own way — the
twenty-two on it by length; the oldest rod beginning with a **year-knot**; and by the door, a swept
iron hook: the step-knot was tied from here. The Office's reader enters lawfully, reads by craft, and
prices the reading at **an ask, properly made.**

The ask is entered of record (the fee: *you become a line in somebody's count*), and the verdict
lands flat as weather: **the school's roll is whole** — as of the last finished teaching. Ira
assembles the basin's most dangerous sentence alone: *he never said whose roll* — the postponement is
over. She reads the definition back to its poster in the square, verbatim; the clerk's hand rises to
take the paper down and drops (*posted means posted*); and the summons comes out of the coat — Room
Nine, where the amendment (*the roll means the Office's roll*) waits for a counter-signature the
register will not give (*the register does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err*).
He files her refusal in his own book — *keepers err; rolls open* — and the register crosses its line:
**a second Office.**

**Nandi** comes to the rods and reads the year: the Office's year-grammar, in an older hand, from
before the Office's year one — *this is my grammar's grandmother; the Office learned to count here* —
and the first hand the school ever taught is signed with **the founder's whorl**: the mark on the
note in Ira's register. Ira asks in thread (*a turn and a loop left open*) and the answer comes at
dawn as two knots: the direction retied, and **an entry-knot left loose**, its long end running
down-lane — *loose is a condition; nobody tightens it but the book that counts her fair.*

The chapter closes the way a knot ends: the three mid-thread hands hold steady and are tied in by
Nima's hands; Nandi's price is paid to the hour (*enter me whole*); and the founder's entry is
tightened by the last hand it was waiting for — the daughter's. The whorl enters beside the year. In
the square, the clerk names the machine to the whole basin and stops the clock with it: **the count
is suspended, not postponed** — the founder stands entered on the school's roll and against entry on
the Office's, by her word, on file, older than their paper. The register takes the street's paper.
On the step at dusk the four-note sentence is whole again. The chapter's smallest-caption ladder —
*knock / ask him / read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in* — lands flat:
**"The roll is whole, keeper."** And the number that closes the arc's forward clock, unspoken
on-panel, is the one that does not fit: *year nineteen.*

## Open threads (carried in)

- **The count is suspended, not taken.** The Office cannot complete its mouth-count while the
  founder's no stands on file — and the founder now stands entered on the school's roll. The
  suspension is an admission in public record; every soul in the square heard the machine get named.
  What the Office does about an uncountable basin is Chapter 013's first engine.
- **The amendment remains armed and unsigned** on Room Nine's table (*the roll means the Office's
  roll*), needing a counter-signature the register will not give. The clerk's refusal-then-silence
  is on his own record. It can still be fired — or withdrawn — in the worst way.
- **Year nineteen.** The number that does not fit the basin's roll closed Page 001 and the arc's
  forward clock; the archive's oldest grammar and the founder's entry now sit one knot apart. The
  door the directions pointed through is entered; what the school was before the basin — and what
  year nineteen counts — is still owed.
- **The three new hands: entered, never named.** By knot, not by name — the discipline held. The
  day the Office learns to ask them for their names is the day the race reopens; the school's roll
  only stays whole while the school stays finished (*the rest is years*).
- **The register as second Office** — a no, an ask, a reading, a refusal, a tying-in slip, and the
  street already brings it paper. Nine days old. The basin now has a counter-book, and the keeper
  knows what the last line of Page 007 cost (*keep it better*).
- **Nandi's standing:** entered whole on the school's roll, cleared of the Office's booked debt, and
  the only living reader of both grammars. A bridge — or a target.
- **Rekhak's ledger:** he was asked, he read, he filed nothing twice; his count is suspended on the
  record of its own honesty; his fee still runs on exactness. The one honest man in the building now
  works for the party the honesty embarrassed.
- **The notes:** four-note sentence whole again, eighteen days of silence answered in thread. The
  fifth note went unanswered by sound — answered at the archive instead. Whether it ever comes again
  is the mother's channel, not the basin's.
- **The swept hook:** empty; the step's loose-entry space swept clean; the direction intact. The
  maintenance continues — the school has a keeper of rooms and no name for her on any paper the
  Office can read.

## Canon rules added in Chapter 12 (binding forward)

- **The definition is posted and alive:** *the count will be taken when the roll is whole; the roll
  is whole when every hand taught by the school stands entered upon it.* It never said whose roll.
  Posted means posted — a posted thing read daily cannot die; taking one down unmakes every posted
  paper.
- **The count is by mouth** — the Loom's last knot (in the Office's chest since Ch. 011) has killed
  the counting-cord. The Office's hardware cannot pull; **grammar is the last thing that pulls**, and
  grammar needs the keeper's hand. **Chain-stop budget:** ONE, unspent to the end — the Office never
  gets its hardware moment.
- **The ask, properly made:** an ask is an entry or it is talk; the ask-log is a record; the asker
  becomes a line in somebody's count. Ira Sutar is the ask-log's first and only line.
- **The verdict, of record:** *the school's roll is whole, as of the last finished teaching.* And
  the legal close of the chapter: **the school does not enrol. It ties in.** Founder entered; the
  Office's count suspended — not postponed — because the founder's no (*do not enrol me*) stands on
  file, older than their paper.
- **The register of the keeper does not countersign corrections to papers that do not err** — and it
  does not sign what it does not correct. A filed refusal is a shut door; shut doors open from either
  side, later, by whoever holds the record.
- **Nandi's terms, paid:** *when the school ties a hand in, it ties mine; then it takes pupils* —
  the school takes taught hands and pupils; entered by knot, never by name.
- **The asking shape:** a turn and a loop left open; a loop is a thing that waits; the answer has to
  close it. Loose is a condition: *nobody tightens it but the book that counts her fair.*
- **The year-grammar's ancestry:** the Office's year-grammar is cut from the school's — *the Office
  learned to count here* — one grammar gone to clerkhood, one kept in thread.
