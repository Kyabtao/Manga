# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 010 — FINALE

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office room nine, and the hatch alley at dusk
**Page type:** Chapter finale — 7 panels

> Beats: three hands braided in the alley before first light, in the open, with consent and an Office
> witness; a thumbprint claimant promises to produce the founder at fourth bell; the schedule requires
> the reader of the bigger book and the keeper to read every entry aloud, in the same words, at the same
> time.
>
> **This page:** what the man with no name actually sells.

> **Chapter 010 budget closed:** no chain-stop used (the chapter's stop was spent in Chapter 009, Page
> 006). Loom never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008
> Page 009. Wax: grey for the Council and the Office, crimson for the principal's private hand.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the room prepared (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: room nine at fourth bell, arranged for an event. The stand in the middle with the
portable chain; the register open on the table in its case; and on the **subject's chair**, a cushion,
because the chair is going to be sat in by somebody old. **Nandi** is seated at the reader's side of the
desk with the bigger book open and her sleeves pinned; **Nima** stands behind her with the card-tray,
promoted one seat; the clerk has the schedule; the Inspector is in his corner.

**Image:** The instrument's full apparatus, present, warm, and waiting for a hand that is not coming.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Fourth bell. Stand, chain, register, two readers, one keeper, one chair with a cushion on it.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Somebody in that building had considered that the founder might be *old.* That cushion is the most
> frightening object I saw in two days, and I have thought about it more than once since: the Office
> planned for her *comfort,* and it planned for it in advance, and it planned for it the way it plans
> everything, which is to say it *scheduled* it.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the claimant (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the claimant brought in. Kshudra, fifty-odd, ninth terrace, a dock labourer's hands
and a coat that has been brushed for the occasion. He is not a cruel man and he is not a coward; he is a
man who has done arithmetic and brought his answer to a counter. Behind him — walked in by a second
clerk, seated in the cushioned chair with her hands in her lap — an **old woman**, eighty at least, in
clean rags, who has been told to hold her left hand out and is holding it out.

**Image:** The bounty's promised production: a poor man, his neighbour, and a chair with a cushion.

> **CLERK:**
> Claimant, name not entered at your own request. Produce the founder.
>
> **CLAIMANT:**
> *(and he does not say a word; he simply lifts the old woman's wrist and turns the palm up)*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it was. A palm, open, root up — and across the root of it, sewn under the skin by somebody who
> had seen the shape in a queue and copied it by lamplight two nights ago, a **crimson strand.**
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Twenty years. Every Office in this sector for twenty years. And he had done it with a needle, a stolen
> thread of the right colour, and a woman who was too old to be asked whether she minded.

---

## PANEL 3 — Large, the reading (~17%)

**Camera:** Large panel: the old woman's palm on the stand, the chain across it, and Nandi leaning in
over the bigger book — with her **sleeves pinned** and her hands flat on the desk, not touching anybody,
and her face entirely without expression. Ira stands at her shoulder, because the schedule requires
both: the reader's words and the keeper's voice, same words, same time. The chain returns nothing; it
returns nothing *badly*, and the reader should see the difference between a braid and a bad copy in the
way the strand lies.

**Image:** Two professionals looking at a forgery and one of them having to say it out loud.

> **NANDI:**
> Copy. Strand laid two nights ago. Crossed left over right, which is how a right-handed person copies a
> left-handed seam. Root is not opened, so there's no gate under it and there never was. Reading:
> **not the founder.**
>
> **IRA:**
> *(out loud, in the same words, at the same time, on the record)*
> Reading: not the founder.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Same words. Same time. I read it in her voice, which was the condition she wrote into the schedule so
> that no entry in this basin would ever go unread — and which, on that afternoon, made me say the words
> *not my mother* out loud, in a room with a clerk in it, on a claim, in the Office's handwriting.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I want to be honest about the second after that. There was relief in it. There was one whole second of
> relief, and I have not forgiven myself for the second, and I do not expect to.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the keeper's dispute (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the old woman's palm again, held in Ira's hands now — and for all the falseness of
the strand, the woman's hand is real: work-worn, arthritic, with a small healing wound where the needle
went in. Ira is not looking at the crimson. She is looking at the wound, and the panel should hold on
that.

**Image:** A mender and a forgery, and the only injury that actually matters.

> **IRA:**
> Keeper disputes verification. This hand holds no founder's strand: the strand is a copy, sewn within
> two nights, by a right hand, into a palm with no root opening and no gate. It is also, *(and here she
> looks up, at the claimant)* an *injury,* made without consent, on a woman who was not asked, and there
> is no fee for that in forty-one and no entry for it either.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(writing)* Claim declined, no strand verified, keeper disputes procedure. *(beat)* Keeper, I need one
> more line for the form. Was the subject harmed?
>
> **IRA:**
> Yes.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(writing)* Subject harmed. *(quietly)* That's the first time that box has been ticked in this room.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the pivot (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the claimant, still standing, and this panel is his — the reader should see a man
who has just lost everything he spent two nights working for and has one thing left to sell. He does not
bluster. He is calm. He looks at the Inspector and says the sentence the whole bounty existed to
produce.

**Image:** The chapter's real cost, offered by a man whose only capital is a hole in a door.

> **CLAIMANT:**
> Keeper's right. It's a copy. She's not the woman.
>
> **CLAIMANT:**
> But I know the door.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> *(and he has not moved, and his voice has not changed)*
> Say it properly, claimant.
>
> **CLAIMANT:**
> Behind the Mendery. Low, in the back door, forearm-sized, shin-high. Open once a week after the third
> bell for forty years. There's a chute for the mending-waste that answers to a weight of two men. And a
> woman's forearms come through that hatch, and I have had two strands put in my own hand through it, and
> so has half the ninth terrace, and I will put every one of those hands on your stand for money.
>
> **CLAIMANT (last line):**
> You wanted a hand, sir. I'll give you the *address.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And that was the whole of it. Not the founder: the *door.* That is what the bounty had been for from the
> first day, and it took a dock labourer with a stolen needle to see it before I did.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> You cannot catch a woman who has been invisible for twenty years. You can wait until her own people are
> hungry enough to sell you the *place where she knocks.*

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the acceptance (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the Inspector's hands, and the sheet — the party-of-record sheet from Page 008, with
the claimant's thumbprint already on it, entered at the desk by a man who would not give a name. The
Inspector does not gloat and does not thank him. He does the paperwork, because that is what he is, and
his last line is the coldest thing in the chapter.

**Image:** The Office accepting a place instead of a person, at a counter, without raising its voice.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> The producing party is entered as the party of record. *(writing)* Record: the place of keeping is
> named as the hatch alley, and the Office will survey it at first bell tomorrow, entry by entry, hand by
> hand, from the fourth step to the water-butt.
>
> **IRA:**
> Survey.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> Your stall is the place of keeping, keeper, and I am obliged to tell you: the Office has never needed
> your mother. It has needed the *address.* She has been at a fixed location, in this sector, for forty
> years, and it took four days of arithmetic to put a price on the door instead of the woman.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He did not say *stupid* and he did not say *trap.* He said *arithmetic.* And halfway down the stair I
> understood the last thing, which was that the Office had *not* outplanned me all along. It had simply
> outspent me: it could afford to pay a claimant, and I could not afford to keep his rent paid.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, dusk in the alley — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the hatch alley at dusk. The Office has already posted it: a **lamp on a pole** at the
alley mouth, grey, official, burning — standing where the school's lantern used to hang, and the two
neighbouring stalls' shutters are down for the first time in years. On the Mendery's step, in the
lamplight, two things: the **knot** that was lifted out of the register on Chapter 010's morning — and
laid beside it, tied with new thread into the same small scrap of paper, a **second knot**, in a hand Ira
does not recognise: untrained, thick, a child's knot, or a stranger's.

**Image:** The school's door posted like a crime scene — and the founder's answer on the step.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The knot she lifted out of the register, back on my step, with the paper still under it. She read the
> message. Nine days late and eighty-one steps up a stair, and she read it, and she sent it home.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And beside it — in the same paper, tied with a thread that has never been in my stall, in a hand that
> has never crossed two strands in the dark — a **second knot.**
>
> **CAPTION (last line):**
> That's a *new enrolment.* Somebody, somewhere in this basin, has been taught this week, and I did not
> teach them, and it was not done in the dark.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> My mother is not hiding and she is not being careful. My mother is *teaching,* openly, in the open,
> without my permission, and she has put a pupil's knot on my step so that I would know she can still do
> the one thing I have spent ten days learning not to do.
>
> **END OF CHAPTER TEN.**

## Writing & art notes

- **The last page must not resolve the bounty — it must reveal what the bounty was *for*.** The Office
  was never hunting a woman it could not find: it was pricing a *door* until somebody poor enough sold
  it. The Inspector's manner is unchanged; the cruelty is entirely in the accounting.
- **The forged strand has a victim and she must be drawn as one.** Old, arthritic, unasked,
  needle-wounded, holding her hand out because she was told to. Ira's dispute is about the *injury*, not
  the forgery — and the clerk ticking the *subject harmed* box for the first time in that room is the
  page's quietest and best beat.
- **Nandi's condition pays off exactly as written.** The reader of the bigger book and the keeper read
  the same words at the same time, so Ira has to say *not the founder* out loud on the record — and then
  admit, in one caption, the one second of relief. Do not soften it and do not explain it further.
- **The claimant stays a person.** No hood, no malice. He has done arithmetic: two nights of work on an
  eighty-year-old neighbour, a stolen thread, and a door to sell. He is the chapter's thesis, not its
  monster, and the school enrolled him once — he says so, on the record, while selling it.
- **The posted lamp at the alley mouth is the chapter's last image and it replaces the school's lantern.**
  Same height, same hook, grey instead of brass. Draw it plainly at dusk: no guards, no rope, no crowd —
  a lamp on a pole and two shutters down.
- **The two knots on the step are the hook and must be drawn at reader-scale.** One worn, one new, both
  tied into the same scrap of register paper. Ira's captions state the inference and stop: a new
  enrolment, made this week, not by her, not in the dark. Chapter 011 opens on who tied it.
