# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 003

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office, room nine
**Page type:** Production page — 7 panels

> Beats: the chit is called; the bounty's first claimant has been declined and charged; every claim in
> the basin will be entered at Ira's counter; Nandi is scheduled in the same room this afternoon.
>
> **This page:** a hand is produced, a chain reads nothing, and the Office pays a name for the first
> time — its own reader's.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, room nine (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: room nine. Small, high-windowed, cold. A table, two chairs — *the subject's chair
faces the window, the reader's faces the door*, and the panel should make that asymmetry plain. On the
table: a **stand** — a low brass frame holding a portable compliance chain, the kind that can be
carried by two men and set up anywhere — and a slate, and an ink-block. On the wall behind: one ruled
sheet, blank except for a room number.

**Image:** The instrument, installed. Not a machine: furniture with a job.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Room nine was nine feet by eleven. No window at eye height, one high one, so the light comes in on
> the subject and the reader sits with his back to the dark. Somebody designed that. Somebody designed
> a *room* around a thing that reads hands.

> **CLERK:**
> Keeper present, subject present, reader present, instrument verified at first bell. Begins.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, Nima (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the reader — a young Kshudra woman, nineteen or twenty, in Office grey cut plain and
cheap, sleeves rolled because Office grey does not fit a working arm. Her hands are a mender's hands:
scars at the knuckles, callus at the thumb, two old burns. Her name is given in the panel's dialogue,
once, flatly, because Ira asked for it in Chapter 009 and the Inspector keeps his words.

**Image:** The Office's new reader, drawn as the school's own product, and not yet explained to the
reader of this page.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Keeper, you asked for the reader's name before the certification. This is she. Nima, of the ninth
> terrace. Taken on at the turn of the year.
>
> **NIMA:**
> Keeper.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> *(genuinely helpful, and that is the whole horror)*
> She came to us from the hatch, as most of the ninth terrace did. She was enrolled at nine years old,
> which I am told is young, and she can count, which is why she has a desk and a chain, and you have a
> stall and a warrant.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nima.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Nineteen, maybe twenty. Enrolled at nine. Which means my mother put a thread into that hand through a
> hole in a door when the girl was old enough to hold a needle and young enough to be picked up, and
> told her nothing, and sent her home.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And today she is the reader. And when Jadi put her palm on that stand, Nima *turned her own left
> wrist inward*, under the table, out of the light.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the production (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: Jadi's left hand laid on the stand's plate, palm up, sleeve rolled, the braid plain —
grey strand and new strand crossing at the root, the school's first finished palm. The portable chain
laid across it. Nima's hands on the frame, not touching the chain, and her eyes not on the palm but on
the **chain**, which is the tell of somebody trained on paper.

**Image:** Two braided hands, four feet apart, in an Office room, one of them being read.

> **CLERK:**
> Subject: one hand, left. Keeper: attest the entry. Reading begins.
>
> **NIMA:**
> *(as she runs the chain)*
> ...nothing.
>
> **NIMA:**
> Nothing year-wise. It returns, it just doesn't return *a clean year.* Two values. It can't hold two —
>
> **CLERK:**
> Nima. Say the finding, not the reason.
>
> **NIMA:**
> *(a beat; and she looks at Jadi's face for the first time)*
> Finding. Unreadable.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She got the *reason* right. Two values, one hand, neither readable.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> That is the entire school written in four words by a girl who was enrolled at nine years old and has
> spent ten years being taught to stop saying the reason.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the receipt (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk, writing, and the receipt he tears off a block and sets on the table in
front of Jadi — a small grey slip with four printed lines and one blank for a mark. Ira reads it over
Jadi's shoulder and the reader reads it with her: *verified unreadable; the subject may not hold, own,
license, employ, wage, tenancy or claim within the sector; subject is not owed and may not be owed;
subject may not be worked upon by any licensed practitioner.*

**Image:** Legal non-existence, issued at a counter with a blank for a signature.

> **CLERK:**
> Mark here, subject. It's just a receipt. It doesn't do anything except say you got it.
>
> **JADI:**
> No.
>
> **CLERK:**
> You can't hold a stall, subject. You can't be owed a debt. You can't be worked on. Those are true
> from this morning whether you mark it or not —
>
> **JADI:**
> Then they're true without my mark, and *with* my mark the only thing that changes is that it's my
> hand on your page. So: no.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(writing, without rancour)*
> Subject declines to mark. Noted.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, Jadi's arithmetic (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Jadi, standing, buttoning her cuff over her knot, and the whole room in the frame's
edge — Nima with her hands still on the frame, the clerk with the slip, the Inspector at the wall. Jadi
is not angry. She is doing what she does: converting it into a number.

**Image:** A woman leaving a building that has just declared her non-existent, and taking the building's
inventory with her.

> **JADI:**
> Ten minutes. One chair, one chain, one girl, one slip.
>
> **JADI:**
> That's what I'm worth to the Office: a *reading* and a *receipt.* Forty-four years, and they've got
> one page and they had to cut a hole in the door of the world to find me.
>
> **JADI:**
> *(then, to Nima, mild, one professional to another)*
> Girl. Roll your sleeve down when they're watching. The chain reads the seam first, and you've got
> one.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nima's face did nothing. Her hand went to her sleeve and stopped half-way and went back to the frame,
> and that was the whole of her answer, and I have thought about it every day since.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the schedule (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Inspector stepping forward as the production closes — not gloating, organising.
He has a schedule in his hand and he reads one line of it out because it is the next item and that is
what a schedule is for. On the wall behind him, a second clerk enters and hangs the afternoon list; on
it, the same line as on the vestibule board.

**Image:** The chapter's three business in one room, announced like weather.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Keeper, one administrative matter and you may go. This room is booked at fourth bell for a witness
> hearing — Nandi, of the Mendery, in custody, to be asked about certain entries.
>
> **IRA:**
> She's not charged. She's not a subject. Why is she in room nine?
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> Because room nine has the stand and the stand is what a witness is shown things on. And because the
> register's keeper must be present whenever entries under it are examined. You are the keeper.
>
> **INSPECTOR (last line):**
> I would rather have you in the room than outside it, keeper. I am aware of how that sentence sounds,
> and I mean it in both ways.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, what Ira notices — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the slate on the table, and under the clerk's hand as he packs up — the *slate's
list of subjects*, several lines for the day, and **against Jadi's line the finding: UNREADABLE.** And
below it, a line neither Ira nor the reader has seen yet, in the same chalk, already written before
they arrived: **SUBJECT — M. SUTAR — RESERVED — NO ENTRY.**

**Image:** The reserved line has a name in a clerk's chalk, months before anybody could have known it.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The clerk wiped the board at the end of the session — everything except the last line, which he left,
> because it wasn't his.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And on it, in chalk, in a room where nothing is left, was the reserved line off my register, written
> out in a dead woman's name. My mother's.
>
> **CAPTION (last line):**
> Not *unknown.* Not *to be produced.* **M. Sutar.** The Office has never had her name. The Office has
> never had her face. But somebody up here wrote it on a slate next to a stand, and left it, so that
> when they brought me into the room I would see it and know that they know.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> And I stood there with a hand's worth of receipt in my bag and understood the trade they were
> offering.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> They don't want my mother. They want me to *put her name in the book myself.*

## Writing & art notes

- **Nima is the chapter's new character and she must be drawn as one of the school's own.** Office grey,
  a working arm, a mender's callus, a braid in her left palm that she hides by turning her wrist
  inward. She is not a traitor and not a victim; she is a Kshudra girl with a desk, and Chapter 010
  Pages 005–006 make her the chapter's hardest question.
- **The room's asymmetry is characterisation of the Office:** light on the subject, dark at the
  reader's back. No bars, no guards, no manifest cruelty. The instrument is *furniture*.
- **Nima's slip — "it returns, it just doesn't return a clean year" — is the leak and it must stay
  small.** She says the reason once, is corrected, and gets it right the second time in four words.
  The Commissioner-style line *"say the finding, not the reason"* is the whole regulatory method of the
  Office in one sentence.
- **Jadi's seam warning is the page's parting shot and a new fact about the instrument:** the chain
  reads the seam first. Keep that consistent with Chapter 005 Page 006 (a chain reads a mark and
  returns a kind and a year) and with Chapter 008 Page 009 (a braid returns both years at once).
- **The receipt's four clauses are the mechanical payoff of Chapter 009's ordinance** and they must be
  lettered as a small grey slip with legible print. *Verified unreadable* is the Office's most
  efficient punishment in the series so far: no jail, no fine, no violence — the removal of the
  capacity to be owed anything.
- **The reserved line's chalk is the closing hook and it is deliberately impossible.** A dead woman's
  name in a clerk's hand, left on a slate for Ira to read. Nobody explains how it got there; the
  Inspector's mildness in Panel 6 exists to make the reader ask whether he put it there or whether
  somebody above him did. No answer in this chapter.
