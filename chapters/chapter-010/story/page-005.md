# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office, room nine
**Page type:** Witness page — 7 panels

> Beats: the tray holds twelve cards copied by a needle-less hand; the first card is the fold out of the
> school's letter; the register's line forty-one was lifted out of its page in Ira's locked cellar.
>
> **This page:** eleven more cards, and the one answer nobody in the room was ready for.

---

## PANEL 1 — Close, card two (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: the second card on the table between Nandi's flat hands. A knot drawn in ink, with a
copyist's little arrow beside it labeling the crossing. Nandi has not touched it and does not need to.

**Image:** The first real identification, and it takes eleven seconds.

> **NANDI:**
> Kshudra. Year thirty-one. Left hand, root of the palm, two strands crossing under, and the outer one's
> thicker because she sewed canvas for the dock after. Enrolled in the winter, third bell.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(writing)* Kind, Kshudra. Year, thirty-one. Hand, left. Witness declines to name.
>
> **NANDI:**
> Witness was not asked to name. Witness was asked what the card is.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Eleven seconds. She had not seen that knot in thirty years and she read it the way I read a seam in
> bad light: kind, year, hand, and what the person did for a living, in that order, with no hesitation
> anywhere in it.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, card five (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the fifth card, and this one is subtly wrong — the copyist has drawn the crossing the
other way, so the strand that should go under goes over. Ira's hand has come off the register's lid and
her thumb is on the table's edge. Nandi looks at the card, then at Ira, then at the clerk.

**Image:** The keeper's one lawful opinion, used for the first time.

> **NANDI:**
> Wrong card.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Witness, the card's from the register —
>
> **NANDI:**
> The card's from a *copy* of the register, and whoever drew it never crossed two strands in his life. A
> left hand crossed that way belongs to a right hand that works backwards, and there are four people in
> this sector like that and none of them are enrolled.

> **IRA:**
> *(to the clerk, flat, on the record)*
> Keeper disputes entry five. The crossing is drawn reversed. Enter the dispute, not the entry.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(writing)* Keeper disputes. Entry five suspended pending re-copy.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> One card. That is the whole of the keeper's power: I can stop one bad
> copy before it becomes a hand that belongs to nobody.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, card seven (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: card seven on the table, and Nandi's forefinger — the first time she has touched
anything — drawing a slow circle over the drawing, not on it. The clerk leans in. Ira watches the
*Inspector,* who has stopped looking at the card and started looking at the witness.

**Image:** The translation room's work, found out by the only person qualified to find it.

> **NANDI:**
> That's not a knot.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Witness, it's drawn from the register —
>
> **NANDI:**
> It's drawn from *two* things. There's a knot in it and there's a counting-marks in it, the sort the
> Office ties on its own slates, and somebody has copied the two of them together because to him they
> looked like the same kind of mark. They are not. One is a hand and one is a *fee.*
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(to the Inspector, without turning)*
> Somebody up here is copying your ledgers into your hands. You should find out who, or in six months you
> will read a fee off a man's palm and call it his enrolment.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And that was the moment the room changed temperature. Not because Nandi was clever — because she was
> *right*, out loud, on a schedule, in front of a clerk, and every word of it went into the record.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The Office's own translator had been inventing entries. And the only reason anybody found out was that
> they had put a woman in a chair who had spent forty years checking one page against another.

---

## PANEL 4 — Large, the last card (~16%)

**Camera:** Large panel: the eleventh card, set down in front of Nandi, and the panel is composed so
that the reader sees what she sees — a small, neat knot with a flat, wide seat, drawn four decades
worn. Nandi's hands come off the table and go to her own coat, and one finger of her right hand hooks
into her left cuff and *stops there,* and the whole room watches her not do anything.

**Image:** A woman recognising her own enrolment, two days into custody, in an Office chair.

> **NANDI:**
> ...
>
> **CLERK:**
> Witness.
>
> **NANDI:**
> That one's mine.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(pen stopping)* Say that again.
>
> **NANDI:**
> That one's mine. Kshudra. Year one. Root of the left palm, flat seam, wide seat, tied by a woman in a
> cellar through a hole in a door in the dark. *I sewed for her after, and I have checked every page in
> that building against every other page for forty years, and I know my own knot the way you know your
> own voice.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Year one.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The school's first year. The first enrolment after the root.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the entry (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk's pen, writing it in. And, in the same frame, the register's second spread
open beside the tray, with the register's own line — the one Jadi signed nothing under — and the new
line being written under it in a copy hand, *Nandi, of the Mendery*, with the finding: **unreadable.**

**Image:** The Office's archivist, entered into the Office's instrument, by her own truthful mouth.

> **CLERK:**
> Entry. Subject: Nandi, of the Mendery, in custody. Finding, unreadable, by the witness's own
> identification. Mark here.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(and she takes the pen)*
>
> **IRA:**
> Nandi —
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(not looking up, writing)*
> Child, I have never once been asked a question I could answer. Sit down.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Jadi would not mark her receipt. I sat nine feet away from both of them in one day and watched one
> woman refuse to put her hand on an Office page and another woman pick up the pen and do it, in the
> same building, for the same law, and I understood that they were the same act.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Jadi has spent forty-four years being invisible. Nandi has spent forty years being the *record.* You
> cannot take a mark off a woman who has never had one, and you cannot protect a woman whose whole life
> has been signatures by refusing to sign.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the price of honesty (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Inspector, standing, reading the sheet back to himself, and then delivering the
consequence of the entry in his ordinary voice — the consequence being the whole reason the school
should have been afraid of Nandi's honesty.

**Image:** The Office discovering, on an ordinary afternoon, that its new instrument runs on the school's own ethics.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Witness, one consequence and then you can sit. That entry is a declaration. Under clause one you may
> not hold, own, license, employ, wage, tenancy or claim in this sector, from this bell. Under clause
> three nobody may work on that hand. And under clause two, a declared hand is *produced* when the
> Office requires it.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> You are, as of now, the only person in Agnikhand who can read an enrolment, and by your own entry you
> are also the only person in Agnikhand who may not be paid for it.

> **NANDI:**
> Then you'll have to keep me in a room and feed me, won't you.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> *(mild, and entirely serious)*
> Yes. That is what I came down here to offer you.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the offer — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the Inspector's hand setting a **key** on the table between them — a room key, plain,
Office-grey, with a paper tag on the ring and a number on the tag. Behind him, the clerk is already
packing the tray. Ira's hand is still on the register's lid, and in the frame's corner, Nima is standing
in the doorway with the afternoon's cards under her arm, waiting to be told what to do with them.

**Image:** The Office offering the school's archivist a shelf in its own building, at the exact price of
the shelf she lost.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Rooms eleven. Desk, lamp, a shelf, two bells a day, and a girl to train. Your custody ends this bell.
> You will not be asked to write a name and there is nothing in the schedule that requires one, because
> the entries have *seats* and the seats are knots.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> You would be the reader of the bigger book, witness. The Office has been running on copies for nine
> months. We would like it to run on hands.

> **NANDI:**
> *(a long pause; and then she asks the question that is not for the Inspector)*
>
> **NANDI (last line):**
> Keeper. If I take the key — does the register still keep its place at the stall, or does the Office
> move the keeping up here where the reading is?

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> And I stood there with my hand on the lid of the register and could not answer, because I could see
> the whole thing at once: the Office had found the way to take the school without taking anybody.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Offer the shelf back to the woman who kept it. Let her read. Let the keeper bring the book. In six
> months the school has a desk in a building, and the building has the only people who can understand
> it, and nobody has been arrested, fined, or hurt at all.

## Writing & art notes

- **Nandi's readings must be *professional*, not clever.** She identifies kind, year, hand, and what
  the person did for a living, in that order, every time. Draw the tray cards close and let her
  precision do the work; the reader should finish the page believing nobody else in the building could
  have done this.
- **Two cards break the hearing and each does a different job.** Card five is a bad copy, which gives
  Ira the keeper's one lawful move (*dispute the entry*) — she must use it exactly once and be careful
  about it. Card seven is *two systems copied as one*, which tells the Inspector that his own
  translation room has been inventing entries. Keep the second discovery dry and procedural: it is a
  finding about copy-work, not a scandal.
- **"That one's mine. Kshudra. Year one."** is the page's payload. Nandi entered the school in its first
  year, which places her beside Jadi as one of the founding hands — and makes her, by her own truthful
  answer, legally non-existent in Agnikhand. Do not let anyone react with awe; the clerk writes it and
  the Inspector prices it.
- **The two marks, one page apart, are the chapter's moral centre.** Jadi refuses the pen; Nandi takes
  it. Ira's caption must hold both without preferring either. This is the last thing the reader should
  be thinking about when the page ends.
- **The key on the table is the chapter's real escalation and it looks like a kindness.** Rooms eleven,
  a shelf, a girl to train — the Office offering the archivist her shelf back inside its own building.
  Draw it small and domestic. Nandi's closing question is aimed at Ira, not at the Inspector, and it is
  about the *keeping* — the place, not the woman.
- **Nima in the doorway is a plant.** She is carrying the afternoon's cards and waiting for orders, and
  she is the person Nandi will be asked to train. Page 006 puts them in a room together; Page 008 makes
  Nima's answer matter.
