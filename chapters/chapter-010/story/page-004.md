# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 004

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office, room nine, fourth bell
**Page type:** Hearing page — 7 panels

> Beats: Jadi's hand has been produced and filed *unreadable*; her receipt is unmarked; the slate carries
> the reserved line in chalk as **SUBJECT — M. SUTAR**; and room nine is booked at fourth bell for a
> witness.
>
> **This page:** Nandi, two days in custody, asked about knots.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the room re-set (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: room nine at fourth bell. The stand has been pushed to the wall and the table turned
around. The register — the original, in its banded case — stands open on the table because the keeper
was required to bring it, and Ira's hand is still on the lid. Beside it: a shallow **tray**, and in the
tray, face up, a dozen cards: small grey rectangles, each with a **knot drawn on it** in ink, in a copy
clerk's careful, needle-less hand.

**Image:** The instrument's paperwork, laid out to be identified by the only person who can read it.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Twelve cards. Every one of them a knot off my register, copied by a man who has never held a needle,
> and every one of them wrong in a way I could see from the doorway and could not say out loud, because
> a keeper who corrects the Office's copies is a keeper who has been reading things she wasn't asked
> about.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> That is the trap of the keeping, and I had been in it for exactly one day: I am the only person in
> Agnikhand who can tell a bad copy from a good one, and I am *forbidden, as keeper, to have opinions
> about the instrument.*

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, Nandi (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the door at the far end, and Nandi brought in. Two days in custody have changed
almost nothing — same coat, same pins, same flat hands. No chains. A clerk walks her to the chair and
she sits down the way she has sat down ten thousand times, which is to say: as if sitting were a task
and she intended to do it properly.

**Image:** The Mendery's archivist, in a room nine feet by eleven, sitting straight.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Two days. Nobody had hit her. She hadn't been charged. They had fed her, and her coat was brushed, and
> she came through that door with her hands folded the way she folds them at the shelf, and it was the
> worst thing I saw that day.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Because the woman who walked in was *comfortable.* She had been made comfortable, by the Office, for
> two days, and I stood at that table with the register open under my hand and understood that
> comfort is a thing the Office can buy in bulk.

> **NANDI:**
> *(sitting, to the clerk, no greeting, no glance at the room)*
> Keeper's here. Then say what you want and write it down.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the rules (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk's hands on the tray, and the schedule card from which he reads the witness's
rights in a flat voice. One line of the card is boxed in ink, and the box is drawn so the reader can
see it is the line about names.

**Image:** The Office, having learned that it does not need names, explaining the rules anyway.

> **CLERK:**
> Witness. You will be shown entries. For each, you may say what it is, or say nothing. There is no
> penalty for saying nothing. You may decline to answer at any point. Answering is entered under your
> name and you will be asked to mark the sheet.
>
> **NANDI:**
> And if what you show me isn't what you say it is?
>
> **CLERK:**
> Then say so. That's the whole of the procedure, witness. It's in the box: *the witness may dispute the
> entry.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it is. The Office drafted a hearing for a woman it has measured, indexed and kept for two days,
> and it gave her — in a box, on a card, in ink — the right to say *that is not what you say it is.*
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I have never hated a piece of good law so much.

---

## PANEL 4 — Large, the register opened (~16%)

**Camera:** Large panel: the table from above — the tray of twelve cards, the register open beside it at
its first spread, and every face in the room bent toward Nandi. Ira's hand is on the register's edge.
And on the register's **last line**, where the unnamed knot should be: a **hole** — a neat, four-sided
cut where the knot was lifted out of the paper, clean, from the inside, with no tearing at all.

**Image:** The one message Ira left for her mother, gone from a locked case in a locked cellar under an
Office desk.

> **IRA:**
> *(quiet, to the clerk, not to the room)*
> The register's last entry. Line forty-one. Where is the seat?
>
> **CLERK:**
> It was in the case when I signed for the case. Keeper — you carried the case up the stair.
>
> **IRA:**
> I carried it up the stair.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Then it was in the case when you left the stall, and it isn't in the case now, and the register's last
> line is a hole in a page. We'll note it. *Line forty-one: seat missing, keeper's custody.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Lift, not cut. Four sides, no tear, the stitch brought up whole the way you bring up a stitch — the way
> my mother does it, the way I did it thirty-one times on Chapter Nine's night, the way nobody else in
> Agnikhand has ever done anything.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Somebody came down to my cellar in the dark, in a locked room, under a desk the Office put there, and
> took one knot out of the Office's own register, and left everything else exactly where it was.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And I stood in room nine with my hand on the lid and thought: *she read the message. Four nights down
> the list, and she came DOWN.*

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, Nandi's eyes (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Nandi's face, and she has not looked at Ira once — but at the words *line forty-one,
seat missing* her eyes come up off the tray, cross the table, and land on Ira's hands, and stay there,
and the panel should hold on that: no expression, no nod, no question. Two professionals in a hostile
room, agreeing about something they will not say.

**Image:** The archivist and the keeper, understanding each other inside a hearing that is being written
down.

> **NANDI:**
> *(still looking at Ira's hands, flatly, to the room)*
> Show me the cards, then. Or don't. It's your bell.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She didn't nod. She didn't blink. She looked at my hands, which are the only two things in that room
> that can do what was done to that page, and then she went back to being a witness.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Two days of comfort, and in the middle of it, in front of a clerk with a pen, the woman who kept the
> shelf for forty years told me *not now.*

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, why she is here (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Inspector, in the room's dark corner where the reader normally sits, and the
panel makes him part of the furniture of the room: he has been standing there since Panel 1 and has said
nothing. Now he explains the hearing's actual purpose, mildly, without a single threat in it.

**Image:** The Office explaining why it keeps a witness it does not need to charge.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Witness, you should know what this hearing is for, because I dislike telling people afterwards.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> The register has forty-one lines and one of them is a knot. We can read the *year* on a chain and we
> cannot read a *hand.* You are the last person alive who has handled the school's enrolments for forty
> years and you will be asked which hands in this sector match which entries. That is the hearing.
>
> **NANDI:**
> And if I say nothing?
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> Then we continue exactly as we are, witness. You go back to your room, the register stays a list, and
> we call the woman who sewed beside you for ten years and ask her instead.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it was. Not a threat: a *substitution.* Nobody was going to be punished for anything. Somebody
> else was simply going to be *asked,* and the asking was going to be entered, and it was going to take
> four minutes either way.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the first card — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk's hand lifting the first card out of the tray and setting it down in front
of Nandi — and it is not a copy of a register knot at all. It is a **fold**: a small square of paper
with a crease pressed into it, drawn from above, in the same copyist's ink, and the crease is drawn with
a bead of wax at one end. Nandi looks at it for one long second, and her hands — which have not moved
once since she sat down — close flat on the table's edge.

**Image:** The first thing the Office wants identified is not a knot. It is the fold from the school's
own letter.

> **CLERK:**
> First entry. Witness, is this a school enrolment?
>
> **NANDI:**
> ...
>
> **CLERK:**
> Witness.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(and she does not look at the card, or at the clerk, or at Ira)*
> That's a fold. That's from a letter.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Whose letter?
>
> **NANDI (last line):**
> Ask the woman who pressed the wax. She's not in this room, and she isn't going to be in any room you
> build.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> And that is how I found out that the Office's first card — the one at the top of the tray, the one
> they brought a witness in for — was the fold out of the school's letter to the principal.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> They don't have the mother's hand. They have her *paperwork.* And they are going to identify her from
> it — with my mother's own archivist in the chair and the keeper of their register standing at their
> elbow, because neither of those two women can walk out of the room without losing the school.

## Writing & art notes

- **Nandi's comfort is the page's most frightening detail and must be drawn, not narrated.** Brushed
  coat, folded hands, a chair with a cushion on it. The Office does not need to hurt her; it needs her
  to be *well enough to answer*, and the reader should understand that this is how it treats the people
  it intends to use.
- **The missing knot is Ira's mother's move, drawn exactly like Ira's own Chapter 009 work** — four
  clean sides, stitch lifted whole, no tear. No fingerprints, no note, no trace, nothing else in the
  cellar disturbed. Nobody should say the word *mother* on this page; Ira's captions infer it and stop.
- **The dispute clause is canon and it matters.** *The witness may dispute the entry.* Chapter 011 uses
  it. Letter it in a box on the schedule card in Panel 3.
- **The Inspector's substitution threat is the Office's whole method: never punish, always offer to ask
  somebody else.** He must be entirely sincere and regretful about the disclosure. No menace in the
  frame; no one else in the room reacts.
- **The first card is the fold, and this is the page's reveal.** The Office has been reconstructing the
  school's correspondence from paper rather than from hands — which means the tray's twelve cards
  include things that are *not knots at all*, and Nandi's next answers (Page 005) will separate the
  entries that are real from the entries that are inventions.
- **Keep the reserved line and the missing knot out of each other's way.** The slate's chalk line (Page
  003) is *the Office's* move; the lifted knot is *the mother's.* Ira should be shown holding both facts
  in the same panel of her face, and she should say nothing.
