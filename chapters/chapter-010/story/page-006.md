# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 006

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Reckoning Office, room nine and the ground-floor corridor
**Page type:** Enrolment page — 7 panels

> Beats: Nandi identified her own enrolment knot out loud and marked the sheet; she is entered as
> *unreadable* in the register she spent forty years keeping clean; the Office has offered her rooms
> eleven, a shelf, and a girl to train.
>
> **This page:** what the school's archivist says to the school's newest pupil, and what the keeper
> carries home.

---

## PANEL 1 — Close, the answer (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the key on the table and Nandi's hand over it — but not taking it. Her fingers are
spread flat above the tag, and her eyes are on Ira, across the table, where they have been for the
whole of the Inspector's offer.

**Image:** A woman answering an Office offer with a condition the Office cannot read.

> **NANDI:**
> Keeper stays the keeper.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> The register remains at the place of keeping, witness. That is the schedule.
>
> **NANDI:**
> Then here's mine, and it goes in the box with the other one. Every entry I read, I read with the
> keeper present, and the keeper reads it out loud with me — same words, same entry, same time. If I'm
> the reader of the bigger book, the keeper's the voice of it.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> *(a pause of one second — the only hesitation he has shown in two chapters)*
> Why?
>
> **NANDI:**
> Because I've kept a shelf of paper for forty years and I know exactly what happens to a record nobody
> reads out loud.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He wrote it in. He wrote it in *gladly.* I watched the pen and I understood that I had just been given
> a desk in the Office of my own instrument, by an archivist, in front of a clerk, in the name of
> openness — and that the whole thing was legal, and I had no grounds to refuse a single word of it.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the two of them (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium, the corridor outside room nine: a bench, ash-light through a high window.
**Nandi**, on her way to be processed as a room-eleven resident, and **Nima**, who has been standing
there with the tray under her arm for the whole hearing. The clerk has stepped away. Ira is two steps
back and has not been introduced. The two Kshudra women look at each other: one is eighty-odd, one is
nineteen, and both of them are wearing a braid in the left palm.

**Image:** The school, meeting itself in a government corridor.

> **NIMA:**
> You said year one.
>
> **NANDI:**
> I said year one.
>
> **NIMA:**
> Then you know my page. *(and she turns her left hand over, palm up, in a corridor, in the light, for
> the first time all day)* — Year twenty-one. Ninth terrace. I was nine.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(reading it the way she read the tray: eleven seconds, no touching)*
> Kshudra. Year twenty-one, spring. Root of the left palm, two strands, the inner one laid by a
> fourteen-year-old because the woman in the cellar had gone two winters with a bad wrist and used the
> children's hands for the fine work. Enrolled after the second bell, sent home before the third.

> **NIMA:**
> ...She used the children.
>
> **NANDI:**
> She couldn't hold a needle steady in a cellar in February, girl. She taught the older ones to sew, and
> then she let the older ones sew you.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the page (~15%)

**Camera:** Close on Nandi's face and Nima's, in profile, facing each other on a bench — and between
them, drawn as if floating, the notional page: the Book of the Hand's layout, one page, one knot, and
one line in the mother's writing. Only the *line* is lettered; the knot is drawn.

**Image:** The amended rule three, delivered by the archivist, in a corridor, for free.

> **NIMA:**
> What did she write. On my page.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(no softening, no pause, the flat voice of a woman reading a shelf)*
> *Nima. Kshudra. Ties two threads at once. Counts to forty without a slate. Will teach.*
>
> **NIMA:**
> ...
>
> **NANDI:**
> That's it. That's the whole page. There was never a name and there was never a year on that page,
> girl, and there is one line — the line I just said — and it is the only sentence about you that has
> ever existed in writing anywhere in this basin.

> **NIMA:**
> *(and it goes into her like a needle: not grief. *Recognition.*)*
> She wrote that I count.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, why they hired her (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Nima's hands, holding the tray, and the reader should see at last — the tray is
**full** of cards, forty-odd, and the one on top is a knot drawn by a hand that copied it wrong. Nima is
looking at it. Nandi's hand comes into frame and turns the top card over.

**Image:** The Office's new reader understanding what she has been hired to do with her own people.

> **NIMA:**
> They gave me the desk in the spring because I count. I count chits, entries, cards, marks. Nobody told
> me what the knots were for. I thought — *(she stops)*
>
> **NANDI:**
> You thought they were paper.
>
> **NIMA:**
> I thought they were paper.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(flat, not unkind)*
> Then sit down, girl, because I have got forty years of shelf in my head and they have given me a
> lamp, and you and I are going to spend the rest of our lives being the only two people in Agnikhand
> who can tell a page from a palm.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And that is how the school's record was handed over: not taken, not bought, not betrayed.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> *Taught.* In a corridor. On a bench. By the archivist to the reader, because the archivist had been
> asked a question she could answer, and nobody in that building — nobody — had thought to say the word
> *confidential.*

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, Ira's ledger (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira, alone at the end of the corridor, with the register in its case under one arm —
and a **clerk** holding out a folded sheet to her, the standard Office wrap-mail, sealed with grey and
countersigned with the day's bell-mark. She has both hands full. She takes it in her teeth, which is
the only undignified thing she does in the chapter, and the reader should enjoy it.

**Image:** The keeper, leaving the building with the instrument and the day's list.

> **CLERK:**
> Keeper. Claims list from the vestibule, for entry at the place of keeping. Fourteen today. They'll
> come to your counter from first light — that's the procedure, it's on the board.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(as she takes it)*
> Fourteen's a lot. It was one this morning.
>
> **IRA:**
> What was it this afternoon?
>
> **CLERK:**
> Fourteen. And the man in claim four has been telling the fourth terrace all day that the Office pays
> for *colours.* *(and he shrugs, the shrug of a man who has watched this happen before)* They'll bring
> you hands now, keeper. That's what a board does.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the list (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the sheet, opened, in Ira's hands on the stair — fourteen ruled lines, each with a
claimant's name in a clerk's hand and, in the *subject* column, not a single name: *subject: mother;*
*subject: wife;* *subject: self;* *subject: neighbour, fourth terrace;* *subject: self (left hand);*
very few of them *subject: child.* And at the foot, the fee line: *audit charged to the party entered.*

**Image:** The bounty's arithmetic, in the basin's own handwriting, arriving at the school's door.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Fourteen claimants. Eight of them men, six of them women, one of them filing a claim against her own
> left hand so that if the Office ever paid out she would be the one who got the money for having been
> born in this basin with a colour under her skin.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And every one of them will be entered by *me.* In my writing. In my cellar. In the morning, at my
> counter, in front of my pupils.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The Office found the one thing I cannot refuse, and it is not a threat. It is that if I stop writing,
> somebody else sits at my desk — and the somebody else is a clerk who has never once looked at a hand
> in his life and will write down whatever the claimant says.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, going down — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close, from above: the Council Stair on the way down, forty-one steps, ash falling thicker,
and Ira three steps down with the register under one arm, the claims list in the other hand, and — in
the frame's lower corner, small, on the fourth terrace below — a shape that has been there for a while:
Jadi, sitting on the terrace's edge-wall, waiting the way a woman waits who has been told what the
teacher did and has decided to walk the teacher home.

**Image:** The keeper going down a stair with two documents and no name for what she is now.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> I came up this morning to produce one hand. One. A knot, a chain, a grey receipt and out.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I am going down with the Office's register under one arm and fourteen claims in the other, and up there
> behind me there is an archivist with a room and a lamp and a girl, and the only reason I have any of it
> is that the school was worth *reading.*
>
> **CAPTION (last line):**
> Jadi was waiting on the fourth terrace, and she didn't ask me how it went. She looked in my hands,
> counted the papers, and said: *fourteen.*
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Then she stood up and walked down with me, and for the first time since I got here I had somebody on
> the stair who had been on it before.

## Writing & art notes

- **Nandi's condition is the chapter's structural bargain and it must be delivered in the school's
  register:** practical, unphilosophical, out loud in front of the clerk. *"I know exactly what happens
  to a record nobody reads out loud."* The Inspector's one-second hesitation is the only crack he shows
  in two chapters; use it once and never explain it.
- **The corridor scene is the chapter's heart and it must not be staged as a betrayal.** Nandi is not
  handing the school over — she is *teaching*, and teaching is what the school has always done at
  benches, counters and holes in doors. Ira's caption names it in one line and then stops.
- **Nima's page is the payoff of Chapter 009 Page 009's new canon** (what each pupil was good at,
  written under the knot). Give three items only: ties, counts, will teach. The *counts to forty without
  a slate* line is why the Office hired her, and the reader should feel the whole irony without being
  told it.
- **"She used the children."** Keep this exchange exact and short. It is a new fact about the school —
  the mother's hands were bad for two winters and the older pupils sewed the younger ones — and it
  reframes the hatch as something other than a single saintly pair of hands. No character condemns it;
  Nandi states it as shelf-fact.
- **The fourteen claims must be drawn as paperwork, not menace.** Ruled lines, clerk's hand, and a
  *subject* column in which almost nobody is named. The one claimant filing against herself is the
  detail that sells the bounty's economy; draw her line plainly and let Ira's caption carry it.
- **Jadi waiting on the fourth terrace is the page's ending and it is warm.** No dialogue beyond
  *fourteen.* The chapter's second movement closes with the two of them going down the stair together,
  which is the school walking home from the Office on its own two feet.
