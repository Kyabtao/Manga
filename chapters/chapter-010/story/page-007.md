# THREADBORN — Chapter 10 — Page 007

**Chapter title:** *Room Nine* / *कमरा नौ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Mendery counter, first light
**Page type:** Counter page — 7 panels

> Beats: fourteen claims for entry at the place of keeping; the keeper must read every produced palm
> herself; Nandi's condition puts her voice on every entry that is read.
>
> **This page:** the morning the bounty does its arithmetic in Ira's own handwriting.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the queue (~13%)

**Camera:** Wide, first light, ash falling: the pot-row outside the Mendery, and a **queue that runs
past three stalls** — not the Chapter 009 queue of pupils wanting to be entered, but claimants. Men
mostly, some holding a second person's hand. Two people in the line are holding their OWN left hand in
their right, palm turned up, rehearsing. At the counter: Ira with the register open and the claims list
under her elbow; Kessa at her end with her ordinary ledger; **Jadi** inside the shutters with a bowl and
an expression.

**Image:** The Office, achieving in one morning what nine months of measuring could not.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There were fourteen on my list at first bell and twenty-six on my step by the second. The Office had
> not sent one clerk down here. It had put eleven words on a board at the foot of a stair, and by
> sunrise the Ashfall had organised itself into a queue.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the duty (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's hands, and a claimant's palm — a man in his thirties, his wife standing behind
him with her hand over her own mouth. Ira reads the palm the way she reads a seam, close, thumb at the
root. No braid. No second strand. A working hand with an old burn across the heel of it.

**Image:** The keeper discovering that her office has made her the basin's palm-inspector.

> **IRA:**
> No strand. Nothing sewn. That's a burn from a kettle and it's older than my licence.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(at Ira's shoulder with the book, writing)* Entry fourteen: no strand. Declined. Fee — charged to the
> party entered. Two coppers.
>
> **CLAIMANT:**
> Two coppers —
>
> **IRA:**
> *(to the clerk, without looking up)*
> Put the fee to the Office. She didn't ask to be read.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Keeper, that's not the schedule.
>
> **IRA:**
> Then write *keeper disputes the fee* next to it, and we'll find out which of us is expensive.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the searched (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: a woman's palm, weathered, and across it — not a cut, **seven small healed cuts**, a
fan of them, old, spread the way a search spreads. Ira's face above it, and she has to write while she
looks at it, and the panel should make the pen the ugliest object on the page.

**Image:** A basin that has already started auditing itself.

> **WOMAN:**
> They did it in the night. Four of them, at my door, with a lamp. I said there's nothing in there, and
> they said the Office pays for colours, so they looked.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(writing, flat)*
> No strand. Nothing sewn. Seven wounds, healed, consistent with a search.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(quieter, to her)*
> Who.
>
> **WOMAN:**
> Keeper, if I tell you, you'll write that down too, and then there'll be an entry, and then there'll be
> a fee for it. Let it go. I want to go home and not be paper this week.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And I did let it go, and that was the tenth entry in my handwriting that morning, and I have thought
> about it since, at night, most nights.

---

## PANEL 4 — Large, the sister (~16%)

**Camera:** Large panel: a man in his forties holding out a **woman's hand** — his sister's, in her
fifties, standing beside him. Her palm is braided: grey strand and a second strand crossing at the root,
forty years old, worn flat with work. She is not being held against her will; her arm is out, her sleeve
is already rolled, and her eyes are on Ira's, and she is the calmest person in the frame. Jadi, inside
the shutters, has come to the window and gone still.

**Image:** The school's own hand, offered for money, in a queue, on purpose.

> **BROTHER:**
> Keeper. My sister. She was enrolled at the hatch in the second year, she'll tell you herself, look at
> the seam —
>
> **IRA:**
> *(to the sister, not the brother)* 
> Ma'am. Do you want this entered?
>
> **SISTER:**
> Enter it.
>
> **IRA:**
> Do you know what an entry costs? You can't hold a stall, sell to a licensed trader, be owed a debt, or
> be worked on by any mender in the basin. Ever. From this bell.
>
> **SISTER:**
> *(perfectly level)* 
> My brother's rent is due. There is no work in this sector that pays coin to a Kshudra woman over
> fifty, and I have had this hand forty years and it has kept me *nothing.* Enter it, take the fee off
> his claim, and let him have the rest.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Forty years. The school's second year. Somebody's mother put two strands into that palm through a hole
> in a door and told her nothing, and today she is in a queue offering it to the Office for the price of
> a room, on purpose, standing up straight, because it is the only thing she owns that the Office has
> ever said it would pay for.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the keeper's answer (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's hand closing the register, deliberately, with the claim half-written — and then
opening it at the **second** spread and writing on a fresh line, in her own hand, one entry only, and
under it a single word the reader can see: **DECLARED.**

**Image:** The keeper's one move: enter the hand at its owner's word, so that no claimant is ever paid
for it.

> **IRA:**
> Then it goes in as *yours.* Declared by the owner, at her own word, at first light, in front of two
> witnesses.
>
> **BROTHER:**
> But the reward —
>
> **IRA:**
> There's no reward for a hand that's already entered, sir. The board says *payment on verification.* I've
> verified it, and it wasn't your hand, and it wasn't your word. *(to the clerk)* Enter the declaration.
> No fee; the subject declared herself.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Keeper, that's —
>
> **IRA:**
> That's clause two, verbatim, and I'll read it out loud for the record if you'd like.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And there it was: the only thing I had learned in room nine, doing the only thing it was any good for.
> I cannot stop the Office paying for hands. But I can *enter the hands first* — at their owners' asking
> — and then there is nothing left to claim but the person.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, what it costs (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the sister's face as she understands that her brother will not be paid — no anger at
Ira, no thanks either. She looks down at her own palm, forty years worn, and then folds her fingers over
it. Beside her, Kessa has come to the counter end and is watching, doing the arithmetic out loud,
because somebody has to.

**Image:** The trap, stated by the only person in the room who does not confuse it with a victory.

> **KESSA:**
> Twenty-six today, child. Sixty tomorrow. You can't enter the basin faster than the basin can claim it,
> and every entry you write takes a wage off the person you're protecting.
>
> **KESSA:**
> You're not fighting a bounty. You're fighting *poverty,* with a pen, one line at a time, in a
> hand-writing that everybody in this sector can recognise.
>
> **SISTER:**
> *(to Kessa, tired, not bitter)*
> She's all right, auntie. She kept my hand mine. I'd have taken the coin, and I'd have hated myself in
> a week, and now I've got nothing and I don't hate anybody.
>
> **SISTER:**
> That's not a small thing in this basin.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the slip from up-terrace — CLOSING HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the young grey clerk, out of breath, threading through the queue with a slip held up
above his head so it does not get wet, and on the slip, in Office grey, one line — and further down the
page, in the blank where a claimant's name goes, a **thumbprint** instead of a name. Ira takes it with
ink on both hands.

**Image:** The claim that is not about hands.

> **CLERK:**
> Keeper — priority, room nine, not from the vestibule. A claimant came up the stair an hour ago and
> filed at the desk instead of by post, which nobody does.
>
> **IRA:**
> What's the claim?
>
> **CLERK:**
> He says he'll produce the founder. Tomorrow, fourth bell, room nine. *(reading)* Claim is accepted —
> production scheduled, keeper to attend as keeper of the register.
>
> **CLERK (last line):**
> He wouldn't give a name and he wouldn't take a fee chit. He put his thumb on it and walked out. Keeper —
> the Office doesn't accept thumbprints. They accepted this one.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> Forty years nobody in this basin has touched her. Four days ago I gave her a list of forty-one names.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> And I have spent that whole morning writing other people's hands into the Office's book, at the only
> desk in Agnikhand the whole pot-row can watch me work at, and *somebody has just offered to sell my
> mother.*

## Writing & art notes

- **This is the chapter's argument in one page: the bounty is an economy, not a policy.** Draw the queue
  as commerce — a moneylender's queue, not a mob, and no violence anywhere except the woman with seven
  healed cuts, which must be small, plain and undramatic.
- **Ira's two unlawful-in-spirit moves must both be *lawful* and both be small:** shifting a fee to the
  Office by dispute, and entering a hand at its owner's own word so no claimant is paid. No rule is
  broken; the instrument is used against itself. Chapter 011 raises the price of doing that.
- **The sister is the page's centre and she is not a victim.** Her arm is out, her sleeve is already
  rolled, and she speaks for herself while her brother speaks over her — Ira addresses her and only her.
  *"I'd have taken the coin, and I'd have hated myself in a week"* is the chapter's most honest
  sentence, and it should be delivered without a shred of self-pity.
- **Kessa must be allowed to be right in Panel 6.** Twenty-six today, sixty tomorrow; entering the basin
  one line at a time takes a wage off everyone protected. Ira does not answer her, because there is no
  answer; the reader should end the page understanding that Ira's victory is small, local, and
  expensive.
- **The claimant's thumbprint is the hook and it stays unexplained.** The Office does not accept
  thumbprints, which means somebody *spoke for him* at the desk. Do not show the man; do not describe
  him; the clerk's puzzlement is the whole delivery.
- **Continuity:** the sister is the second braided hand the register has seen after Jadi; Nima's and
  Nandi's situations are untouched by this page; the fee economy established on Page 002 is the engine
  of the whole sequence and must stay visible in the clerk's book.
