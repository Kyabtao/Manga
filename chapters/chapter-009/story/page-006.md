# THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 006

**Chapter title:** *The Reading* / *पाठ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Mendery, the cellar, first bell
**Page type:** The held stop — **CHAIN-STOP SPENT** — 7 panels

> Beats: the certification is signed, the objection is on the sheet and on Kessa's thread, and the
> Office has said out loud that it came to buy its reader rather than to catch forty-one people.
>
> **This page:** the chain stands still from the first word to the last, and the lie is written where
> it cannot be unwritten. **The chapter's one chain-stop is spent here. No other stop anywhere in
> Chapter 009.**

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the ceremony (~13%)

**Camera:** The cellar, first bell, cold and careful. The register open on the desk; the certification
sheet beside it; the clerk's pen already dipped. Ira stands at the desk with one hand flat on the
keeper's box. Rekhak stands opposite. The Inspector is on the third step, hands behind his back.
Kessa is in the doorway with her arms folded. **The first pupil is not in the room** — the stool she
sat on yesterday is against the wall, empty, and the reader should notice it before Ira's caption
tells them why.

**Image:** A room with four people in it and a fifth who was deliberately not asked.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> I did not call Jadi. She would have come. That's the reason I didn't call her: she would have come,
> and stood in the room, and watched the law get a signature put to it, and there is no version of
> that where the school keeps anything.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Her stool was against the wall. I moved it out of the way with my foot.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the mechanics (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on the chain in Rekhak's hand — the counting-chain, brass warm with use, the one
dark link thumb-side. His fist closes around its middle. The clerk reads the procedure from the
schedule in a flat voice, exactly as written, because that is what a clerk is for.

**Image:** The instrument of the chapter's cost, held in a fist so it cannot run.

> **CLERK:**
> Reader. Close the chain. From the first word of the sentence to the last word of the sentence the
> links shall be motionless. Any motion voids the oath and the certification is taken up-terrace.
>
> **CLERK:**
> There is no penalty for a void oath. There is a *note* on the sheet. That's all it says. I'm reading
> it as written.

> **REKHAK:**
> I know the rule, boy. I've read it at other men.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the sentence (~15%)

**Camera:** Close on Rekhak's mouth and jaw, the chain below it in frame, **frozen** — settled links,
vanished motion-blur, sudden detail, drawn per the physics established on Chapter 002 Page 006. Ira's
face occupies the frame's other half, at the desk, reading him as he speaks, because as keeper and
mender she is the only one in the room who can see the words go in.

**Image:** The Office's drafted sentence, spoken by a man who knows every word of it is false.

> **REKHAK (sworn, first balloon):**
> A braided hand returns no year.
>
> **REKHAK (sworn, second balloon):**
> And no hand in this basin can be made to return two.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Eleven words. He said them the way a reader says everything — level, and at the pace of a man
> counting, and he never once looked at the desk.

---

## PANEL 4 — Large, the stop older than the sentence (~17%)

**Camera:** Large panel: the room, held. Rekhak has finished speaking and has not released the chain.
The clerk's pen is above the sheet and not moving. The Inspector's mild face, unchanged. Kessa's arms
still folded. And on the desk, the register's first spread, with the ink on its **first line** gone a
half-shade *deeper* — the drawn palm, in the corner of the panel, darkening, as if something had been
entered without being written.

**Image:** The silence after an oath that was not broken, in a room where four people know the
sentence was a lie — and one of them can see what that lie is doing to a page.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Held. One breath would have done it. Two would have made him safe.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And in the middle of it — I don't know how to say this in a document — I watched the drawn hand on
> line one get *darker.* No hand went near the desk. Nothing was written. It was as though the page
> had been told something and had agreed.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the deposit (~15%)

**Camera:** Close, macro: Rekhak's own **ledger** — brass-cornered, oil-cloth, the fore-edge line of
light from Chapter 009 Page 003 now a thin open seam — and on the page, a line being written in
**his own hand**, his letters, his pressure, his angle. Four words and a full stop, and then the hand
stops. Ira's face, half-lit at the frame edge, reading it across the desk.

**Image:** A false word under a held stop, returned to its owner in the only ledger he answers for.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The deposit doesn't call him a liar. That would be a mercy. It writes the true version of the false
> sentence, in his handwriting, on the page he answers for:
>
> **CAPTION (lettered large, art):**
> *A hand in this basin has been made to return two. I read it.*
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I read it off the page as it came up, and nobody in that room knew I was reading anything but a man
> standing at a desk. Kessa would have known. Kessa was in the doorway with her arms folded, looking
> at his *hands*, and when the writing finished she unfolded them.

> **KESSA (one line, flat):**
> There it is.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the release (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the release. His fist opens **one finger at a time** — the Chapter 002 physics,
reused deliberately: the chain starts like a heart, not a switch. The clerk marks the reader's box.
Ira marks the keeper's box. The certification sheet is lifted, blotted, and laid flat to dry. The
Inspector comes down the last two steps and looks at the sheet, and at the register, and does not
touch either.

**Image:** A document completed by four people, three of whom were only doing their jobs and one
of whom was not.

> **CLERK:**
> Reader certified. Keeper certified. It's a register.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> Thank you, Reader. Twenty years and I have never once had cause to question a reading of yours.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> You will now.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> Yes. That is what we bought.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He said it without pleasure and without shame, the way you'd say a weight, and then he turned to me
> with an administrator's face for the next item on the list, and I understood that the buying of
> Rekhak was not the purpose of the morning.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> It was the *paperwork before* the purpose of the morning.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the next item — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the Inspector, mild, holding out a **second sheet** — thinner, cheaper, a form with
a blank at the top the width of a name. In the panel's lower corner, small: up-terrace, on a wall, the
compliance chain, **running**, and beside it a fresh sheet wired to the same board, still curling at
one corner.

**Image:** The instrument, having been certified, doing the only thing an instrument does: asking for
an identification.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> One administrative matter, keeper, and then I'm out of your cellar.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> The instrument read a hand last night, up-terrace. Under clause two that reading is a declaration —
> automatic, no form required. The register therefore stands with one line to fill.
>
> **IRA:**
> Then fill it.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> We can't. *We don't have the hand.* The register requires the keeper of the place of keeping to
> identify it — that's the whole reason the Office keeps a register in a stall instead of a vault.
> You have three days from this bell.
>
> **INSPECTOR (last line):**
> Write the name, keeper. Or write *unknown* and tell me, on the record, that you do not know whose
> hand it was that you put a second strand into at the fourth hour past third bell.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> They didn't make me the keeper of a list.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> They made me the *pointer.*

## Writing & art notes

- **BUDGET: this is the stop. No chain stops anywhere else in Chapter 009 after Panel 3.** Later
  panels show the chain running; the dark link stays exactly where canon puts it (one darkened still
  link, thumb-side, always, from Chapter 002 Page 006 until a much later arc spends it). Do **not**
  add a second darkened link for the deposit — the deposit is written in his **ledger**, not in the
  brass.
- **Draw the stop as physics, not magic:** settled links, no motion-blur, sudden detail; the release
  in Panel 6 is one finger at a time. The chain starts like a heart, not a switch.
- **The deposit is text in art and it must be legible** (it is the one place this chapter breaks the
  no-legible-prose rule besides the word RESERVED): *A hand in this basin has been made to return
  two. I read it.* Four sentences' worth of consequence in eight words, in **his** hand — draw the
  letterforms as his, not the Office's.
- **The register's first line darkening is the page's strangest image and it must be almost
  invisible.** No glow, no light, no effect; the drawn palm simply sits a half-shade deeper at the
  end of the panel than at the start. Nobody in the room reacts except Ira, in caption, and she
  admits she cannot explain it in a document. The same effect recurs on Page 007.
- **The Inspector's mildness is structural.** He thanks Rekhak sincerely, confirms the purchase, and
  moves to the next line item. He never threatens; the threat is the *agenda.*
- **The hook must be a request for a name and nothing else.** No soldiers, no arrest, no summons for
  Jadi — an identification form with a blank at the top and a three-day clock. Everything Chapter 009
  Pages 007–010 do comes out of that blank, and no character should explain the trap: Ira's last two
  captions state it and stop.
- **Continuity carried forward:** Rekhak's readings are now reviewable (the Office can re-open twenty
  years of basin records); Kessa's thread holds the second, unauditable copy of the objection; the
  register is kept in Ira's cellar with entry one reserved; Nandi is one day into custody with no
  charge; Jadi's palms were braided before the commencement bell and she has made no mark on any
  Office page.
