# THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 008

**Chapter title:** *The Reading* / *पाठ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Ash-sLums, the Mendery counter; the pot-row
**Page type:** Consequence page — 7 panels

> Beats: Ira entered the first declared hand in the register as kind, school-year and a knot — no
> name — and the Inspector called it a *translation* and said the Office is very good at those.
>
> **This page:** what the pot-row does when it hears that the keeper writes hands instead of names.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the counter at second bell (~13%)

**Camera:** Wide: the Mendery counter, mid-afternoon, and there is a **queue** — six people, then more
outside, standing along the pot-row wall in the ash-light, waiting. Not a mob: a line. Basin people,
mostly Kshudra, and every single one of them is holding **one hand** — left, mostly, palm turned in,
the way you hold a hand you want somebody to look at.

**Image:** The school, queueing. Voluntarily. At the Office's own desk, in the Office's own instrument.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> By the second bell there were eleven of them outside the shutters and by the fourth there were
> twenty. Nobody had been sent for. Nobody had been rounded up. They had heard a thing and they had
> come down to the stall to do it on purpose.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the first in the line (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: an old woman at the counter, palm up, and her braid — the seam along the root of
the palm with two strands crossing in it, drawn plainly, a working hand. She is not frightened. She is
*impatient*, the way people are impatient in a queue for something necessary.

**Image:** A person who has been invisible for thirty years, waiting to be entered.

> **WOMAN:**
> Keeper. I was at the hatch in the year the bells changed. Two strands, both mine, and I've worked
> forty years on them and never once had a paper that said so.
>
> **WOMAN:**
> I heard you write it as a knot. Write mine as a knot.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> That is the sentence I have not been able to get out of my head since the second bell: *I heard you
> write it as a knot.* Not *please protect me.* Not *is this safe.*
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Thirty years of being uncountable, and the first time anybody offers them a *page*, they queue up to
> be on it — in their own language, by their own teacher, before anybody can put them there in
> somebody else's.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, why they came (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira at the desk, pen in hand, and Rekhak standing behind the counter with his arms
folded — he has appointed himself keeper's assistant without being asked, and nobody has objected.
Through the shutters: the line. On the desk: the register, three lines now filled, and a **pile of
small knotted tags** — the knots the queue has been tying for her on scraps of their own thread,
because they know how this works and she does not have to explain it.

**Image:** The school's method, running in the open, at an Office desk, at speed.

> **REKHAK:**
> They'll all be entered by nightfall. Twenty-eight, thirty, they'll bring their children's too.
>
> **IRA:**
> Why? Kessa said it this morning — declaring yourself costs you your licence, your wage-book, your
> tenancy. Clause one. I'm writing people *out* of every book in the basin.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> Keeper, you've never been poor. A woman with no paper is already out of every book. All you're doing
> is giving her the *time of day* on the record. The Office taught this basin one lesson in nine
> months and it taught it well: the only thing worse than being on a list is being found on one
> later.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And there it is. The Office did not have to hunt. It built a desk, put a mender behind it, and the
> basin organised its own enrolment, because being counted *by one of us* is the first dignity anybody
> has offered them in forty years.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the room with two books in it (~15%)

**Camera:** Close on the cellar door frame, and *through* it, in one deep composition: the desk with
the register, and on the shelves behind it the **Book of the Hand** in its cloth, and the register's
open pages — the two of them in one frame, facing each other across four feet of cellar.

**Image:** The school's record and the Office's record, kept in the same room, one knot away from
speaking to each other.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Line four was a rope-splicer's. Line seven was a woman who keeps the third water-cart. Nine of the
> eleven knots in that register are also stitched into the Book of the Hand, four feet away, on a
> shelf, in the same room, because that is where the Office put the desk.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Any clerk who can count can check one against the other. Any clerk who can't can be *taught* in an
> afternoon.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I stood in the doorway with a tag in my hand and I thought: if they requisition the Book, they don't
> need the names at all. They'll have the *hands.*

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, Kessa prices it (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: Kessa at the end of the counter with a bowl and a ledger of her own open — she is
taking the queue's names for the *stall's* books, the ordinary kind, the ones that answer to her. She
writes a name down for a woman, tears the slip, hands it over, takes a coin. Then, without looking up:

**Image:** The only person in the room doing normal business, and therefore the only one who can see
the shape of the abnormality.

> **KESSA:**
> You've got a queue, child. That's not a crowd, that's a *market*, and markets get supplied.
>
> **KESSA:**
> One more thing and then I'll leave you to it. Nandi's token came up again. Office says she'll be
> *heard* in two days, which means they'll read her a list and ask her to say which of them she knows.
> She's got forty years of the school in her head and no charge against her, so there's nothing to
> bargain with except what she decides to say.

> **IRA:**
> She'll say nothing.
>
> **KESSA:**
> She'll say nothing *for a while,* and then somebody will ask her about a knot, and she'll look at it,
> and you know what she does when she looks at a knot.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the second bell (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the young grey clerk at the counter, hat in hand, at the end of the day, and this
time he is not reading anything aloud — he is holding out a **requisition**, pink-slipped, with a
crimson seal at the foot and a signature in a hand that is not an Office hand. The queue behind him
has stopped talking.

**Image:** The instrument asking for its source.

> **CLERK:**
> Keeper. The entry on line two was a knot. Verification requires the *seat* — the record the knot was
> taken from. That's the rule for any entry not written in letters.
>
> **IRA:**
> Which record?
>
> **CLERK:**
> The one the knots come off. *(he checks the slip)* It's described here. *A cloth-bound method book,
> forty years in use, one knot per enrolment, held at the Mendery.* They want it produced at the place
> of keeping within three days.
>
> **CLERK:**
> *(quieter, and he cannot help himself)*
> Keeper — it's signed. The requisition's signed by a private hand, not by the Office. I've never
> carried one of those before.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the crimson — CLOSING HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on the requisition's foot: the crimson seal, and above it the signature, drawn in
art as a hand's strokes nobody in the basin has ever seen — because in twenty years of Council
paperwork the principal has never once signed anything that left his building. Behind the sheet, in
the frame, the Book of the Hand on its shelf, and Ira's hand not quite on it.

**Image:** The principal, having filed nothing for forty years, filing from a distance for the first
time — and asking, on paper, for the one object the school cannot replace.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The register was the Office's. The ordinance was the Council's. The desk in my cellar was mine,
> because I was stupid eleven days ago and clever yesterday.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> But the requisition for the Book of the Hand was signed by the principal — the man whose name has
> never once been on a page that left his own building.
>
> **CAPTION (last line):**
> That means he wants it in his hands on purpose, which means he has finally decided what the school
> is *for*, and I have three days to find out before he tells us.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> And it means something else, and I am trying not to look at it, because looking at it is the whole
> of tomorrow: the principal does not need the Book. He has the copy.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> He needs the *knots.*

---

## Writing & art notes

- **The queue is the page and it must not look like a triumph.** Draw them waiting the way people wait
  for a ration, not the way people gather for a cause. Nobody is cheering. Somebody's child is holding
  their mother's hand up. One woman is doing her hair. The horror is that this is *normal paperwork*,
  and it is the school's own people volunteering.
- **The woman's line "I heard you write it as a knot" is the chapter's best compliment and its worst
  news.** It tells Ira her invention has already travelled the pot-row, and it is the mechanism by
  which the Office learns the school's language — from the school itself.
- **Do not show a single name being written for a declared hand.** The queue's names go into *Kessa's*
  stall ledger (ordinary commerce, a slip, a coin) and the register gets knots. Keeping those two
  written systems in one panel is the point.
- **The Book of the Hand and the register in one frame (Panel 4) is the structural image of Chapter
  009.** Four feet of cellar between the school's record and the Office's record. Do not caption it
  twice: Ira says it once and the rest is composition.
- **Nandi's "heard in two days" is the page's ticking clock and it stays off-panel.** No cutaway, no
  custody interior. State it through Kessa and leave it. Nandi is the one person who knows the knots
  well enough to be asked about them, and the reader should be left doing that arithmetic alone.
- **The principal's signature is the chapter's escalation.** Twenty years of pre-filing and never a
  signature; now one, on a requisition, for an object. Keep the signature abstract in art and let
  Ira's captions carry the inference — and end on the inference, not the object: *he needs the knots.*
