# THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Reading* / *पाठ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Mendery, the counter and the hatch alley
**Page type:** Night page — 7 panels

> Beats: the certification is signed, the Office intends to buy its reader, and the keeper has entered
> a written objection to the form of words before marking the box.
>
> **This page:** the lesson, the thread, and the first time Ira reads a man's oath off his own brass.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, lesson two (~13%)

**Camera:** The counter, evening, shutters half down over the alley. Jadi has her left hand open on
the counter and Ira has both of hers around a cup she is not drinking. This is the school's second
lesson and it is being conducted at a shop counter, at night, with a register open in the next room —
and the register is why the lesson is legal nowhere else in the basin.

**Image:** The amended rule three, in practice: the teacher doing the thing her mother never did.

> **IRA:**
> Rule three, as amended. The teacher's strand goes in first, and then the teacher tells you what she
> did. So before the lesson: yesterday I put a second strand in your palm because a braid carries two
> years and a chain that reads two years gets neither. That was the whole of what I did and the whole
> of why.
>
> **JADI:**
> And tomorrow?
>
> **IRA:**
> Tomorrow I stand at that desk as the keeper of the place of keeping and I read the words while a
> reader swears to them on a held stop.

> **JADI:**
> Good. That's the amendment working. You told me.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, Jadi's instruction (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on Jadi, flat, unimpressed, entirely practical. She is not moved by Ira's honesty
and she is not going to be told a rule and admire it; she is going to say what the school's first
pupil actually needs.

**Image:** Forty-four years of being ruled, making one request.

> **JADI:**
> Now hear mine. Tomorrow, when you're standing at the desk with your hand on the sheet, they will
> have a line for me. Seven, or nine, or twelve. Somewhere in the middle of a ruled page.
>
> **JADI:**
> They will want to write *unreadable* next to it, and a year, and then they will want me to make a
> mark under it so they know it was me. That's what a declaration is, girl. It's a mark.
>
> **JADI:**
> So don't let them put my mark under it. Not because I can't. Because a Kshudra mark on an Office
> page is the Office's, that's all it is, and you said rule three, so I get to say what I want in
> mine. Say it back.

> **IRA:**
> No mark under your line.
>
> **JADI:**
> *No mark under my line.*

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, Kessa's cuff (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Kessa's cuff turned back, her tally-thread across her knee in the lamplight, and
her fingers mid-knot. She is not doing this as a ceremony — she is doing it the way a trader does
arithmetic. The newest knot is a different shape from the others: a short bar, flat, like a line
crossed.

**Image:** The only copy, kept on a strand that has never been a document.

> **KESSA:**
> Objection noted twice: once on their sheet, once here.
>
> **KESSA:**
> Two versions, child. Theirs on paper that answers to them, mine on thread that answers to nobody.
> Cost me two inches and it can't be audited. Don't look at me like that — it's not sentiment, it's
> *stock.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Kessa has been keeping the school's business in her cuff since Chapter Seven, and I have never once
> heard her say that out loud. I count that as the closest she comes to saying what she is.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, Rekhak at the counter (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium, night, the stall closed. Rekhak stands, does not sit, and holds the chain out over
the counter with his fist closed around the middle of it so that it **cannot run**. Not a held stop —
a *presentation*: he has come to have something read.

**Image:** A man asking the only reader who can read him to do it, once, out loud, before tomorrow.

> **REKHAK:**
> Keeper. Before the bell, I want the last thing I swore read back to me.
>
> **IRA:**
> You were there when you swore it.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> I don't get to read brass, keeper. That's the joke of my whole trade. I wear a book I'll never open
> and I read everybody else's. You read thread and marks and stopped links. Read it.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the read (~15%)

**Camera:** Close, macro: the dark link between Ira's thumb and finger, off the chain, held up to the
lamp. Her eyes are half closed, mender-style, reading *through* the metal. In the link's interior,
drawn as a fold in the seam of a palm, the three sentences sit as faint ruled lines — abstract
strokes, only Ira's face tells the reader they are words.

**Image:** A mender reading an oath the way she reads a seam: not letters, a fold.

> **IRA:**
> The palm held a fold, not a door. I saw the fold. In the palm, I saw nothing else.
>
> **IRA:**
> Three sentences. They're true, all of them. That's the fold I read on the stair the morning the
> light went out.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> They were true and they were *small.* The hour held more than the oath spent. That's why it's
> written in my brass at all — the auditors wanted to see whether I'd cut it down. I did. They
> recorded that I could.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And that's how I learn what a held stop really does to a man. It doesn't mark him. It *files* him.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, what opens (~15%)

**Camera:** Close on Rekhak's hands turning the chain, and drawn behind them, small and grey in the
panel corner of the frame: the **ledger** — his own, brass-cornered, oil-cloth cover, fore-edge line
of light — and above it a hand that is not his reaching for it.

**Image:** The consequence of a false word under a held stop, stated plainly, once.

> **REKHAK:**
> You know why they want it under a held stop and not a breath-stop? A breath-stop is a *tell.* A
> held stop is a *ledger.* Word by word, in my hand, on the page I answer for.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> And when a deposit lands on a page, that page opens. I've read the rule out loud for twenty years
> and never once for myself. From tomorrow, the Office can open the ledger of the basin's reader and
> read every line I ever entered.
>
> **IRA:**
> ...Line one.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> *Nothing more.*
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Nine days ago, with the light gone out over the whole basin, a reader stood in front of me and said
> *nothing more* about a hand, and his chain stopped, and a Council record was never written. I was
> eleven days into a licence and I thought that was the world's business.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the read is promised — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: two pairs of hands on the counter — Rekhak laying the chain down between them
open, and Ira not taking it; her fingertips resting on the dark link instead. Above them, through the
half-closed shutters, the pot-row lamps; somewhere out there, one lamp is not lit.

**Image:** Two people agreeing, without either of them saying the word *debt*, that tomorrow one of
them is going to read the other's perjury out of a brass link.

> **REKHAK:**
> So when it lands tomorrow — you'll see it before I will, you'll see it before the clerk writes the
> certificate. Read it, and then decide what you do with it.
>
> **IRA:**
> Decide what? It's your ledger. It's your word.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> It's a lie somebody can read, keeper, and you're the somebody. What you do with it is going to be
> the difference between the Office owning this basin and the Office owning forty-one people.
>
> **REKHAK (last line):**
> That's not a favour. That's the whole inventory of your stall, and I'm putting it in your hand one
> night before you find out what it costs.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> And I stood there and I thought: this is the second old person in three days to hand me a thing
> they want out of their own body.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Jadi asked me to pull a thread out of her hand. He is asking me to read a lie out of his. I said no
> to her and I am going to say yes to him, and I have not yet worked out why.

## Writing & art notes

- **Jadi's lesson is the chapter's heart, not its plot.** She is unmoved by Ira's honesty; what she
  wants is a *promise about paperwork.* "No mark under my line" is the first pupil's whole
  relationship to the law, and it must be lettered as a firm statement, not a plea. It pays off on
  Page 007.
- **The oath reading is the series' first on-page use of a mender reading brass.** Keep it
  consistent with Chapter 002 Page 006's canon: the held stop wrote itself into the chain; a reader
  of chains can read the words; Ira reads *folds*, not letters — draw the words as ruled strokes
  inside the link and let her face carry the legibility.
- **"It files him" is the page's thesis on the held stop.** A held stop is not a punishment and not
  a mark; it is a permanent record of the smallest sentence a man was willing to swear. Ira should
  arrive at that line as an observation about her mother's ledger-keeping — the family trade applied
  to a man.
- **Kessa names the tally-thread as *stock*, not sentiment.** This is the setup for Chapter 010: her
  version of the record is the one that answers to nobody. Her knot for the objection must be drawn
  distinctly (short bar, flat) so a scrolling reader recognises it later.
- **Rekhak's "whole inventory of your stall" is deliberately commercial.** He is not being noble; he
  is handing over a liability with an attached opportunity and he says so. Do not let Ira frame it as
  a gift in her captions.
- **The unlit lamp in the pot-row (Panel 7) is the only trace of the mother this page.** No caption
  mentions it. One lamp out, four nights down a list of forty-one — the reader can connect it, or
  not.
