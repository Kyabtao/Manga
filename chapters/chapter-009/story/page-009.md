# THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 009

**Chapter title:** *The Reading* / *पाठ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Mendery, the cellar and the counter, night into morning
**Page type:** Answer page — 7 panels

> Beats: the Office has requisitioned the Book of the Hand, signed by the principal's own hand, three
> days to produce it; the queue has filled eleven register lines with knots; the Book and the register
> are four feet apart in one cellar.
>
> **This page:** the school's answer, which is the answer the school has always given — and it is not
> keeping the record. It is *giving the record back.*

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the plan (~13%)

**Camera:** The cellar, night, the queue gone, the shutters down. The Book of the Hand open on the
desk at its back third — page after page of enrolment, one knot stitched down per pupil — and beside
it the register, open, eleven lines of knots. Ira with the needle-roll open; Rekhak opposite; Kessa
leaning in the doorway. Lamps low. This is a working session, not a council.

**Image:** Two books and a teacher deciding which one is the school.

> **IRA:**
> They want the seat. The seat is the page. If I give them the Book with the pages in it, they match
> eleven knots in an afternoon and they've got eleven hands *and* the method for the other nine
> hundred.
>
> **REKHAK:**
> Then burn it and take the penalty.
>
> **IRA:**
> No.
>
> **IRA:**
> My mother took the names out of the Roll and left the knots, because a knot can't be looked up. That
> was the right move for a woman who could carry forty years in her head. It isn't the right move for
> a school with a *desk.*

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the amendment, again (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's hand flat on the Book's open page, over a knot. She is not being reverent; she
is doing arithmetic with her fingers on the page, the way Kessa does with coins.

**Image:** The teacher applying her own amended rule to the school's own archive.

> **IRA:**
> Rule three, as amended. The teacher's strand goes in first — and then the teacher tells you what she
> did.
>
> **IRA:**
> Every knot in this book belongs to a person who was never told. They were twelve, or fourteen, and a
> woman put a thread in their hand in the dark through a hole in a door and never said why. Forty years
> of people carrying a mark they were never allowed to *understand.*
>
> **KESSA:**
> So tell them.
>
> **IRA:**
> So I give them back their own knot. Every one. The page is theirs — it was always theirs, it's the
> only paper that has ever had them on it — and the Office's requisition says *produce the seat of the
> knots.* It doesn't say in what condition, and it doesn't say the seat has to be a book.

---

## PANEL 3 — Large, the cut (~16%)

**Camera:** Large panel, macro: Ira's wax knife lifting the first knot out of the Book of the Hand —
not cutting the thread, **opening the page**: the little loops of the stitching coming up from the
inside, the Chapter 004 method, the same hand that opened her own palm on Chapter 008 Page 009. The
knot comes free with a small square of its own page still under it, like a scrap of skin with the
stitch in it.

**Image:** The school's archive, taken apart by the teacher — deliberately, at night, for the second
time in ten days, and this time on purpose.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Eleven knots, then twenty, then thirty-one, and the knife never once cut anything. *Lifting* is not
> *cutting.* You go in under the stitch and you bring it up whole, and the page stays a page and the
> knot stays a knot, and by the fourth bell my hands were doing it without my eyes.

> **CAPTION:**
> Under each knot there was something I did not expect. The Book has no names — I knew that. But under
> every knot, pressed flat into the paper, there is one line in my mother's writing.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Not what they were called. What they *did.* *Sews sailcloth.* *Ties rope for the dock.* *Can hold
> two needles.* Forty years of one woman writing down what her pupils were good at, in a book nobody
> could read, for pupils who never saw it.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the giving back (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium, morning: the counter, and the queue, and Ira handing each person a small folded
paper with their own knot in it. Nobody takes it casually. One old man holds his up to the light; a
young woman goes straight for her own cuff and starts sewing it in, in public, the way Kessa wears
hers; one of them refuses for a moment and then takes it with both hands.

**Image:** A school giving its pupils the only proof it ever had of them, because a page can be
requisitioned and a cuff cannot.

> **IRA:**
> It's yours. It was always yours. You were enrolled on the day you tied this, and the year is the
> school's year, and the kind is what you are, and none of that is a name and none of it is a file.
>
> **IRA:**
> Sew it into something. Keep it on you. If a reader ever asks you to produce it, you are not required
> to have it — it belongs to you, not to the Office, and there is no clause in forty-one that says
> otherwise.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Thirty-one people, and every one of them asked me the same question, and it was never *is this
> safe.*
>
> **CAPTION:**
> It was: *does this mean I'm enrolled?*

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, Jadi's knot (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: Jadi's palms, both, and in the middle of them the last knot out of the Book — the
first knot, cut from the first page, the enrolment of the school's first pupil, tied when she was
twelve. The paper under it is the oldest in the book and almost brown. Her hands are not shaking.
Ira's hand is beside them, not touching.

**Image:** Sixty-two years of a woman and the one page that has ever been about her.

> **JADI:**
> That's mine.
>
> **IRA:**
> That's yours. Page one. Kind, year, and a knot, and under it she wrote: *ties two threads at once,
> will teach.*
>
> **JADI:**
> *(a long pause, and she does not cry, and this is deliberate)*
> Forty-four years I told that whole basin I was the root. I never knew she'd written it *down.*

> **IRA:**
> Rule three, as amended. That's what I did tonight, Jadi. That's the whole of it. I opened your page
> and gave you your knot and I am telling you now, in a room where you're standing up.
>
> **JADI (last line):**
> ...Then the lesson's finished. Say the next one's day.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the last line under the last knot (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the very last knot on the very last page — the one that is less than a year old,
which Ira has not removed, because it is not a pupil's, or is not *yet.* Under it, in the mother's
writing, one line, and Ira reading it with the lamp held low. Nobody else in the room.

**Image:** The Book's final secret, left for exactly this moment of the night.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> I left one knot in the book. Page last, less than a year old, tied by a hand that was copying
> somebody else's hands — I saw that on Chapter 008 Page 003 and I put it down to another clerk with a
> needle.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Under it, in my mother's writing, there is one line. It is not a name and it is not what anybody is
> good at.
>
> **CAPTION (lettered, art):**
> *If you are reading this, I am out of days. Everything I did I did so that this page would never
> need a name. — Do not enrol me. — S.M.*

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the Book goes up — CLOSING HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the requisition slip on the desk, and on it the attesting line Ira has written as
keeper — *source record produced; seats of the entries are with their owners; no name entered* — and
beside it the Book of the Hand, bound, wrapped, sealed by the keeper with grey wax and *not* with
crimson, ready to go up the Council Stair at first bell. In the corner of the frame, small: the
register, and the last line of it, and beside the line a knot — the **newest** one, which Ira has this
moment stitched into the register's own last line, from the last page of the Book, and which does not
belong to anybody yet.

**Image:** The teacher answering a requisition in full compliance, and leaving one knot behind in the
Office's own instrument.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> I wrapped it myself and sealed it grey, because grey is the Council and this is paperwork, and I sent
> the Book of the Hand up the Council Stair at first bell with thirty-one pages cut clean and a
> keeper's attestation that the seats are with their owners.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> And in my register, on the last line, I stitched one knot — the newest one, from the last page — and
> left it unnamed.
>
> **CAPTION (last line):**
> They requisitioned a book to find out which hand is which.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Instead they've got forty-one lines and a knot with no owner on it, in their own instrument, in a
> stall they have to walk into every second bell.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> If my mother comes down that stair, she'll read it in four seconds. It says: *the school is open.*

## Writing & art notes

- **This is the second unbinding in ten days and it must rhyme with the first.** Chapter 008 Page 007
  showed the archive taken apart by the mother; this page shows the Book taken apart by the daughter —
  and the difference is that the mother opened the spines to *free* the pages and Ira opens the pages
  to *free the pupils.* Same method, same knife, no tearing.
- **The lines under the knots are the page's emotional payload and they are new canon.** The Book has
  no names and never did; under each knot the mother wrote what the pupil was *good at.* Draw them as
  short handwritten strokes on the page and letter **one** of them fully (*ties two threads at once,
  will teach*) plus Jadi's reading of it. Do not narrate more than three.
- **Jadi's beat is the chapter's warmest and must stay flat.** She does not cry. She asks what the next
  lesson's day is. That is the school's register of feeling and the reader should have learned it by
  now: enrolment is how these people say thank you.
- **The mother's note is text in art and it is the page's only legible prose besides the attestation.**
  Four lines, her hand, no signature but initials. It establishes that the mother *expected to be taken
  and planned for a reader* — and it forbids the one thing the register was built to do to her (*do not
  enrol me*), which is the exact clause the principal moved this morning.
- **Do not let Ira solve the requisition.** She complies with it fully and beats it with its own
  wording — *produce the seat* — which is the family trade (Ch. 009 Page 004's objection on the sheet)
  and the reason the Office keeps finding her useful and dangerous.
- **The unnamed knot on the register's last line is the chapter's forward hook and it must be visually
  plain:** one knot, no entry beside it, on the instrument's own paper. The reader should understand
  it is a message and not know yet who will read it.
