# THREADBORN — Chapter 09 — Page 002

**Chapter title:** *The Reading* / *पाठ*
**Arc:** II — The Mendery
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Mendery, the cellar, mid-morning
**Page type:** Procedure page — 7 panels

> Beats: the ordinance is served and it is kept at the Mendery, because the Mendery answers to the
> principal. Ira has three days, a desk in her cellar, and no way to refuse either.
>
> **This page:** what a register looks like when it has been waiting for you.

---

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the cellar (~13%)

**Camera:** The Mendery's cellar, mid-morning, lamp on. The desk is in it — plain, Office-made,
three-legged, one corner shimmed with a folded ordinance. On it: a **case**, banded, grey-waxed,
sealed at three points. Around it the room's real contents — racks, cold jars, Nandi's unshelved
shadows, one empty shelf where the Book came off.

**Image:** A new instrument in an old room, and the room has not been rearranged for it. Nobody has
moved anything; the desk simply appeared among the jars.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They didn't clear a space. They put it where a space happened to be, between the salt jars and the
> water-butt, and that is somehow worse than a new room would have been.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the seals (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on the case's three seals: grey wax, Office stamp, and under the third seal a paper
tag with a date and a *slot number.* One seal has been pressed with a thumb wider than the clerk's —
the seal was pressed where it was written, not where it was delivered.

**Image:** The register arriving the way all Office things arrive: already stamped, already older
than the journey.

> **CLERK:**
> I can't open it. Not mine. The reader opens it, and the keeper keeps it open.
>
> **IRA:**
> The keeper.
>
> **CLERK:**
> That's the word. Slot four, place of keeping. That's you.
>
> **IRA:**
> I'm a mender.
>
> **CLERK:**
> Yes. It says that on the schedule too. It says *keeper: licensee by provisional entry, Mendery,
> slot four, Ashfall.*

---

## PANEL 3 — Large, the register (~16%)

**Camera:** Large panel: the case open. The register, open at its first spread. The reader should be
able to count: **forty-one ruled lines** on the right-hand page, ruled by hand, evenly, from the top
of the leaf to the bottom, and one line at the very top already written. The left-hand page is blank.

**Image:** A list that exists before the people do. The single most important object of Chapter 009.

> **CLERK:**
> Ruled at the Office. Forty-one lines, see. They rule ahead — saves coming back.
>
> **KESSA:**
> *(from the stairs, flat)*
> Forty-one.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Forty-one.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Jadi said forty-one, in a doorway, holding up a palm, three hours ago. Forty-one measured in nine
> months, basin, every part of the Ashfall. And the Office ruled for forty-one lines before anybody
> declared anything to anybody.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the first line (~15%)

**Camera:** Close on the top line of the right-hand page. There is no name in it, no kind, no year.
There is a drawing: a palm, inked small and exact, with a **seam** drawn across it, and the seam is
drawn *shut* — one line across, closed, no loop, no lift. Where a date should be there is a date.
Where a name should be there is nothing at all.

**Image:** The register's first entry predates the register.

> **IRA:**
> This line's already written.
>
> **CLERK:**
> It was standing when the case was issued. I was told not to explain it. I'd be guessing.
>
> **IRA:**
> Guess.
>
> **CLERK:**
> ...I think somebody drew the shape they were waiting for, and then waited.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The date in the margin is eleven days old.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Eleven days. The day I stood in front of an Inspector in this room and said the word *provisional*,
> and a clerk wrote me into a ledger, and I thought I had made myself a stall.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, who may declare (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the clerk at the desk, going down the schedule with a finger, reading out the
practical part. Ira listening with her arms crossed. Kessa not listening — she is looking at the
register the way you look at a person you have already priced.

**Image:** The mechanics of denunciation, explained cheerfully by somebody who does not think of it
that way.

> **CLERK:**
> Declaration can be made by the owner, or by any person who knows it. Neighbour, employer, kin,
> trader. No proof required — a declaration is a declaration. If a hand is read and found unreadable
> by an Office instrument, that's a declaration as well, automatic.
>
> **CLERK:**
> And nothing happens to the person who declares. That's in there. *No liability attaches to a
> declarant.* I read that bit twice, I wanted it to say something else.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it is. Not a hunt. A form, and a rule that says nobody has to be brave to fill it in.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Forty-one lines and every one of them can be filled by the person who lives next door.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, Nandi (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on Ira's hand on the counter, and on the counter, next to it, a **clay token** from
the Office's custody — the one that came up from the stair this morning in place of anything Nandi
could have sent. Grey, unlettered, one thumbprint. Ira has not put it away.

**Image:** The chapter reminding the reader that one of the school's own is already inside the
machine, and no charge has been read out.

> **KESSA:**
> Token came up at first bell. No charge, no hearing date, no paper. Just the token, so the stall
> knows she's alive.
>
> **IRA:**
> She's been in there one day.
>
> **KESSA:**
> Forty years she was the one holding the register, child. Now she's a line somebody hasn't ruled.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the second bell — CLOSING HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Close on the cellar stair's top step, where a shadow is standing that has been standing
there for the length of the page. Then the Inspector, stepping down into the lamp-light with his
hands behind his back, exactly as on Chapter 008 Page 001, and behind him, in the doorway, the
Mendery's — *Ira's* — own slates, with the second bell still ringing out over the basin.

**Image:** The return that Ira's own legal move purchased in Chapter 008. He said he would come every
second bell. He has come at the first one.

> **INSPECTOR:**
> Good of you to take the keeping.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> I'm told you already looked at the first line. That's allowed. You're the keeper; looking is the
> job.
>
> **INSPECTOR:**
> *(last line, mild)*
> What you may not do — and I say this as a courtesy, because the instrument will say it to you
> anyway on Page seven — is *work on a declared hand.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> Page seven. He has read the register. He knows what is going to be written on it.

## Writing & art notes

- **The register is a *place*, not an object.** Draw it as furniture: it stays open on the desk for
  the rest of the chapter and every later page of Chapter 009 should show it in the frame if the
  scene is in the cellar. A scrolling reader should end the chapter unable to stop seeing it.
- **Forty-one lines must be countable.** Rule them visibly, evenly, from the top of the leaf to the
  bottom. Do not stylise them. The number is the page's payload and it must match Jadi's roster
  (Ch. 008 Page 007) and the copy of the Roll.
- **The first entry is a *shape*, not a name.** A closed seam on a palm, inked by a hand that had not
  seen it yet. No lettering in art — no name, no kind, no year, only the date in the margin (abstract
  strokes) and one drawn palm. Play the pre-filing canon straight: the principal began this register
  eleven days ago, before the ordinance, because he knew what would be found.
- **"No liability attaches to a declarant" is the page's real horror.** It must be delivered by a
  clerk who is relieved to read it, because it means *he* is safe. Keep the young clerk sympathetic
  throughout; he is the ordinary person the Office is made of.
- **Nandi's token is small and must stay small.** One unlettered clay thing with a thumbprint. No
  cutaway, no custody interior, no dialogue from her.
- **The Inspector does not gloat.** Warm, procedural, courteous — the same register as Chapter 008.
  His courtesy *is* the threat, and his last line must be the last thing on the page.
