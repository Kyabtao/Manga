## Synopsis

Chapter 008 ended on a slate: a compliance chain read a braid, returned both years at once, and a
clerk wrote one word — **unreadable** — and underlined it twice. *There is no procedure for that,
which means by morning there will be one.*

By first bell there is one. The Office does not retaliate; it **codifies.** The Provisional Ordinance
for Unreadable Hands does three things: an unreadable hand may not be entered in any ledger, licence,
wage-book or charter; every unreadable hand must be **declared** and entered in a new register at the
Reckoning Office; and no licensed practitioner may work a declared hand.

The count did not disappear. It **moved**: the Office can no longer date a braided palm, so it will
count the *absence of a date* instead. And because the Mendery was declared third-register property
in Chapter 008, the new register is kept *there* — Ira's own legal move comes back as the Office's
counter, and the school's newest teacher is made the unpaid custodian of the instrument that indexes
her own people.

Below that sits the chapter's real cost. A register of unreadable hands is worthless unless the
basin's chain-readers swear it complete, and the basin has exactly one chain-reader. **Rekhak** is
summoned and required to swear, under a **held stop**, that he has entered every braided hand he has
ever read. Under a held stop a false word is not a tell — it is a **deposit**, and it writes itself
into the one ledger he has never been permitted to open: his own. He takes it, for Ira, and the
Office gets what it actually wanted — the only man alive who can explain what a braid does to a
compliance instrument can never again be believed about it.

Meanwhile the second school opens for its one pupil, Kessa's tally-thread turns out to be the only
copy of the crease-writs, and the mother is four nights down a list of forty-one.

## Canon rules added in Chapter 9 (binding forward)

1. **Provisional Ordinance forty-one — "Unreadable Hands."** Three clauses. *(a)* A hand that returns
   no year may not be entered in any ledger, licence, wage-book, charter or roll — forty years of the
   school's work, handed back as a punishment. *(b)* Every unreadable hand must be **declared**, by its
   owner or by any person who knows it, within three days: the count moved from the date to the
   *absence* of a date. *(c)* No licensed practitioner may mend or instruct a declared hand, which
   makes the second school illegal the day it opens. The commencement clause takes effect from the
   third bell of the previous night — earlier than the deed it governs.
2. **The register is kept at the place of keeping** — the Mendery, slot four — because the stall
   answered for itself as third-register property in Chapter 008. Ira is its keeper: the instrument
   indexes her own people and she is its unpaid custodian.
3. **The register reads aloud.** The certification sheet binds reader and keeper to read every entry
   in the same words at the same time. Written to keep a record honest; used in Chapter 010 to make
   Ira say *not the founder* out loud on the record.
4. **A false word under a held stop is a deposit.** Not a tell: the true version of the sworn sentence
   writes itself into the owner's own ledger, in his own hand. Rekhak's deposit stands in his ledger
   forever, and with it *They made me a finger.*
5. **Declaration carries no liability.** Any person who knows a hand may declare it; the declarant is
   not punished, and an entry made at its owner's own word costs no fee. (Ch. 010 turns this into the
   keeper's lawful defence: enter the hand first and there is nothing left for a claimant to sell.)
6. **The school's answer to the blank is a translation, not a name.** Kind, school-year and the
   pupil's first knot go into the Office's instrument; the name box stays empty. The Inspector names
   the practice himself.
7. **A name on a page is a handle.** The Roll of Hands never held one, and refusing to write one is a
   strategy, not a scruple — but a name is also the only thing an entry can be *paid* for.
8. **The knots come back.** The Book of the Hand is lifted, not cut: every pupil's enrolment returns
   to its owner, with what she was good at written under it in the mother's hand. Under the last knot:
   *Do not enrol me. — S.M.*
9. **The Office's answer to a lawless hand is a department, not a raid:** a copyist, a bigger book, and
   eleven words in crimson on the reserved first line — **PRODUCE THE HAND. REWARD RESERVED.**
10. **The bounty needs no officials.** Eleven words on a board, a fee, and a queue of neighbours do
    everything enforcement would have done.
