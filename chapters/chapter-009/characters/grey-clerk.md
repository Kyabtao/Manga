## Appearance (art continuity)

- **Build:** spare, ordinary, slightly stooped from years over a desk. A body shaped by sitting —
  the opposite of Bhan's width and the Inspector's comfort.
- **Face:** plain, forgettable, easy to lose in a crowd of two — **that is the design brief**. A
  middle-aged human man, thinning **greying flat hair** combed without conviction; pale, indoor
  skin; eyes that read everything and settle on nothing. No beard, no ornament. His face must never
  carry an expression strong enough to quote.
- **Hands:** ink-stained on the right index and middle finger; the **dip-pen grip** is his resting
  state — the ref sheet's hands panel. In Ch. 013 Page 008 he presses his **bare thumb** into wax
  beside the keeper's knot: the one gesture in the series where his hands do something his office
  never taught.
- **Costume (in office):** long **office-grey coat with a high plain collar**, no tie, no hat, no
  crest — the machine's grey, nothing else. The **record book hangs on a cord at his neck** (detail
  panel): knot-marked, fat, the Office's working memory.
- **Costume (stripped — Ch. 013 Pages 008–010):** plain **undyed wool coat**, **bare head**, and the
  cord at his collar an **empty loop** — no seal, no book. The absence is the costume. Any panel of
  him off-duty must read as "the same man with everything that spoke for him removed."
- **Kit:** dip-pen, ink pot, blotting cloth, the record book on its cord, and — from Ch. 013 — one
  kept object that never appears in the Office inventory: the register's terms, fair copy, carried
  inside his coat.
- **Silhouette test:** a grey question mark over a desk. Stripped: a plain man in a plain coat, and
  somehow still identifiable — the empty cord-loop does it.

## What he is

The Office's best hand. In Ch. 012 he nearly out-lawyered the school with a lease saved for the
right morning; in Ch. 013 he learned to aim (the mint clause), learned what unwritten paper costs,
and finally **crossed the counter's threshold as a man** — the chapter's one chain-stop — to ask for
the register's terms in writing and sign for receiving them with his **thumb-mark**, the basin's
grammar, unpracticed in his hand. The machine answered by deleting him: *the act of an individual,
without authority.* The register answered by protecting him: **his name, entered, not read aloud.**
By the finale he is a queue figure — standing with the basin's people at dusk, holding no paper.

> **Rule:** he is never a villain and never a convert. He is the only man in the Office who knows
> what the register is — and the machine's fear of exactly that knowledge is what unmade him.
