# Chapter 013 — Glossary

New terms and usages introduced in this chapter. Chapter 012's terms are defined in
`chapter-012/other/glossary.md`.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **"Pending the count"** | The Office's notice on its shut public hatch (Ch. 013 Page 001): no stamps, no validated papers, no fees, no disputes heard — the machine cannot count, so the machine does nothing. The suspension's cost lands on its own authors and pushes the basin's paper to the keeper's counter. |
| **"No stamp, no pay"** | The paymaster's rule (Page 001): a pay-chit is worth nothing unstamped, and the only stamp in the basin is behind the shutter. The first thing the second Office cannot mend with grammar — a paper hostage to a suspension. |
| **The inventory count** | The Office's workaround (Page 001, Rekhak's warning): the count of hands is suspended, so the Office counts what hands hold — granary seals, fuel-yards, lamp-oil. Inventory does not refuse; inventory has no founder whose no stands on file. The chapter's strategic clock. |
| **The counter-book's docket** | What the street brings the register (Page 001): tally-sticks, boundary-cords, debt-braids, disputes the counting-lesson women could not settle. A queue is a verdict the street has already reached; the keeper is the hand that writes it down. |
| **"Enforceable but unbindable"** | The register's first hard ruling (Page 002): the lease is genuine and the schedule honours it — but a paper binds names, the tenancy holds hands, and the school's three are on no roll the Office can read. Possession by knot, entry by knot, so finds the register. The Office's own grammar used to make the Office's own paper helpless. |
| **The keeper's certificate** | Page 002's answer to the stamp question: the register cannot stamp, so it certifies — a debt named on a paper is disputed, pending the count, entered of record. A foreman must honour it or defy a record in writing, with his name where the keeper's is. First credit minted in the basin by a book that is not the Office's. |
| **The inspection demand** | The clerk's answer by dusk (Page 002): the Office's inventory grammar pointed at the school's alley room — they want to count what is in it. Filed the same day the ruling lands, with the amendment file sent for behind it. Armed, not fired. |
| **"A keep is not a mint"** | Kessa's limit, set the hour the certificate is honoured (Page 002): certify the debt, never create it. The register's own entry: one certificate per disputed debt, no interest, void at the count's end, every certificate signed with the keeper's name — each paper leaves with a neck attached. |
| **The amendment** | The Office's counter-grammar (Page 004), drafted by the clerk, delivered by runner, read aloud of record at the counter: two clauses — a person is *entered* who is named upon a roll kept by the Office, no other entry is entry (the empty-row finding closed by definition); and what is *record* — paper under stamp only, all else not record. Its real target is the mint: the keeper's certificates ruled out of existence on taking, by definition. The register reads it in public and enters it unanswered. |
| **The permit-dark** | The Office's lamp-hours (Page 004): lamp-oil rationed by permit, dusk hours posted sealed at the hatch — as if dusk were theirs to license. The first rationed dusk takes the square dark window by window, and leaves one lit stall. The counter's answer: the flame trimmed low and exact, four hours a night knotted into the till's own cord — observed, and not obeyed. |
| **The reading of record** | What the counter now does for the basin (Page 004): the Office publishes in its own grammar; the street fetches the paper to the counter; the keeper reads it aloud, line by line, of record, before the queue. The Office's publication is answered at the counter — logistics, not rebellion. |
| **The tally-slate** | The Office's inventory instrument (Page 003): rows of tally groups, a measuring cord, one seal — the most dangerous object in the basin, because it counts correctly and completely and still catches nothing that matters. It can carry a knot in its count column; it cannot carry a hand in its name column. |
| **The empty row** | The slate's PERSONS row after counting the school's alley room (Page 003): the row the whole inspection demand was filed to fill, left blank because the room's people are entered by knot, not by name. The blank is the menace — and, entered of record, it becomes the school's shield: a room where nobody lives is a room the Office cannot tax. |
| **The lamp-oil schedule** | The inventory's arrival at the basin's light (Page 003, Rekhak's forecast): fuel-yards sealed, lamp-oil rationed by count and sold by permit — and the stall's own lamp burns Office-counted oil. They cannot make the basin's paper stop, so they will make the basin's light ask permission. The counter's answer is already folded in Kessa's box, un-invoiced. |
