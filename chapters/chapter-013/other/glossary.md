# Chapter 013 — Glossary

New terms and usages introduced in this chapter. Chapter 012's terms are defined in
`chapter-012/other/glossary.md`.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **"Pending the count"** | The Office's notice on its shut public hatch (Ch. 013 Page 001): no stamps, no validated papers, no fees, no disputes heard — the machine cannot count, so the machine does nothing. The suspension's cost lands on its own authors and pushes the basin's paper to the keeper's counter. |
| **"No stamp, no pay"** | The paymaster's rule (Page 001): a pay-chit is worth nothing unstamped, and the only stamp in the basin is behind the shutter. The first thing the second Office cannot mend with grammar — a paper hostage to a suspension. |
| **The inventory count** | The Office's workaround (Page 001, Rekhak's warning): the count of hands is suspended, so the Office counts what hands hold — granary seals, fuel-yards, lamp-oil. Inventory does not refuse; inventory has no founder whose no stands on file. The chapter's strategic clock. |
| **The counter-book's docket** | What the street brings the register (Page 001): tally-sticks, boundary-cords, debt-braids, disputes the counting-lesson women could not settle. A queue is a verdict the street has already reached; the keeper is the hand that writes it down. |
| **"Enforceable but unbindable"** | The register's first hard ruling (Page 002): the lease is genuine and the schedule honours it — but a paper binds names, the tenancy holds hands, and the school's three are on no roll the Office can read. Possession by knot, entry by knot, so finds the register. The Office's own grammar used to make the Office's own paper helpless. |
| **The keeper's certificate** | Page 002's answer to the stamp question: the register cannot stamp, so it certifies — a debt named on a paper is disputed, pending the count, entered of record. A foreman must honour it or defy a record in writing, with his name where the keeper's is. First credit minted in the basin by a book that is not the Office's. |
| **The inspection demand** | The clerk's answer by dusk (Page 002): the Office's inventory grammar pointed at the school's alley room — they want to count what is in it. Filed the same day the ruling lands, with the amendment file sent for behind it. Armed, not fired. |
| **"A keep is not a mint"** | Kessa's limit, set the hour the certificate is honoured (Page 002): certify the debt, never create it. The register's own entry: one certificate per disputed debt, no interest, void at the count's end, every certificate signed with the keeper's name — each paper leaves with a neck attached. |
| **The amendment** | The Office's counter-grammar (Page 004), drafted by the clerk, delivered by runner, read aloud of record at the counter: two clauses — a person is *entered* who is named upon a roll kept by the Office, no other entry is entry (the empty-row finding closed by definition); and what is *record* — paper under stamp only, all else not record. Its real target is the mint: the keeper's certificates ruled out of existence on taking, by definition. The register reads it in public and enters it unanswered. |
| **The permit-dark** | The Office's lamp-hours (Page 004): lamp-oil rationed by permit, dusk hours posted sealed at the hatch — as if dusk were theirs to license. The first rationed dusk takes the square dark window by window, and leaves one lit stall. The counter's answer: the flame trimmed low and exact, four hours a night knotted into the till's own cord — observed, and not obeyed. |
| **The reading of record** | What the counter now does for the basin (Page 004): the Office publishes in its own grammar; the street fetches the paper to the counter; the keeper reads it aloud, line by line, of record, before the queue. The Office's publication is answered at the counter — logistics, not rebellion. |
| **"A definition rules forward"** | The register's answer to the amendment (Page 005, in duplicate, sealed before noon): a definition cannot reach backward over a record already entered — the keeper's certificates stand until the count ends. The Office may rule what its own stamp means; it may not rule what was already witnessed. The legal engine of the whole certificate economy. |
| **Protection of record** | The second Office's new function, granted at the foreman's asking (Page 005): a name that cost something to help the register is ENTERED, NOT READ ALOUD — held the way the register holds debts, usable when its owner says, not before. The register's first sealed names — and its first dependency on people worth protecting. |
| **The endorsement demand** | The clerk's trap (Page 005, night): certificates are valid only when presented for endorsement at the Office's public counter — the counter that is shut pending the count. Honour on paper, refusal in fact; a promise with no road to keep it. Entered of record; the register's answer owed the basin by morning. |
| **The endorsement window** | The register's answer to the trap (Page 006, morning): a hinged window built into the stall's shutter — paper presented here is endorsed here: witnessed true, entered of record, road intact, answerable at this counter. Every endorsement carries the keeper's name at its foot (a neck attached — the mint-limit applied to witnessing). Takes papers no office would read: acknowledged debts kept soft with years, hire-promises knotted but never sealed. A promise witnessed is a debt that can be counted. |
| **The filing choice** | The Office's unwritten counter (Page 006, dusk, by the runner as sent-speech): the window is unlicensed — unless every endorsement is filed weekly at the count-house, in duplicate, in Office grammar, under Office seal. Refuse and the window is named with the unlicensed things; accept and the keeper's book becomes, copy by copy, readable and citable by the machine. The first move of the war that exists nowhere on paper. Answer owed by the runner's return. |
| **The counter-condition** | The register's third-grammar answer (Page 007): the register will file weekly — IF the count-house delivers its own inventory counts, seal-logs, and permit-ledgers weekly to the counter, in duplicate, under seal, in the register's grammar, for entry and public reading. Both books, both ways, or neither. Symmetry as siegecraft: the clause the clerk cannot accept without the basin hearing the counts read aloud. |
| **The courtesy filing** | The blink (Page 007, dusk): a small slip at the hatch — one instance of the register's filing accepted as a courtesy, WITHOUT the reciprocal term. Unsigned, unsealed, in a clerk's hand, posted before the lamps: one man in a quiet room, moving alone to keep the square from ever hearing the counts read. Entered as received, read, unanswered — the register does not chase informality; it waits for it to become official. |
| **Sent-speech** | The Office's unwritten channel (Pages 006–007): terms delivered by the runner, by mouth, complete and deniable — the arm's-race answer to a counter that makes everything written. The register's own use of it (Page 007) is the first time the runner has carried words instead of paper — not a promotion. |
| **The formalized courtesy** | The Office's sealed notice (Page 008, morning): the window's filings "received at the count-house as a courtesy" — the clerk's unsigned slip inflated, overnight, into an agreement that was never made. Read at the counter side by side with the slip it was built on; the Office's two grammars impeaching each other in the register's presence. |
| **The thumb-mark** | The clerk's receiving-mark (Page 008, dusk): no seal, no signature — a bare thumb pressed deep into the wax beside the keeper's knot, the basin's grammar unpracticed in his hand. The first Office hand to accept a record the Office did not write; the body-at-a-table grammar of the widow and the claimant, learned from two years of reading the register. |
| **The chain-stop (spent)** | Chapter 013's ONE chain-stop, spent on the threshold (Page 008): the machine's hands entering the counter as a man — bareheaded, cord empty, no seal to speak for him — to ask for the register's terms in writing and sign for receiving them. Not violence, not rescue: the Office's grammar running out one step short of the lamplight, and a man finishing the distance on his own feet. |
| **The disavowal** | The Office's answer to the thumb-marked receipt (Page 009, sealed, prompt): the document "represents the act of an individual, undertaken without authority" — the terms neither accepted nor refused, and the clerk never named. Erasure by passive voice: nobody did anything; an act became unowned; a man became an individual. The register's answer: the Office may decline its clerk's act — it may not decline his mark; the terms stand, both books, both ways, or neither. |
| **The unnamed** | What the machine manufactured on purpose (Page 009): the first man it ever stripped a name off of — for being useful. The mirror of the three entered hands, who gave their names up and were armoured by it: the clerk kept his inside the machine and was unmade by it. The register's response is its newest institution pointed at its own enemy — his name entered under protection, not read aloud, sealed beside the courtesy slip: the counter protecting the man the machine erased. |
| **The weekly delivery** | The terms, running (Page 010): the count-house delivers weekly to the counter — granary counts, fuel-yard rolls, permit-ledgers — in duplicate, under seal, for entry and public reading. No apology accompanies the acceptance; the machine behaves as if the terms were always its own idea, and no one will ever find the seam. Routine is the strongest grammar there is: the revolution arrives looking like clerking. |
| **The unrationed dusk** | The permit-dark's end (Page 010): the lamp-hours struck from the board because a light read in public cannot be rationed in the dark — and the square staying lit past the old posted hour, every window, the stall's lamp indistinguishable in them at last. The basin learned lighting from the counter. Three knots remain on the oil-cord: nothing learned in fear gets unlearned because the fear moved on. |
| **The tally-slate** | The Office's inventory instrument (Page 003): rows of tally groups, a measuring cord, one seal — the most dangerous object in the basin, because it counts correctly and completely and still catches nothing that matters. It can carry a knot in its count column; it cannot carry a hand in its name column. |
| **The empty row** | The slate's PERSONS row after counting the school's alley room (Page 003): the row the whole inspection demand was filed to fill, left blank because the room's people are entered by knot, not by name. The blank is the menace — and, entered of record, it becomes the school's shield: a room where nobody lives is a room the Office cannot tax. |
| **The lamp-oil schedule** | The inventory's arrival at the basin's light (Page 003, Rekhak's forecast): fuel-yards sealed, lamp-oil rationed by count and sold by permit — and the stall's own lamp burns Office-counted oil. They cannot make the basin's paper stop, so they will make the basin's light ask permission. The counter's answer is already folded in Kessa's box, un-invoiced. |
