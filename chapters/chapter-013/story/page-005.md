## PANEL 1 — Medium, morning, the answer written in duplicate (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall, morning. Two papers flat on the counter, one already sealed; Ira
finishing the second in the register's cleanest grammar, the open register beside for entry. Kessa
trims the lamp's wick with small scissors, exact. The queue at the rail, quiet — the whole street
knows what today is.

**Image:** The register owed an answer by dusk. The register pays its debts before noon.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> A register that lets its answers go overdue is just a woman with opinions. Two copies. One for the
> hand that asked. One for the gate that wobbled.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the grammar of the answer (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: the sealed copy flat under the lamp — rows of knot-marks and tally groups, one
wax seal, and at the foot a small careful knot: the keeper's own sign, pressed into the wax.

**Image:** The legal engine, stated in the Office's own grammar because it is the only grammar that bites.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, to the queue, before the runner takes it)*
> Entered before, answered against after. A definition rules forward — it cannot reach backward
> over a record already entered. The certificate stands until the count ends. They may rule what
> their stamp means. They may not rule what was already witnessed.

---

## PANEL 3 — Wide, midday, the gate receives the record (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: the fuel-yard gate, midday glare off split wood. The grey runner holds up the
register's sealed paper — and the **foreman** takes it with both hands: a broad, careful man, heavy
jaw, leather apron over a wool coat, gate-keys at his belt, a gate-keeper's face that gives away
nothing early. The claimant stands to one side with his certificate in his fist. Men pause mid-load
to watch.

**Image:** The Office sent its definition to this gate yesterday. Today the gate receives a better paper.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He reads the way gate-keepers read — slow, twice, like a man checking a knot he will have to
> trust his whole weight to.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the payment in view (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: inside the yard's mouth: the foreman hands the claimant a full measure-sack —
the weight visible in the claimant's arms — in front of the watching men, the certificate held up
between them a moment like a receipt.

> **FOREMAN:**
> *(carrying, for the yard to hear)*
> Paid against the keeper's paper. It stands till the count ends — that is this gate's rule now.
> Any man who argues it can argue with me first.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He did not wait to be sure. He waited to be *seen* — and then he made the gate's rule on the
> spot, the way I made mine: out loud, where the people who need it can hold him to it.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium-close, dusk, the foreman's price (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the stall at dusk, the counted lamp low. The foreman at the counter
IN PERSON, cap in both hands, leaning in, voice down. Kessa still at the till, missing nothing.

> **FOREMAN:**
> *(quiet)*
> I will honour every paper that comes off this counter. But my name is on a record now, against
> the Office's own word. A gate is not a wall, keeper. When their clerk comes for names, mine is
> the easy one. Can your book keep a name quiet?
>
> **IRA:**
> *(level, of record)*
> Entered, not read aloud. Protection of record: the register holds your name the way it holds the
> basin's debts — usable when you say, not before. That is the best my book does. It is not nothing.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Protect the name.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, night, the trap (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide, night: the square black except for a ring of hand-lamps at the hatch board. A
fresh long notice, one seal, going up under Office escort — and the basin reading it by lamp-flame:
**certificates valid only when presented for endorsement at the Office's public counter.** The
claimant at the ring's edge, his paper in his fist, staring.

**Image:** The trap, stated plainly: honour the paper — after a journey that cannot be made. The
only endorsement counter in the basin is the one they shut.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There is the clerk's best work. He learned from my certificate. He did not forbid it — he gave it
> a road, and the road ends at a door he boarded up. Honour on paper. Refusal in fact. And the
> basin left holding a promise with no road to keep it.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the trap read aloud — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the stall by the counted flame — the oil-cord's four knots hanging by the
till. Kessa lays the fresh notice on the counter; Ira reads it once, then writes. The queue watches
the pen the way it watched the ruling.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, entering)*
> Entered: the endorsement demand. A valid paper, an impossible road. Entered: the answer is owed
> the basin by morning.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They built a door out of a rule, and they think that settles it. But a counter is not a place,
> keeper. I keep having to tell people this.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Then we open a window.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the register pays its debt first; the foreman pays at a price; the trap
  lands and is read aloud.** The page's spine is credibility: the register said "by dusk tomorrow,
  by name, in writing" — so the answer goes out before noon, in duplicate, sealed. Never let Ira
  gloat about being early; the caption frames it as bookkeeping, because it is.
- **The foreman is on-panel for the first time — keep him CAREFUL.** Broad, heavy-jawed, leather
  apron, gate-keys; he reads twice; he makes the gate's rule out loud because that is how gates
  hold; he comes to the counter in person at dusk because names matter more than pride. He is not a
  convert and not a rebel — he is a gate-keeper choosing which paper his gate will trust, and then
  buying protection for the choice. Do not make him warm; make him solid.
- **Protection of record, granted, cost visible:** "entered, not read aloud" is the second
  institution-building beat after the mint-limit. The register gains a new function (sealed names)
  and a new dependency (people worth protecting). Do not resolve the tension — the register now
  holds names the Office would pay to read. Leave that on the books.
- **The trap must be stated plainly and left armed.** The clerk's move: certificates valid only on
  endorsement at the Office's own counter — the counter that is shut. Do not have anyone solve it
  on-panel; Ira enters it and owes the basin an answer by morning. The smallest caption
  (*Then we open a window.*) is the turn — the endorsement window in the stall's shutter is Page
  006's opening image. DO NOT fire it this page: no shutter opened, no window built, only the
  sentence.
- **The chain-stop check is explicit this page:** had the foreman defaulted, the register's
  credibility would have broken and the chain-stop budget would have spent itself here. He paid.
  Note it in the captions' confidence, not on-panel: the budget holds at ONE, unspent.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Protect the name.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 013: ONE, unspent (checked on-panel by outcome, not
  stated). Mother never on panel, never speaks — no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands
  off-panel. The amendment stands; the certificate question is ANSWERED (stands till the count
  ends); the endorsement trap is OPEN and owed an answer by morning. The oil-cord (four knots) hangs
  by the till. The foreman's yard pays against paper — the gate's rule is now on the record. Kessa
  keeps the till; the box stays shut. The counting-lesson women may cameo in the hatch-ring (they
  are the basin's readers now); do not feature them.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The register's answer: two
  papers, knot-marks and tally groups, one wax seal each, the keeper's knot-signature pressed in the
  wax. The trap notice: long paper, one seal, knot-marks. Hand-lamps only in the hatch ring; the
  counted lamp low in the stall; night square black.
- **Numbers:** one answer in duplicate; one runner; one foreman (first on-panel); one measure-sack
  paid; one protection of record entered; one trap notice; one answer owed by morning; one oil-cord,
  four knots; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
