**Sector:** Agnikhand — the stall's shutter, morning to the runner's dusk
**Page type:** The endorsement window — 7 panels; a door built in the middle of a trap, and the price of being worth copying

## PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the window opens (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: the stall in morning light — and the shutter has changed overnight: a small
hinged window-frame set into the wood, freshly knot-marked, its own little seal hanging beside it on
cord, and beneath the sill a knot-marked board like a shop's sign. A semicircle of basin folk stands
in the alley staring at it. Inside the frame: Ira at a window-seat, the register open on her knees,
Kessa at the till behind.

**Image:** They demanded a counter. Here is one.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The clerk built a door out of a rule, so I built a door out of a shutter. Paper presented here is
> endorsed here — witnessed, entered, answerable at this counter. He wanted a road that ends at a
> boarded door. I gave the road a new ending.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the first endorsement (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the claimant first in line — fourth morning running — passing his certificate
through the open frame with both hands, the way you hand over something you have been afraid for.
Ira reads it once. Behind him, the line: a dozen basin folk, papers and cords already in hands.

**Image:** The wobble ends where the window begins.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, writing)*
> Presented at the endorsement window. Witnessed true, entered of record, road intact. Valid till
> the count ends — and now it says so on its own face.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the cost at the foot (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: through the frame — the certificate flat on the window-seat under Ira's hand,
fresh knot-marks added at the foot, and Kessa's fingers dripping the window's own little seal into
new wax. The keeper's name goes on at every foot: the register's reach, one name longer.

**Image:** What each endorsement costs the register: everything it has. Its name.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Every paper out of this window carries my name at its foot. That was the limit I set on the mint —
> a neck attached. It applies to the window too. The day I seal a paper I have not read is the day
> the neck is mine either way.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the papers nobody expected (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the line moves — an old woman holding up a folded scrap gone soft with years (a
debt acknowledged by a dead man's seal, never paid, kept in a fold next to the skin); a young
dock-hand presenting a hire-promise cord, knotted at both ends, no seal anywhere. Kessa receives the
cord with both hands. The window takes papers the Office forgot it had made possible.

> **KESSA:**
> *(appraising the cord, not unkind)*
> No seal. Never worth anything at their hatch. Worth something here — a promise witnessed is a
> debt that can be counted. Presented, hand. The window will make it true.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium-close, the window's price (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the counter, early afternoon, the line thinned. Kessa knotting the
till-cord by the lamp: candle-hours, till-time — the window's running cost, counted the way the till
counts everything now. Ira watches the knot go in.

**Image:** The limit holds: endorsement is witnessing, not lending — and witnessing has hours.

> **KESSA:**
> *(tightening the knot)*
> One candle a week, two hours of the till a day, and the keeper's name at every foot. That is the
> window's price, keeper, and the till can carry it. What the till cannot carry is lending. We
> witness. We do not mint. Your own rule — I am only knotting it where you can see it.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, dusk, the runner's choice (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: dusk, the line dismissed, the window being shuttered — and the grey runner already
at the frame, hands on the sill, leaning in to be heard. No paper changes hands. The square is close
enough to see but not to hear, and everyone in it is watching anyway.

**Image:** The Office's answer, delivered in person because it must not exist in writing.

> **RUNNER:**
> *(low, flat, sent-speech)*
> From the Office: the window is unlicensed. The Office will tolerate it on terms. Every endorsement
> filed weekly — at our count-house, in duplicate, in Office grammar, under Office seal. File, and
> the window stands. Refuse, and the window is named at the hatch with the other unlicensed things.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There it is. Not a notice — an offer. The first thing that clerk has ever sent me that was not
> already written down. He is learning what a register is. He should not have taught me what he is.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the choice received — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: night, the stall. The window shuttered from inside, the lamp behind it
making the frame's edges glow like a lantern's seams. Ira sits with the register open and the pen
not moving — for once, the register waits on her. Kessa's hand rests on the till. The oil-cord hangs
its four knots between them.

**Image:** The choice: refuse, and the window is outlawed with the unlicensed things. Accept, and
every copy the Office files is a hook in the keeper's grammar — the book becoming a branch of the
machine, week by week, in its own hand.

> **IRA:**
> *(to the register, not entering)*
> The window answers to the record. The record does not answer to a count-house. Whatever I write
> next has to be true with their runner standing in the square and the basin watching the lamp burn.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Answer it anyway.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the window opens (the fired promise, first image); the endorsements cost
  (the keeper's name at every foot; the limit holds); the runner's choice (the trap beneath the
  tolerance).** The window must land as clean grammar answering clean grammar — not a joke exactly,
  but a move so procedural it reads like one. Do not let the crowd cheer; a semicircle staring is
  the right register. The basin has learned to watch.
- **The claimant's fourth visit closes his arc-wobble:** his certificate now says its own validity
  on its face. Keep his screen-time short — he is the first in line, not the subject. The one-off
  papers (the old debt acknowledged under a dead man's seal; the hire-promise cord) are the page's
  proof of scale: the window is discovering how much paper the basin has been holding that no office
  would ever read.
- **Kessa's two beats are the page's ledger:** the appraiser's line at the window (a promise
  witnessed is a debt that can be counted) and the till-cord pricing (one candle, two till-hours, a
  name at every foot — we witness, we do not mint). Her limit-quoting is not deference; it is the
  till protecting the register from its own success.
- **The runner's speech is sent-speech — flat, complete, deniable.** No paper, no seal: the Office's
  first unwritten move, because it must not exist as record. Note the asymmetry in the captions: the
  register made the Office write everything down; now the Office tries unwritten. Ira's read on it:
  *he is learning what a register is* — and the last clause is the cold one: *he should not have
  taught me what he is.*
- **The choice must be laid out clean and left unflipped.** Refuse → named at the hatch with the
  unlicensed things (the window outlaws itself). Accept → weekly duplicate filing in Office grammar →
  the keeper's book, copy by copy, becomes readable, crossable, and citable by the machine — a branch
  office with a lamp. The answer is Page 007's opening line; this page ends with the pen NOT moving.
  DO NOT have Ira refuse or accept on-panel.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Answer it anyway.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 013: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks —
  no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel. The foreman not featured — his name is
  never said (protection of record holds). The oil-cord: four knots, burning its hours. The
  certificate question: settled, and now stamped on the papers themselves. The amendment stands; the
  endorsement trap is answered by the WINDOW, and the window is now itself the open question. Kessa
  keeps the till; the box stays shut; the till-cord carries the window's price.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The window: a wooden hinged
  frame with knot-marks burnt into the sill-board, a small wax seal on a cord, no lettering. The
  endorsement: knot-marks added in fresh ink, wax dripped at the foot. The runner: plain grey,
  empty-handed. Lamp-light only; the shuttered window glowing from behind at night like lantern
  seams.
- **Numbers:** one window; one claimant (fourth visit); one old debt acknowledged; one hire-promise
  cord; one till-cord knotting; one candle a week; two till-hours a day; one runner, empty-handed;
  one choice received, unanswered; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
