> counter — inventory counts, seal-logs, permit-ledgers, in duplicate, under seal, for entry and

## PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the acceptance (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the hatch board, morning — a sealed notice posted square, the machine's regular
wax, and the counting-lesson women untying it while the crowd is STILL ARRIVING: this time the basin
does not wait to be told what the paper is. It moves with them.

**Image:** The street learned the drill in ten days: sealed paper at the hatch, read aloud at the
counter. The machine publishes; the basin assembles.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Ten days ago a sealed notice was weather, and the basin walked around it. This morning they fell
> in behind two women and a knotted cord like it was a wedding. The Office thinks the counter took
> its readers. The counter gave the basin its own.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, read by the basin (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: at the stall's open window — and it is NOT Ira reading: one of the
counting-lesson women stands at the frame with the acceptance held up, reading it aloud to the
square, her sister-woman pointing the line for her; Ira stands inside, of record, attending her own
counter's reading and saying nothing; Kessa's till-warmth behind.

**Image:** The institution completes itself: the machine's paper, read by the basin's voice, at the
counter of record.

> **THE ELDER READER:**
> *(reading, slow and clear)*
> "...and the count-house shall deliver weekly to the keeper's counter: the granary counts, the
> seal-logs, the permit-ledgers — in duplicate, under seal — for entry and public reading. So it
> shall be filed."
>
> **IRA:**
> *(of record, one line, entering)*
> Entered: the terms, accepted. The register notes for the record that nothing was withdrawn,
> corrected, or regretted. Machines do not regret. They file.

## PANEL 3 — Close, the acceptance's grammar (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: the acceptance flat under the lamp — the machine's regular knot-rows, and at its
foot, beside the seal, the tallier's diligent hand-work; and laid against it, corner to corner, the
keeper's fair-copy terms with the thumb-marked seal — proposed and accepted, one paper.

**Image:** The disavowal of the disavowal, achieved by pure paperwork.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> There is no line in it about the individual. There is no line about the notice. The machine has
> simply begun behaving as if the terms were always its own idea — which is how machines accept
> anything. Somewhere in that grey building, my conditions became their procedure, and no one will
> ever be able to find the seam.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, dusk, the first delivery (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the window-frame at dusk — a count-house hand (not the runner: a lesser clerk,
nerveless, efficient) passing the first weekly bundles through the frame: a sealed satchel of
granary counts, a fuel-yard roll, a packet of permit-ledgers, each tagged in knot-work. Ira receives
them with both hands, the register already open. At the queue's edge, Rekhak watches with
professional eyes — the folded paper a hard rectangle in his coat pocket, staying there.

**Image:** Nine chapters the basin's paper travelled to the machine. Today the machine's paper came
to the counter — under seal, in duplicate, at the keeper's grammar's mercy.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The counts smell of the count-house — dust, wax, and somebody's lunch, kept warm on a ledger. It
> is the most ordinary thing the Office has ever given me, and it is the whole revolution: their
> numbers, on my table, weekly, readable by anyone who walks up.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium-close, the permit-dark struck (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: at the hatch board, the permit-dark notice is being taken down — an Office
hand pulling the knot free, methodical, unhurried — while the counting-lesson women hold their lamp
high for him to see by, magnanimous and unmistakable. Inset or beside: Kessa's fingers at the
oil-cord, untying ONE of the four knots — three remain.

**Image:** A light that is read in public cannot be rationed in the dark.

> **KESSA:**
> *(untying it, not looking up)*
> They took the hours off the board because the hours stopped scaring anyone. We will keep the count
> anyway, keeper. Ours is older.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Three knots left on the cord, and a winter in them. The till does not throw away a good counting
> because the enemy stopped needing it. Nothing in this basin that was learned in fear gets
> unlearned because the fear moved on.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, dusk, the first unrationed dark (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the square at dusk — and the WINDOWS STAY LIT: one by one, then whole rows, every
household burning past the old posted hour, nobody checking a board first, nobody counting the cost
aloud. The stall's lamp burns among them, indistinguishable now — one warm window in a lit street.
Above the roofs, the first stars; the year turning somewhere in the dark, unannounced.

**Image:** The first unrationed dusk. The basin learned lighting from the counter.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> No speech, no decree, no returning of what was paid. The ration ended the way our rulings end:
> a notice came down, and everybody went home and did the obvious thing with their lamp. If the
> Office wanted its hours back it should not have taught the square to read its board.
>
> Somewhere behind the stars, a year is turning that none of us have invoiced yet. Let it turn.

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, both, then — FINAL HOOK (~16%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: night, the stall, the last entry going in. On the counter: the Office's
sealed delivery flat — machine wax, machine knot-rows — BESIDE the open register, keeper's grammar
and basin marks, each paper lying in the other's reach. Kessa's hand rests easy on the TILL — not
the box. Ira writes. The window is shuttered; the lamp is trimmed; three knots hang quiet on the
cord.

**Image:** Two books at one table, each under the other's grammar now — and the table is nobody's
uncle's.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, the chapter's last entry)*
> Entered: the terms, accepted; the first delivery, received; the hours, struck; the school's
> three — still unnamed; the founder's mother — still off the record, where she stays. Entered: a
> counter where paper goes to be read, and nobody owns the table.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Both, then.

## Writing & art notes

- **The finale's three jobs, in order: the acceptance in the machine's own hand; the first delivery
  and the struck hours; both books at one table.** The acceptance must be SHORT and machine-cold —
  no apology, no reference to the disavowal, no line about the individual: the machine begins
  behaving as if the terms were always its own idea. That seamlessness is the point; Ira's caption
  names it once (you will never find the seam) and the page moves.
- **The institution completes itself in Panel 2:** a BASIN voice reads the machine's paper at the
  counter of record. The counting-lesson women's arc lands here — from queue (P001) to fetchers
  (P004) to readers (P010). Keep the elder reader's reading halting and clear, not eloquent. Ira
  says one line only; the counter attends its own reading. This is the chapter's thesis made flesh:
  paper goes to be read, and the reading belongs to whoever came to hear it.
- **The delivery must be ordinary.** Dust, wax, somebody's lunch on a ledger — the revolution
  arrives looking like clerking, because it IS clerking. The lesser count-house hand, not the
  runner: the runner earned his rest; a nerveless efficient stranger shows the machine intends this
  as ROUTINE, which is the true victory — routine is the strongest grammar there is.
- **Rekhak's paper stays folded.** One visual beat of it (the hard rectangle in the pocket, his eyes
  on the delivery) and no more — his page is another chapter's warmth. His caption-line of presence
  is professional attention, not menace.
- **The knot-untie is the till's benediction:** three knots remain — the basin does not unlearn what
  it learned in fear. Play it small: fingers, cord, one knot gone, three quiet.
- **The unrationed dusk is the visual finale-before-the-finale:** lit windows in rows, the stall
  indistinguishable at last. The year-nineteen whisper lives HERE (one caption clause — "a year is
  turning that none of us have invoiced") and nowhere else. Do not explain; the next chapter opens
  on it if the story wants it.
- **The final image is composition, not event:** two papers side by side in the lamplight, each in
  the other's reach. The last entry is a LIST — the chapter's whole state, itemized — and the
  smallest caption is the method itself: *Both, then.* Mother absent by design; the thread quiet;
  her last answer still working. The three hands stay unnamed — itemized unnamed.
- **The imperative ladder closes its chapter run:** *Read both.* (Full ladder: knock / ask him / read
  it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 013: ONE — SPENT (Page 008). Mother never on panel,
  never speaks. Loom absent. The three entered hands unnamed (itemized in the last entry). The
  clerk: absent from panel — he is a queue figure now, not a plot; do not feature him. The foreman:
  not featured. The counting-lesson women: seventh appearance, the readers. Rekhak: professional
  eyes, paper folded, staying folded. The oil-cord: three knots after this page. The window: running
  into routine. The box: shut, untended — Kessa's hand is on the TILL in the final beat, which is
  the safety the chapter earned. Kessa keeps the till.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The acceptance and delivery:
  machine seal, regular knot-style, knot-work tags. Hand-lamps at dusk. Lamp-light everywhere; no
  window-glow reads as electric. The lit square is oil-light in every window.
- **Numbers:** one sealed acceptance; one reading by a basin voice; one first weekly delivery (three
  bundles: granary, fuel, permits); one permit-dark struck; one knot untied (three remain); one
  unrationed dusk; two books at one table; chain-stop ONE — SPENT (Page 008).
