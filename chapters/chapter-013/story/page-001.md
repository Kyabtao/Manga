# THREADBORN — Chapter 13 — Page 001

**Chapter title:** *The Second Office*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the stall and the square, first morning after
**Page type:** Chapter opening — 7 panels, ends with the counter-book's first hard ruling owed

> Beats: **the queue** — dawn, and the basin is already lined up at the stall: the street has decided
> where its paper goes now; **the first ruling of record** — a claim the Office would have sat on for
> a season, ruled in a morning, in the Office's own grammar, in her own book; **the shut counter** —
> across the square, the Office's own hatch is closed "pending the count," and the basin learns what
> a suspended count costs the suspension's authors; **the caught claimant** — a man whose paymaster
> will only honour a paper the Office has stamped, and the Office has stopped stamping; **the honest
> man's warning** — Rekhak, off shift, says the quiet part: they cannot count hands, so they are
> counting what hands hold; **the crack** — a claim the register cannot refuse without bias and
> cannot grant without hurting the school; and **the ruling owed** — she takes it, of record.
>
> **This page:** the street brings its paper. The second Office opens for business, and the first
> hard case walks in before the lamp is warm.
>
> **Chapter 013 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — no thread
> this page; her last answer is still working. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered
> hands remain unnamed.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, dawn, the queue (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: first light, the stall, shutters opening — and a **queue**. A dozen basin folk
along the alley wall with papers, knots, and faces: a widow with a tally-stick, two neighbours and
one knotted boundary-cord between them, a young couple with a debt-braid, the counting-lesson women
with a dispute they could not settle themselves. **Kessa** at the counter, already appraising the
line like a rich man appraising weather.

**Image:** The street decided overnight where its paper goes.

> **KESSA:**
> *(not moving from the lamp)*
> They started gathering at the fourth bell. I let them. A queue is a verdict the street has already
> reached, keeper — you are only the hand that writes it down.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nine days ago this counter mended seams and appraised knots. Today it is the only table in the
> basin where a record gets made and nobody's uncle owns the table.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the first ruling (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the counter, lamp-low. The widow's tally-stick on the wood; the **register
open**; Ira reading the notches against the Office's own schedule — and entering the ruling in the
back of the register, in the basin's grammar, with the widow's thumb-mark beside it.

**Image:** The Office would have sat on this for a season. It takes a morning, because the book is
hungry and the keeper is not.

> **IRA:**
> *(entering, level)*
> The notches say what the notches say. Owed: six measures, lent at hollow-interest — which the
> schedule calls void. You are owed six measures and your thumb goes here.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Ruled in the Office's own grammar, because that is the only grammar the basin's debts were ever
> written in — and entered in my book, because their book is shut.

---

## PANEL 3 — Wide, the shut counter (~13%)

**Camera:** Wide: across the square, the Office's public hatch — shuttered, a knot-marked notice
nailed to the board: **the counter is closed pending the count.** A handful of basin folk stand
before it, unreadable, then turn and walk toward the stall.

**Image:** The suspension has a price, and the Office is paying it in trade.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They shut their own counter "pending the count" — which means no stamps, no validated papers, no
> fees collected, no disputes heard. The machine cannot count, so the machine does nothing, and the
> basin's paper has to go somewhere.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> It goes to my counter. They built the plug. The plug is working.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the caught claimant (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: a worn-out man at the counter — a **pay-chit** in his hand, Office stock, blank
seal: his paymaster will only honour it **stamped**, and the only stamp in the basin is behind the
shutter. He is not asking for a ruling. He is asking what a paper is worth when its office stops.

**Image:** The first case the register cannot mend with grammar: a paper hostage to a suspension.

> **CLAIMANT:**
> The yard foreman says: no stamp, no pay. The Office says: pending the count. My family says:
> nothing to eat by winter.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> And there is the crack under the whole morning. I can rule debts void. I cannot rule a stamp into
> existing. They have found the one thing my book cannot do without becoming them: *be the only
> door, and lock it.*

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the honest man's warning (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the queue's edge. **Rekhak Vahni**, off shift, no warrant showing, a paper of
his own folded in his hand — and he gives the counter the day's real news at a murmur, professional
to professional.

**Image:** The one honest man in the building, telling the counter-book what the building is now for.

> **REKHAK:**
> *(quiet)*
> The count of hands is suspended. So they have started counting what hands hold. Granary seals this
> morning. The fuel-yards. The lamp-oil. Inventory, keeper. A machine that cannot count people will
> count *things*, and things do not have words on file to stop it.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Inventory does not refuse. Inventory does not have a founder whose no stands on file. They are
> going to count their way around the wall they cannot read.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close-medium, the crack (~14%)

**Camera:** Close-medium: the last claim of the morning, and it is the trap: a **bound agreement**
old Office stock, one seal, two knot-hands — a lease. The tenants are the **school's three** — the
entered hands, unnamed, holding their tenancy of the alley room together. The landlord's claim,
filed proper: the school's room owes a year's back-rent under a paper the Office stamped before the
school ever held the keys.

**Image:** Refuse it and the register is biased. Grant it and the rent collectors get a lawful key
to the school's own door.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Filed proper. Sealed proper. Old enough to predate the definition — old enough to predate the
> school's *name*. Somebody has been saving this paper for exactly this morning, and I do not think
> it was the tenants.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the ruling owed — FINAL HOOK (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: Ira with the lease flat on the counter under the lamp, the register open
beside it, Kessa's hands folded still, the queue at her shoulders not breathing. Her gloved hand
takes the pen.

**Image:** The register's first hard ruling, taken of record, before the basin, with the school in
the balance and the Office's inventory-count rolling in the background like weather.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record)*
> The register will rule.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The Office is counting what hands hold. The street has brought me everything else. So the second
> Office starts where the first one is weakest — with a paper they sealed, against people they
> cannot name, in a room they cannot count.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Rule it, keeper.

---

## Writing & art notes

- **The chapter opener's job is the new equilibrium and its first crack, in that order.** The queue
  is the victory lap (the street chose the counter-book); the shut Office hatch is the cost of the
  suspension landing on its own authors; the pay-chit is the cost landing on the basin; Rekhak's
  inventory warning is the strategic clock; the lease is the first crack. Do not resolve the lease —
  the ruling is Page 002's whole business.
- **The queue must be specific, not a crowd.** Named-by-object claimants: a widow's tally-stick, a
  boundary-cord, a debt-braid, the counting-lesson women's dispute. Objects first, faces second —
  the basin brings *records*, which is the whole point of a counter-Office.
- **The widow's ruling is the page's tutorial.** One ruling, shown end to end (notches, schedule,
  entry, thumb-mark), teaches the reader how the second Office works: the Office's grammar, the
  keeper's book, the basin's mark. Keep it small and fast — it is the contrast that makes the lease
  land as *the one she cannot simply read*.
- **Rekhak's beat is strategy, not menace.** Off shift, own paper in hand (never explained this page
  — his own business at the counter is a later page's warmth). The inventory warning is the
  chapter's strategic engine: they cannot count people, so they will count everything people hold.
  Do not have Ira answer it this page — the caption carries the dread.
- **The lease trap must be legally clean.** Office-stamped, predating the school's name, tenants
  unnamed-but-identifiable (the three entered hands — their unnamed-ness is now a vulnerability:
  papers cannot name them, and a room can be taken from the nameless). "Somebody has been saving
  this paper" — the clerk, or the landlord at the clerk's suggestion; do not say on-panel. The
  chapter's villain remains procedure.
- **The imperative ladder opens its new chapter run:** *Rule it, keeper.* (Ladder so far: knock /
  ask him / read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 013: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks —
  no thread this page; her last answer is still working (the direction knot stays tied; the entry is
  in the book). Loom absent. The three entered hands remain unnamed — and their unnamed-ness is the
  lease's leverage (papers bind names; the nameless can be dispossessed on a technicality). The
  amendment is still armed on Room Nine's table. The Office's counter is shut pending the count; the
  register is the basin's working table. Kessa keeps the till; the box stays shut under the counter.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage; all papers are Office stock,
  tally-sticks, knot-cords, braids — no lettering anywhere; the shut-hatch notice shows knot-marks
  and one seal only.
- **Numbers:** one queue, a dozen deep; one ruling entered (six measures, hollow-interest void); one
  hatch shut; one pay-chit unstamped; one inventory count beginning (granary, fuel, lamp-oil); one
  lease, old stock, one seal, three unnamed tenants; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
