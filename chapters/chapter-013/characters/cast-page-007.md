## IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the third grammar)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Both books, both ways, or neither."
```
> **Page 007 note:** answers the filing choice with symmetry as siegecraft — the register files, IF
> the count-house delivers its own counts, seal-logs, and permits weekly, in duplicate, in the
> register's grammar, for entry and public reading. Reads the term aloud to the square, reads it
> word-for-word to the runner, and — when the clerk blinks informally — enters the unsigned slip as
> received and goes back to her book. The register does not chase informality. It waits for it to
> become official.

## KESSA (the till; the appraiser's read)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"This is not the Office answering. This is one man in a quiet room, hoping."
```
> **Page 007 note:** counts the morning candle, and then delivers the page's sharpest appraisal: the
> bare slip is not the machine's hand — it is a clerk's, alone, out of hours. At the final beat her
> hand on the box is company, not warning: the till knows the difference between a trap and a man.

## THE RUNNER (fourth appearance — made a messenger)
No full sheet; Ch. 004, 005, 006, 013. Plain grey. For two days he carried what seals decide; today
he must carry what a keeper said — the counter-condition, word for word, into a room that punishes
error.

```
The Runner / Manav / Agnikhand / plain grey, uncommitted / paid by the road /
Carry: what a seal decides, to whose door it lands /
"Both books, both ways, or neither."
```
> **Page 007 note:** receives the sealed answer and the spoken terms both — and stands still while
> the keeper reads them to him slowly, with the obvious questions answered before he can ask. The
> face does not change; the hands get too careful with the paper. It is not a promotion.

## THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (fourth appearance — the readers of the bare slip)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–013. The pair who fetch the Office's paper to the counter. Tonight the slip
they untie has no wax on it, and they turn it over twice before they run it across the square.

```
The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Fetch: bring the Office's paper to the counter for reading of record /
"This one is not sealed. Is it still real?"
```
> **Page 007 note:** their double-check of the missing seal is the basin learning to read paper the
> way the counter reads paper — the seal was the proof, and its absence is now legible as a message.
> They are becoming the street's own appraisers.

## THE PORTER (one-off — the other thing)
No full sheet; Ch. 013. A big man with sacks' shoulders, mid-crowd, who laughs helplessly at the
reading — one hand on his knee, bent, not because it is funny.

```
The Porter / Manav / Agnikhand / sack-worn, winter-hungry / owed the contents of his own granary /
Carry: what the seals say, to nobody /
"It is not a funny laugh. It is the other thing."
```
> **Page 007 note:** has carried sacks past the granary seal all winter and never once been allowed
> to know what it said. The counter-condition is the first paper he ever heard that was about HIM
> being read. The laugh is where the queue's quiet breaks — and then the queue reforms, because he
> is not the only one.
