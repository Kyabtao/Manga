## THE SURVEYOR (the Office's inventory grammar, walking)
No full sheet; Ch. 013. Never named. A plain grey figure: waxed-canvas coat, tally-slate slung on
cord, measuring cord at the belt, one wax seal on a neck-cord.

```
The Surveyor / Manav / Agnikhand / grey, unspooled / counts, and does not refuse /
Slate: enter a thing's count — things only /
"Racks, four. Contents by count?"
```
> **Page 003 note:** not a villain — procedure with a brush. Counts the room correctly and
> completely, copies the one thing the school's people can safely give (a knot), leaves the name
> column blank without comment, seals the count, and files a room where nobody lives. The menace is
> the empty row, not the hand that holds it.

## THE THREE ENTERED HANDS (shown, not named — cooperation as the weapon)
No full sheet; Ch. 012–013. Entered by knot at the school's racks (Ch. 012), never by name. **Shown
this page, standing test:** an elderly Kshudra woman, rope-scarred knuckles; a stout middle-aged
Manav man, dock-worker's hands; a young Manavkin, counting-cord wound at the wrist. None speaks.

```
The Three Entered Hands / Manavkin, Kshudra, Manav / Agnikhand / knot-entered, no names /
Hold: possession by knot, entry by knot /
"Racks, four. Persons —" *(the row stays blank)*
```
> **Page 003 note:** present, visible, cooperative, and impossible to enter: they offer the entered
> knot for the slate to copy and no name to hang it on. The Page 002 finding — the tenancy holds
> hands, not names — walks into the room ahead of the register. Keep the three visual descriptions
> stable for future pages; their unnamed-ness is now load-bearing.
