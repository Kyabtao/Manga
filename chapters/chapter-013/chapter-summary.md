## Synopsis

The Office suspends its own count — *pending the count* — and discovers that a machine that cannot
count can still not do anything: no stamps, no validated paper, no disputes heard. **The basin's
paper has to go somewhere, and it goes to the Knot & Nail.** Chapter 013 is the ten days in which a
seamstress's counter becomes the basin's working table of record — the second Office — and learns,
clause by clause, what that costs.

It opens with **a queue at dawn that is itself a verdict** (the street decided overnight where its
paper goes), the first ruling in the keeper's book — six measures, hollow-interest void, in the
Office's own grammar because that is the only grammar the basin's debts were ever written in — and
the first crack: **an old Office-stamped lease against the school's three unnamed hands**, saved for
exactly this morning by somebody who is not the tenants. Ira's ruling makes the chapter's method:
**enforceable but unbindable** — a paper binds names, the tenancy holds hands, and the register so
finds. The Office's grammar, used to make the Office's paper helpless.

The stamp question ripens into the counter's first mint: the register cannot stamp, so it
**certifies** — a debt disputed pending the count, honour it or defy a record by name — and the
foreman pays, and Kessa bridles the same hour: **a keep is not a mint.** The Office answers with
inventory (granary, fuel, lamp-oil), an inspection demand on the school's room, and the amendment
file — and finds nothing: **the slate can carry the knot, never the hand**; the room counts correct
and files as a room where nobody lives, which the Office cannot tax. The clerk's real stroke is the
mint clause — *paper under stamp, all else not record* — read aloud at the counter by the keeper
because the street now routes the Office's own paper through the counter to be read (the reading of
record: an institution nobody planned).

The wobble (does a certificate still count?) is answered by the register paying its debts before
noon: **a definition rules forward; it cannot reach backward over what was already witnessed** — and
the foreman honours in view of his yard, then buys the new thing: **protection of record — entered,
not read aloud.** The trap that follows (endorsement only at the Office's counter — the counter that
is shut) is answered by a door built in the middle of it: **the endorsement window.** *They demanded
a counter. Here is one.* The clerk's counter-counter is the first unwritten move — sent-speech:
file weekly in Office grammar or be named — and the register's third-grammar answer is symmetry as
siegecraft: **both books, both ways, or neither.** The clerk blinks informally (unsigned, unsealed,
out of hours), the register enters the slip as received and waits — and when the Office formalizes
the courtesy it never agreed to, the two papers are read side by side, and the clerk **crosses the
threshold as a man**: the chapter's ONE chain-stop, spent on the lamplight's edge. His bare
**thumb-mark** in the wax beside the keeper's knot is the basin's grammar learned from two years of
reading the register.

The machine eats its one flexible part: **the disavowal** — "the act of an individual, without
authority" — erasure by passive voice, the first man it ever stripped a name off of, on purpose.
The register answers with the paper (it may decline his act; **it may not decline his mark**) and
with the mirror of its own school: **protection of record, granted to the enemy** — his name
entered, not read aloud — and enters the last fact without comment: *seen, at the hatch, dusk,
standing in the queue of the basin's people. Him, too.* The finale closes the loop: the terms
**accepted in the machine's own hand** — no apology, no seam anyone will ever find — the first
weekly delivery arriving looking like clerking (the revolution IS clerking), the permit-dark struck
because **a light read in public cannot be rationed in the dark**, the first unrationed dusk with
the stall's lamp indistinguishable at last — and **both books at one table, each under the other's
grammar**, at a counter where paper goes to be read and **nobody owns the table.**

## What the chapter built (state carried forward)

- **The second Office, in routine:** endorsements at the window; the weekly delivery (granary,
  fuel, permits — duplicate, sealed, publicly read); the reading of record performed by basin
  readers; the acceptance seamless into procedure. Routine is the strongest grammar there is.
- **New institutions, priced:** the mint and its limit (*a keep is not a mint* — one certificate
  per disputed debt, no interest, void at the count's end, the keeper's name at every foot);
  **protection of record** (*entered, not read aloud*) — first used on the enemy; the window's
  price knotted in the till-cord (**three knots remain** — nothing learned in fear gets unlearned).
- **The certificate economy:** running. The foreman's gate rule stands (paid against the keeper's
  paper). The claimant is paid; his arc closed at the window.
- **People:** the clerk erased by his own machine and protected by the register — a queue figure
  now, not a plot; the counting-lesson women arc queue → fetchers → **readers of record**; Rekhak's
  folded paper shown (the FOLD) and **not presented** — his page is another chapter's warmth; the
  runner promoted to messenger and rested; the foreman protected, unnamed on-panel.
- **Unspent on purpose:** the mother — never on panel, never speaking; her last answer still
  working. Loom absent all chapter. The three entered hands **still unnamed** — armoured, itemized
  unnamed in the final entry. Year nineteen turned once, in a caption, unannounced.

## Chapter engine (what made it run)

- **Grammar as siegecraft, both directions:** every Office move is a definition; every counter-move
  is a third thing — not grant, not refuse (rule), not file, not refuse (symmetry), not written,
  not refused (entered as received). The ladder this chapter: *rule it, keeper / count it, then /
  light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway / read it back — both
  ways / cross, then / him, too / read both.*
- **Records are the weapon and the prize:** papers wanted to be unreadable; the counter's whole
  trade is making them readable — at the price of the keeper's name on everything that leaves.
