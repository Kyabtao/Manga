# Chapter 002 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Minute That Owes* / *वह मिनट जो ऋणी है*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — up-terrace, the re-review chamber (first interior)
**Page type:** Single-room pressure page (first in the series) — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

> Beats carried from Page 004: Rule 9's broken night, the self-initialled cross-out, "everything the
> Office touches" as the page's knife-edge. Beats carried from Chapter 001: the falsified audit
> ("Nothing more", Pages 008–009), the chain grammar (**running = control, stopping = his tell**, and
> Ira is the room's one reader of chains), the neck-line past the jaw, the Fraying arc ledger.
>
> **Chain canon, harmonised (do not contradict Ch. 001):** a *momentary* stop is a tell — costless,
> involuntary, readable (Ch. 001 Pages 008–009). A **held stop** is the oath: chain stood still from the
> first sworn word to the last, counting nothing but its owner while it stands. Under a held stop a
> false word is not a tell, it is a **deposit** — debt added on panel, instantly. The oath weaponises
> Rekhak's own tell against him. The Ch. 001 card rule (*The Council Kneels*: playable only while a
> Reckoner's chain is stopped) remains valid under both readings.
>
> **Budget:** Chapter 2's single chain-stop is *demanded* here and **spent on Page 006**, on something
> smaller than the truth. Nothing stops on this page. The chain runs in every panel it appears in.

## PANEL 1 — Wide (~14%)

**Camera:** Up-terrace corridor, morning, day five: one door in a wall of identical stone. Rekhak
before it, standing, greatcoat, collar high.
**Image:** The **re-review chamber door, drawn for the first time**: plain grey wood, no crest, no
number, no plate, not even a hinge-shadow out of true. Around it the corridor is exactly as clean as
every other corridor, which is the point. Rekhak's hand is not on the door; the door is already open
half a hand's width. Procedure does not knock and does not wait.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> The re-review chamber has a door. The door has nothing on it. No crest, no number, no name. In a
> building where every cross-out has a hand, the room that judges hands is the only unmarked thing.

## PANEL 2 — Medium (~13%)

**Camera:** Inside, from the door's side: the curved stone table, three seated **AUDITORS** in grey
(waist-up, faces plain and ageless — extras, no names, no return), one docket-slate before the middle
seat. No fourth chair. Rekhak enters the frame standing; the chain in his hand runs.
**Image:** The trinity of procedure. They do not look up as he enters; the middle auditor turns the
slate one quarter-turn toward him, which is the whole ceremony. Stone light, zero ash, no window — the
chamber's only light is the corridor's, through the open door behind Rekhak.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> Three auditors. No fourth chair. The reviewed stand; standing is not punishment, it is only what the
> reviewed do, the way the seated are only ever clerks.

## PANEL 3 — Close + inset (~13%)

**Camera:** On the docket-slate as the middle auditor reads from it; inset strip, macro: the audit in
**Rekhak's own hand** — line after line, and beside every line a small grey initial, his, dry with age
or fresh from the hour, all of them his.
**Image:** The read-back, procedural as breathing: the auditor's flat voice (off-panel balloon tail
into the frame edge) while Rekhak's thumb, in the present, moves one link of the running chain. The
inset is the page's quiet knife: **the Office initials everything it writes, and he initialled
everything he wrote** — every line of the audit that lied.

> **AUDITOR ONE (dialogue, off-panel, level):**
> Your hand. Your initial. Every line.
>
> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> A lie in ink is not the crime. A lie in ink with your own initial beside it is a filing the Office
> can hold forever. They are not reading the audit. They are reading the initials.

## PANEL 4 — Medium close (~14%)

door: the corridor rail where the chamber's docket-slate lives (planted, unfocused).

> **AUDITOR TWO (dialogue, level, almost kind):**
> The Office initials everything it touches, Reckoner.
>
> **AUDITOR TWO (dialogue, second balloon, same level):**
> Everything it touches.

## PANEL 5 — Medium (~13%)

**Camera:** Auditor Three, waist-up, one grey hand open on the slate; then Rekhak's face at frame edge,
collar high, the under-eye line hidden, the brow suture catching stone light.
**Image:** The demand, in procedure-speak, no heat: at tomorrow's first bell, the commissioned hour
sworn on a **held stop** — chain stood still from first sworn word to last. While it stands it counts
nothing but its owner, and it hears him. Rekhak's one line of the page arrives here, flat as a filed
chit — the same sentence he has given every review since the neck-line grew: a statement of fact that
is also a refusal.

> **AUDITOR THREE (dialogue, level):**
> First bell. The hour, sworn on a held stop. First word to last, the chain stands still. While it
> stands, it counts only you — and it hears you. A false word under a held stop is not a tell,
> Reckoner. It is a deposit.
>
> **REKHAK (dialogue, flat, his only line):**
> It always runs.
>
> **AUDITOR THREE (dialogue, level, closing the slate):**
> Tomorrow it will stand.

## PANEL 6 — Macro (~13%)

**Camera:** His fist at his side, the brass chain running through the fingers; inset strip: the
**neck-line past the jaw**, restated on panel for the first time since Chapter 001, stone light on it.
**Image:** The stakes, drawn as arithmetic: the chain's links bright with running; the neck-line dark
under the collar's edge. Caption does the maths the chamber won't say aloud: his debt is already past
the jaw; a deposit under oath does not add a line, it adds a **fray** — and the Council does not fray
where the basin can see. The demand is not a question about the hour. It is a price tag on his neck.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> They are not asking for the truth. They are asking what the truth costs him, link by link, in the one
> place his chain cannot hide.

## PANEL 7 — Macro (~17%) — HOOK

**Camera:** Leaving, corridor: the chamber's **docket-slate** on its rail beside the door; Rekhak's
thumb at its edge; shallow focus; the open chamber door behind, empty seats beyond.
**Image:** The hour's entry on the docket: initialled in **crimson, dry** — and two columns of
date-strokes beside it, *filed* and *scheduled*. The scheduled stroke stands one stroke **left** of the
filed stroke. One day earlier. The re-review of the falsified audit was on the docket **before the
audit was filed** — before Rekhak said "Nothing more", before the ink of his lie was dry downstairs.
His chain runs; his thumb does not move; the corridor light is exactly as clean as it was this morning.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> The principal did not react to the lie. The principal *pre-filed* it. Which means the buyer knew what
> the Reckoner would see, and what he would say about it, before the Reckoner knew himself.
>
> **CAPTION (none spoken, smaller):**
> Tomorrow's first bell has two owners now. Only one of them is procedure.

## Writing & art notes

- **First single-room page in the series.** The basin does not appear; its absence is continuity — day
  five of the owned quiet, day five of the dead shutters. Do not cut away to the market "for air"; the
  pressure is the point.
- **Nothing stops on this page.** The chain runs in every panel it appears in (2, 5, 6, 7). Chapter 2's
  one chain-stop is spent on Page 006, on something smaller than the truth.
- **Held-stop canon is new but backward-compatible:** momentary stop = tell (Ch. 001 grammar, unchanged,
  costless); held stop = oath (new, lethal). Ira's chain-reading (Ch. 001 Page 009: "the room's one
  reader of chains") is the reason this oath will matter later — a held stop is *readable afterwards*
  by anyone who can read chains (payoff seed for Page 006's hook).
- **The auditors are extras:** three, grey, nameless, waist-up maximum, no return this chapter. They are
  procedure's trinity, not characters; do not give one of them a distinguishing scar, ring or tick.
- **Colour rule:** crimson appears once (Panel 7 docket initial), dry, alone in its panel. No grey wax
  shares the frame with it. The chamber is stone-grey throughout; the corridor light is the only warm
  note and it comes from outside the room.
- **No lettering in art:** slate lines, initials and date-strokes are abstract strokes; the crimson
  initial is one abstract glyph (same hand-feel as Page 004's self-initial — *keep them visually
  identical, they are the same hand*).
- **Rekhak's one line** ("It always runs.") is his first spoken words since Chapter 001 Page 006's
  "Don't." Keep it uninflected in lettering notes.
- **The four notes stay silent; the Loom never speaks;** no hum change on panel; Kessa, Ira, Lekh, the
  cuff-chit all sleep this page — name their absence in the cast file, not on panel.
- **Door geometry:** the chamber door opens *inward*, half a hand's width, and is never shown closed in
  Chapter 2. When a later arc closes it, that closure is an event.
