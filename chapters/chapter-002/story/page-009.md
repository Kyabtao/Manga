# Chapter 002 — Page 009

**Chapter title:** *The Minute That Owes* / *वह मिनट जो ऋणी है*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the shut Knot & Nail (night, day nine)
**Page type:** The voice behind the shutter — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

> Beats carried from Page 008: the two-wax chit, the bell-tallied cuts, the slate's waiting line, the
> breath-held seam. Beats carried from Chapter 001: Kessa's lockbox and its shadowless cut-end (Page
> 010), the sewer-box lock rule, Ira's palm-stitch grammar (Page 001), "Kshudra remember" (Page 007).
>
> **Shutter rule, refined:** Kessa's voice through the wood is NOT an opening. Day nine: no slit, no
> face, no hand, no interior — only sound through a finger-gap in old boards. The box stays locked; the
> lock turning is heard, never shown.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide (~14%)

**Camera:** Night, basin, day nine: the shut Knot & Nail from the crate's side; no lamp at the stall
(Kessa keeps no light behind shutters — the stall's dark is her dark). Ira arrives with the kit and the
two-wax chit, lantern hooded low.
**Image:** The owned quiet at night is different: not suppressed, **listening**. Stall-row shutters
closed but stall-row ears awake; a cat on Guthli's stall-ledge faces the Knot & Nail like everybody
else. Ira sets the lantern on the crate, hood toward the shutter — offering the stall its only light.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> Nine days shut. I stopped asking the wood on day four. Tonight I brought the wood paperwork.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium (~13%)

**Camera:** Ira seated on the crate facing the shutter, chit open on her knee at the two waxes; the
boards dead dark above her head-height.
**Image:** She reads the chit aloud to the shutter — not the prose, the **seals**: grey first, crimson
second, touching. Her mender's thumb taps each wax in turn like tapping a tear's edge for depth. The
boards give nothing. Then, from behind them, dry as tally-thread: the first voice through the wood in
the series.

> **IRA (dialogue, flat, to the wood):**
> Grey says safety. Red says owner. They touch here. One chit, two hands.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, from behind the shutter, dry, no face anywhere):**
> Two waxes on one chit is two owners on one hand, child. And hands that share a chit will soon share
> a desk.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close (~13%)

**Camera:** On Ira's face at the voice: no startle, only settlement — the way she sits down at a tear.
The shutter boards behind her head stay dead dark; no slit, no eye, no shadow-line.
**Image:** The first lesson lands and is filed: the Council and the principal are becoming
**colleagues**. Caption keeps it in her register: she has been bought by one and scheduled by the
other; now the two are learning each other's filing habits, and the basin is the desk they will
eventually share.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> I had two enemies with good handwriting. Kessa just told me they're learning to write together.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium close (~14%)

**Camera:** Ira's gloves opening her kit on the crate: needle, filament coil, and — laid beside them —
a charcoal rubbing she made of Bhan's cut-fray (Page 008's crate-lid strokes transferred to paper).
**Image:** The second lesson, asked for in mender's dialect: she holds the rubbing up to the boards,
cut-angle toward the wood, the way you show a wound to a doctor's door. From behind: the question that
re-aims the whole chapter.

> **IRA (dialogue, small, holding up the rubbing):**
> One stroke. Nightly. Same angle, same depth. Steadier than me.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, from behind, slower now):**
> Read the cut again, child. Whose hand opens a tear the way a *seam* opens?

---

## PANEL 5 — Macro (~13%)

**Camera:** The rubbing, extreme close, under the hooded lantern turned toward it: the cut-stroke's two
ends drawn out by her charcoal — and the reveal, in texture: the cut does not end where a blade ends.
It ends where a **thread ends**: a tiny turn-back at each extremity, the stroke's own knot-off.
**Image:** The cutter is not cutting. The cutter is **unpicking** — opening Bhan's mark nightly the way
a seam is unpicked from its own end: stitch-grammar, not blade-grammar. And there is one stitch-grammar
in this city that Ira knows intimately: the one sewn across her own palm. Someone is copying her
stitch's handwriting onto a dock worker's arm, nightly, to see what it does when it is re-opened.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, smallest):**
> Blades leave ends. Seams leave turn-backs. Whatever opens Bhan every night opens him the way I was
> closed.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium (~13%)

**Camera:** Ira still seated, rubbing lowered, lantern low; the boards dark; her face doing the one
thing the series has never shown it do: asking the unforgivable question out loud.
**Image:** It escapes her — three words, flat, worse for flatness. The boards do not answer at once;
when the voice comes it is not refusal, it is **triage**: the old appraiser putting the cheaper debt
first, because tonight the expensive one would kill them both.

> **IRA (dialogue, flat, three words):**
> Who sewed me?
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, from behind, after a beat, dry as ever):**
> Not tonight. Tonight you need the cutter's name, not the sewer's.

---

## PANEL 7 — Macro (~17%) — HOOK

**Camera:** The shutter boards at lock-height, dead dark, from Ira's seated eye-line; the lantern's
hood-light reaching no higher than the wood's middle. Nothing visible but grain and old nail-heads.
**Image:** Sound as image: from behind the boards, one sound only, dry and exact — **a lockbox lock
turning**, first time since Chapter 001 Page 010. Then a second sound, softer: tally-thread paid out
one handful. Then silence, and the stall stays shut, and no light leaks, and no face comes. Kessa is
consulting the shadowless cut-end without opening her stall — comparing, tonight, its turn-backs to
something the rubbings just told her.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> The box stays locked. The lock still turns. In four Unspoolings the Knot & Nail has closed before noon
> once, spoken through its boards once, and opened its lockbox at night twice — and both times, it was
> about the same thread.
>
> **CAPTION (none spoken, smaller):**
> Ira Sutar sat in the dark and listened to her own stitch's grammar being checked against a stranger's
> arm, and understood, finally, what Kessa has been appraising all along: not the tear. The hand.

## Writing & art notes

- **Voice-only Kessa:** no slit, no face, no hand, no interior, no silhouette through boards. Lettering
  note: her balloons carry a wood-grain edge tint (style-guide: off-panel voices get texture, not
  faces). Day nine of the shutters; the voice is not an opening.
- **The lockbox lock is HEARD, never shown** (Panel 7): the sewer box stays locked per canon; no
  interior panel, no cut-end on panel this page. The thread paid out is sound-only (a soft rasp), not
  visible.
- **The unpick-reveal is the chapter's re-aim:** the nightly cutting is stitch-grammar copied from
  Ira's own palm-suture. This does NOT name the sewer; it names the *method's family*. Keep the
  sewer-box locked and unnamed; the cutter's name remains unrevealed until Chapter 3.
- **"Who sewed me?" is Ira's first out-loud ask** of the locked question (Ch. 001 Page 002: Kessa asked
  it first, the other way). Kessa's triage line postpones, never refuses — the refusal rule stands
  (she won't say who; she will say when it's safe, much later).
- **The cat on Guthli's ledge** (Panel 1) is the basin's chorus tonight: one animal, facing the stall
  like everybody else. It appears once, here, and never speaks (obviously) and never returns as a
  motif.
- **Chain budget closed; four notes silent; no chain on panel; the Loom never speaks.** No hum change.
  Rekhak, Lekh, Patra, Bhan, Guthli, Pira all off-panel; the census suppression continues unshown.
- **Night palette:** hooded-lantern warm against board-dark; no moon; ash falls visible only in Panel
  1's wide. Panel 7 is the darkest panel in the series so far — let it be nearly black with grain.
