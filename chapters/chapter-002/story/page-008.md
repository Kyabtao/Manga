# Chapter 002 — Page 008

**Chapter title:** *The Minute That Owes* / *वह मिनट जो ऋणी है*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — dock road & dock gate (night shift), Council Stair foot (dawn)
**Page type:** The proof lands / two waxes on one chit — 7 panels, hook at the bottom

> Beats carried from Page 007: the nightly cut-fray, the doubled wage, the commission's payment line,
> Ira noticing the lend. Beats carried from Page 004: grey-and-crimson as two rooms' waxes; from
> Page 006: the bell (first-bell oath) as up-terrace time-grammar; from Ch. 001 Page 003: the market's
> bell-warning habit (bells are civic time the basin feels in its lamps).
>
> **Colour-rule event:** grey wax and crimson wax **touch on one chit** this page — the first time the
> two share a surface. Everywhere else in Chapter 2 they stay in separate panels.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide (~14%)

**Camera:** Dock road, dead daylight between shifts: crate stacks, rope-walk shadow, one bollard. Ira
and Bhan at the crate-lid bench; her charcoal drawing the cut's angle on the wood: one stroke, then the
same stroke again beside it, identical.
**Image:** The proof, in a mender's only dialect: *frays wander, cuts don't.* Bhan looks at the two
strokes a long time. His belief does not shatter; it **settles**, like a load finding a weaker back.
His coin-purse is open now: the doubled wage lying in it, new-struck, unwelcome.

> **BHAN (dialogue, quiet, to the wood):**
> Then somebody's been holding me still.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> He didn't get angry. Dock men don't. They get arithmetic.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium (~13%)

**Camera:** Two-shot at the bollard; Bhan's coat half off his shoulder as he reaches an inner pocket;
Ira's kit closed, her whole attention on the pocket.
**Image:** What he never thought to call strange, named at last: every night shift at the dock gate, a
**chit-boy** logs his arm with a slate stylus before the shift — a "safety inspection", Council grey,
free of charge, since the re-stamping began. He had thought it was kindness. The basin's owned quiet
had thought so too.

> **BHAN (dialogue, flat, handing over):**
> They look at the arm every night. Before the shift. Safety, they say.
>
> **IRA (dialogue, small, hand already out):**
> Show me the chit.

---

## PANEL 3 — Macro (~13%) — colour-rule event

**Camera:** The inspection chit in Ira's gloves, lantern-less daylight: paper grey as Office stone.
**Image:** **Two seals on one chit:** the Council-safety grey wax, standard, thumb-sized — and beside it,
touching its edge, a **small crimson counter-sign**, dry, formal, the same hand-feel as every other
crimson in Chapter 2. Grey and crimson sharing a surface for the first time in the series. No lettering;
the seals are the sentence.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> Two waxes on one chit. In the filing end that would be two hands on one claim. In the basin it is
> simpler and worse: two owners on one arm.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium close (~14%)

**Camera:** Night mend, third commission mend, hooded lantern: needle and filament at the cut-fray;
Ira's face low over the work; Bhan's face turned away out of dock courtesy.
**Image:** The test: mid-stitch Ira **holds her breath** — and the second filament, the fold's unasked
lend, dims and stops with her breath. The seam finishes on her air alone: slower, uglier, entirely
hers. By the knot-off she is gasping quietly into the lantern hood; Bhan hears it as effort and respects
it. First control over the bleed. It costs her the whole seam's worth of breath, and she pays it
gladly.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> I held my breath. It stopped. My own stitch went on without me lending me anything. Ugly seam. Mine.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close (~13%)

**Camera:** After: Ira alone at the crate, the chit open at its inspection column under the lantern;
Bhan asleep against the bollard at frame edge (double shifts take their tax).
**Image:** The mender-eye read, drawn as column-rhythm: cut-times logged in bell-strokes, not
shift-strokes. Ira's charcoal circles three of them: the same bells the up-terrace rings for filings —
the bells the market's lamps gutter to, the bell her oath-page morning rang to. The tearing keeps
**Office hours**.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> Shifts slide with the tide. Bells don't. This arm is being opened on a schedule rung from a terrace
> it has never seen.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide (~13%)

**Camera:** Dawn: the Council Stair foot from the basin side: Ira standing at its first step, kit on her
back, looking up into clean air; ash thinning with height as always.
**Image:** The bridge, seen from below: two rooms she has stood in — the stair (Ch. 001 Page 005's climb)
and the dock road — joined by a bell-rope she can't see. Caption states the inference and the
restraint: somewhere up there a filing bell rings and a dock arm opens; she does not climb. Not yet.
Menders wait at the tear; the tear, for once, is up there.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):**
> I know the stair's price now. I also know mine. So I stood at the bottom and let it wait for me.

---

## PANEL 7 — Macro (~17%) — HOOK

**Camera:** That night, dock gate: the **chit-boy's slate** on its standing desk under a shielded lamp;
his stylus paused mid-air; Bhan's shoulder passing the gate at frame edge, arm logged.
**Image:** The inspection column, abstract strokes and bell-tally marks — and at the column's foot,
**a new line added in the same formal hand as the commission prose**, waiting, unlogged: one more name,
one more arm. The stylus hovers over it the way a needle hovers over a tear's edge. Caption names what
art must not letter: the new line reads *Sutar, I.* The farm has come for the mender's own arm.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> The inspection has a second client as of tonight. The slate already knows her name; names are a count,
> and counts are a filing category, and the category wins.
>
> **CAPTION (none spoken, smaller):**
> Somewhere between the bell and the blade, the mender became the mark.

## Writing & art notes

- **Two-wax chit is the page's colour-rule exception:** grey and crimson touch ONLY on the chit (Panel
  3). Everywhere else in Chapter 2 the waxes stay in separate panels; do not reuse the touching-waxes
  image until the Chapter 2 close-out folds it into the grey-and-crimson correspondence hook.
- **The breath-hold is Ira's first agency over the bleed** and must cost her: the seam is visibly uglier
  (art: uneven stitch-rhythm) and she gasps at knot-off. No triumph caption; a mender's flat inventory.
- **Bhan's belief breaks as arithmetic, not anger** (Panel 1): keep his register dock-flat; his opened
  coin-purse is the page's only raised object.
- **The chit-boy is an extra:** child-clerk, slate stylus, no name, no face wider than Panel 7's
  shoulder-crop; he logs what he is given (Lekh's grammar, basin end).
- **Bell-times canon:** cuts logged in bell-strokes ties the dock farm to up-terrace filing bells — the
  same bells as Page 006's first-bell oath. Do not name which bell-ringer; the schedule's owner stays
  on paper.
- **Ira does not climb** (Panel 6): restraint beat; the stair's price (Ch. 001 Page 004: climb or be
  struck) is still unpaid debt in her ledger of memory.
- **Chain budget closed; four notes silent; shutters day eight, no slit; cuff-chit sleeps; Patra
  off-panel; the Loom never speaks.** No chain on panel; no hum change.
- **Art lettering rule:** slate lines, bell-tallies and the new line are abstract strokes; *Sutar, I.*
  exists only in caption.
