**Page type:** Chapter opening / rumour — 7 panels, ends on a seal

## PANEL 1 — Medium wide, the steam-pot rumour (~14%)

**Camera:** Street level in the basin, morning. Steam rising off a food pot between two stalls.
**Image:** **GUTHLI** (broad, ladle still in hand) and **PIRA** (thin, arms full of somebody else's
laundry) with their heads together over the steam. Around them the market works one octave too
quietly. Ash falls as always. Nobody says the word *yesterday*.

> **CAPTION:**
> The basin woke with a memory it had agreed to call a story.
>
> **GUTHLI (dialogue, low):**
> The Spindle blinked.
>
> **PIRA (dialogue, lower):**
> Spindles don't blink.
>
> **GUTHLI (dialogue):**
> Then I blinked. Four hundred of us blinked together, and my marks went quiet in me, and I felt the
> Loom *stop*.
>
> **PIRA (dialogue, already walking away):**
> Spindles. Don't. Blink.

## PANEL 2 — Wide, fear looks like paperwork (~13%)

**Camera:** Down the Reckoning Office's rows of black stone desks, morning light hard and clean.
**Image:** Every clerk re-stamping last night's records. **Twice.** The second stamp lands on the same
page as the first, half a finger off — a page with two seals is a page somebody might later say was
never sealed at all. No ash anywhere. No talking anywhere.

> **CAPTION:**
> Up-terrace, fear does not run or shout.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> It files.

**Camera:** Macro on the slip under Rekhak's thumb, held at the edge, unbroken. Beyond it, out of focus,

## Page notes for continuity

- **The rumour is the silent second's public face.** Nobody names the hour, the hum or the flicker in
  the same sentence as Ira. The basin's story stays folkloric ("four hundred of us blinked") while the
  reader knows the count was one second. Keep the two registers separate forever; the day they merge is
  a reveal.
- **Two stamps on one page = the Council's fear grammar.** A re-stamped record is deniable. Use the
  doubled seal as the up-terrace visual for "this never happened" from now on.
- **The mother-debt instalment is paid early and received without ink.** Kessa's thumb-rub is Kshudra
  writing; nothing is written down anywhere. The four notes are now stored in exactly one place in the
  world: a goblin's memory. That is both the safest and the most stealable vault in Agnikhand.
- **The four notes are a planted key, not a payoff.** Off-key, four notes, never words. Do not let them
  match anything audible before Chapter 3 at the earliest; when they finally match, it must match
  *wrongly* first.
- **Kessa closing the stall is her alarm bell.** She has heard a seam in the world (unpulled thread, a
  sealing, a swatch in her lockbox) and now a melody that she recognises without being able to say from
  where. She closes the stall to think. Do not show her thinking; show the shutters.
- **Lekh is named here** (reserved on Page 005): the visitors'-rail clerk. Young, procedural, and the
  only up-terrace face the basin will ever trust, later. Keep them seated-or-leaving in every scene;
  clerks never stand for anyone, except once, in a later arc.
- **Crimson wax grammar, now hard canon:** the Council's wax is **grey**; crimson belongs to no Sector on
  record. Any future crimson seal is the principal's paperwork, and the principal never appears — only
  slips, call-ups, re-reviews. The buyer reads lies for what they are: locks.
- **Chain budget for Chapter 2: one stop.** Do not spend it before Page 006, and spend it on something
  smaller than the truth.
- Sewer locked, principal unseen, knot-shape ownerless, Loom silent.
