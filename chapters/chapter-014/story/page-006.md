# THREADBORN — Chapter 14 — Page 006

**Chapter title:** *Year Nineteen*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the hatch board at dawn, the stall, the counter at dusk, deep winter
**Page type:** The hour that is not on the roll — 7 panels; the Office posts its hours and one of them is uncounted, the till opens to fund the school, and a man checks his fold

> Beats: **the posting** — overnight the Office has strung a cord under the hatch notice: its hours,
> tied in knot-work, no lettering, no clerk, exactly what the counter demanded; **the reading of
> record** — the counting-lesson women read the Office's hours aloud at dawn, and the basin learns
> when the machine is open, because it wrote the law that made that reading compulsory; **the gap** —
> one hour-group at the cord's end does not match: a night shift at the fuel-yards that appears on no
> roll the basin has ever read, an hour the Office's own schedule does not carry elsewhere. The
> register enters the SHAPE's first mechanism — the uncounted hour — and does not guess what happens
> in it; **the till opens** — Kessa lifts the lid, shut these two pages, and pays the winter's
> lesson-lines out of it in one entering, naming the risk as she does: a school paid by the till is a
> school the till can be accused of owning; **the fold is checked** — Rekhak, off shift, at dusk,
> without a word, puts two fingers on the register's back pocket to confirm his paper is still
> folded, and leaves; **the entry** — the register writes down the only thing that happened: the fold
> was tested, and held.
>
> **This page:** the machine's compliance as a confession by omission; the till becoming an
> institution. Nobody explains anything, and the page's smallest line is the whole page.
>
> **Chapter 014 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread
> quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk
> is never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure of Page 004 does not appear —
> the absence stays open.

## PANEL 1 — Wide, dawn, the posting (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the square before first light, the hatch board's post crusted with snow — and a new
cord strung taut beneath the sealed notice, hung with knot-work in even groups, no lettering
anywhere. Two of the counting-lesson women are already at the board with a hand lamp, one holding it
up, the other's fingers on the cord; behind them the queue is arriving faster than it has all winter.

**Image:** The Office has answered the symmetry before the basin was awake to see it done.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nobody saw it posted. No clerk, no seal-cart, no boots in the snow but ours. The machine worked at
> night — which, on a page about hours, is the first thing it told us.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, arriving)*
> Do not lift it down. Read it where it hangs. If it was given to the board, the board is where the
> reading happens.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the hours under fingers (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the new cord in the lamplight, the fern of knot-groups along it — six groups, each
group a shift, each knot an hour, evenly spaced and evenly finished. The elder's fingers walk the
groups; at the cord's end a seventh group hangs slightly apart, its knots a little deeper, tied in a
finish the basin now knows: the hand that is not the machine's.

**Image:** Six shifts the machine keeps in public, and a seventh that somebody keeps anyway.

> **THE ELDER READER:**
> *(counting the groups under her breath)*
> First to sixth — the yards. Second to fifth — the offices. Third to fourth — the count-house. Six
> of them. Six is the whole Office.

> **IRA:**
> *(quiet, of record)*
> There is a seventh.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the reading of record (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the counter of record, the queue packed in under the awning, the elder standing
where she always stands to read — the machine's paper read aloud by the basin's voice, lantern-lit,
snow in her hair. Ira inside the window, entering as it is spoken. Kessa at the till, arms folded,
listening the way an appraiser listens.

**Image:** The law the counter wrote, turned on the machine: the Office's hours, read in public, at a counter.

> **THE ELDER READER:**
> *(reading it out, slow and clear)*
> The Office is open, first bell to sixth, at the yards. Second to fifth, the offices. Third to
> fourth, the count-house.
>
> *(a pause; the cord's end)*
>
> And the fuel-yards, at night.

> **KESSA:**
> *(flat, to the queue)*
> That is not an hour. That is a hole with a rope around it.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the gap (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the counter's wood, the school's open book laid flat beside the posted cord's
rubbing — the old tally of Office hours in Nandi's dry hand, six groups only, no seventh. Ira's
finger between the two: the rubbing's extra group and the book's empty margin. Kessa's chalk beside
them, a stroke that stops where the roll stops.

**Image:** The machine has posted an hour it does not count — and the basin's oldest tally says so without a word.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, working it out loud)*
> Six groups on the old school's tally, going back before the Office had a wall to hang it on. Seven
> on the new cord. Whatever is happening at the fuel-yards at night this winter, the Office is not
> counting it in its own rolls.

> **KESSA:**
> *(entering it her way)*
> Then it is being done in an hour that does not exist. Those are the cheapest hours there are.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the till opens (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the till's lid up — the first time in the chapter — and inside it, in the
lamplight: stacked paper tallies bound in cord, a shallow tray of Office coin, Kessa's ash-ink pot.
Her hand has taken a pinch of the tallies and laid them on the school's book beside the lesson
column: nine lines, one transaction, entered. The queue watches; the school's book is between them
and it, on purpose.

**Image:** The second Office's first public purse: the counter paying for a school that teaches the machine's grammar.

> **KESSA:**
> *(counting the tallies down, formally)*
> Nine lines, the winter's lessons. Paid from the till, in one entering, so the book shows one hand
> and not nine pockets.
>
> *(and then the price, because she never lets an entry lie)*
>
> Enter this too, keeper: a school paid by the till is a school the till owns. They will say it. In
> the spring they will say it louder.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record)*
> Entered: the risk, both halves. The counter pays; the counter can be accused. A school nobody pays
> for is a school that closes.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium-close, dusk, the fold tested (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: dusk, the stall's counter, the lamp just lit and the window still open to
the snow. Rekhak stands on the queue side — ash-grey Council greatcoat, high collar, ash on his
shoulders that he has not brushed off, the ledger-slate hanging at his hip, the counting-chain
looped once around his fingers, still. His other hand has reached across the counter and two fingers
rest on the register's back pocket, on the fold's edge: not drawing it out, not opening it. He is
looking at the pocket, not at Ira. He says nothing.

**Image:** The owner of the fold, confirming that the register has kept its word — without asking it to.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He did not come to present it. He did not come to take it back. He came to put two fingers on the
> fold and feel that it was still folded, in a place where a whole square could see him do it.

---

## PANEL 7 — Close, the entry — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: night, the register open at the day's last page, the back pocket's corner and the
fold's edge visible at the frame's side, the lamplight low, snow past the shutter's slats. Ira's pen
has written one line and stopped; her hand is still. Behind, out of focus, the door's cold blue
rectangle where Rekhak went out.

**Image:** The register's only honest entry for a thing it cannot read: what the fold did.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, the day closed)*
> Entered: the Office's hours, read aloud, six counted and one not. Entered: nine lines paid from the
> till, and the risk with them. Entered: the fold was tested at dusk, in public, by its owner, and it
> held.

> **KESSA:**
> *(from the till, not looking up)*
> He never asked what was on it.

> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Still folded.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the uncounted hour (a mechanism, not a mystery); the till becoming a purse
  (an institution, with its risk entered in the same breath); the fold tested (a wordless test of the
  register's honour).** The page must not explain the night shift, must not name what the till will
  be accused of beyond the sentence Kessa says, and must not let Rekhak speak. Restraint is the
  page's whole method: six counted shifts, one that is not, and one line written under it.
- **The posting's chief fact is HOW it was posted.** No clerk, no seal-cart, no boots in the snow:
  the machine complied at night, in knot-work, with nobody to thank. Play Panel 1's emptiness as the
  confession — a machine that will do the public thing only when the public is asleep. Ira's order
  (*read it where it hangs*) keeps the counter's law intact even for a gift.
- **The gap must be found by comparison, not by intuition.** Panel 4 lays the new cord's rubbing
  beside the old school's tally of Office hours — six groups, older than the Office's walls. The
  seventh group is the gap, and it is tied in the wrong hand's finish (continuity with P.003–P.004):
  the basin can see the shield's shadow in the machine's own posted hours. Do not say what happens
  at the fuel-yards. The register's discipline: enter the hour that does not exist, and wait.
- **The till is an institution now, and Kessa says the cost out loud.** Panel 5 is formal: lid up,
  tallies counted down, nine lines, one entering, the school's book between the till and the queue.
  She names the accusation before anyone else can — *a school the till owns* — and Ira enters the
  risk in both halves. Do not let the moment read as generosity; it is a public purse being founded
  in front of witnesses who will remember the price.
- **Rekhak's silence is the page's centre and its hardest art problem.** Two fingers on the fold's
  edge, eyes on the pocket, chain looped and still, ash on the shoulders; the whole square able to
  see him do it. No dialogue, no eye contact with Ira, no reaction shot from the queue that turns it
  into a scene of menace. He is not threatening the fold; he is checking it. Then the empty blue
  rectangle of the door. The register writes only what happened: the fold was tested, and it held.
- **The ending is one line.** *Still folded.* No underlining, no crowd murmur, no final beat of
  music. Kessa's *he never asked what was on it* is the page's only gloss, and it should land as a
  fact about the register, not about Rekhak.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Still folded.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it / enter the thin / read
  the tag / let him / note the absence / still folded.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 014: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks —
  no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel and unnamed. The grey clerk: not on the
  page. The snow-heavy figure: does NOT appear; the absence stays open and is not mentioned again
  this page. Rekhak's paper: shown only as the fold's edge, never opened, never described. The
  runner: not on the page. Nandi: not on the page (her tally is, in her hand). The counting-lesson
  women: two at the board at dawn, the elder reading at the counter. The oil-cord: THREE knots,
  unmoved. The window: open, snow. The box: the till — opened this page (first time in the chapter),
  entered. Kessa keeps the till and now keeps a purse.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The posted hours: knot-groups
  on a cord, no lettering, no numerals. The school's tally: chalk and old knot-marks. The till's
  contents: paper tallies bound in cord, Office coin, ash-ink pot — nothing modern. Lamp, dawn, dusk
  and snow-light only.
- **Numbers:** one posting; one cord; seven hour-groups; six counted shifts; one uncounted night
  group; one old tally with six; one till opened; nine lines paid; one risk entered in both halves;
  one fold checked; zero words from Rekhak; one day closed; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
