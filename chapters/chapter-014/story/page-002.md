## PANEL 1 — Medium, morning, the thin delivery entered (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall, morning, snow still falling past the shutters. The delivery satchel
open on the counter: TWO bundles where the rhythm of ten weeks says three. Ira's gloved hands turn
the satchel's knot-work tag over — a NEW tag, knotted in the machine's grammar, tied where the old
one hung. The queue cranes. Kessa has already set the ledgers' receipt-rod ready and it stays
empty.

**Image:** The machine is short of something — fuel, hands, or candour. The register enters the
shortness, not the guess.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, entering)*
> Received: the weekly delivery — two bundles of three. Received: the satchel under a new tag, tied
> in the machine's grammar. The permit-ledger is not in it. Entered as received — thin, tagged, and
> unexplained.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Ten weeks of rhythm, and the first changed note is a subtraction with a knot on it. Machines do
> not run short by accident. Somebody decided what this week's paper would not say.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium-close, the basin reads the shortness (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the window-frame from outside — the first claimants of the day looking at
the two bundles the way basin folk look at a neighbour's shutters: counting what is missing without
saying it. The counting-lesson women at the rail's end, and the elder one's mouth a flat line.

**Image:** The counter reads the machine now — and the machine knows it has been read.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They saw it before I finished the entry. Ten weeks of full bundles, then the year is read out
> loud at midnight, and the next paper comes in short. Nobody in this queue believes in accidents.
> Neither does the machine. That is what makes it a message — and messages, at my counter, get
> entered, not answered.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the ask, entered as a debt (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the school's side room, the archive wall's table: Nandi's own book open — small,
thick, older than any roll in the Office — and Ira's name going into it in the OLD GRAMMAR, knot
by knot, Nandi's hands guiding the line while Ira writes. The keeper entered in somebody else's
record, as a debtor, for one reading.

**Image:** The second Office's founder, a line in a count older than the machine.

> **NANDI:**
> *(steady, guiding the knot-line)*
> The old grammar, keeper. A debtor's line says what was taken and what settles it. You took a
> reading. You settle in lines, not coin. Yours, now, on my books — until the count you bought is
> paid forward.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(entering herself, level)*
> Then enter it whole. Ira Sutar. Took: one reading, paid one line. The register does not ask for
> smaller.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the keeper learns entries from the other side (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: back at the stall's counter, lamplight: Kessa turning the till's lid once
— not opening it — and looking at Ira across the wood with the appraiser's oldest face, the one
that is almost fond.

**Image:** The till has been waiting for the keeper to learn what an entry feels like from the
other side.

> **KESSA:**
> *(almost fond)*
> Ten weeks of putting other people in books, keeper. And it took one reading to put YOU in one.
> Look at your hand. It shook.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(sitting down with the register)*
> It did not shake at the debt, Kessa. It shook at the grammar. Their book never once asked me what
> I owed. Hers asked before I finished sitting down.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the rod runs forward (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the archive table, lens and lamp: the oldest rod unrolled a hand's width across
the book — the dense year-knot at its head, then season-knot after season-knot marching down the
silver thread, tight, unhurried, unbroken. Nandi's finger at the fourth knot; Ira counting under
her breath.

**Image:** Thirty-nine years, every knot after the first a season the Office has no line on — and
the count still whole: no gap, no stolen stretch, no year somebody's uncle ruled out.

> **NANDI:**
> *(counting)*
> Every knot a season. Snow to snow. Thirty-nine, unbroken — theirs is nineteen and full of white
> spaces. Which of us, keeper, has been keeping the basin's actual record?
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Hers. It has always been hers. I built a counter because their paper was cruel. She kept a count
> because the paper never told the truth. We are not the same work. We are the same direction.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the second chain, named as far as Nandi will go (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the archive room, the two of them at the table, the rod between them like a
witness. Nandi re-rolling the rod with the care of a woman putting a child to bed, and answering
the question Ira has not asked out loud — one step, no further.

**Image:** The basin has been counted before, by something older — and that count never stopped.

> **IRA:**
> *(careful)*
> The knot said second chain. Second implies —
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(tying the roll)*
> It implies nothing you can invoice yet, and I read no further than what is ON the thread. Ask me
> again when your line is paid. The old school counts forward, keeper — not back. That is the whole
> of its grammar. Forward, and whole.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Two refusals in one winter — the machine's, and now hers. Hers at least is priced.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the fresh page — FINAL HOOK (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: night, the stall: the register open to a fresh page under the lamp, the
thin two-bundle satchel at its elbow, the new tag's knot hanging over the page's corner. Ira's pen
touches the paper and stops — and she turns instead to the BACK of the register, where Rekhak's
folded paper sits sealed under protection, and lays the fresh page's first mark as a SEE-ALSO: the
thin week pointing at the protected fold, at the year-knot, at Nandi's book. Kessa's hand rests on
the till. Three knots on the cord, snow past the shutters.

**Image:** The invoice's first column: a thin delivery, a keeper's line on older books, a rod that
runs thirty-nine unbroken years — entered, linked, and aimed.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, entering)*
> Entered: the week, thin. The tag, new. The keeper's line, on the school's books. The rod:
> thirty-nine, unbroken. The question the week is actually asking — *what is the machine saving
> its paper for?* — entered as the winter's open count.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Enter the thin.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the thin delivery entered (shortness, not suspicion — the guess is never
  written); the ask as a debt (the keeper's line on the school's books, in the old grammar); the rod
  runs forward (thirty-nine unbroken — the basin's actual record identified).** The page's discipline:
  enter everything, explain nothing. The missing permit-ledger stays a fact with a knot on it; Nandi
  refuses the second-chain question at her price; the caption's open question (*what is the machine
  saving its paper for?*) is the chapter's engine going forward.
- **The debtor's line is the page's heart.** The founder of the second Office, entered in somebody
  else's record, in a grammar that asks what you owe before you sit down — and her hand SHAKES, once,
  at the grammar, not the debt. Kessa's "almost fond" is the register's family showing: the till has
  been waiting for this lesson since the first certificate. Do not sentimentalize; play it as
  bookkeeping that happens to be tender.
- **Nandi refuses exactly one step of it.** She reads what is ON the thread and not a knot further —
  the old school counts forward, not back. Her price is not evasion; it is grammar. The two refusals
  (the machine's silence, Nandi's priced one) sit side by side in one caption: only one of them is
  honest about being a refusal.
- **The tag is the page's cold hook.** The satchel came re-tagged in the machine's grammar — a new
  knot where the old one hung. Do not decode it. Enter it. The basin's readers clock it instantly
  (P2); the register's discipline is to hold it as fact until it means something on its own.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Enter the thin.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it / enter the thin.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 014: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks —
  no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel. Rekhak's paper: on the shelf of
  protections, untouched — one SEE-ALSO mark, never opened. The clerk: not featured. The foreman:
  not featured. The counting-lesson women: cameo at the rail (the flat line). The oil-cord: THREE
  knots. The window: open, snow. The box: shut; the till's lid turned once, not opened. Kessa keeps
  the till. Nandi's book: small, thick, older than any Office roll — new prop, keep it consistent
  (dark cover, old grammar, no lettering).
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. Papers: knot-marks, tally
  groups, wax; the new tag: machine-grammar knot-work. The rod: silver thread, dense knots — never
  lettering. Lamp and daylight only; snow past the shutters.
- **Numbers:** one delivery, two bundles of three; one new tag; one permit-ledger missing; one
  debtor's line (Ira Sutar, took one reading, paid one line); one rod unrolled a hand's width:
