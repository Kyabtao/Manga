# THREADBORN — Chapter 14 — Page 009

**Chapter title:** *Year Nineteen*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the stall's door, the counter, and the long night before dawn, deep winter
**Page type:** The hour's keeper at the door — 7 panels; the snow-heavy figure crosses the threshold (the chapter's ONE chain-stop, SPENT), lays the paused paper down, and the register promises to read it aloud at dawn

> Beats: **the door** — dusk, snow, and the figure standing at the stall's door without knocking,
> hood up, snow loaded on the shoulders; the queue inside has gone quiet; **the decision** — the
> register entered protection of record two pages ago with the name-space empty, and now a man has
> come to use it: whether the door opens is decided out loud, in front of the square, by whether the
> counter meant what it wrote; **the threshold — CHAIN-STOP** — he steps inside, the chapter's ONE
> chain-stop spent on the crossing itself: the machine's uncounted hour entering a counter's public
> room as a man, hooded, in silence; **the paper** — the figure keeps the hood, sets one folded sheet
> on the counter, and steps back one pace with his hands loose; nothing is opened; **the entry** — the
> register takes it AS RECEIVED: one sheet, unread, from a hand it cannot name, under a protection
> entered blind; **the custody priced** — Kessa names the two ways this can go wrong (the machine may
> demand the paper; the machine may not know it exists) and enters both; **the promise** — the counter
> will read it ALOUD, at the window, at dawn, in the basin's hearing — because a counter that
> protects a paper by hiding it has become the thing it fought.
>
> **This page:** the gift that is also a custody — and the one chain-stop this chapter was given,
> spent on a doorstep rather than a blow.
>
> **Chapter 014 budget:** ONE chain-stop — **SPENT on this page, on the threshold.** Mother never on
> panel, never speaks — the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands
> remain unnamed. The grey clerk is never named, never faced — if this is the clerk, the page does not
> say so, and the register does not guess. The figure keeps the hood indoors; his face is never shown.
> Rekhak's paper stays folded, untouched. Nandi does not appear.

## PANEL 1 — Medium, dusk, the door (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall's door from inside, open a hand's width on the dusk-blue street — and in
that gap, the snow-heavy figure standing still, hood up, shoulders loaded with snow, one gloved hand
hanging at his side, not raised to knock. Behind him the dark street and the falling snow. Inside,
the queue has gone quiet in the way a room goes quiet; a woman half-rises from the bench and stops.

**Image:** The hand that has been protecting the basin from a distance, arriving at the one door it ever uses.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He had never come past the sill. Two winters of knots and one sheet of paper on a counter, and now
> the door. He did not knock. I think a man who has spent the winter not being seen does not know
> which of us the knocking would be for.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the decision (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the counter's edge — the register open at the protection of record, its name-space
visibly empty, the wax seal and four knots in place; and Ira's hand leaving the page to take the door's
bar. Kessa's grey glove is on the counter beside it and does not close over Ira's wrist. Behind them,
out of focus, the queue's faces turned to the door.

**Image:** Whether the door opens is whether the counter meant what it wrote.

> **THE READER:**
> *(quiet, to the room, not to the door)*
> The paper says *whoever keeps it.* It does not say *whoever we like.*

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, lifting the bar)*
> Entered before he knocked: the protection covers the man at the door, or it is a sentence we wrote
> about ourselves and hung in the window. Let him in.

> **KESSA:**
> *(flat, and she moves her glove away from Ira's wrist)*
> Then let the square watch what we do with a hand we cannot name. It will be asked of us in spring.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium-close, the threshold — CHAIN-STOP (~16%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the doorway, lamp-warm on the inside and dusk-blue on the other side — the
figure one step in, snow melting from the shoulders and dripping onto the boards, the hood still up,
the head still a shape with no face inside it, the threshold's plank under his boot. The counter's
lamp throws his shadow back out into the street behind him. The queue has made a lane. Nobody speaks.

**Image:** The uncounted hour, entering a public room as a man — hooded, silent, and admitted.

> **CHAIN-STOP (Chapter 014's ONE — spent here, on the threshold):**
> Not a blow and not a bargain: a man the machine will not own walking into the second Office's
> lamplight out of the dark, on a paper that a counter wrote blind, because the counter said
> *whoever.* The register enters him as it enters anyone: present.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the paper laid down (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the counter's scarred wood; the figure's gloved hand setting down one folded sheet
— machine paper, blank face, the fold worn soft at its edges as though carried a long way under a
coat — and withdrawing. Only the hand and the sleeve in frame, the plain heavy cuff; the hood's shadow
falling across the paper's near edge. Behind, one pace back, the hem of his coat and his boots on the
boards.

**Image:** The thing the hold was for, laid on the counter by the hand that tied it.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, very low)*
> Entered: one sheet, folded, placed on the counter by the hand that placed it. Entered: not opened.
> It is not ours to open on the counter's decision; it is ours to read in the basin's hearing.

> **KESSA:**
> *(barely a whisper, for Ira only)*
> He has carried that all winter. Look at the fold.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the entry as received (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the register and the folded sheet side by side in lamplight — the sheet's worn fold
visible, a small machine knot at the paper's head; the register's fresh entry wet, Ira's pen still
touching the page; her other hand flat on the counter in front of the paper, not on it. The paper's
whole surface is blank; nothing is written on it that the panel shows.

**Image:** An entry that protects a thing by naming only its arrival.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, writing)*
> Entered, as received: one folded sheet from a hand the register cannot name, laid down in silence
> at dusk, under protection of record entered blind. Not read. Not opened. Held.

> **IRA:**
> *(and then, to the room)*
> It will be opened at dawn, at the window, and read aloud, and the whole square will hear it at the
> same time I do. That is the only way a counter can hold a paper like this and stay a counter.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the custody priced (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: the counter's long view — the folded sheet under the lamp, the register beside it,
the queue seated again along the wall with their coats on, the figure standing where he stepped back,
hood up, one pace from the counter, absolutely still. Kessa at the till with the slate, chalking, and
the oil-cord's three knots beside her hand.

**Image:** A gift that is also a custody, priced in two lines by the only person who prices things here.

> **KESSA:**
> *(chalking the night's count, not looking up)*
> Two ways this goes wrong, and I will enter both before either happens. One: the machine learns we
> hold it, and demands it back — lawfully, with a seal, and a case we would lose. Two: the machine
> never learns we hold it, because nobody up there knows it exists — and then the hand that carried it
> is alone with what he did.

> **KESSA:**
> *(setting the chalk down)*
> Both are in the till's book now. Do not ask me which is worse. The book says both.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the promise — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: deep night, the stall — the folded sheet lying closed under the trimmed lamp,
the register shut beside it, a cord laid in a ring around the paper and not tied; the queue asleep
along the benches in their coats; the figure gone from the frame, the door shut, snow at the slats. Ira
sitting with her hands in her lap, looking at the paper. Three knots on the oil-cord. Nobody touching
anything.

**Image:** The counter's answer to a custody: it holds the paper closed for one night, and gives the reading to the square at dawn.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, the night closed)*
> Entered: the sheet stays folded until dawn. Entered: one hand the register cannot name is answerable
> to nobody until it is read. Entered: we are the only room in this quarter that will know it first,
> and the whole quarter will know it at the same hour.

> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Read it at dawn.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the door (a protection coming due); the threshold (the chapter's ONE
  chain-stop, spent on a crossing); the promise (public reading as the only honest custody).** The
  page must feel like a legal event that happens to be made of snow and lamplight: nobody fights,
  nobody is unmasked, and nothing is opened.
- **The threshold is the chain-stop and must be written plainly.** Ch. 013 spent its ONE on the
  clerk crossing as a man; Chapter 014 spends its ONE on a hooded man crossing the same kind of
  line in the other direction — the Office's *disowned* hour entering the counter's public room
  under the counter's own blind protection. Put it in one gated line in Panel 3 and let the panel's
  silence do the rest: the melting snow, the lane the queue makes, the lamp behind him.
- **The face never comes out of the hood, even indoors.** The panel list must keep the head a shape:
  hood's shadow in P4, still figure in P6, empty door in P7. No reveal, no eye-light, no profile. If
  he is the clerk, the page does not know it and the register does not guess — the reader may.
- **The fold is the page's only evidence of the winter.** Kessa is the one who sees it and says the
  least: *he has carried that all winter. Look at the fold.* The art must show a paper worn soft and
  furred at the crease by months under a coat, not a fresh document.
- **The custody is priced by Kessa, in two lines, and both are entered.** Do not resolve which danger
  is worse. The till's book says both, and the page's final entry should not prefer one. This is the
  chapter's method: name the cost, then act anyway, in public.
- **The promise is the page's turn and the next page's engine.** The paper is not opened on the
  counter's authority; it is opened at dawn, at the window, read aloud, everyone at once. The cord
  laid in a ring around it and left untied is the art's whole statement of custody without secrecy.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Read it at dawn.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it / enter the thin / read
  the tag / let him / note the absence / still folded / answer in kind / whoever keeps it / read it at
  dawn.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 014: ONE — **SPENT (this page, the threshold).** Mother
  never on panel, never speaks — no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel and
  unnamed. The grey clerk: never named, never faced; if this is he, the page does not say. The
  snow-heavy figure: hood kept indoors, no words, no face, no name — present in P1–P6, gone in P7.
  Rekhak's paper: folded and untouched in the register's pocket; not mentioned. The runner: not on the
  page. Nandi: not on the page. The counting-lesson women: two of them along the wall, the reader
  speaking the one line about the paper. The oil-cord: THREE knots. The window: shut for the first
  time in the chapter — the night is the door's, not the window's; it opens again at dawn. The till:
  open for chalk only; Kessa's slate has the night's count. The box: the register, shut at the close.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The sheet: blank, one small
  machine knot at its head, fold worn, never lettered. The register's entry: the existing book. The
  cord in a ring: plain cord, untied. The slate: chalk tallies. Lamp, dusk-blue and snow-light only;
  no daylight this page, because the reading is tomorrow.
- **Numbers:** one door; one decision; one threshold; ONE chain-stop — SPENT; one folded sheet; one
  pace; one cord left untied; two dangers entered; zero words from the figure; one reading scheduled
  for dawn; one night closed in silence.
