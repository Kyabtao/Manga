# Chapter 014 — Locations

Places used in this chapter. Basin locations from earlier chapters are defined in
`chapter-013/other/locations.md` and earlier.

## THE STALL IN SNOW

The Knot & Nail through its first winter: the window open in snowfall, papers kept dry inside coats,
hot cups set into the frame for the first in line, the oil-cord's three knots hanging by the till.
The counter that owes the basin an honest account of the machine — including the days the machine's
paper does not come.

## THE ARCHIVE WALL, MIDWINTER

The school's oldest rack at the year's turning: the lowest rod silver with age, the dense year-knot
at its head, Nandi's brass lens and the keeper's lamp on it together at midnight. Thirty-nine years
of the old school, counted in a grammar the Office's nineteen-year roll cannot read.

## NANDI'S BOOK

Small, thick, older than any Office roll: the reader's own count in the old grammar, no lettering —
where debtor lines live when the price of a reading is a line in somebody's count. From Page 002 it
holds the keeper's line: *Ira Sutar — took one reading, paid one line.* The second Office's founder,
a debtor in a record older than the machine.

## THE HATCH BOARD IN WINTER

The Office's public notice-board under snow, and the basin's morning weather-report of record:
whatever is posted here is fetched across the square and read aloud at the counter before the
machine's own hands arrive. From Page 003 it carries the season's confession — the permit-ledger
discontinued, the light folded into the fuel, *for the season* — and the basin moves on it together,
before anyone official arrives.

## THE STALL WINDOW, KEPT OPEN

The stall's open shutter as a stated policy from Page 004: same hours, same terms, same readings, all
winter, said from the sill so the whole queue hears it. After this page the open window is not
hospitality and not stubbornness — it is the counter's answer to a hold, kept open on purpose where an
unknown protector can watch the basin be careful with his gift.

## THE HATCH BOARD AT DUSK

The Office's public notice-board seen at the other end of the day: blue snow-light, the notice under a
crusted ridge of snow, the board's cords stiff with cold. From Page 004 it is also where the protector
works — a snow-heavy figure, no face, reading the season notice back by touch and re-tying two knots
on one public cord in machine grammar, leaving a glove-mark in the snow. The basin watches from the
stall's far window and does not move.

## THE SCHOOL'S SIDE ROOM, WINTER CLASS

The archive room with its table pushed to the wall and benches brought in: coats kept on, a short
practice cord in every lap, the brass lens set down unused because the lesson is about finish and not
thread. From Page 005 it is the basin's first schoolroom for machine grammar — nine women, one
teacher, the till at the door-frame taking a line a head. Cold, crowded, lit by one lamp and the
window: an institution, not a lecture hall.

## THE RECEIVED TRAY

The shallow tray at the stall's counter where delivered papers go face-down when they are entered but
not signed. From Page 005 it holds the Office's courtesy form: the first machine document the counter
has accepted without a line of its own on it. The tray is the physical shape of the register's third
grammar — *entered as received* — and it sits in the queue's sight, on purpose.
