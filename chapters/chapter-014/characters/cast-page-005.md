## IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper who answers a kindness with a grammar)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Not a grant, not a refuse — entered as received."
```
> **Page 005 note:** enters the lesson's meaning in one line (a grammar taught outside the Office is
> the first grammar the Office does not own), reads the courtesy's whole text aloud because the
> counter's law says everything delivered is read, refuses to sign — and answers in the same
> grammar: if the Office may know the basin's hours, the basin may read the Office's. She does not
> pretend the kindness is not one; she prices it.

## KESSA (the till — the school's price and the knife's mildness)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"One line each. Nothing taught here was ever free."
```
> **Page 005 note:** charges the lesson line by line on the school's book, because a free school is a
> captured school, and names the trap from the till without raising her voice: *open by our word and
> shut by theirs. Kindly.* The till's arithmetic is the page's second grammar lesson.

## NANDI (the reader — the teacher, refusing the step past the cord)
Full sheet (Ch. 006): [`../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md`](../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png`](../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png)

```
Nandi / Manav / Agnikhand / sight-white, lens-borne / reads, and is paid /
Read: thread-years at the price of an ask, properly made / "A break is a fact. A hold is a decision."
```
> **Page 005 note:** opens the school's table to the basin for the winter's first lesson and teaches
> exactly one thing: hold versus break, read from the finish, not a knot further. The same refusal as
> Page 003, turned outward — what she will not read for a fee, she will teach for a line, because a
> grammar the basin can read cannot be used against it silently.

## THE RUNNER (fifth appearance — a junior clerk, carrying a courtesy)
No full sheet; Ch. 004, 005, 006, 013, 014. Now in plain grey with the Office's small collar —
promoted out of errand-work into a clerkship, which is exactly why the Office gave him this to
carry: the basin reads him and does not fear him.

```
The Runner / Manav / Agnikhand / plain grey, uncommitted / paid by the road /
Carry: what a seal decides — and now, what a seal leaves out /
"They will not like that."
```
> **Page 005 note:** sets the form down with both hands and steps back a pace, as taught — then
> volunteers the second page at real cost to himself: *the Office may amend the hours by notice.*
> Face never enters frame all page; his hands are too careful with the paper. The register protects
> the fold, so the register does not enter his decency — but the page does, in the queue's hearing.

## THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (nine at the benches, two at the window)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–014. The readers of record become students, then witnesses of an absence —
the institution learning the grammar it has been reading aloud for a season.

```
The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Read: the machine's paper aloud — and now, the machine's knots /
"...only one of them has a person in it."
```
> **Page 005 note:** nine at the benches, practice cords in their laps, one failing a knot and
> laughing at herself without a sound; the elder corrects a grip. At dusk two of them stand at the
> stall's far window where they stood yesterday, looking at nobody. They are how the page measures
> the winter: first readers, now literate, now waiting.

## THE MACHINE (off-panel — the courtesy)
No full sheet; the Office entire. Speaks this page through a blank form, a seal, and a second page
that permits amendment.

```
The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Regularize: write the basin's openness into its own schedule, amendable by notice /
"It is a courtesy. It has a date."
```
> **Page 005 note:** answers Page 004's open window not with a hold but with paperwork — the
> machine's most polite move and its most invasive: to make the basin's openness a line in the
> Office's schedule, at the Office's ordinary discretion. The page shows the mechanism, not just the
> menace, and the counter's reply is symmetry rather than refusal.
