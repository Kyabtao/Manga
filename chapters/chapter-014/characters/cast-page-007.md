## IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper who answers in kind)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "It says the only word the counter has: read."
```
> **Page 007 note:** keeps the watch an hour of the window, refuses to let anyone follow the figure,
> and at dawn ties one knot of the basin's own and sets it where his lay. The register's entry is
> careful to name the difficulty (*it was harder than it looks on paper*) and the consequence: a
> register that answers has begun something it cannot end.

## KESSA (the till — the night's ledger and the barred door)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"Tonight is the night that costs. Shut the door and take your watch."
```
> **Page 007 note:** chalks the night's oil at the till — three watches, one measure — because a
> policy with no ledger is a mood, and bars the door with her arm when the queue wants to follow the
> man at the sill. She names the asymmetry plainly and pays it: the counter said it would leave him
> be, and this is the night that promise is expensive.

## THE READER AT THE WINDOW (the counting-lesson women — the law kept with no audience)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–014. The school's reader, alone at the counter through the night, reading the
Office's own posted hours aloud to an empty street.

```
The Reader at the Window / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owes lessons, and is owed them /
Read: whatever arrives, aloud, at whatever hour it arrives /
"Not yet. Keep reading. Do not follow the hour."
```
> **Page 007 note:** reads the Office's hours twice an hour to nobody, then reads the sill's knot in
> the grammar the school taught her on Page 005 — three clauses, no inference, the finish checked
> before the meaning. The lesson paid for itself one page later, in the dark, without applause.

## THE SNOW-HEAVY FIGURE (never faced, never named, never followed — the protector)
No sheet by rule: the page may not identify him. Silhouette only: heavy coat, snow loaded on the
shoulders and hood, head a dark shape with no face in it, one gloved hand.

```
The Snow-Heavy Figure / Manav (presumed) / Agnikhand / none given — snow takes the face / uncollected /
Hold: pause a count from inside — and ask, in the other grammar, for what he needs /
*(one knot on a sill: not yet)*
```
> **Page 007 note:** comes to the SILL rather than the board — in reach, closer than he has ever
> been — sets one knot down, steps back, and does not speak, look up, or enter. Gone by dawn. The
> knot asks for three things and no more: patience, the reading, and that nobody follow the uncounted
> hour. He is the reason the page's hardest order is *do not follow*.

## A YOUNG DOCK-HAND (the queue's voice — right, and overruled)
No sheet; one-off on this page, of the basin's young watch-keepers. Present as a hot, specific,
entirely reasonable objection.

```
A Young Dock-Hand / Manav / Agnikhand / scowling, unbrushed / owed nothing, and counting anyway /
Demand: the obvious thing, at the obvious moment /
"He was at the sill. He was there — an arm's length."
```
> **Page 007 note:** says what half the square is thinking, correctly, and is answered by Kessa with
> the arithmetic of the promise. He is not a fool and the page must not draw him as one: the counter
> refuses him because it said it would, not because he is wrong.

## THE QUEUE (the watch's second and third watches)
No sheet; the square entire. Two women on a bench under one blanket, a third asleep sitting up, the
young ones at the glass when the figure comes — the institution's night made of ordinary bodies.

```
The Queue / mixed kin / Agnikhand / as the day finds them / owed, and keeping count /
Watch: the counter's hours, in turns, for nothing /
*(they will remember that nobody followed)*
```
> **Page 007 note:** the page's texture and its proof: the night watch is staffed by people who owe
> the counter nothing and are paid nothing, which is the difference between an institution and a
> guard. No individual lines beyond the dock-hand's.
