## IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — the keeper who lays the pen down)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "The counter did buy a school. Entered, before anything else: yes."
```
> **Page 001 note:** opens the spring by admitting the accusation before answering it — a keeper who
> concedes the fact and then puts the till's book on the sill — and closes the page by entering the
> Office's charter without a guess. Her hand, not her face, is the page's answer: the pen laid down
> flat, then the book carried out.

## KESSA (the till — the column closed, the purse read in the street)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"That is what the till is. If the quarter wants it to buy nothing, say so."
```
> **Page 001 note:** rules off the fuel column with one stroke, chalk worn to a stub, then stands in
> the street and reads her own till aloud to the people whose money it is — fuel given, oil spent,
> nine lines out, four paid back, five carried, no loans, and the counter's only takings a cord and
> two knots. She defends nothing; she prices everything and lets the street choose.

## THE CLAIMANT (owed seven measures — and asking a better question)
No full sheet; one-off on this page, of the basin's ordinary claimants. A thin man in a mended coat
with a grain-broker's tally-case under one arm, owed money he does not ask for here.

```
The Claimant / Manav / Agnikhand / mended and exact / owed seven measures, at the count's end /
Demand: the account of a purse that is not the keeper's own /
"The counter bought a school. What else does the counter buy, and who decided?"
```
> **Page 001 note:** the page's conscience and the chapter's first honest opponent: he concedes the
> counter's right to rule on debts and then asks the question the winter never answered — whose money
> pays for a school, and who gave the keeper leave. He is not a fool, not a plant, and not wrong;
> Kessa's line — *he is not wrong to ask it; he is only early* — is the register's own verdict on him.

## THE COUNTING-LESSON WOMEN (the term posted, the charter met with a lamp)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–015. Readers become clerks of the school: nailing up the spring term in the
school's grammar, reading it aloud to the crowd, then meeting the Office's charter at dusk with a
lamp.

```
The Counting-Lesson Women / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owe each other lessons /
Read: the machine's paper aloud — and now, their own term /
"Three lessons a week. One line a head, carried, not demanded."
```
> **Page 001 note:** they post the term where the Office's hours hung all winter, and read it back to
> the crowd for correction — the school's first act of spring is to make the quarter hold its count.
> At dusk the same women hold the lamp up to the charter and read it slowly, because reading it fast
> would flatter it.

## THE QUEUE (the quarter the till belongs to)
No sheet; the square entire, now in lighter coats and thaw-mud. The page's jury: they hear the
accusation from one of their own and the columns from the till.

```
The Queue / mixed kin / Agnikhand / as the season finds them / owed, and keeping count /
Judge: a purse read out loud, line by line, in the street /
*(they will decide what the till is for, not the keeper)*
```
> **Page 001 note:** the addressee of every line Kessa reads. Their presence in the accusatory
> middle and the listening end is the page's structure: the question and the answer are both theirs.

## THE MACHINE (off-panel — the charter)
No full sheet; the Office entire. Speaks this page through one sealed notice and a budget-cord at the
hatch board, and through no clerk at all.

```
The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Charter: receive the school, at the Office's cost, with a seal on every lesson /
"Every lesson then counted, and every count theirs."
```
> **Page 001 note:** does not deny that the counter bought a school — it offers to buy it properly.
> The charter is the new grammar the close of Chapter 014 predicted: a machine that can no longer
> disown an hour adopts the institution that embarrassed it. No clerk carries it; it simply appears
> on the board at dusk, which is worse.
