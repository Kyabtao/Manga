## NANDI (the reader — the school answers for itself)
Full sheet (Ch. 006): [`../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md`](../../chapter-006/characters/nandi.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png`](../../chapter-006/characters/nandi-ref.png)

```
Nandi / Manav / Agnikhand / sight-white, lens-borne / reads, and is paid /
Read: thread-years at the price of an ask, properly made /
"The Office may own the grammar. The count stays here."
```
> **Page 002 note:** answers the charter because the school is what is being bought — not the keeper,
> not the till. Her division is dry and practical: five clauses accepted (lessons, seals, money,
> licensed hours, Office grammar taught to the quarter's children at the Office's cost) and one
> refused (the school's book as a roll). Two hands, two books: one on the charter, one on her own.
> She states the collision in two sentences and does not try to solve it.

## KESSA (the till — the mechanism, named quietly)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"They give you a door and a bill for the hinges, and they make the door so good that saying no is the unnatural thing."
```
> **Page 002 note:** names the courtesy's mechanism under her breath and then, at the door, the
> symmetry of the outcome: the machine will call it a victory because it got a school; the basin will
> call it one because it kept a book; both are right, which is how a real ruling looks. No chalk this
> page — the columns are already closed.

## THE READER (the counting-lesson women — the charter read in full)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–015. The school's reader takes the charter off the board flat, seal up, and
reads all six clauses at the window, the empty one last and twice.

```
The Reader / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / owes lessons, and is owed them /
Read: everything delivered, aloud, at the counter of record /
"Six clauses. Five of them gifts. One of them a list of names with nothing on it yet."
```
> **Page 002 note:** reads the Office's best paper as flatly as she read the Office's worst, which is
> the counter's whole discipline in one performance. It is she who notices the document's shape before
> its words — five gifts and a list — and she says so before the crowd can be won by the gifts.

## THE WOMAN IN THE SHAWL (the quarter's honest yes)
No full sheet; one-off on this page. A mother in a patched shawl with a nine-year-old daughter who
already reads a tally, stepping forward before the reading is finished.

```
The Woman in the Shawl / Manav / Agnikhand / patched, practical / owes nothing, and wants one thing /
Ask: sealed lessons — the one gift that cannot be taken back for being ours /
"Enter her — she is nine and she can read a tally already."
```
> **Page 002 note:** the page's fairness test. She is right, her reasoning is sound, and the machine's
> offer is materially good for her child. The register does not overrule her and the art must not
> patronise her: the charter's generosity is real, and the page says so out loud before it names the
> price.

## THE MACHINE (off-panel — the charter)
No full sheet; the Office entire. Present this page as one document: six clauses, five gifts, one
seal, one budget-cord, and a schedule ruled for names.

```
The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Charter: buy the school properly — grammar, seals, hours, teachers, money — and take its roll /
"Five of them gifts. One of them a list of names."
```
> **Page 002 note:** the new grammar from Chapter 014's last page, in full flower: a machine that
> cannot disown an hour offers to own the institution instead, at real cost to itself, and asks only
> for the roll. The page never shows an Office interior and never explains the offer's motive; the
> terms do that work.
