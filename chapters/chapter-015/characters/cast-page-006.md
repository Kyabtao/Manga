## KESSA (the till — the coin spent, the column balanced)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"It makes it clear. Fair is a word for people who have not had to do the sums."
```
> **Page 006 note:** the page's executor. Spends the Office's coin in the open market, makes the
> chandler say the price twice, rules the receipt's verdict in her own hand (*spent on children not on
> your roll*), enters both wages against the two purses and draws the first chalk rule in nineteen years
> under a column that has the Office in it. Her last advice is unsentimental and right: take both wages,
> put one in your sleeve and one in your shoe, and teach eleven.

## THE LICENSED WOMAN (one of ours, now paid exactly twice)
No full sheet; Ch. 011–015. Art reference: [`../../chapter-015/images/page-004.png`](../../chapter-015/images/page-004.png) (fourth tier), [`../../chapter-015/images/page-005.png`](../../chapter-015/images/page-005.png) (fourth tier).

```
The Licensed Woman / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, learning / paid by a hand she will never see, and by a cracked bowl /
Teach: the Office's grammar sealed — and the two off the roll, in the same hour /
"I know which of these two I will be thinking about at the end of the term."
```
> **Page 006 note:** kneels to put the two new slates into two children's hands, then stands at the
> counter holding the machine's purse and the quarter's bowl in the same two hands and refuses to rank
> them aloud. She takes both wages as instructed and asks nothing of anybody; her line is the page's
> quiet centre and her face should not resolve which hand she prefers.

## IRA SUTAR (PROTAGONIST — of record, four entries)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "Fair is a question the book keeps asking and the Office keeps filing under other."
```
> **Page 006 note:** enters the purchase, the bowl, the two wage lines and the dawn notice — and asks
> the one question the page will not answer (*does that make it fair?*). In the final panel her pen stops
> mid-entry on the word *unlawfully*, which is the first time the register has flinched at its own
> vocabulary.

## THE GRANDMOTHER (the refusal that pays anyway)
No full sheet; second appearance (Ch. 015 P.005, P.006). Courteous, unimpressed by seals, one
granddaughter in the unsealed class.

```
The Grandmother / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, weathered / owes nothing, and will not sign /
Refuse the name, and pay for the child — the two are not the same coin /
"I am not paying for the seal. I am paying for the child. Understand the difference and we will get on."
```
> **Page 006 note:** the page's thesis in one sentence, delivered flat into a cracked bowl with her own
> hand. She watches a new slate go into her granddaughter's hands, asks what paid for it, and gives the
> Office's money the only compliment it will get this chapter: the first honest thing it has done here.

## THE FATHER (the second refusal, gruff and steady)
No full sheet; second appearance (Ch. 015 P.005, P.006). A poor man in wet wool whose son was refused
the roll.

```
The Father / Manav / Agnikhand / worn wool, set jaw / owes nothing, and pays anyway /
Refuse the roll, then pay the wage the Office will not pay /
"Somebody must pay the woman, and it is not going to be them. It will be us, in petties, from a bowl."
```
> **Page 006 note:** turns his refusal into a wage. No eloquence, no apology for the earlier coldness:
> he puts his coin in and states the arithmetic — two lessons a day for two children the Office will not
> count, and the payer is a bowl.

## THE TWO UNSEALED CHILDREN (the boy and the granddaughter)
No full sheets; Ch. 015. The two children the roll does not hold, now the two with new slates.

```
The Unsealed Two / Manav / Agnikhand / undyed, unwritten / owed a slate, and given one /
Sit: in the licensed class without a line on the licensed roll /
"The two they cannot count will be the two in the room."
```
> **Page 006 note:** the page's last look and its whole argument: two children on a step holding slates
> bought with the Office's own coin, beside nine children with older chipped ones, waiting for the second
> hour in a class that is about to be counted. Draw them looking at the slates and then at each other —
> no tears, no gratitude.

## THE MACHINE (off-panel — the audit)
No full sheet; the Office entire. Present this page as a small notice with a bright seal and a
sentence that ends in *unlawfully*.

```
The Machine / — / Agnikhand / nineteen years of white spaces / counts, and does not refuse /
Audit: verify the roll against the children present, and record what is not on it /
"Any child present who is not on the roll will be recorded as taught unlawfully."
```
> **Page 006 note:** never appears; no official, no cart, no soldier. It answers a clever boycott with a
> procedure, and the procedure is genuinely within its rights — which is the chapter's whole problem.
