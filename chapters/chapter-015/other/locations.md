# Chapter 015 — Locations

Places used in this chapter. Basin locations from earlier chapters are defined in
`chapter-014/other/locations.md` and earlier.

## THE SQUARE IN THAW

The same square the winter filled with snow, in meltwater: eaves dripping in a steady line, gutters
running black, a slab of snow sliding off the stall's awning and bursting on the flags, the snow grey
and full of footprints. From Page 001 the season's first setting and its quiet proof — the queue has
not shortened, the papers are the same, and the counter's window stands open to wet air.

## THE SILL AND THE STREET, READ FROM

The stall's window sill, used this page as a lectern: the till's book propped open on it while the
till's keeper stands in the street and reads her own columns aloud to the quarter whose money they
record. The same wood the protector's knots lay on, and the page does not linger there — the spring's
business is public arithmetic, not correspondence.

## THE HATCH BOARD, CHARTERED

The Office's public notice-board under its little roof, wet with thaw, where the school's spring term
hung in the morning and where the Office's sealed charter appears by dusk — machine knot-style, seal
at the foot, a budget-cord hanging from it, no clerk to answer questions. From Page 001 it is the
place the machine now speaks, because a board cannot be asked why.

## THE SCHOOL'S TABLE, CHARTERED

The archive table with the charter laid flat on it and the school's thick old book open beside it —
from Page 002 the room where the school answers for itself. Nandi stands at its head with one hand on
the Office's paper and one on her own book: the grammar accepted, the count refused. The chapter's
argument is made at this table in a single gesture, and the first schedule's blank line is where it
stops.

## THE WINDOW, NOON READING

The stall's serving window on a thaw noon, a reader standing on the sill's step with an Office charter
held up, the quarter packed close under a dripping awning. Same window that read the winter's counts;
on Page 002 it reads the machine's best paper at the same pace and with the same flatness, which is
what makes the sixth clause audible.

## THE STREET SCHOOL

The stall's step and the wet flags before it, turned into a schoolroom under the licence: benches
carried out crooked, a plank across two barrels for a table, nine women and eleven children with
cords and chalk-slates, the licence nailed to the wall beside them, and the awning dripping the only
music. From Page 003 it is the second Office's school running without a penny of Office money —
unpaid, lawful, and utterly public.

## THE STEP AT THE SECOND HOUR

The strip of stone outside the window where the term is held at the second hour after noon, because
the side room is the school's and the street is everybody's. From Page 003: where the mother enrols
her daughter without a fee and where Nandi teaches with her lens put away, so that no one can say the
lesson depended on a reader's sight.

## THE NINTH BENCH

The benches in the school's side room, counted inward from the wall: from Page 004 the one where a
licensed teacher sits among eight unlicensed ones, her sealed leaf on her knee and her grip on a
lesson-cord corrected out of pure habit by the woman next to her. The page's physical answer to the
Office's split — membership is a seat, not a seal.

## THE TWO-LESSON STEP

The stall's step at dusk, used for two lessons at once from Page 004: the Office's sealed grammar
taught under a nailed leaf at one end, the old grammar taught unsealed two paces away, the same
eleven children drifting between them. The school's book is visible through the window behind the
whole arrangement, open, which is the point.

## THE COIN'S END OF THE COUNTER

The scarred stretch of the counter of record where the first Office coin was set down in Page 005 and
still sits — out of the drawer, untouched by any hand, ruled *not ours* on the slate beside it. From
that day it is the till's coldest piece of furniture: a coin that has to be answered before it can be
spent.

## THE ROLL TABLE

The school's long night table under the lamp, where the roll-slate was filled and then copied into the
register in Page 005 — two sheets side by side, the machine's eleven lines and the counter's eleven, and
beside them the old book open at the page with its name-space still empty.

## THE CHANDLER'S STALL

A wet-market stall in the first hour of the day where the Office's first coin in the basin was spent in
Page 006: two slates, a twist of chalk and a receipt, with the price said out loud twice at Kessa's
insistence. The stallholder is now the quarter's witness that the money was spent in the open.

## THE BOWL'S PLACE

The middle of the school's scarred table, where a cracked clay bowl mended with wire sits from Page 006
with a handful of petties in it — the quarter's wage for the two children off the roll — between an
unlit lamp and the till's slate. Anyone may look into it. Nobody counts it aloud.
