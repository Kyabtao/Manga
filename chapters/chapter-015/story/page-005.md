# THREADBORN — Chapter 15 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Spring Term*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the step, the counter of record, and the night table
**Page type:** The first coin and the first roll — 7 panels; the Office's money arrives without a debt against it, and asks for eleven children by name

> Beats: **the first coin** — an Office errand-boy in grey, hooded, no face shown, sets a stamped purse
> and a receipt slip on the step at the end of the licensed lesson: the first Office money paid into
> the basin since the terms, and it is paid to a woman the school did not expel; **the danger read
> cold** — Kessa enters it at the till as *not ours* and will not put it in the drawer, because coin
> that arrives with no debt against it is the most dangerous money there is: it does not buy a lesson,
> it buys a habit; **the condition of the seal** — a sealed lesson cannot be withdrawn, but a seal
> requires a roll, and a roll requires names, eleven children in an Office hand; **the parents answer
> two ways** — the woman in the shawl writes her daughter's name because it is the first promise she
> has ever been able to make that cannot be taken back, and two families step back and refuse, and are
> right too; **the counter's answer** — the licensed woman teaches all eleven anyway, nine sealed and
> two unsealed, and Ira copies the roll into the register so the quarter keeps its own copy of what
> the machine now knows; **the page's last look** — the school's book, its old name-space still empty:
> the Office has eleven children's names and still not one man's.
>
> **This page:** the Office's first money as a quiet infection, and a counter that keeps a copy of
> everything the machine learns.
>
> **Chapter 015 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread
> quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is
> never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear; the sill's knots

## PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the purse on the step (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: the step in thin morning light at the end of the licensed lesson — children still
seated with slates, the nailed leaf on the wall, and at the step's edge an Office errand-boy in a plain
grey hood, his face not shown, crouched to set down a small stamped purse and a narrow receipt slip;
the licensed woman standing over him with her lesson-cord; two paces away the unlicensed women's groups
have stopped teaching to watch.

**Image:** The Office pays, in the open, into a school that has already refused it once.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The Office did not send a clerk to argue with us. It sent a boy with a purse, at the end of a lesson,
> in front of the children. That is how a machine says a thing it will not put in a letter.

> **THE LICENSED WOMAN:**
> *(quiet, to the boy)*
> For the lesson? Only the lesson?

> **THE ERRAND-BOY:**
> *(not looking up, hood down over his face)*
> For the lesson. There is a slip with it. The slip has to come back.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, one coin on old wood (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the school's table — the stamped purse opened, one bright coin standing on the scarred
wood, a narrow receipt slip beside it carrying knot-rows and a small dry seal, and Kessa's chalked slate
laid flat next to both with the school's column on it. Nobody's hand is on the coin.

**Image:** Office money on the school's own table, with a receipt and no debt.

> **KESSA:**
> *(flat, of the coin)*
> One. One coin for one lesson, and a receipt that says *paid, no claim*. In nineteen years of this
> counter I have never seen the Office pay a thing without writing down what it was owed for.

> **IRA:**
> *(carefully)*
> So it is a gift?

> **KESSA:**
> *(without looking up)*
> It is worse than a gift. A gift is a thing you can hand back.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the till that will not open (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the counter of record, late morning — Kessa behind the till with the drawer open and
empty-looking, the one coin still on the counter's wood and not in the drawer, her own hand ruling a
fresh line on the slate: *not ours*; Ira standing on the customer side with the register; over Kessa's
shoulder the school's window shows the step and the children.

**Image:** The till refuses the coin by accounting, not by argument.

> **KESSA:**
> *(ruling the line, hard)*
> Entered: one coin, paid by the Office, no claim attached. Not entered against any debt, because
> there is no debt. Not entered as ours, because it is not. It sits on this counter until the school
> says what it is.

> **IRA:**
> And what will you tell them it is?

> **KESSA:**
> *(dry)*
> That the first coin a machine pays without a debt against it is the most dangerous money in the
> quarter. It does not buy a lesson. It buys a habit — and the habit is that the Office may set money
> on our step and be thanked for it.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the condition (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the step, the purse's receipt in the licensed woman's hand and the roll-slate
she has been given with it — a narrow board ruled with eleven short lines, no names yet — while the
woman in the shawl stands before her with her daughter's hand in hers. Behind them the children are
still on the benches. The nailed leaf and its fine clause are visible at the frame's edge.

**Image:** A seal that cannot be withdrawn, priced in children's names.

> **THE LICENSED WOMAN:**
> *(to the parents, plainly)*
> The seal is not a wage. The seal is the promise — a sealed lesson cannot be withdrawn, not by the
> Office, not by the clerk, not by a hand writing a new clause at night. But the seal wants the roll.
> Eleven lines. Eleven names, in my hand, going to theirs.

> **A FATHER AT THE BACK:**
> *(cold)*
> Then you are their clerk with our children's names in your pocket.

> **THE LICENSED WOMAN:**
> *(not flinching)*
> I am their clerk with your children's names in my hand, and I am standing on this step in the rain
> teaching them for a coin. Those are both true. You may weigh them.

## PANEL 5 — Close, the first name (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the roll-slate on the licensed woman's knee, her hand guiding a small girl's fingers
to lay the first name-line in the ruled column; the girl's mother, the woman in the shawl, crouched at
her daughter's shoulder with her own hand flat on the slate's edge to steady it; the girl's face lit
with concentration, tongue between her teeth.

**Image:** The first Office name in the basin written by the child who owns it, with her mother holding the board.

> **THE WOMAN IN THE SHAWL:**
> *(steady, close to her daughter's ear)*
> Write it. Write it properly, the way she shows you.
>
> *(to the licensed woman, not looking up)*

> I have promised this child a great many things I could not keep. Food in a bad winter. A roof that
> stayed. A brother who came back. This is the first thing I can promise her that a clerk cannot take
> off her in the night.

> **THE LICENSED WOMAN:**
> *(quietly)*
> Nine more lines.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the two families who say no (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the step, the same hour — nine of the eleven lines filled, and at the step's edge two
families gathered and stepping back: a father with his hand on his son's shoulder, a grandmother drawing
a girl behind her skirts, their faces set and courteous. The licensed woman has lowered the board. Nandi
stands at the window's edge with her lens in her hand, watching, saying nothing.

**Image:** A refusal that is also right, honoured on the same step as the acceptance.

> **THE GRANDMOTHER:**
> *(flat, courteous)*
> My granddaughter learns here, and I pay nothing, and I owe nothing. Put her name in their hand and
> she is theirs for good, and I have seen what *for good* means in that grammar.

> **NANDI:**
> *(from the window's edge, quietly, to the licensed woman)*
> Then teach them unsealed. Nine sealed and two not, in the same hour, on the same step. The seal is
> yours. The class is not.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, night, eleven lines in the register — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the night table under the lamp — the register open, Ira's hand laying the roll's
eleven lines into it in her own hand (nine matched to the Office's roll, two marked with a bare gap and
a small private notch), and beside the register the school's thick old book, open at the page with its
deliberately empty name-space, the four-knot answer-cord still tied through the licence. The window
behind is dark; the sill's two knots are untouched.

**Image:** The counter's copy of what the machine now knows — and one line on the same page that nothing has filled.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, writing)*
> Entered: eleven children in the licensed class. Nine sealed and named to the Office. Two unsealed and
> unnamed, and entered here all the same, because this book counts the class and not the roll.

> *(she sets the pen down, and looks at the old page beside it)*

> Entered: the Office now has eleven names out of this quarter, written in a child's own hand. We still
> do not have one name of our own — and it sits on this page, in this hand, empty, and I have been
> keeping it empty for two seasons.

> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Enter the eleven.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the coin (paid in public, no debt against it — read as arithmetic and as
  danger, never as bribery); the roll (a seal that cannot be withdrawn, priced in names, answered two
  ways by parents who are both right); the counter's copy (eleven lines in the register, nine the
  Office has and two it does not).** The page's subject is not the Office's wickedness. It is what
  money and names do to a school that has decided to keep a book.
- **The coin must be inert in the art.** It stands on the table with nobody's hand on it through two
  panels. Do not put a shine on it beyond the ordinary. Kessa's refusal is a ruled line on a slate, not
  a speech from behind a raised hand — she never touches it either.
- **The errand-boy: hooded, faceless, unnamed, and not the grey clerk.** He is a boy doing an errand in
  bad weather for a wage, and the page should not be cruel to him. He gets one line and does not look
  up. The Office's face stays off the page; the quarter's new problem is that it does not need a face
  any more.
- **The two refusals must be honoured.** The grandmother is courteous and correct; her objection is
  the chapter's own objection (*for good* in that grammar). Nandi's answer is not persuasion — nine
  sealed and two not, in the same hour — and the class survives the split, which is the school's rule
  applied a second time in three pages, at a smaller scale.
- **The woman in the shawl's speech is the page's heart and must stay plain.** No rhetoric about the
  Office; three things she could not keep (food, a roof, a brother) and one she can (a sealed lesson).
  The brother is not a plot seed unless a later page wants it; keep him unnamed and unremarked.
- **The final panel is the register, not the coin.** The last thing the reader sees is the counter's own
  copy — eleven lines, two of them with a bare gap — and beside it the old page whose name-space has
  been empty for two seasons. Do not fill it, do not explain it, do not let anyone look at it.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Enter the eleven.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it / enter the thin / read
  the tag / let him / note the absence / still folded / answer in kind / whoever keeps it / read it at
  dawn / give it back read / choose the owner / write the name / teach it anyway / count her too /
  enter the eleven.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 015: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Loom
  absent. The three entered hands off-panel and unnamed. The grey clerk: not on the page. The snow-heavy
  figure: absent; the sill's knots untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper: folded, unopened,
  unmentioned. Nandi: window's edge, one line. Kessa: counter, three lines, the slate. The licensed
  woman: the page's spine, the roll in her hand. The reader: not needed; keep her off the page. The oil
  cord: THREE knots, off-panel. The window: open by day, dark at night. Lesson cords: short, undyed.
  No numbering anywhere but knot-rows, tally groups, ruled columns and name-marks.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage, no lettering. No coin design
  beyond a plain stamped disc. The roll-slate: a narrow ruled board with short tally lines and
  name-marks only. The register and the old book: knot-marks, ruled columns, wax, blank space.
- **Numbers:** one errand-boy; one purse; one receipt; one coin; zero debt; zero drawer; one column;
  eleven lines; nine sealed names; two refusals; two families; eleven children taught; two seasons of
  an empty name-space; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
