# THREADBORN — Chapter 15 — Page 001

**Chapter title:** *The Spring Term*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the thawing square, the stall, and the hatch board at dusk
**Page type:** Chapter opening — 7 panels; the thaw, the purse read aloud, and the first public charge that the counter owns what it pays for

> Beats: **the thaw** — the square dripping, snow sliding off the awnings in slabs, the queue still
> there in lighter coats and the same paper; the counter's first winter survived, which is a fact
> with gutters in it; **the column closed** — Kessa shuts the fuel column in the till's book with one
> stroke and opens the school's, and the winter's cost becomes a line the quarter can read; **the
> spring term posted** — the school puts up its term at the hatch board: three lessons a week, one
> line a head, open to the quarter, in knot-work, in public; **the accusation** — a basin claimant,
> owed money and owed nothing else, says it to the keeper's face in front of the queue: *the counter
> bought a school. What else does the counter buy?* — and the page does not make him a fool; **the
> answer chosen** — no denial: the counter answers an accusation about money with the ledger, read
> aloud, line by line, from the window, by the till itself; **the reading of the purse** — fuel, oil,
> school lines, nine lines paid, one measure a night, every entry in the till's book read to the
> square by the woman who wrote it; **the machine's new grammar** — at dusk a sealed charter notice
> goes up at the hatch board: the Office offers to CHARTER the school, to own it properly, with a
> budget line and a seal — the machine's answer to being read back at is to adopt what it cannot
> accuse.
>
> **This page:** a chapter that begins in mud instead of snow, and the first move of a machine that
> has learned its dodge is spent.
>
> **Chapter 015 budget:** ONE chain-stop, **fresh and unspent.** Mother never on panel, never speaks —
> the thread quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The
> grey clerk is never named, never faced, not on the page. The sill's two knots are untouched and
> unmentioned. Rekhak's paper stays folded, unopened, unmentioned.

## PANEL 1 — Wide, morning, the thaw (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: the square in thaw — eaves dripping in a steady line, a slab of snow sliding off the
stall's awning and bursting on the flags, gutters running black, the snow gone grey and full of
footprints. The queue is still a queue: shorter coats, the same papers held dry. The window is open
to wet air. Somewhere out of frame, water is falling into water with a noise the whole street has
forgotten over the winter.

**Image:** The first winter survived, which is a fact with gutters in it.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nobody celebrates a thaw. The square woke to water running in the gutters and stood in it the way
> it stood in snow, with paper in its hands, and by the second bell the line was the same length it
> had been in the worst week of the year. That is what an institution looks like from the inside: not
> a crowd that came, a queue that kept coming.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the column closed (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the till's book open on the counter, the fuel column ruled down its length in
chalk with a final stroke drawn across its foot, and beside it a fresh column headed only by a knot;
Kessa's grey-gloved hand lifting from the last stroke; the oil-cord's THREE knots hanging into frame.
A stub of chalk, worn to nothing, set down like a full stop.

**Image:** The winter's cost, closed into a line the quarter can read.

> **KESSA:**
> *(flat, to the book rather than to Ira)*
> Fuel, closed. Four hundred and one measures, one a night, and the Office's own count proving where
> the last of them went. Oil, closed. School, open — nine lines in, four paid back, five carried.
>
> *(she sets the chalk down)*
>
> I have kept a till in this square for eleven years and never once been able to show the street a
> column it would understand. This is the first one.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record)*
> Entered: the winter's columns closed at the till. Entered: the first column in the second Office's
> history that is not a debt somebody owes us.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the spring term posted (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the hatch board in the wet morning, snow sliding off its roof, and one of the
counting-lesson women nailing up a short cord of knot-groups — the term, in the school's grammar —
while two others hold a second copy flat against the board and read it aloud to the people already
stopping. No clerk. No seal. The basin reading the school's hours before the Office has thought to
object.

**Image:** A school that posts its term in the open air, in the grammar of the street that pays for it.

> **THE READER:**
> *(aloud, to the knot at the board)*
> Spring term. Three lessons a week, the second hour after noon. One line a head, carried, not
> demanded. Open to the quarter. Read it back to me if I have it wrong.

> **A WOMAN IN THE CROWD:**
> *(promptly, and badly)*
> Three lessons — and the line is *carried*, not owing. I have it.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, from the window)*
> Entered: the term posted where the Office's hours were posted, in the grammar the Office taught us
> to read. The school does not ask leave to teach. It asks the quarter to hold the count.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the accusation (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the counter at midday — a basin claimant, a thin man in a mended coat with a
grain-broker's tally-case under one arm, standing square to the window with the queue behind him,
saying it out loud and not shouting it: his face tired and exact, a man who has thought about it. Ira
behind the counter with the register open; Kessa at the till, chalk still in her hand; the queue
listening in the way the basin listens.

**Image:** The promise made on the last page of the winter, kept to the counter's face in spring.

> **THE CLAIMANT:**
> *(plain, to Ira, with the street behind him)*
> I am owed seven measures and I will have them at the count's end, and that is not my business
> here. My business is this. Nine lines went out of that till to buy lessons. The till is not yours
> — it is the counter's, and the counter's money is ours, because we are the ones who bring paper to
> it. So: the counter bought a school. What else does the counter buy, and who decided?

> **KESSA:**
> *(from the till, mild as a knife)*
> He is not wrong to ask it. He is only early.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the answer chosen (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's hands on the register — the pen laid down, deliberately, and her hand turned
flat on the page instead; beside it the till's book, closed, with the till's key still in its lock.
Her face is not in frame; only the hands and the two books, and beyond them the claimant's
tally-case set on the counter, the one thing he brought.

**Image:** An accusation about money, answered not with a sentence but with a book.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, laying the pen down)*
> The counter did buy a school. Entered, before anything else: yes. Nine lines, and the risk entered
> beside them on the day it was paid.
>
> *(and then, to the room)*
>
> You want to know what else the till buys and who decides. That is not a question a keeper answers
> from behind a window. Bring the book out.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the purse read aloud (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall's window with the till's book propped open on the sill, Kessa standing
in the street in her coat reading her own columns aloud to the queue — chalk-strokes turned into
words, one entry at a time — while the claimant stands at the front with his tally-case, listening
with his jaw set. Ira beside the window, entering as it is read. Behind them the dripping eaves and
the grey snow.

**Image:** The till's arithmetic, read by the woman who wrote it, to the people whose money it is.

> **KESSA:**
> *(reading, flat, unhurried)*
> Winter, fuel: four hundred and one measures, one a night, none of it bought — given, and given by a
> hand the Office denies. Oil, the night watch: sixty measures. School: nine lines out, four paid
> back, five carried. Loans to the counter: none. Gifts to the counter: one cord, two knots, and a
> page with no name on it.
>
> *(she turns the book so the street can see the columns)*
>
> That is what the till is. If the quarter wants it to buy nothing, say so, and the school shuts and
> the five carried lines stay carried. If the quarter wants a school, then the quarter has just
> heard every line of it.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record)*
> Entered: the purse read aloud, in the street, by the till. The answer to *who decided* is a column
> and a window, and it is the only answer this counter has ever had.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium, dusk, the charter — FINAL HOOK (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: dusk at the hatch board, the wet street going blue, snow sliding off the board's
little roof — and on the board, where the school's term hung this morning, a new sealed notice set
square in the Office's regular knot-style: a charter, in machine grammar, with a seal at its foot and
a budget-cord hanging from it. The counting-lesson women stand before it with a lamp; behind them the
queue has stopped. Nobody is reading yet.

**Image:** The machine's answer to being read back at: it offers to own the thing it cannot accuse.

> **THE READER:**
> *(slowly, with the lamp up)*
> A charter. The Office offers to receive the school — under the Office, at the Office's cost, with a
> seal on its lessons and a line in its budget. Every lesson then counted, and every count theirs.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, from the back of the crowd)*
> Entered: the Office has answered the winter by offering us exactly what we accused it of. Not a
> refusal. A charter. Read it aloud in the morning, all of it, including the seal.

> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Choose the owner.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the thaw and the closed columns (the institution's ordinary survival); the
  accusation answered by a ledger (the purse read in the street); the charter (the machine's first
  move of a new kind).** The chapter opens in mud, not snow: the war of the winter is over and the
  politics of the spring is *who owns the school*. Let the page be wet, grey and procedural.
- **The claimant must be honest, specific and sympathetic.** He is owed seven measures; he is not
  asking for them here; his question is about the purse. Give him the middle of the page and let him
  land it in three sentences. Kessa's *he is not wrong to ask it; he is only early* is the page's
  fairness. Do not let anybody in the panel win against him.
- **The answer is a reading, not a defence.** Ira lays the pen down — the art must show the gesture,
  not her face — and says *yes, it did* before saying anything else. Then the till's book goes out on
  the sill and Kessa reads her own columns: fuel given, oil spent, nine lines out, four back, five
  carried, no loans, one cord and two knots received as gifts. The column that matters is the last
  one: the counter has taken almost nothing from the quarter.
- **The charter is a *courtesy* attack, in the winter's grammar, and it must look legal.** Sealed
  notice, machine knot-style, a budget-cord hanging from it: the Office does not deny that the
  counter bought a school — it offers to buy it properly. Every lesson would then be counted, and
  every count theirs. Nobody explains the trap on the page; the reader sees it.
- **No third knot on the sill, and no snow-heavy figure.** The brief is explicit: the correspondence
  waits. The sill appears only in the caption-less background of P2 at most; the page does not go to
  it.
- **The imperative ladder takes its first run for Chapter 015:** *Choose the owner.* (Ladder so far:
  knock / ask him / read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is
  whole / rule it, keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name /
  answer it anyway / read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it /
  enter the thin / read the tag / let him / note the absence / still folded / answer in kind /
  whoever keeps it / read it at dawn / give it back read / choose the owner.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 015: ONE, fresh and unspent. Mother never on panel, never
  speaks — no thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel and unnamed. The grey clerk:
  not on the page. The snow-heavy figure: not on the page; the sill's two knots untouched and not
  mentioned. Rekhak's paper: folded, unopened, unmentioned. The runner: not on the page (the Office
  did not send a man; it used the board). Nandi: not on the page; the term is hers in substance. The
  counting-lesson women: posting the term, reading the charter. The oil-cord: THREE knots, still
  hanging. The window: open. The till: closed, chalked, its book read in the street.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The term: knot-groups on a
  cord, read aloud. The till's book: chalk columns and knot-marks, no lettering. The charter: machine
  knot-style, seal at the foot, a budget-cord, no lettering. Thaw light, wet grey, dusk blue; no
  lamps before dusk.
- **Numbers:** one thaw; two columns closed; one term posted; three lessons a week; one line a head;
  one accusation; one purse read aloud; nine lines out, four back, five carried; one cord and two
  knots received; one charter offered; one seal; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
