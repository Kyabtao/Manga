# THREADBORN — Chapter 15 — Page 002

**Chapter title:** *The Spring Term*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the stall's window, the school's table, and the hatch board at noon
**Page type:** The charter — 7 panels; the machine's best paper read aloud in full, a school that answers by splitting it, and one blank line nobody can fill

> Beats: **the charter comes down off the board** — taken down carefully, seal intact, and carried to
> the window, because no paper is answered where it was hung; **the reading in full** — clause by
> clause, the most generous document the Office has ever offered the basin: the school received, its
> lines paid, its lessons sealed, its teachers licensed, its term filed in Office grammar; **the
> generosity lands** — a woman in the queue steps forward with her child before the reading is even
> finished, because the first thing the quarter hears is that the lessons are free; **the cost,
> clause by clause** — in exchange the school's book becomes an Office roll, and every student is
> entered by name; **the answer is the school's, not the keeper's** — Nandi and the counting-lesson
> women take the Office at its word on the half it can own: the Office may own the GRAMMAR; **the
> count stays the street's** — the school's own book kept outside the charter, unsealed, unlicensed,
> in the old grammar; two books at one table, each under the other's grammar; **the first schedule,
> and the blank line** — the charter requires a roll of students by name, and the register still holds
> an entry whose name-space is empty, and signing would mean writing a name the school does not have.
>
> **This page:** the machine's best offer, answered by a school that has learned to split a document —
> and a blank line that neither side can fill.
>
> **Chapter 015 budget:** ONE chain-stop, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — the thread
> quiet. Loom never speaks, does not appear. The three entered hands remain unnamed. The grey clerk is
> never named, never faced, not on the page. The snow-heavy figure does NOT appear; the sill's two
> knots are untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper stays folded, unopened, unmentioned.

## PANEL 1 — Medium-wide, morning, the charter comes down (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium-wide: the hatch board in grey noon light — two of the counting-lesson women easing
the sealed charter off the board's pegs with both hands, the budget-cord gathered up carefully and
looped, the seal kept face-up; a third holds the board's ladder steady; the quarter stands back to let
them work. Behind, the thawed street and its running gutters. Nobody tears anything.

**Image:** An Office paper taken down as carefully as it was hung, because the counter reads what it is given.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> We did not answer it at the board. Nothing gets answered where it was hung — a board can be spoken
> at all day and never once have to listen. It came down, seal intact, and went where every paper in
> this quarter goes: to the window.

> **THE READER:**
> *(to the crowd, carrying it flat)*
> To the counter, then. You will hear all of it, in order, and then we will say what we say.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, the charter's face (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the charter held flat in two pairs of hands in daylight — the Office's rigid
regular knot-style in ordered rows down the face, the seal at the foot, the budget-cord coiled and
hanging, and at the bottom a schedule blank with even ruling: a roll, its first line empty. Nothing
lettered; everything counted, spaced and ruled.

**Image:** A generous document with a ledger's teeth in its last clause.

> **THE READER:**
> *(reading the shape before the words)*
> Six clauses. Five of them gifts. One of them a list of names with nothing on it yet.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record)*
> Read all six. Read the empty one last, and read it twice.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the reading in full (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the stall's window at noon, the reader standing on the sill's step with the
charter up, reading it to the crowd — the thaw behind them, the queue packed close, the stall's
awning dripping. Ira at the window's edge, entering; Kessa at the till, arms folded, listening the way
an appraiser listens to a price.

**Image:** The counter's law applied to the richest paper the machine ever sent: read in public, in order, before anyone answers.

> **THE READER:**
> *(flat, unhurried, clause by clause)*
> One: the Office receives the school. Two: the Office pays every line of its teaching. Three: every
> lesson taught under the charter is sealed by the Office. Four: every teacher is licensed by the
> Office and taught its grammar. Five: the term is filed in Office grammar, in the Office's hours.

> *(a pause; the crowd has begun to murmur)*

> Six: the school's book becomes an Office roll, and every student is entered by name.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium-close, the generosity lands (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the front of the crowd — a woman in a patched shawl has stepped forward with
her child before the reader has finished, one hand on the girl's shoulder, the other half-raised; the
child is looking at the charter, not at anyone's face. Around them the quarter's faces have gone from
suspicion to arithmetic: free lessons, paid by the Office, sealed and licensed. Behind, Kessa has not
moved.

**Image:** The first thing the quarter hears is that the lessons are free — and it is true.

> **THE WOMAN IN THE SHAWL:**
> *(quickly, before anyone can answer)*
> Paid by the Office. Sealed by the Office. Then it cannot be taken back for being ours. Enter her —
> she is nine and she can read a tally already.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, gently)*
> Nothing is entered yet. Hold her where she is, and hear the last clause twice.

> **KESSA:**
> *(quietly, to the window's edge)*
> That is how it is done, keeper. They give you a door and a bill for the hinges, and they make the
> door so good that saying no is the unnatural thing.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the school answers (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the school's side room, the archive table — Nandi standing at its head with the
charter laid flat, the counting-lesson women ranged along the benches, cords in their laps, the
school's thick old book open at her hand and its own blank name-space visible on the open page. Ira at
the room's edge, not at the table: the keeper is not the one answering.

**Image:** The answer comes from the school, because the school is what is being bought.

> **NANDI:**
> *(flat, to the room, one hand on the charter and one on her own book)*
> The Office may own the grammar. It is a fine grammar and it taught us to read a hold. It may own
> the lessons, the seals, the money and the licensed hours — let every child in this quarter be
> taught in the Office's own hand, by Office teachers, at the Office's cost. Take that.
>
> *(her hand moves to her own book)*
>
> The count stays here. The school's book is not a roll. It does not file, it does not seal, it has
> no officer. Whoever sits at this table is counted in the old grammar and owed nothing by it.

> **THE READER:**
> *(carefully)*
> Then there are two books at one table.

> **NANDI:**
> There have been two books at one table since the night the machine accepted our terms. We are only
> saying out loud which side of the table each one sits on.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the answer written (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the charter's margin under Nandi's thick-knuckled fingers — the school's answer
being entered in plain knot-work, four knots and the school's own plain cord tied through the paper's
corner; beside it, the school's book open, its old grammar visible, the empty name-space on its page;
the Office's seal untouched at the charter's foot.

**Image:** The Office keeps its seal and its money; the school keeps its count.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, writing it for the register)*
> Entered: the charter answered in two halves. Grammar: accepted, sealed, licensed, paid. Count:
> refused, kept, unsealed. Entered: neither side loses the thing it actually wanted.

> **KESSA:**
> *(from the door, dry)*
> And the machine will call that a victory, because it got a school. And we will call it a victory,
> because we still have a book. Both of us will be right, which is how you know it is a real ruling.

---

## PANEL 7 — Medium-close, the first schedule — FINAL HOOK (~12%)

**Camera:** Medium-close: the charter's last clause laid beside the school's open book on the table —
the Office's schedule ruled for names on one side, and on the other the register's protection of
record with its name-space still blank, wax seal and four knots in place. Nandi's finger rests
between them. The room is silent; the benches have gone still. Beyond the window, the thaw drips.

**Image:** A charter that cannot be signed without writing a name the school does not have.

> **NANDI:**
> *(slowly, the whole problem in one breath)*
> The first schedule wants a roll of students by name. And our own book has an entry with no name in
> it, on purpose, for a hand nobody here can name. Sign the schedule and we write him down in an
> Office hand. Keep the blank and the charter cannot be witnessed.

> **IRA:**
> *(of record, the page's last entry)*
> Entered: the school's answer is accepted in principle and blocked in practice, by one line. The
> wheel of this is that the Office's best paper and our best protection want the same empty space.

> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Write the name.

## Writing & art notes

- **Three jobs, in order: the reading (generosity landed before cost); the split (the school's answer,
  in the school's voice); the blank line (the collision, unresolved).** The page's engine is that the
  machine's offer is *good* — genuinely, materially good for every child in the quarter — and the
  school says yes to it and no to the same document in the same breath.
- **The generosity must land first and land hard.** Panel 4 is the page's conscience: a mother steps
  forward with her daughter before the reading is even finished, and she is *right* — sealed lessons
  cannot be taken away for being the basin's. Do not undercut her with a reaction shot from Ira. Let
  Kessa name the mechanism quietly afterwards, and let nobody in the panel smirk.
- **The answer is Nandi's and the school's; Ira stands at the edge.** The keeper does not rule on this
  one — the school is the thing being bought, so the school answers. Nandi's division is dry and
  practical: the Grammar (lessons, seals, money, licensed hours) accepted; the Count (the school's own
  book, old grammar, no officer, nobody owed by it) refused. *Two books at one table* is the Chapter
  013 formula coming back as a school's own practice, not a lawyer's trick.
- **Panel 5's art carries the whole argument in two hands:** Nandi's hand on the charter, then her
  hand on her own book. Nothing else in the panel should compete with that gesture.
- **The hook is a blank line, and the page must not fill it.** The charter's first schedule needs
  names; the register's protection of record has a name-space deliberately empty for *whoever keeps
  the night hour*. Both documents want the same space. Do not resolve it, do not hint at the answer,
  and do not let anyone propose writing a false name — the page ends in the school's silence and
  Nandi's two-sentence statement of the problem.
- **The imperative ladder takes its next run:** *Write the name.* (Ladder so far: knock / ask him /
  read it back / sign nothing / keep it better / ask her / tie her in / the roll is whole / rule it,
  keeper / count it, then / light it anyway / enter it anyway / protect the name / answer it anyway /
  read it back — both ways / cross, then / him, too / read both / invoice it / enter the thin / read
  the tag / let him / note the absence / still folded / answer in kind / whoever keeps it / read it at
  dawn / give it back read / choose the owner / write the name.)
- **Continuity:** chain-stop budget Chapter 015: ONE, unspent. Mother never on panel, never speaks — no
  thread. Loom absent. The three entered hands off-panel and unnamed. The grey clerk: not on the page.
  The snow-heavy figure: absent; the sill's knots untouched and unmentioned. Rekhak's paper: folded,
  unopened, unmentioned. The runner: not on the page. Kessa: present at the till and at the door, one
  line each. The counting-lesson women: three at the board, the rest on the benches; one reads.
  Nandi: answers the charter. The oil-cord: THREE knots. The window: open, thaw dripping.
- **§3.1:** no electric light, no modern fixtures, no printed signage. The charter: knot-rows in rigid
  regular style, seal at the foot, budget-cord, and a ruled schedule with no lettering. The school's
  answer: four knots and a plain cord through the corner. The school's book: old grammar, knot-marks,
  the blank name-space visible. Noon grey, thaw light; lamps unlit.
- **Numbers:** one charter; six clauses; five gifts; one roll; one seal; one budget-cord; one mother
  and one child; two books at one table; four knots for the answer; one blank line; zero names
  written; chain-stop ONE, unspent.
