# REKHAK VAHNI — रेखक वह्नि

**Status:** Antagonist-of-the-chapter → recurring institutional threat
**First appearance:** Chapter 01, Page 003, Panel 4 (silhouette at the stair mouth), named in Panel 5
**Kind:** Manav (मानव) — single-strand, burns bright and fast
**Sector:** Agnikhand (Ash Sector), up-terrace — serves the **Ash Council**
**Age:** 41 (drawn older; Manav Fray earliest, and he has been spending since nineteen)
**Occupation:** **Debt-Reckoner** — audits Sutra-marks and debt-ledgers for the Council
**Mark location:** Over the left eye → **Sight** type. Power put into *knowing*.

---

## Appearance (art continuity)

- **Build:** tall, thin, slightly stooped at the shoulders — a man who has spent his life bending over
  ledgers. Never broad. Never imposing by size; imposing by *accuracy*.
- **Face:** narrow, clean-shaven, pale eyes (wash them out — pale grey-blue, almost colourless). Faint
  dark capillaries of debt-line at the temples.
- **The Sight-mark:** a fine dark suture-like mark on the bone above the left brow. When he spends it,
  a hairline crack of new dark marks spreads downward under the eye — **always in-panel** (canon rule).
- **Debt:** **Deep.** Thin dark lines climb his neck to the jaw and disappear into his collar. Do not
  draw him Clear. Do not draw him Terminal (no lines at the eyes yet — that is his future).
- **Clothes:** ash-grey Ash Council greatcoat, high collar, black basalt buttons in a double row. Under
  it, a slate-coloured clerk's waistcoat and cuffs that are ink-stained. No armour. No weapon.
- **Props (all three must be present whenever he is on panel):**
  1. **Ledger-slate** — a hinged black stone tablet on a cord at his hip. Pages of stamped record.
  2. **Counting-chain** — a chain of knotted brass rings he runs through his fingers when he reads.
     *The basin counts with thread; the Council counts with metal.*
  3. **The crimson-sealed page** — one folded page in his ledger sealed with crimson wax. Never explained
     on panel, never opened in Chapter 1. (Off-Sector buyer. Do not name it.)
- **Ash:** it settles on his shoulders and he does **not** brush it off. An up-terrace man who lets the
  basin's ash sit on him — that is a character note, not an oversight.
- **Silhouette test:** tall thin coat + hanging ledger-slate + the counting-chain held out at hip height.

> **Rule:** Rekhak is drawn **clean** and **symmetrical** where the basin is crooked. He is the first
> person in the series whose clothes have no mends in them. That is what makes him frightening.

## Personality

- **Polite to the point of insult.** He never raises his voice, never threatens, never sneers. His
  cruelty is expressed entirely as *precision* — he tells you your exact debt, out loud, in public,
  because it is simply information.
- **Interested.** This is the crack in Chapter 1's indifference: he is the first person who *wants*
  something from Ira. Interest in Rekhak is more dangerous than anger.
- **Procedural, not ideological.** He does not hate Manavkin; he is mildly annoyed that two ledgers
  disagree. A contradiction in a book offends him more than a person does.
- **Honest about being an errand.** He says openly that the Council is only the messenger, that somebody
  up-terrace wants to meet her. He believes transparency costs him nothing, and he is usually right.
- **Spends himself to see.** He keeps using the Sight-mark even though it is climbing his throat. He
  knows what that ends in. He does it anyway. Workaholism as a death wish.

## Voice

- Complete sentences. Formal register. Contractions only when he is bored.
- Speaks in **numbers, dates and laws** — never in adjectives. ("Heavy, four years old, Jalkhand law.")
- Uses people's full names. Every time. *Ira Sutar.* Never "child", never "girl" — that is Kessa's
  register, not his. The difference is deliberate: Kessa's "be" is affection; his full name is a file.
- When he is genuinely interested his voice gets **softer and slower**, never louder.

## Abilities

**Sight-thread (learned at Unspooling, over-eye mark):** he can read a Sutra-mark and a debt-ledger
directly — Kind, thread colour, age of debt, Sector law, and whether a mark has been tampered with.
It is not mind-reading. It is *audit*: he sees records, not thoughts.

**Hard canon rules:**
1. Every use of Sight adds debt-marks on-panel. No free looks. (See `02-power-system.md` §4, §7.)
2. His debt is already **Deep** — lines at the throat. When he speaks after spending, others can hear the
   Loom through him: a small *hmmmm* under the words. This is his audio signature, the same SFX as the
   Loom on Page 001.
3. He is **physically weak**. He has never fought anyone on panel and would lose to Kessa. His power is
   bureaucratic: he can have someone registered, unregistered, bound, or sold without touching them.
4. He cannot see the **stitch's author**. Sight reads marks and tampering, not identity. Do not let him
   open the locked box — he can only confirm that the box exists.

## Relationship to Ira

- The first authority figure who treats her as **a record worth chasing** rather than a record to skip.
- He wants to deliver her, not to hurt her. That is worse, and it should read as worse.
- He is the doorway to the **two-ledger contradiction**: the Ash Council refuses to register Manavkin,
  yet the Unspooling Court recorded her. She officially does not exist *and* she is on file. Chapter 1's
  engine.

## Relationship to Kessa

- Old acquaintances, mutually armed with information. She bribes him without a word; he does not accept
  and does not report her. He has appraised her stall before and called it "an honest hole."
- She is the only person in the basin he speaks to in full sentences.
