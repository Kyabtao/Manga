# THREADBORN — Chapter 01 — Page 008

**Chapter title:** *The Girl With No Thread*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — the Knot & Nail, before dawn
**Page type:** The hour / vision — 7 panels, ends on a movement
**Canvas:** vertical webtoon strip, full colour

> Beats carried from Page 007: sixty hour-knots, the sealing-word, the mother-debt, Kessa's eyeline to
> the lockbox. This page spends the hour the whole chapter has been pricing — and shows the reader what
> is under the stitch without ever naming who put it there.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the stall before dawn (~13%)

**Camera:** From the stall door, the whole room in one held breath.
**Image:** Pre-dawn blue through the shutters; one lamp. Kessa sits behind the counter with the loupe
**down** over one cat eye, the knot-cord tied to her own thick wrist, its other end tied to Ira's. Patra
stands in the corner where the lamplight pools — and his shadow pools **toward** the lamp, a dark puddle
at his feet. Two stools, one table, sixty knots between two wrists. The fourth chair is empty and
everyone knows who it is for.

> **CAPTION:**
> The hour arrived the way everything official arrives in the basin:
>
> **CAPTION:**
> without knocking.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the Council kneels (~13%)

**Camera:** Low, from the floor of the stall, looking up — then immediately *down*, because that is
where he goes.
**Image:** **Rekhak kneels** beside Ira's stool, greatcoat pooled in the basin's dust, and takes her
gloved hand to slide the glove off her right palm himself, finger by finger, with a clerk's care. Ash
has stopped falling outside: even the sky is holding still. Kessa's thumb rests on the first knot.

> **REKHAK (dialogue, procedural, quiet):**
> The hour begins at my word and ends at hers.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, thumb on knot one):**
> The hour begins at knot one and ends at knot sixty.
>
> **REKHAK (dialogue, not arguing — kneeling deeper):**
> …Agreed.

## PANEL 3 — Macro, palm to Sight (~14%)

**Camera:** Straight down onto Ira's bare stitched palm in Rekhak's two hands. The stitch line crosses
the field like a road.
**Image:** His pale eyes close. The dark suture-mark above his brow **opens its hairline** — Sight
spending — and thin new debt-lines step visibly up his cheek toward the under-eye, one panel-step per
breath. The brass chain in his other hand runs, faster than its usual rhythm. Ira's face is out of
focus at the top edge, watching her own hand be read like a ledger.

> **SFX:** *chk-chk-chk-chk* (the chain, quickening)
>
> **IRA (thought, italic):**
> My hand. My whole hand.
>
> **IRA (thought, italic):**
> Nobody has ever looked *in* it before.

---

## PANEL 4 — VISION PANEL, full width, unlettered (~16%)

**Camera:** Inside the Sight. No balloons, no captions, no sound. The art language changes: flat
colour-fields, hard edges, like a record being read.
**Image:** What Rekhak sees: Ira's palm as a **fold**, not a door. Her thread — drawn as a length of
colourless filament — folded inward once, twice, a dozen times, pressed flat and bundled small under
the stitch, the way a mender presses cloth. It is not missing. It is **packed**. And round the bundle,
sewn in neat surgical rows: the sealing thread — colourless, and casting **no shadow at all** in a
vision where everything else casts one.

> *(no lettering — this panel is read, not heard)*

---

## PANEL 5 — VISION PANEL 2, the sleeping bundle (~14%)

**Camera:** Closer inside the vision. The folded bundle fills the frame.
**Image:** In the centre of the pressed fold, a **knot** — not sewn, *grown*: the thread's own grain
twisted into a sleeping whorl. And across the whorl, faint as breath on glass, the suggestion of
movement — one fold shifting a hair's width against its neighbours, the way a sleeper's shoulder
moves. Everything else in the vision is perfectly, terribly still.

> *(no lettering)*

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, the silent second (~12%)

**Camera:** Smash-cut out of the vision to the widest shot of the page: the whole Ashfall Basin at pre-
dawn, rooftops and drifts and the Spindle's pillar of light in the distance.
**Image:** For **one second**, the Loom's hum is not there. The Spindle's white light **flickers** — the
first flicker in four hundred years. On every rooftop, in every window, threaded people stop and look
up, hands going to their marks. No sound effect at all in this panel. The absence is the sound.

> **CAPTION (small, centred, the only lettering):**
> For one second, Rangbhoomi held its breath.

---

## PANEL 7 — HOOK — back in the stall, it moved (~18%)

**Camera:** Tight three-shot at the table: Ira surfacing from the shared vision, gasping soft; Rekhak
still kneeling, eyes still closed, the chain in his fist **stopped**; Kessa's thumb between knot fifty-
nine and sixty, frozen. Patra's shadow in the corner has straightened half a beat before Patra has.
**Image:** Ira is looking at her own empty palm. It looks the same as it ever did. It is not.

> **IRA (dialogue, whisper, the smallest voice in the chapter):**
> It moved.
>
> **IRA (dialogue):**
> Fifteen years it never moved.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, not letting go of the cord):**
> What moved, be?
>
> **IRA (dialogue, eyes on her palm):**
> Something in my hand turned over in its sleep.
>
> **CAPTION (final box, bottom):**
> Knot count: sixty. The hour was not over.

## Writing & art notes

- **The fold is the chapter's reveal and not the box's key.** The reader now knows: Ira's thread was not
  taken — it was folded, pressed and sewn down *sleeping*. Who folded it, why, and with what right stay
  locked. Do not let any character say "her thread is still there" aloud this chapter; the vision is
  unlettered for exactly that reason.
- **Vision panels are a new art language.** Flat colour-fields, hard edges, no lettering, everything
  casts a shadow except unpulled thread. Reuse this language for every Sight-vision in the series; the
  shadow rule is the reader's lie-detector (Page 010's swatch will break it on purpose).
- **The silent second is citywide canon.** The Spindle flickered once; every threaded person in the
  basin felt their mark go quiet for a beat. Chapter 2 opens in a city that remembers this second. Do
  not retcon it, do not explain it this chapter, and never let the Council admit it happened.
- **Rekhak kneels.** The Council does not kneel. He does it twice (Panel 2: to the stall; deeper, to the
  hour). Track it: by Page 009 he will stand again and the difference should read as a man re-entering
  his uniform.
- **Chain stops inside the vision's aftermath (Panel 7)** — second stop in the series. First stop (Page
  004) was fear at a question about himself; this stop is awe or dread at what he is reading. The reader
  now has two data points; a third stop must not happen before Chapter 2 or the tell inflates.
- **Knot count is visible discipline:** Panel 2 knot one under Kessa's thumb; Panel 7 thumb between
  fifty-nine and sixty. The hour is *not* over at page end — Page 009 opens inside the last minute.
- **Patra's shadow straightens before Patra does** (Panel 7). Preta shadows lead the body when something
  large happens. Artist rule from here on.
- Sewer locked, buyer unnamed, principal unseen. The vision shows material only: fold, knot, whorl,
  shadowless thread. No hands, no faces, no cuffs, no Mendery.
