# THREADBORN — Chapter 01 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Girl With No Thread*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand (Ash Sector) — Up-Terrace clear air → the Reckoning Office
**Page type:** Offer / temptation — 7 panels, ends on a price
**Canvas:** vertical webtoon strip, full colour

> Beats carried from Page 004: the third bell, the borrowed lines under her sleeve, the stopped-then-
> running chain, the crimson page (which stays shut in his ledger today). This page is the first sight
> of the up-terrace — and the first face of the errand: a broker who speaks only of "my principal."

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the clear-air terraces (~14%)

**Camera:** From just above the stair mouth, looking out over the up-terrace as Ira steps into it.
**Image:** The first panel in the series with **no ash**. Pale clean stone terraces, laundered banners,
sunlight with no haze in it, a sky with no smoke column anywhere. Ira is small in the frame, still in
her basin hunch, one hand half-raised to catch flakes that are not there. Her robe hem is too short and
here, at last, somebody could afford to notice.

> **CAPTION:**
> The third bell had not yet rung.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Up-terrace, the air was clear. No ash. No smoke. No sky she recognised.
>
> **IRA (thought, italic):**
> Nothing is falling.
>
> **IRA (thought, italic):**
> I don't know where to put my hands.

## PANEL 2 — Medium wide, the Reckoning Office (~12%)

**Camera:** Down the length of a long clean room. Rows of black stone desks in perfect lines. Not one
fleck of ash on any of them.
**Image:** Clerks in grey do not look up. Ira stands at the visitors' rail with her sleeve pulled down
over her forearm. At a side door, waiting, **Rekhak** — and the brass counting-chain is **running**
again, rhythmic, controlled. The hairline of debt on his neck has climbed **past his jaw overnight**.
Nobody mentions it.

> **CLERK (off-panel, without looking up):**
> Visitors' line.
>
> **IRA (dialogue):**
> I'm not a visitor.
>
> **CLERK (off-panel):**
> Then you're a record. Which line?

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the broker's small room (~13%)

**Camera:** Level, through the doorway, as Ira enters. The room is small, warm-grey, and stacked with
ledgers that are all closed.
**Image:** **PATRA** sits at a plain table — a Preta: translucent at the edges (fingertips, hair, hem),
bone-grey broker's robes with many pockets, a cord of **blank name-tags** at the belt. The window light
falls from the left; Patra's shadow on the wall falls **toward** the window. The face is plain, average,
and already slipping out of memory.

> **PATRA (dialogue, mild):**
> Sit, Ira Sutar.
>
> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> Yes — the name is real. It is currently the only legal thing about you.
>
> **IRA (thought, italic):**
> I will remember this face.

> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> Sign nothing today. Only look at what a line costs.
>
> **IRA (dialogue):**

> **PATRA (dialogue):**

## PANEL 5 — Two-shot across the table (~13%)

**Camera:** Side-on, Ira and Patra facing each other over the form. On the wall behind Patra, the wrong

**Image:** Ira's face is at its flattest — pricing. Patra's almost-smile is the warmest thing in the
room, which is how you know to check your pockets.

> **IRA (dialogue):**
> What does he want with a closed door?
>
> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> My principal does not collect rooms, child. He collects locks.
>
> **IRA (dialogue):**
> And you? What's your line in this?
>
> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> I was struck from a book once. Now I put people into other books. It pays.

## PANEL 6 — Tight, the slipped sleeve (~14%)

**Camera:** Down at the table edge: Ira's sleeve has slipped. The **three painted lines** of ash-ink are
visible on her forearm, beside the blank form.
**Image:** Two pairs of eyes are on those lines in the same instant — Patra's, and Rekhak's from the
doorway. Neither face changes. Ira's other hand quietly pulls the sleeve back down. Nobody speaks.

> **IRA (dialogue, small, to Patra):**
> …Patra. Was that it?
>
> **PATRA (dialogue, soft, almost kind):**
> You asked me that at the door.
>
> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> It does not hold. Nothing about me holds. That is the trade.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Two people in that room could see through three coins of ash-ink.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Both let her keep them.

## PANEL 7 — HOOK — the price (~20%)

**Camera:** Close on Patra sliding the form across the table — and for exactly one instant the face
**holds still** and becomes memorable, which is the most frightening thing Patra does all page.
**Image:** Window light through the translucent edge of Patra's hand on the paper. In the doorway,
Rekhak's silhouette, silent, chain mid-swing. Ira's stitched palm rests flat on the table, nowhere near
the form — and Patra's eyes keep sliding to it and away, like a tongue over a missing tooth.

> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> The payment is the line, Ira Sutar. Name. Kind. Thread. You will *exist*.
>
> **PATRA (dialogue, leaning in; the shadow goes the wrong way again):**
> All it costs is your palm for one hour, child.
>
> **PATRA (dialogue):**
> He doesn't want the door opened. He wants to be certain it stays closed.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> For fifteen years the book had said she was not there.
>
> **CAPTION (final box, bottom):**
> Now it was offering a line — and the price was the only thing she had left that was hers.
>
> **SFX:** (faint, from the doorway, rhythmic) *chk. chk. chk.* — the chain, running.

## Writing & art notes

- **No ash above the third landing. Ever.** The up-terrace is drawn clean from this page on; the basin
  is the only place in Agnikhand where the sky falls. Ira's raised-hand *ash-catch* is her up-terrace
  tell — reuse it every time she climbs, until the day she stops doing it (that day matters).
- **Preta tells, every panel Patra is in:** translucent edges; shadow falling toward the light; a face
  and a name that do not hold (Panel 3 sets up the forgetting, Panel 6 pays it — it is canon physics,
  not Ira being rude). Artist rule: draw Patra plain and average in every panel **except one per page**,
  where the face holds still and sharp. The held face is the tell that Patra is about to cost someone
  something.
- **Patra never lies.** Omissions, misdirection, letting people mishear — yes. An outright false
  sentence, never. This is what makes the broker trustworthy enough to be dangerous.
- **Rekhak: silent all page, chain running.** The stop on the stair was fear; the running means he has
  put the fear in a box and locked it. A Rekhak who has boxed his fear is more dangerous than one
  showing it. His neck-line crossing the jaw overnight is **not explained this chapter** — do not let
  anyone remark on it aloud.
- **The ash-ink is seen through, and kept.** Patra and Rekhak both read the painted lines in Panel 6 and
  both let her keep them. Rule going forward: nobody mentions the borrowed lines aloud until someone
  wants to hurt her with them.
- **The registration form is never lettered in art.** Three blank ruled lines, always. The moment it
  gains writing is a future page and should be drawn like a wound opening.
- **The principal stays unnamed.** Nothing crimson on Patra's person — bone-grey only. The crimson wax
  lives inside Rekhak's ledger, and his ledger does not open today.
- **Ira neither signs nor consents on this page.** Page 006 is her counter-offer; menders inspect the
  tear before they sew.
