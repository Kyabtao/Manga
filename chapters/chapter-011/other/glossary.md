# Chapter 011 — Glossary

Terms introduced, seated or paid off in this chapter. Terms already listed in earlier chapters keep
their first entry; the Ch. 011 column notes what this chapter adds.

| Term | Devanagari | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **The survey** | सर्वेक्षण | The Office walking an address one step at a time: *entry by entry, hand by hand*, with the keeper of the register obliged to attend. Not a raid — a measurement, and measurements get filed. |
| **The fourth step** | चौथा पायदान | Where the survey begins, counting out from the hatch. Chosen because the hatch's sill is step one and the alley's mouth is step three; four is the first step a person stands on when they knock. |
| **The water-butt** | पानी की बौड़ी | Where the survey ends. Forty paces, eleven doors, one lamp. The whole of the school's address, walkable in a morning. |
| **Clause four** | धारा चार | *The licence lapses if the place of keeping fails to produce.* A timer, not a fine — the Office never threatens, it expires. Spoken aloud to the keeper for the first time on Page 001. |
| **Produce** | पेश करना | The verb the Office uses for people and places alike. *"Produce what?" / "Whatever is in the register, keeper. You're holding it."* |
| **The grey lamp** | धूसर लालटेन | A lamp on a pole at the alley mouth, burning at first light because the Office pays for oil. Same height, same hook the school's brass lantern hung on for forty years. |
| **The second knot** | दूसरी गाँठ | Found on the Mendery's step beside the lifted one: thick, uneven, tied with too much thread, crossed wrong for either hand. A *new enrolment* made in an untrained hand. |
| **An untrained hand** | अनाड़ी हाथ | Somebody shown once, in daylight, by somebody who had somewhere else to be. Not a mender's knot and not a thief's knot. |
| **Shadowless thread** | छायाहीन धागा | Thread that casts no shadow under any light. The cut-end in Kessa's lockbox is made of it; Ira's palm was sewn with it. On Page 001 a stranger's knot turns out to be tied in it. **Draw it literally:** a physical filament that simply fails to darken what is under it — never a glow, aura or sparkle. |
| **The cut-end** | कटा सिरा | A hand's width of shadowless thread, severed at both ends, kept on grey felt in Kessa's lockbox. Forty years old; she has never asked what it was cut from. |
| **Counting** | गिनती | Jadi's lesson, now loose in three households: count your own knots so no clerk can ever tell you what your own hands say. In this chapter it changes the shape of the queue. |
| **Two trades in one line** | एक कतार में दो धन्धे | The queue at the Mendery counter: hands that want to be *counted* (facing each other) and hands that want to be *claimed* (facing the counter). Only one of the two pays. |
| **Card-tray** | कार्ड-ट्रे | What the reader-second carries. Nima has not put hers down. The standing thread: the day she is told to put a hand on the stand, she will refuse out loud. |
| ***Do not enrol me. — S.M.*** | *मुझे दाखिल मत करना। — स.मा.* | The last knot in the Book, in the mother's own hand. Read aloud into an Office book on Page 001: she enrolled people through the hatch for forty years and never enrolled herself. |
| **Year one** | वर्ष एक | The earliest entry the survey reaches — before the water-butt had a stand, before the alley had a lamp, before there was a form for any of it. |
| **Place of keeping** | रखने का स्थान | The Mendery, Ashfall, slot four. Named on the record on Ch. 010 Page 010; surveyed on Ch. 011 Page 001. |
| **Clause two** | धारा दो | *A hand may be entered at its owner's own word: no fee, and no reward for anyone else.* The keeper's smallest lawful counter to the bounty — enter the hands first and there is nothing left to claim but the person. On Page 002 it is used **without an owner**, which is the first lie in the register. |
| **Entered at the owner's word** | मालिक के शब्द पर दर्ज | A declaration, not a verification. The register has no box for a keeper who does not know whose hands are in her alley, so the lie is held as true. |
| **Hold** | पकड़ना | What the Office asks the reader-second to do on Page 002 — *hold the subject's hand for the count.* Not read: hold. The one thing Nima's bargain never covered. |
| **Reader-second declines** | प्रशिक्षु पाठक ने इन्कार किया | An entry, not a punishment. The Office writes the refusal down rather than disciplining it, which makes the defiance part of the record. |
| **The whorl** | घुमावदार गाँठ | The spiral end-knot at the end of a piece of thread. First seen on Chapter 001 Page 010; on Ch. 011 Page 002 the cut-end and the new knot are shown **finishing in the same whorl** — the series fingerprint. |
| **The question under the counter** | काउटर के नीचे का सवाल | *What was it cut from?* Kessa has kept the cut-end forty years and never asked it out loud. Ira asks it on Page 002 and gets a delay, not an answer. |
| **Two ends of one thread** | एक धागे के दो सिरे | The hook's finding: the cut-end and the second knot are the same thread — same gauge, same twist, both shadowless — forty years apart. A cut-end is an *end*, so once that thread ran into something and somebody cut it off it. |
| **Crimson and grey** | किरमिज़ी / धूसर | Crimson: the principal's private hand. Grey: the Council, the Office, the lamp on the pole, and now the survey's slate. |
