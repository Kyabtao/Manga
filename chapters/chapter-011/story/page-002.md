# THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 002

**Chapter title:** *The Fourth Step* / *चौथा पायदान*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the Mendery step and the Knot & Nail counter
**Page type:** The step is surveyed — 7 panels, ends on the cut-end

> Beats: the survey reaches the Mendery step and asks what is on it; Ira has exactly one lawful
> instrument — clause two, an owner's own word — and no owner to name, so she enters the second knot
> as her own work and puts the first lie in her own register; the Office asks **Nima** to hold a hand
> and she refuses out loud in the alley; and Ira finally asks **Kessa** the question she has never
> asked in forty years — **what was the cut-end cut from?**
>
> **This page:** the keeper learns she is also the pupil.
>
> **Chapter 011 budget:** ONE chain-stop, **not spent on Page 002** (still held). Loom never speaks.
> Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009 — ten days now.
> Wax: grey for the Council and the Office, crimson for the principal's private hand.

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the survey reaches the step (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: mid-morning, and the survey has walked the whole alley. The grey clerk stops at the
**Mendery step** — the tenth entry, one step before the water-butt. The second clerk sets down the
folding stool and opens the folding slate. On the step, still lying where it was left at first light,
the scrap of register paper with **two knots** in it. The queue has stopped talking. The counting
women have turned around.

**Image:** The measurement arriving at the one step in the alley that belongs to the keeper.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> Step four, tenth entry. Object on the step: one paper, two knots. *(beat)* Keeper, what is on it?
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> They have walked forty paces and eleven doors to stand on my doorstep and ask me what I left
> outside my own door. That is the whole instrument. It is not clever. It is just patient.

---

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the one lawful move (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira at the counter's end with the register open, the paper in her gloved hand.
Behind her the counting women have stopped counting and are watching, hands still half-raised. She
knows the instrument: **clause two** — an owner may enter their own hand, at their own word, for no
fee and no reward. Enter the hands first and there is nothing left to claim but the person.

**Image:** A keeper holding the only legal tool she has, and a knot whose owner she does not know.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Clause two. I have used it twice and it has worked twice: enter the hands before anyone can claim
> them, and the bounty starves.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> But clause two needs an *owner,* and I do not know whose hand tied this. I know only that it was
> tied with my thread, in my alley, by somebody my mother taught this week.

---

## PANEL 3 — Close, the entry (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: Ira's gloved hand lays the paper flat on the counter, then her bare hand beside it,
palm down. Her face does nothing. Then she says it, in the flat voice of a woman giving a measurement.

**Image:** The smallest lawful act, and the first dishonest one.

> **IRA:**
> Entry: one knot, ash-grey, tied within the week, entered at the owner's word. Owner: the keeper.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(writing, not looking up)*
> Tied by the keeper.
>
> **IRA:**
> I tied it.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> That is the first lie I have entered in my own register. There is no box on the Office's form for a
> keeper who does not know whose hands are in her alley, so the clerk writes it down and the register
> holds it, and now it is true in the only way this basin has for true.

---

## PANEL 4 — Two-shot, Nima is asked (~13%)

**Camera:** Two-shot: **Nima** with the card-tray still in her hands, and the grey clerk turning to
her. He does not ask her to read. He asks her to hold. Behind them, the counting women and the two
claimants, all facing the same way now.

**Image:** The Office asking for the one thing Nima's bargain never covered.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> Reader-second. Hold the subject's hand for the count.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Her bargain was about a *book.* The Office pays her not to be written down, and in exchange she
> counts and does not read — and in eleven days they have never once asked her to touch anybody.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the refusal (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: Nima sets the card-tray down on the counter — deliberately, the first time all
morning — and says no. Out loud. The alley hears it: three counting women, two claimants, a
stall-keeper at each shutter. The grey clerk does not raise his voice and does not repeat himself. He
writes.

**Image:** A girl saying the one word her wage was built on never saying, in daylight, with the row
watching.

> **NIMA:**
> No.
>
> **NIMA:**
> I count. I don't hold. You can write that down or you can take the tray back, but I am not putting
> my hand on somebody so that somebody else can say what it is.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(writing)*
> Reader-second declines. Entry noted.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> The whole row heard it. That was the point of asking her in the alley instead of in a room.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the question (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the stall interior afterwards, shutters half down, lamplight. Ira at the counter
with the knot laid on the wood between them. Kessa's **loupe is down** over one eye. Ira asks the
question the old appraiser has never once asked herself in forty years.

**Image:** A mender asking the woman who keeps the evidence what the evidence is.

> **IRA:**
> What was it cut from?
>
> **KESSA:**
> *(a long beat; she does not look up, and her hand stays flat on the counter)*
> Forty years I have had that question sitting under my counter in a locked box, and I never once
> asked it out loud.
>
> **IRA:**
> Well?
>
> **KESSA:**
> *(still not looking up)*
> Well, now you have said it in my stall, so it is in the room, and I will have to answer it.

---

## PANEL 7 — Macro, two ends of one thread — FINAL HOOK (~18%)

**Camera:** Macro: the lockbox open on the counter, and on its bed of old grey felt **two** things
lying side by side for the first time: the **cut-end** — a hand's width of shadowless thread, severed
clean at both ends — and, laid beside it under the loupe, **Ira's second knot.** Under magnification
they are the same: same gauge, same twist, same refusal to cast a shadow. And the cut-end finishes in
a **whorl.** So does the knot.

**Image:** Two pieces of the same thread, forty years apart, in one box, under one lens.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Same thread. Same twist. Same nothing where the shadow should be. Forty years apart, and one of
> them was tied this week by somebody my mother taught.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The swatch has no owner on panel. Neither does this. But a cut-end is an *end* — which means once,
> a long time ago, that thread ran into something, and somebody cut it off it.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Two ends of one piece of work. Somebody cut it once and put the piece in a box, and my mother has
> spent forty years handing out the rest.

## Writing & art notes

- **The lie is the page's centre and it must not be prettified.** Ira claims the knot as her own work
  because clause two is the only instrument she has and it requires an owner. Draw her face doing
  nothing. She is not proud of it and not ashamed of it yet — she is *recording*, and that is worse.
- **The clerk never reacts.** He writes. That is the Office's entire emotional range and it is what
  makes Panel 3 land. No eyebrow, no pause, no "interesting."
- **Nima's refusal is the chapter's first open defiance and it happens outdoors.** She **sets the tray
  down** — the one thing she has carried all morning — and that gesture is the refusal as much as the
  word is. The Office's answer is to *write it down*, which is worse than punishing her: it makes the
  refusal part of the record.
- **Kessa's beat is a delay, not an answer.** She has kept the question unasked for forty years on
  purpose. Her line — *"now you have said it in my stall, so it is in the room, and I will have to
  answer it"* — is the closest this series gets to Kessa admitting she chose not to know. She does not
  answer on this page.
- **The hook must be drawn literally.** Two lengths of thread on grey felt under a loupe: identical
  gauge, identical twist, and **neither casts a shadow.** The whorl at each end is the series
  fingerprint first seen on Chapter 001 Page 010. Do not render the thread as glowing, sparkling or
  magical — it is a physical fibre that fails to darken what is under it.
- **The mother stays off panel.** Her presence is thread, a teaching hand inferred, and forty years of
  arithmetic. The four notes are silent and the count is now ten days.
- **The queue is a witness throughout.** The counting women and the claimants do not intervene; they
  watch, which is what makes the alley a public room. Keep them in frame at the edges of panels 1, 2,
  4 and 5.
