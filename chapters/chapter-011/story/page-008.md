# THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 008

**Chapter title:** *The Fourth Step* / *चौथा पायदान*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the stall, the morning after
**Page type:** The counter-attack, bought and failed — 7 panels, ends at a full threshold

> Beats: the **morning reading with the Office standing in it** — the clerk follows his own copy and
> finds what he copied: not one hand; **the purchase** — the arithmetic claimant returns with a
> **reader-for-money**, and the verdict he buys is *hand: none*, which is true of every length of
> basin thread in existence, so it proves nothing and indicts everything; **the pivot** — the Office
> cannot refuse the entries, so it will *require* them: entered hands may be asked to work when the
> count runs short; **the telling** — the fold comes back light by thirteen, and through the day they
> arrive outside the stall and do not come in.
>
> **This page:** the trap that closes is made of the keeper's own protection.
>
> **Chapter 011 budget:** ONE chain-stop, **not spent on Page 008** — the chain-shaped problem
> (required hands) lands on the table here; the drawer stays shut. Loom never speaks. Mother never on
> panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009 — sixteen days now.

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the morning reading (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: morning, the counter. **Nandi** reads the day aloud in the voice she always uses.
**The grey clerk** stands in his own place with the night's copy in his hands, following her line by
line. He stops her.

**Image:** A man listening to his own handwriting read back to him.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> That one. Read it again.
>
> **NANDI:**
> Kind: basin. Year: nineteen. Hand: —
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(turning pages of his copy, faster than is composed)*
> None of the night's entries carry a hand.
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(reading on, unperturbed)*
> No. And the forty-one before them don't either. Kind and year are what basin thread holds. You have
> been copying basin thread for years, clerk. You knew that before I said it.

---

## PANEL 2 — Two-shot, the purchase (~16%)

**Camera:** Two-shot, midday: the **arithmetic claimant** back at the counter, and beside him a man
who does not belong to the basin — city shoes, city coat, a reader's loop of watch-chain brass at his
thumb, a small fee-book in knot-script. **A reader for money.** The claimant lays the Office's copy of
the entries on the counter like a man laying down a winning hand.

**Image:** The basin's first purchased reading, performed in the basin's own register.

> **ARITHMETIC CLAIMANT:**
> Bought and paid for. Read the night's lengths, city man.
>
> **PURCHASED READER:**
> *(lens up on its cord, the worked half-lengths under glass; professionally)*
> Kind: basin. Year: true. Hand: none.
>
> **ARITHMETIC CLAIMANT:**
> None! You hear it? No hand — no knot of theirs ever touched thread — no word was said — the entries
> are ash! Strike them!
>
> **IRA:**
> Read the forty-one.
>
> **PURCHASED READER:**
> *(a beat; he knows; he says it anyway, because he was paid for a reading and not for a side)*
> Kind: basin. Hand: none.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(quiet, to the row)*
> He has sold you the news that thread is thread. For a fee, he will say it again.

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the pivot (~15%)

**Camera:** Medium: the clerk, and he is not stupid. He watched the purchase fail and he watched it
fail *publicly*, and the Office's instrument came out of it untouched because the keeper built it out
of thread that cannot testify. So he does the thing the Office does instead. He closes his copy.

**Image:** The counter-attack that is not an argument: a rule, already written, arriving on time.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The entries stand. Reading stands — your rule, keeper, and mine.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> The basin's count runs short this month. Entered hands may be required.
>
> **IRA:**
> *(the floor not moving, and she feels it move)*
> Those entries protect them from claimants. They were never meant to —
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> They are on the roll, keeper. The roll is the roll.
>
> **NIMA:**
> *(from the counting table, still in the coat, not looking up)*
> Now you have twenty-two more hands where mine are. I could have told you what the book was for. I
> did tell you what the book was for.

## PANEL 4 — Close, the suspect (~12%)

**Camera:** Close: the clerk looks at Nima. The flags read to four people; three accounted. Nima
meets it without moving.

**Image:** The Office trying to count its losses and finding one of the losses is on its own roll.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The survey's flags were read to four people. Three of them are accounted for.
>
> **NIMA:**
> The fourth is on your roll, clerk. Entered. Declared. Everything I did yesterday, I did as Office.
> If the Office wants to ask me, it asks in writing, and my answer goes in the book.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She built that out of her own entry on Tuesday and I did not see it. You cannot discipline the roll
> without teaching the basin what the roll is for.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, the fold (~13%)

**Camera:** Close: **Kessa** at the box, unwrapping the fold to put the morning's remainder away. Her
hands stop. The fold is tied off — old-style knot, shadowless thread — and it is light. Thirteen
halves gone from a bundle that does not open for anybody.

**Image:** A custody that has held for forty years, opened from outside.

> **KESSA:**
> *(flat, and old, and certain)*
> Thirteen. Gone. Not cut — taken, and the fold tied after.
>
> **IRA:**
> Who has the fold but you?
>
> **KESSA:**
> One other person has ever had the fold in her hands, keeper. She came for her people's halves
> between dark and dawn, and she tied my knot behind her, because it is my knot and she does it
> properly.
>
> **KESSA:**
> The telling has started.

---

## PANEL 6 — Wide, the day's arrivals (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the afternoon, through the stall's front opening. One at first — a woman Ira does
not know, standing in the alley, a half-cut of grey thread held in her fist at her side. Then two
more. Then a handful. Nobody speaks to them. Nobody asks them in. They do not come in.

**Image:** The queue from Page 004, standing outside it.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Thirteen taught to stay out of rooms with books. They are keeping the teaching.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Each of them is holding the half that matches a line in my register. They are not here to be
> entered. They have been entered. They are here for the other thing my book does.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Nobody has told me what that is. Not Kessa. Not the thread. She has not spoken to me in nine
> years, and today she was ten feet from my counter.

---

## PANEL 7 — Wide, the threshold — FINAL HOOK (~17%)

**Camera:** Wide, from inside: the counter in the foreground, lamp lit against the dusk, and past it
the stall's one step down into the alley — and the alley full. Thirteen silent figures with grey
thread in their fists, not crossing, not leaving, waiting out the light. Ira's hand comes to rest on
the counter's edge.

**Image:** A keeper's geography: everything comes to the counter. The whole design of a counter.

> **KESSA:**
> *(putting out the box's lamp, not unkindly)*
> They are not waiting for the book, keeper. They are waiting for you.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Hands come to the counter. That is the whole design of a counter, and I have kept it for nine
> years, and the design has never once been wrong.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> One step down, into the alley, in the open, in the dusk. My mother taught in the open. It is the
> only thing she ever did that the Office could not find a rule for.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> I pick up the lamp.

## Writing & art notes

- **The purchase must fail honestly.** The purchased reader is not a fraud and must not be drawn as a
  villain: he is a professional performing a competent reading and reporting exactly what basin thread
  shows — nothing. His verdict is *true of all sixty-three lengths*, which is why it is useless to the
  claimant and dangerous to nobody but the Office's peace of mind. His fee-book is knot-script; his
  lens hangs on a cord and his mark is a plain brass thumb-ring — no §3.1 props anywhere near him:
  no watch, no chain fob, no printed signage. He is the first city instrument to enter the basin
  on-panel in this chapter.
- **The clerk's pivot is the page's load-bearing move.** He does not dispute the entries — he cannot,
  on the record, in front of the row, after Page 005's precedent. So he accepts them *and collects
  them*: entered hands may be required. The chapter's protection — clause two, the roll, the
  nameless entries — turns out to be the Office's lending ledger too. Ira's horror is structural
  again: her shield and their chain are one object. Play her line (*the floor not moving, and she
  feels it move*) as stillness, not shock.
- **Nima's two lines are the chapter's bill coming due in her voice.** She warned exactly this on
  Page 005 and was entered anyway; now the count runs short and the warning is policy. Her second
  beat (the suspect panel) is her being untouchable *by the Office's own construction* — discipline
  her and the Office admits its roll was used against it. Draw her still in the coat; the coat comes
  off on nobody's command, ever, in this chapter.
- **The fold is the mother's first move, discovered not shown.** Thirteen halves, gone between dark
  and dawn, and the fold tied off again in Kessa's own knot — done *properly*, by the one person
  besides Kessa who has ever held it. The mother never on panel, never speaking: her action is a
  lightness in a bundle and a knot tied from outside. Kessa's certainty in this panel is the oldest
  relationship in the chapter — forty years — and she is not afraid; she is *acknowledged.*
- **The thirteen's arrival is a held note, not an event.** No speeches, no faces in close-up, no
  individual identification — they are a count standing in an alley. They hold the halves the way the
  riveter held his: at the side, fist closed, not brandished. The teaching they are keeping is
  *silence in rooms with books*; the stall is a room with a book in it, and they will not enter.
- **The hook hands Ira the chapter's title.** The counter's design — hands come to it — has been the
  geometry of every page since Chapter 001. The fourth step is the keeper's: one step down, out of
  the counter's geography, into the open, where the mother taught. The lamp matters: whatever happens
  in the alley happens by *her* light, carried out, not the stall's. Do not show her step down on
  this page — the page ends on the hand and the pick-up.
- **Chain-stop discipline:** the drawer stays shut and the object is not drawn. The chain-shaped
  problem is now on the table in words (*entered hands may be required; the count runs short*) — that
  is what the budget is for, and the spending decision belongs to Page 009 or 010.
- **Continuity:** the purchased reader's verdict rhymes with Nandi's — *kind: basin, year: true,
  hand: none* — same words, different mouth, different fee. Four notes silent sixteen days: one
  caption's worth at most, or nothing. Rekhak stays off-panel and unspent — he is a city reading
  adjacent; resist using him here, the purchased reader fills the slot without burning him. Numbers:
  sixty-three lengths, twenty-two entered, forty-one named, thirteen told, four notes silent sixteen
  days, chain-stop ONE unspent.
- **The title beat:** if the chapter is *The Fourth Step*, the steps so far: the count taught in the
  open (1), the declaration queue (2), the entries in the book (3), the keeper leaving the counter
  (4). Do not print this list anywhere — it is a spine for the art, not a caption.
