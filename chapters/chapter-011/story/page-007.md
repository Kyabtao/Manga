# THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 007

**Chapter title:** *The Fourth Step* / *चौथा पायदान*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the stall's back room, one night
**Page type:** The night's work — 7 panels, ends at a copying desk at dawn

> Beats: **Nima's purchase pays out** — she knows the flagged doors because she held the hands, and
> she is not doing it for Ira; **the night's work** — nine of the twenty-two come in person and
> thirteen do not, and Ira enters all of them anyway, at a word that for thirteen of them is only
> thread; **the lie** — the years are true, but the roll will read them as years of enrollment, and
> the entries are false on their face; **the cost** — Nandi reads every entry aloud, including the
> false ones, and she knows exactly what she is reading; and at dawn the Office comes to copy.
>
> **This page:** the entries go in, and not one of them carries a name.
>
> **Chapter 011 budget:** ONE chain-stop, **not spent on Page 007** — it stays in the drawer. Loom
> never speaks. Mother never on panel, never speaks. Four notes silent since Chapter 008 Page 009 —

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the coat at the shutter (~13%)

**Camera:** Medium: night, the back shutter of the stall. **Nima** stands in the doorway and does not
come inside, and she is still wearing the Office's grey coat. She does not take it off. She recites
doors from memory — no paper, nothing written, her two flat hands empty at her sides.

**Image:** The thing she paid for with her hands is the thing that will keep twenty-two people off a
claimant's list tonight.

> **NIMA:**
> I held every hand they flagged this afternoon. I know which door each of them walked back to.
>
> **NIMA:**
> I'm not doing it for you. You put me in the book. A line in the book gets used. So use me.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She will not come inside and she will not take the coat off, and I understand both. I do not thank
> her. There is no line on any form in this basin for what to say to her.

---

## PANEL 2 — Wide, the first word (~15%)

**Camera:** Wide: the back room turned into a night counter. The first of the twenty-two — an old
riveter with burn-scarred knuckles — sits with his hand flat on the wood. **Ira** lays one of the
mother's grey lengths across his knuckles: width, not length. It matches. She cuts. **Nandi** reads
the mother's length in her pinned sleeves.

**Image:** The whole ritual, shown once, in full, in order.

> **IRA:**
> Half stays with you. Half goes in the book. If anyone ever asks, your word is the half in your fist.
>
> **NANDI:**
> Kind: basin. Year: sixteen. Hand: —
>
> **NANDI:**
> *(the same pause as last night)*
> There is no hand in it.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Clause two says a hand may be entered at its owner's word. It does not say the word has to be said
> in daylight, or to a keeper, or in words at all. Tonight his word is a width of thread and a
> half-cut in his fist, and my book will call it a word.

---

## PANEL 3 — Wide, the night as rhythm (~14%)

**Camera:** Wide: the night as an assembly of hands. The queue along the wall. Lamps burning down.
Ira measuring; Nandi reading; **Kessa** in the corner by the box. Small reading-balloons stack like
counting: *Kind: basin. Year: twelve. — Kind: basin. Year: nineteen. — Kind: basin. Year: eleven.*

**Image:** Nine of them come. Thirteen empty spaces in the queue that nobody fills.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Nine of the twenty-two come. Their word is spoken in my back room by lamplight, and it is theirs.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Thirteen do not come. She taught them too well. For thirteen there is only the thread, and the
> width of a hand that is not there.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The years are true — she counted true. But the roll will read them as years of *enrollment*, and
> the basin's book is not old enough to hold a year nineteen. Every entry tonight is false on its
> face. They will be true anyway. That is what my register is for. It does not find truth. It makes
> it.

---

## PANEL 4 — Close, the pause (~12%)

**Camera:** Close: Nandi's hands stop in the middle of the night. She holds a length she has not read
yet, sets it down, and aligns it with the finished ones instead of reading it. The only exchange of
the night happens over it.

**Image:** The reader counting the lie she is reading in.

> **NANDI:**
> How many more?
>
> **IRA:**
> Eleven.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> That is the whole of what we say for an hour.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> She has read for the Office every morning for years. She knows exactly what she is reading in
> tonight, and into whose book, and what it will be used for by morning. She keeps reading.

---

## PANEL 5 — Close, custody (~12%)

**Camera:** Close: **Kessa's** scarred hands passing one length out of the fold — one at a time, each
one accounted. The fold in her lap is visibly smaller than it was.

**Image:** A pawnbroker controlling disbursement, at one in the morning, for free.

> **KESSA:**
> One at a time, keeper. You get them as you need them. The fold comes back to me when it is done —
> all of it. The Office copies your book, not my box.
>
> **KESSA:**
> And the thirteen who didn't come — nobody tells them tonight. When it gets told, it gets told by
> the one who taught them to stay out of books.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She has not sat down all night, and she is not doing it for me either. Forty years of pledges
> taught her exactly where people sleep.

---

## PANEL 6 — Close, the last entry (~15%)

**Camera:** Close: the last length of the night. **Nandi** is holding her own. She does not hand it
over. She reads it herself, in the reading voice, with the book open in front of her — the woman who
reads the roll aloud every morning, reading herself into it.

**Image:** Her word was four words long, and she said it to a counter, and it is in the book now like
anything anybody ever said.

> **NANDI:**
> Kind: basin. Year: nineteen. Hand: —
>
> **NANDI:**
> Entered at the owner's word.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Her word was four words long. *That one is mine.* She said it to a counter with her sleeve pinned
> up, and it is in my book now, the same as if she had stood in a queue.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I write the line. She reads the line. Neither of us looks up while the ink is wet.

---

## PANEL 7 — Wide, dawn and the copying — FINAL HOOK (~19%)

**Camera:** Wide: dawn, grey coming up outside the shutter. Knock. The **grey clerk**, slate under his
arm, lamp out, morning shift. The register is open on the counter with twenty-two new lines in it.
Behind him, in her morning place, **Nandi** opens the big book and begins the day's reading in the
voice she always uses. The clerk does not know what he is copying.

**Image:** The Office copying its own supper out of the keeper's book, line by line, in its own hand.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> The numbers go in this morning. I'll copy the night's entries first.
>
> **IRA:**
> All of them go in.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(copying; he stops over a line)*
> Keeper. The basin's roll is not old enough for these years.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> I say nothing. The Office cannot disown its own rule in front of a witness; it taught this basin
> that itself, on Tuesday, in the square.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> By the time he finishes copying, the list of addresses in his other hand is already wrong.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> Twenty-two hands entered. Not one name. Somewhere in the basin this morning, a claimant is already
> asking what a name costs.

## Writing & art notes

- **The lie is a translation, and the page must be exact about it.** The mother's years are true —
  kind and year are *her* record and Nandi confirms them. What is false is what the roll will do with
  them: read a teaching-year as an enrollment-year, so twenty-two people appear to have been in the
  Office's book since before the Office's book could hold them. Every entry is false on its face and
  true anyway. This is Page 002's lie — *a true entry nobody made* — twenty-two times, and the page
  should feel the arithmetic of that and refuse to be relieved by it.
- **Nine and thirteen.** Nine come in person; their word is real. Thirteen do not come — the mother's
  teaching kept them out of rooms with books in them, and it worked. Ira enters them anyway, at a word
  that is only thread. Do not soften this: for thirteen of the twenty-two, *entered at the owner's
  word* is written about owners whose word was never asked and never said. That is the page's cost,
  and Kessa names the consequence: when it gets told, it gets told by the one who taught them.
- **Nima's beat is four sentences.** No forgiveness of the Office, no long gratitude, no embrace. She
  stands in the doorway, in the coat, recites doors from memory, states her terms — *use me* — and
  the page moves on. The coat staying on is the point: the Office is in the room while its roll is
  being loaded against it, and the coat is how.
- **Nandi's cost is real time, not retroactive.** After tonight, the question is no longer *did she
  know about the Page 003 entry.* She reads twenty-two face-false entries aloud, one at a time,
  knowing exactly what they are and where they are going. Her pause is one beat, her question is four
  words, and then she keeps reading — because stopping would be worse, and because she of all people
  knows what a roll is for. Both readings of her are worse now, and the page does not resolve them.
- **The year-count discrepancy is deliberate and unresolved.** Thread-years versus roll-years do not
  agree, the clerk says so on-panel (*the basin's roll is not old enough for these years*), and
  nobody explains it. It is ammunition for the Office (the entries are attackable on their face) and
  a mystery about the mother's count (how old is the teaching?). Leave it standing.
- **Panel 2 shows the ritual once and in order** — measure, cut, read, write — so Panels 3 and 6 can
  compress it. The twin is the chapter's new object: half in the fist, half in the book, the word
  made portable. Draw the entries as ink-line gesture and knot-script only; **no readable writing
  anywhere**, per canon — the register's lines are visible as marks, never as text.
- **The chain-stop stays in the drawer** and is not shown — it was shown last page; showing it again
  would beg the question. Four notes silent fifteen days: one line of caption or none — do not make
  a beat of it; Ira is not waiting for rescue and the page shouldn't either.
- **Kessa's custody is the page's stubbornest fact.** She hands out lengths one at a time and the
  fold comes back to her. The Office will copy the keeper's book, never the pawnbroker's box — the
  difference between the record and the evidence is going to matter.
- **Panel 7 is played as procedure, not confrontation.** The clerk is not an enemy at dawn; he is a
  man with a slate and a job, and the trap closes without anyone raising a voice. The hook's last
  caption names the next page's threat quietly: a nameless entry is only as good as the Office's
  willingness to accept it, and the basin has readers for money.
- **Continuity:** Rekhak remains unspent and off-panel. The mother is present only as the fold, the
  years, and Kessa's sentence about who does the telling. Loom never speaks; no §3.1 props; lamps
  only. Numbers: sixty-three lengths, twenty-two entered, nine words spoken, thirteen withheld, four
  notes silent fifteen days, chain-stop ONE and unspent.
