# THREADBORN — Chapter 11 — Page 005

**Chapter title:** *The Fourth Step* / *चौथा पायदान*
**Arc:** III — The Second School
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the Mendery counter, the terrace edge
**Page type:** The false declaration; the subtraction — 7 panels, ends on the arithmetic

> Beats: the **first false declaration** — a claimant tries clause two on a hand that is not his, and
> clause two contains no provision for refusal; the answer is a **reading**, performed by the woman the
> Office humiliated with it; **Nima discovers the specific cost of being on the roll**; and Ira sees
> that the second school and the survey are the same instrument pointed in opposite directions.
>
> **This page:** the keeper refuses nothing and stops a man anyway.
>

## PANEL 1 — Medium, the false declaration (~12%)

**Camera:** Medium: the Mendery counter, mid-morning, the day after the declarations began. A man is at
the front of the queue with a fold of cloth on the counter — knots in it, a good many of them, the work
of one hand over years. He is the man who stood at the back yesterday doing arithmetic. He has been
watching a woman declare her own hands and he has worked out what that is worth.

**Image:** A form being filled in, in the wrong hand.

> **CLAIMANT:**
> Enter them. At my word. Mine. I tied them.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He says exactly the words she said, in exactly the order, because there is a form and the form has a
> sentence in it and he has learned the sentence.

---

## PANEL 2 — Close, no line for no (~14%)

**Camera:** Close: the open register, the pen, Ira's hand not moving. Her eyes go down the clause as
written — the words she wrote herself, four days ago, to keep an honest woman safe. She is looking for
the line that lets her refuse.

**Image:** A form with no line for *no.*

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Clause two. *A hand may be entered at its owner's word.* Nothing in it about the word being true.
> Nothing in it about the keeper asking.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> I wrote it so the Office could not refuse an honest woman. I did not write a way to refuse a
> dishonest man, and I cannot add one now, because the Office is holding the copy.

---

## PANEL 3 — Medium, the reading (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira does not refuse. She calls for a **reading**. **Nandi** comes forward with the
bigger book, sleeves pinned the way she always pins them, and takes the knots out of the cloth — and
reads them aloud, which is the condition the schedule put on her and which she has never once
questioned. The grey clerk is present. The row is listening.

**Image:** The instrument the Office made as a humiliation, turned around and aimed back at the Office.

> **NANDI:**
> Kind: basin. Year: nine. Hand: —
>
> **NANDI:**
> Not this one's.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She stops there. Nandi can always name the hand — that is the thing the schedule took from her and
> could not use — and this time she does not name it.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Whether she knew the entry she read back yesterday was a lie, I still cannot tell. She has just
> refused to hand a woman's hands to a man for the second time in two days, and her face is a book.

---

## PANEL 4 — Medium, the row hears it (~13%)

**Camera:** Wider: the counter and the row. The claimant does not argue — he folds his cloth, nods
once, and goes. But three men at the back of the queue are writing on slates, and Ira knows exactly
what they are writing down.

**Image:** A man leaving without a fight, and three other men learning from it.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> *(writing)*
> Reading stands. Entry refused: declaration and reading do not agree.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> He does not argue. There is nothing to argue with — the Office has been insisting a reading is
> evidence since the first morning of the survey, and I have simply agreed with it in front of
> witnesses.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> But three of them are writing it down. There are readers in this city who will read a knot for money
> and say whatever name you are paying for — Rekhak's own deposit was recorded by one of them — and
> that is a thing I have just taught the row as well.

---

## PANEL 5 — Medium, Nima's cost (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: **Nima** at the counter with her tray, and the grey clerk reading her her own entry
back. She is on the roll now. He is not gloating; he is being exact, which is worse.

**Image:** A girl being told what the thing she chose actually costs.

> **GREY CLERK:**
> Now that you are on the roll: when the count runs short, an enrolled hand may be required. You will
> hold when you are told to hold.
>
> **NIMA:**
> I said I'd rather be a line in your book than a pair of hands you can borrow.
>
> **GREY CLERK:**
> You are the line. The line is what lets us borrow you.
>
> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> She paid for it with the exact thing she was protecting, and she is not stupid, and she does not cry.
> She sets the tray down and she counts the hands they put in front of her, and the basin can see her
> doing it.

---

## PANEL 6 — Medium, the terrace, the same geometry (~14%)

**Camera:** Medium: Ira at the low terrace wall above the basin, looking down. In the near ground the
teaching rows of the second school — people sitting in open daylight with thread in their hands, in
lines anybody could walk past. In the middle distance the survey's markers: the counted doors, running
from the fourth step toward the water-butt. **The same geometry.** She sees it and does not like what
she sees.

**Image:** Two instruments with the same shape, pointed in opposite directions.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Forty-one names on the roll. Sixty-three lengths in the felt. Twenty-two people taught in the open,
> in daylight, where anybody walking past could count them.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> The survey counts doors by walking past them. My mother taught twenty-two people where anybody could
> walk past. I have spent four days doing the same thing in the same square.

---

## PANEL 7 — Macro, the subtraction — FINAL HOOK (~19%)

**Camera:** Macro, straight down at the counter at the end of the day. Three counts laid out side by
side on the wood. **Left:** the survey slate, rows of abstract door-tallies, no lettering. **Middle:**
the unrolled Roll of Hands, forty-one abstract entries. **Right:** the row of grey thread lengths. Ira's
finger is doing the arithmetic on the wood, and at the bottom of the frame **twenty-two lengths have
been set apart in a row of their own.**

**Image:** A sum anyone could do, laid out in thread on a counter.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice):**
> Doors with hands in them. Hands with no entry. That is all it takes — anybody could do this sum, and
> the survey has been doing it one door at a time since Monday.
>
> **CAPTION:**
> Twenty-two of them. Every one taught in the open, every one with no year on the roll and no keeper,
> every one of them exactly what a claimant is paid to find.
>
> **CAPTION (smallest):**
> The survey reaches the end of the alley tomorrow.

## Writing & art notes

- **The false declaration must be boring.** This is the page's whole argument: the trap Ira made is not
  a dramatic one, it is a *procedural* one. He says the sentence, she looks for the line that says no,
  and there isn't one. No confrontation, no raised voice, no threat. A form is a form.
- **Panel 2 must show her reading her own clause with fresh eyes.** She is not looking for a loophole
  to exploit — she is looking for a refusal she believes she wrote, and discovering she never did. The
  horror is that she wrote it *well.*
- **The reading is the answer and it comes from the Office's own machinery.** The schedule forced Nandi
  to read every entry aloud as a humiliation; Ira simply uses it. The grey clerk ratifies it without
  expression in Panel 4 — the Office cannot disown its own rule in front of the row. This is the
  cleanest thing Ira has done all chapter and it costs her nothing, which should bother her.
- **Nandi's beat is a refusal she performs with a silence.** She can name the hand. She doesn't. That
  is the second time in two days she has declined to hand a woman's hands to a man, and the page still
  does not say whether she knew about the lie — the reader is meant to keep guessing, and Ira is meant
  to keep not asking.
- **Nima's cost must be exact, not cruel.** The clerk is not gloating. He is reading a rule. She has
  bought the right to be on the record by surrendering the right to say no, which is a genuinely
  terrible bargain and also the only one available. Do not let her cry and do not let her grandstand.
  She sets the tray down. She counts.
- **Panel 6 is the chapter's structural turn.** The second school and the survey are the same shape.
  This is the first time Ira understands that her teaching is not separate from the count — it is the
  count's feed. She has been running the school that makes her mother's pupils countable.
- **The hook must be drawn as arithmetic, not as revelation.** Three counts on a counter: abstract
  door-tallies, abstract roll entries, physical lengths of grey thread. No lettering on any of them.
  Twenty-two lengths set apart at the bottom of the frame. Ira's finger on the wood. No glow, no
  shadowless thread in this panel — this is a woman doing a sum.
- **The last line is eight words and must be the smallest text on the page.** No exclamation. The
  reader does the arithmetic themselves: twenty-two people, one alley, tomorrow.
- **Continuity:** Kessa is not on this page — she gave a date and no name and has nothing more until
  Ira asks her a better question. Rekhak's recorded readings and the translation room's invented
  entries are *named* here for the first time in the chapter, to be spent later. Four notes silent
  thirteen days. The mother is off panel; her presence is twenty-two lengths and the geometry of an
  open square.
