## IRA SUTAR — इरा सुतार  (PROTAGONIST)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/ira-sutar-ref.png)

```
Ira Sutar / Manavkin / Agnikhand / ash-grey braided, blank core / zero, and climbing /
Mend: cancel target debt instead of paying it / "He has sold you the news that thread is thread."
```
> **Page 008 note:** wins the morning and loses the month. The purchase fails on her testimony —
> *"Read the forty-one"* — and the clerk's pivot lands while she is still standing in the win: the
> twenty-two are on the roll, and the roll lends. Feels the floor move without moving. At dusk she
> picks up the lamp: the fourth step is hers, out of the counter's geography, into the open where her
> mother taught. Does not step down on-panel — the page ends on the hand and the pick-up.

## KESSA — केसा  (custody, acknowledged)
Full sheet (Ch. 001): [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa.md) · Art: [`../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png`](../../chapter-001/characters/kessa-ref.png)

```
Kessa / Kshudra / Agnikhand / knotted grey / light /
Appraise: reveal and cancel target debt; her knots cannot be cut /
"One other person has ever had the fold in her hands, keeper."
```
> **Page 008 note:** unwraps the fold at the box and finds it light — thirteen halves gone between
> dark and dawn, the fold tied off again **in her own knot, done properly.** Forty years of custody,
> opened from outside, and she is not afraid; she is *acknowledged*. She names the telling: *"The
> telling has started."* At dusk she puts out the box's lamp and gives the page its turn: *"They are
> not waiting for the book, keeper. They are waiting for you."*

## THE GREY CLERK — धूसर लिपिक  (the pivot)
No full sheet; Ch. 009–011. The Office's voice with a pen in it.

```
Grey Clerk / Manav / Agnikhand / grey, procedural / none — spends other people's /
Pivot: what cannot be refused can be collected /
"They are on the roll, keeper. The roll is the roll."
```
> **Page 008 note:** not stupid, not kind. Follows his own copy through the morning reading; watches
> the purchase fail publicly; cannot dispute the entries after Page 005's precedent — so he accepts
> them and **collects** them: the count runs short, entered hands may be required. Then tries to
> count his own losses, finds one of them on his own roll, and writes nothing. The chapter's best
> adversary does the Office's will without once raising his voice.

## THE ARITHMETIC CLAIMANT — हिसाब-वाला दावेदार  (recurring; bought and paid for)
No full sheet; Ch. 011 Pages 004, 005, 008. The man who does arithmetic at the back of queues.

```
Arithmetic Claimant / Manav / Agnikhand / grey, for hire / heavy — he spends other people's /
Arithmetic: watch one lawful thing work, then cost out doing it too /
"Bought and paid for. Read the night's lengths, city man."
```
> **Page 008 note:** watched a woman declare her own hands on Page 004, tried the form himself on
> Page 005, and now buys the strongest counter the basin has seen: a city reader, paid to read the
> night's lengths in public. His arithmetic is right and the answer sinks him — the thread cannot
> testify against anyone because it cannot testify at all. Not a villain: a man whose sums keep
> coming out true and meaning nothing.

## THE PURCHASED READER — ख़रीदा हुआ पाठक  (new; one page, probably more)
No full sheet; Ch. 011 Page 008. The first city instrument to enter the basin on-panel.

```
Purchased Reader / Manav / city / brass thumb-ring, lens on a cord / whatever the fee is /
Read for pay: a competent reading, honestly reported, honestly priced /
"Kind: basin. Year: true. Hand: none."
```
> **Page 008 note:** not a fraud and not drawn as a villain — he reports exactly what basin thread

> paid for a reading and not for a side."* His verdict rhymes with Nandi's — same words, different

## THE THIRTEEN — तेरह  (told; waiting)
No full sheet; Ch. 011 Pages 007–008. The mother's pupils who keep the teaching.

```
The Thirteen / mixed / Agnikhand / grey, half-cut in fist / various /
Keep: the teaching is silence in rooms with books — they will not enter /
No line on this page. They are not waiting for the book. They are waiting for you.
```

> **Page 008 note:** told overnight, by the only person who could have told them — each woke with a
> half-cut that matches a line in the register. They arrive through the afternoon and hold the alley

> stall is a room with a book in it, and they will not enter.

## THE MOTHER — माँ  (never on panel, never speaks)
No full sheet, by canon. Her presence on this page is a lightness in a bundle and a knot tied from
outside.

```
— / Manavkin / Agnikhand / shadowless, cut / unknown; never entered /
Tell: tell her people what was done for them, in her only language — thread, delivered /
"Do not enrol me. — S.M." — thirteen halves, between dark and dawn, and Kessa's knot tied after.
```

---

**Page note:** the morning reading happens with the clerk standing in it and he finds what he copied:
not one hand. The purchase fails honestly — *hand: none* is true of all sixty-three lengths — and the
pivot lands in the same hour: the entries stand, and the count runs short, and entered hands may be
required. Nima's bill comes due in her own voice, and she is untouchable by the Office's own
construction. The fold comes back light by thirteen, tied off in Kessa's knot. By dusk the alley is
full of people who will not come in. Hook: *"They are not waiting for the book, keeper. They are
waiting for you."* — and the smallest caption: *"I pick up the lamp."*
