## Synopsis

The Office's answer to Chapter 010 was a bounty and a copyist. Chapter 011 is what the basin did with
clause two — and what clause two did to the basin. It opens on the Mendery's step, where the knot the
mother lifted out of the register has been joined by **a second knot in an untrained hand**: somebody
in the basin is enrolling people without the keeper, and not in the dark. From that knot the chapter
builds a machine out of one sentence of the keeper's own law — *a hand may be entered at its owner's
word* — and then spends five pages watching everybody in the basin learn to use it, including the
people the Office pays.

The machine has three speeds. First the survey: doors with hands in them, hands with no entry,
resolved into a list of addresses and delivered to the water-butt — and when the day's entries are
read back, Ira has to say her own lie out loud in the machinery of Nandi's condition, and it costs her
nothing, which is what should bother her. Then the bounty-hunters: a counting woman walks into clause
two and makes it everybody's instrument; the arithmetic claimant tries it on a hand that is not his;
and when the clause turns out to contain no provision for refusal, the keeper refuses nothing and
calls for a reading — the cleanest thing she does all chapter, at no cost, which bothers her more.
Then the Office collects: *entered hands may be required*, the shield and the chain turn out to be one
object, and Nima — entered, lent, used — is the proof and the warning at once.

Against that, the counter-school works by night. Nima buys her way inside the count with her hands and
pays the basin back with the flagged doors; nine of the twenty-two come in person and thirteen stay
away, taught too well to enter a room with a book in it; and the keeper enters all twenty-two anyway —
at a word that for thirteen of them is only thread. The entries are false on their face (the mother's
teaching-years read as enrollment-years the basin's roll is too young to hold) and true anyway: *the
register does not find truth. It makes it.* Nandi reads the lie in, all night, including her own line,
and at the end of it she is standing in the count: *that one is mine.* The Office has been hearing an
unenrolled woman read its roll aloud every morning, and the keeper spent the whole chapter before she
understood what that made the Office's instrument worth.

The chapter turns in the open. The keeper crosses her own threshold — nine years of counter-geography
broken by her own foot — and the reading happens in the alley, by lamplight, where thread answers
thread and a secret read aloud stops being a handle. The Office does not argue; it counterpoints, with
a requisition read over the reading, and its last realization is the chapter's cruelest economy: a
roll-call by thread does not need names — *it has the other halves.*

So the finale spends the one thing the stall has never been asked to spend. The chain-stop is the
Loom's last knot — pledged at the counter unnamed in the Freedom Year, kept nine years, never read,
never explained, because explaining it would mean saying the pledger came at all. At the morning
count, with the Office obliged to ask the keeper to run the measuring, she ties the knot into the
count-cord instead: a link that fits every hand cannot pull. The purchased reader certifies it for two
coppers — *kind: all, year: all, hand: all* — the clerk writes **count not taken** like a man closing
a window, and the Office postpones, which is what the Office does instead of losing. The empty fold
comes back to Kessa, all of it. At dusk the school keeps its own lamp, with Nima teaching under it,
and the keeper's only line on the page is permission, blessing and surrender in four words: *leave the
lamp burning.* Nobody writes anything down.

## Canon rules added in Chapter 11 (binding forward)

- **The Loom's last knot exists.** The Loom never speaks — its final utterance was a knot, said in
  thread, that fits every hand. A chain containing a link that fits everything cannot pull. One home,
  ever; once tied in, it never comes back out. It now hangs in the Office's chest.
- **The Freedom Year pledge.** Someone came to the counter in the dark in the Freedom Year, left a
  fold of cloth, and gave no name. The pledge was the visit; the pledger was the mother; the keeper
  spent it at dawn in front of the whole basin.
- **Clause two is everybody's instrument.** *A hand may be entered at its owner's word* contains
  nothing about the word being true and nothing about the keeper asking. Declaration, false
  declaration, reading-as-refusal, entered-by-thread: the latch is public and cannot be un-publiced.
- **Readings are evidence, on or off the schedule.** The Office's own first-morning rule, cited back
  by the row and certified by a paid reader (*a reading is a reading*). Nandi closed the big book
  off-schedule — the first reader to end a reading.
- **Entered hands may be required** when the count runs short: the shield and the chain are one
  object. A by-length roll-call needs the keeper's register and the keeper's hands — the Office cannot
  run one without its adversary.
- **"Count not taken"** is a valid procedural outcome. The Office does not lose; it postpones.
- **Thread answers thread:** publication is protection. A secret read aloud stops being a handle; the
  twenty-two stopped being a list by becoming a roomful.
- **The twin:** a word made portable. Half in the fist, half in the book; entered hands can be called
  by their other halves.
- **Kessa's custody** was discharged in full: the fold came back, empty, all of it. The cloth stays
  in the box, and the box stays hers.
- **The coat:** Nima's Office coat stays on for the entire chapter and comes off on nobody's command.
