# THREADBORN — Chapter 04 — Page 006

**Chapter title:** *The Weight of Thread* / *धागे का बोझ*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the Knot & Nail + up-terrace (morning)
**Page type:** The confession's consequence — 7 panels, hook at bottom

> Beats: Page 005 — the stitch unties loop by loop, the sixth thread, Lekh's passing.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the morning (~14%)
**Camera:** Morning: the Knot & Nail's counter holds the coin-slate, the lockbox, and — new — a grey envelope
delovernight. No crimson seal. Grey wax only. A Council communication without the principal.

> **CAPTION:** Grey only. No crimson. The Council writes alone for the first time.

## PANEL 2 — Medium, the letter (~13%)
**Camera:** Kessa opens the envelope: a formal notice, grey ink. The Council acknowledges the lockbox's
contents and requests — not demands — a *schedule of custody*. A list of what is in the box,
signed by the custodian.

> **GREY CLERK (in the letter):** The Office requests a schedule of custody, signed by the
> custodian of record.

## PANEL 3 — Close, Kessa's response (~14%)
**Camera:** Kessa's hands: she sets the notice face-down on the counter — her refusal grammar — and then
does something new. She writes on the back of the notice, in Kshudra knot-script: one line, one
knot-pattern. The knot-script says: *open the box and count*.

> **KESSA (dialogue, dry):** If they want a list, they can open the box themselves.

## PANEL 4 — Medium, up-terrace (~13%)
**Camera:** Up-terrace: Rekhak at his desk, chain running, oath-link dark. Lekh stands at the rail — standing,
this one time, because the notice is in his hand and the cuff-chit in his sleeve has grown. The
notice bears Kessa's knot-script reply.

> **LEKH (dialogue, standing):** The custodian replied in knot-script. The Office cannot read it.
>
> **REKHAK (dialogue):** I can.

## PANEL 5 — Close, the reading (~14%)
**Camera:** Rekhak's fingers on the knot-script: chain-reader, now knot-reader. He reads Kessa's reply:
*open the box and count*. His chain hums. The oath-link is still.

> **REKHAK (dialogue, soft):** She says open the box and count.
>
> **LEKH (dialogue):** Will the Office comply?
>
> **REKHAK (dialogue):** The Office never opens what it cannot close.

## PANEL 6 — Wide, the basin afternoon (~13%)
**Camera:** Afternoon: the Knot & Nail works. Ira mends at the counter — a dock worker with a torn mark, the
routine work of the stall. The stitch on her palm trembles as she works, responding to the needle.

> **CAPTION:** The stitch trembles at the needle. It knows the needle is not its own.

## PANEL 7 — Macro, the stitch loosens — HOOK (~19%)
**Camera:** Ira's palm, mid-mend: the stitch loosens one more loop. The old thread — the sewer's thread — is
now visibly different from Ira's own mending thread. Two threads, two hands, one palm. The stitch
is *separating* from the palm's surface, loop by loop.

> **SFX:** *—*
>
> **CAPTION:** Two threads. Two hands. One palm. The stitch is separating. And the girl who was
> sewn shut is, loop by loop, being opened.

## Writing & art notes

- **The grey-only letter** is the Council writing alone — one wax, one hand — and Kessa's knot-script reply is the stall's own grammar, not something the Office can file.
- **The Office never opens what it cannot close.** The letter is a test of whether the stall answers, and the answer is the hook.
