# THREADBORN — Chapter 04 — Page 009

**Chapter title:** *The Weight of Thread* / *धागे का बोझ*
**Arc:** I — The Unspooling
**Sector:** Agnikhand — Ashfall Basin, the Knot & Nail (evening)
**Page type:** The mark reads — 7 panels, hook at bottom

> Beats: Page 008 — the stitch opened, the mark is visible, the thread is in Ira's hand.

---

## PANEL 1 — Wide, the evening stall (~14%)
**Camera:** Evening, Knot & Nail: the lockbox is closed. Ira's palm is open on the counter, the Sutra-mark
visible. Kessa's loupe is down, reading the mark the way she has read a thousand others — but
this one she has never seen before.

> **KESSA (dialogue, slow):** Projection mark. Palm. Closed. Dormant.

## PANEL 2 — Macro, the mark (~13%)
**Camera:** The mark extreme close: a fine dark pattern in the skin, dormant — not dead. The mark is closed
the way a bud is closed: waiting for the right season. The stitch was the winter; the opening is
the spring.

> **CAPTION:** The mark is not dead. It is dormant. It has been dormant since the day it was sewn
> shut.

## PANEL 3 — Medium, Kessa's reading (~14%)
**Camera:** Kessa's loupe on the mark: she reads the thread-pattern inside the closed mark. What she sees
makes her hands stop.

> **KESSA (dialogue, quiet):** The thread inside is braided.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue, quieter):** Two strands. One is yours. One is not.

## PANEL 4 — Close, the implication (~13%)
**Camera:** Ira's face: braided thread means Manavkin — humankin. Mixed blood. The mark carries two strands,
one of which was sewn into her palm before she could speak. The second strand is the sewer's.

> **IRA (dialogue, flat):** The sewer braided their thread into my mark.
>
> **KESSA (dialogue):** Before you could speak. Before you could choose.

## PANEL 5 — Medium, the weight (~13%)
**Camera:** Ira's hand closes over the mark — the gesture from Chapter 1, covering the stitch. Except now
the stitch is gone and the mark is beneath. The same gesture, the same hand, different knowledge.

> **CAPTION (Ira's voice, small):** The stitch is gone. The mark is here. The thread that held me
> shut is the same thread braided into what I am.

## PANEL 6 — Wide, the lockbox at night (~14%)
**Camera:** Night: the lockbox on the counter, Kessa's hand on the lid. Seven objects inside now: cut-end,
census, posting order, gift-thread, sixth thread, seventh thread (child's knot), and the
sewer's thread — the one that just came out of Ira's palm.

> **CAPTION:** Seven objects. One thread. One hand. One girl.

## PANEL 7 — Close, the basin sleeps — HOOK (~19%)
**Camera:** The basin at night: the Knot & Nail's lamp is low, the shutters are up. On the roof above the
stall, unseen by anyone, a small mark glows faintly in the dark — not the Spindle, not the Loom.
A projection mark, dormant, catching the light of a turning lattice overhead.

> **CAPTION (none spoken):**
> The mark catches the Loom's light. For fifteen years it was dark. Tonight it is not.
>
> **CAPTION (smaller):**
> The hand has opened. The mark has been seen. And the Loom, which does not speak, has noticed.

---


## Writing & art notes

- **Two strands, one palm** is the visual fact of the page: the braid inside the mark belongs to the sewer as much as to Ira.
- **The mark catches the Loom** for the first time. Keep the light coming from above; the mark itself never glows on its own at this stage.
